## 1. Meta’s new commercial terms will allow the scraping of news sites: experts {#story-1}
*#2 on HN · [niemanlab.org](https://www.niemanlab.org/2026/10/metas-new-commercial-terms-will-allow-the-scraping-of-news-sites-legal-experts-say/) · 189 points · 75 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50111852)*

Nieman Lab 报道称，Meta 更新的商业服务条款于 10 月 30 日生效，要求商业账户授权 Meta 使用「网页抓取器、机器人、蜘蛛、爬虫和用户代理」访问、索引、缓存并定期更新与其关联的域名和 URL，并允许 Meta 使用这些内容来「开发、改进、研究、训练」Meta 产品（其服务条款将 AI at Meta 定义为其中之一）。条款还允许 Meta 创建「测试凭证」，这似乎会绕过注册墙或付费墙；唯一的退出方法是停止使用 Meta 的商业产品。三位审查了这些条款的法律专家——Matt Topic（Loevy & Loevy）、James Grimmelmann（康奈尔大学）和 Benjamin Sobel（威斯康星大学）——称这场变动非同寻常；Grimmelmann 将其比作反垄断中的「搭售」，Sobel 将其与 Google 要求出版商同意 AI Overviews 的做法相提并论。Meta 表示这只是整合了已有的抓取语言，不会改变其爬取方式，robots.txt 仍然适用。报道源于 The Steampunk Explorer 创始人 Stephen Beale 的线索，他正在考虑在 10 月 30 日前关闭自己的账户。

**Discussion**: 最大的争论是这些条款在法律上是否真的有意义：amluto 最先发问，如果出版商并不拥有那些从第三方授权的内容，又怎能授予 Meta 这样的许可；kazinator 反驳说，同意抓取并不等于将第三方权利再授权——他将其比作同意搜查邻居的口袋，这对邻居的权利毫无影响——而 mopsi 则指出，图库图片许可证通常是不可再授权的。几位评论者（g-b-r、sandeepkd、incompatible）认为 Meta 本来就在爬取，这些条款主要是为未来诉讼准备的法律掩护，nananana9 补充说，要求合作伙伴授予许可会削弱 Meta 在其他场合关于训练无需许可的公平使用论点。拥有并投放 Meta 广告的 VladVladikoff 表示，他早已屏蔽了 Meta 的爬虫，并声称 Meta 将抓取器伪装成 Facebook 应用内浏览器用户；atomicfiredoll 报告称，生成链接预览的 oembed 机器人与其 AI 爬虫使用相同的 User-Agent，因此屏蔽其中一方会破坏另一方；zenincognito 称 MetaBot 是他们日志中最具攻击性的爬虫。关于用户能否真正离开，讨论中出现明显分歧——有人说企业会继续留在这个寡头垄断平台上，有人说这是一起可能的反垄断案件——nradov 和 kazinator 则反驳说，企业对企业条款与消费者点击式许可不同，抱怨的公司大可直接关闭账户。

**Worth reading**
- [VladVladikoff](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50113445): 作为 Meta 上的广告主兼商业主，称 Meta 将爬虫伪装成来自爱尔兰的 Facebook 应用内浏览器用户，并表示他将继续屏蔽该爬虫，不管条款如何。
- [nananana9](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50114667): 认为要求合作伙伴授予训练许可，可能反而成为 Meta 无需许可论点的证据，并有助于确立许可要求。
- [atomicfiredoll](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50113969): 指称 Meta 的链接预览 oembed 机器人与 AI 爬虫共用同一个 User-Agent，因此屏蔽抓取会破坏 Meta 产品中普通链接分享的预览。

## 2. '500-hour' Order of the Sinking Star is as complex and exhausting as it sounds {#story-2}

Polygon 的一篇上手印象，作者玩了 20 多个小时——由 Jonathan Blow 领导的 Thekla 工作室推出的开放世界推箱子（Sokoban）游戏——官方宣传为「500 小时」。游戏重制了四款获授权的独立推箱子作品（Alan Hazelden 的 Mirror Isles 与 Skipping Stones to Lonely Homes、Silver Spaceship Software 的 Promesst、Jonah Ostroff 的 Heroes of Sokoban），再把它们的机制在网格大地图的各个象限中组合起来。作者称赞谜题设计出色，但认为难度持续升级、缺乏起伏，常常分不清自己是卡住了还是已经走成死局，剧情平淡且评测版的对白配音是糟糕的占位音频，另外还提到 Blow 的政治言论以及部分原作者的疏远所带来的争议。

**Discussion**: 评论对「长度」分歧明显：danpalmer 称玩到 20 小时仍在表层但几乎每关都在教他新东西，是值得慢热的作品；npinsker 则认为 30 小时后小点子的重复感明显变强、后半不如开头精彩（danpalmer 回应说完成四条主线后确有同感）。怀疑者（dwroberts、__alexs、bob1029）把 500 小时视为营销，认为 Blow 的公式只是不断重复同一想法的变体，且不像 The Witness 那样一个发现能照亮整张地图。另一条主线是文章伦理之争：有人抱怨文章变成对 Blow 的人身攻击，随即被反驳——在谈到创作者之前已有约 1700 字讨论玩法，且对 Blow 的评价是克制而复杂的。技术向子讨论集中在自研 Jai 语言与引擎：没有脚本层、清洁构建约 2 秒编译完成，Jai 编译速度约 150 kloc/s，Blow 称目标是 1 mloc/s，语言 beta 预计在游戏修 bug 之后再公开。

## 3. RPKI now protects 70% of internet routes {#story-3}
*#4 on HN · [ipinfo.io](https://ipinfo.io/blog/rpki-protects-70-internet-routes) · 113 points · 34 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50112330)*

一篇来自 IPinfo 的 João Tomé 撰写的分析文章，利用 RPKI 数据衡量路由安全状况：RPKI 有效的路由前缀占比从 2025 年 9 月的 54.0% 上升到 2026 年 9 月的 70.3%，其中中国贡献了大部分增幅（其 IPv4 地址有效路由占比从 3.7% 猛增至 83.0%，约占全球按地址计算增幅的 60%）。文章指出，无效路由几乎无法传播（通常只被约 2 个观测点看到，而有效或无记录前缀约为 596 个），认为按国家统计的无效路由统计数据会严重夸大风险（西班牙的 28.2% 无效前缀在按观测点加权后降至 0.057%），并警告称，16.2% 的 RPKI 覆盖 IPv4 路由所在的 ROA 比已公告路由更宽松——这种漏洞曾被用于 2026 年 8 月 28 日针对 Hetzner 的 AS24940 的劫持事件以获取 TLS 证书，且符合 RFC 9319 的 maxLength 最佳实践。ASPA 路径验证也快速增长，从 131 个网络（2025 年 9 月）增至 3,272 个网络（2026 年 9 月），验证了约 3.5% 的路由。

**Discussion**: 讨论几乎完全集中在顶部的反对意见上，而非文章的测量数据：cowlevel 警示称，将俄罗斯允许的路由集中到一个位于荷兰的注册管理机构数据库中，并自动执行，会让荷兰政府（或任何政府）通过篡改该数据库来切断某个国家的网络连接。多名评论者对此进行反驳——gizmo686 认为，没有 RPKI 时，BGP 劫持已经可以更轻易地实现同样的结果，而且任何持续性的网络分区始终都是一个政治决策问题；gucci-on-fleek 则从实践角度指出，如果俄罗斯运营商关闭 RPKI 执行，这种断网最多只会持续几个小时，并援引 .de DNSSEC 故障为例。greyface- 提出，独立的 RPKI 信任锚可以成为替代选项，这对传统 IPv4 持有者也有用，而 bawolff 和 maxclark 则指出，RPKI 并非必需，因为政府已经可以施压运营商。在政治侧线上，jeremyjh 针对 daishi55 提出的“一个国家不应单方面切断另一个国家的网络连接”反驳道，“一个国家为什么会想这样做？”，其他人则以 ICC 制裁为例；另有数名评论者（avazhi、gigatexal、stouset）公开表示，切断俄罗斯的网络连接可以接受，mzajc 则反驳说，结果只会是一个分裂的互联网。cto 一旁还有一条实用评论：1473-bytes 报告称，其所在机构在让自有路由及下游客户路由通过 RPKI 验证方面取得了进展，并正在考虑采用 ASPA；而 0xbadcafebee 仅仅抱怨文章读起来像报税单。

**Worth reading**
- [gizmo686](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50113134): 若无 RPKI，BGP 劫持就能达到同样效果，而且任何持久性的断网始终是一个政治决策问题——对其他 ISP 是否会继续对俄罗斯进行对等互联做出权衡。
- [gucci-on-fleek](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50114530): 俄罗斯运营商只需禁用 RPKI 执行，因此中断只会持续数小时，援引 Cloudflare 关于 .de DNSSEC 故障的分析；并指出，注册管理机构单方面转移 IP 空间会更难撤销。
- [greyface-](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50113831): 独立的 RPKI 信任锚可以成为应对注册管理机构被干预的反制措施，并且会帮助目前无法参与 RPKI 的传统 IPv4 持有者。

## 4. What I self-host {#story-4}
*#7 on HN · [viceconsole.fyi](https://viceconsole.fyi/blog/2026/10/10/what-i-self-host/) · 417 points · 260 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50109715)*

A self-hoster's tour of his own stack: Jellyfin, Actual Budget, Audiobookshelf, Automatic Ripping Machine, Backrest/Restic, Beszel, Caddy, Cockpit, Forgejo, Frigate (8 IP cameras), Gonic, Gotify, Home Assistant, Homepage, Immich and MPD over Snapcast, all on a Beelink S12 Pro with an Intel N100, 16 GB RAM and a 512 GB SSD bought for EUR 180 (the same listing is now EUR 359), plus a 6 TB media drive. His rules: nothing except router-hosted VPNs is exposed to the public internet (his ISP uses CGNAT for IPv4), he prefers native binaries or systemd/Podman over Docker, and he deliberately keeps media at 720p to save CPU, RAM and disk. The post was prompted by HN questions about how all of this fits in 16 GB.

**Discussion**: The loudest disagreement is the author's dismissal of Docker: many commenters say Compose is exactly what makes self-hosting low-maintenance — one config per app directory, uniform logs/updates/backups, isolation from the host, trivial migration — while others side with the author or land on NixOS + Podman/quadlets as the middle ground; one calls the author's setup 「pretty ridiculous and seemed hard to maintain」. The claim that the *arr stack (Sonarr/Radarr/Lidarr/Prowlarr/SABnzbd) is a resource-hungry fragile mess is heavily disputed: measured idle figures of ~157 MB for Sonarr, ~159 MB for Radarr and ~330 MB for Jellyfin are cited, and multiple people report 5-10 years of set-and-forget operation, with the caveats that Lidarr's search broke for months and Readarr was retired. On databases, many argue SQLite is all a self-hosted app needs, while others defend the Postgres-per-app pattern (one notes Immich needs Postgres + VectorChord for natural-language photo search, e.g. 「a dog on a beach」). Hardware anecdotes split on the N100 — one user reports a Beelink that overheated, rebooted and wouldn't keep BIOS settings, others report cool, quiet years of service, and a used 8th-gen Intel NUC is offered as better value. Self-hosting your own email is covered as a cautionary tale (port 25 blocked by ISPs, Gmail bouncing mail, residential-IP blacklists), while several people say Jellyfin's clients — not the server — are its weak point for HDR, H.265, subtitles and frame pacing.

**Worth reading**
- [eleventen](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50110986): Measured counter-argument to the article's 「resource-intensive mess」 claim: Sonarr and Radarr idle at 157 MB and 159 MB with ~no CPU, Jellyfin at 330 MB, plus a single docker-compose.yml showing how the *arrs, Transmission and Jellyfin wire together.
- [don-code](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50112046): Twenty years of self-hosting email, with the receipts: an ISP that refused to unblock inbound port 25, Gmail silently bouncing mail the day SPF became mandatory, and a residential IP stuck on a policy blacklist it can never leave — he advises nobody start.
- [ww520](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50112020): A dissenting data point on the article's hardware pick: a Beelink N100 that overheated and rebooted intermittently, with BIOS settings that refused to stick and useless support, recommending used HP/Dell/Lenovo mini-PCs instead.

## 6. Any window from your Mac, on your iPhone and iPad {#story-6}
*#9 on HN · [github.com](https://github.com/Saffsanity/sill) · 123 points · 25 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50112739)*

Sill (github.com/Saffsanity/sill) is a free, open-source (Apache 2.0, by Noah Saffer) pair of apps that mirrors a single Mac window — or the whole desktop — to an iPhone or iPad and sends touch, trackpad, keyboard and Apple Pencil input back. The Mac side lives in the menu bar, captures with ScreenCaptureKit, encodes HEVC via VideoToolbox on the hardware encoder, and streams over TLS 1.3 with no account and no server, supporting LAN, USB cable, peer-to-peer Wi-Fi, or a user's own VPN such as Tailscale. Requires Apple silicon on macOS 14+ and iOS/iPadOS 17+; pairing arrived in Sill for Mac 0.4.0 / iOS 0.5, and the project warns that 0.3.1 and earlier made local, cable and wireless connections unencrypted and unpaired.

**Discussion**: The first thread is a derail: people want the reverse app (driving an iPhone from a Mac), and the answer is that Apple's iPhone Mirroring is unavailable in the EU, laggy for some, and only partly replaced by Xcode's Device Hub on iOS 27/macOS 27; several agree typing on iPhones keeps getting worse. A recurring counterpoint is that Apple's Sidecar already does this on iPad, though commenters note Sidecar can't stream an individual window, requires the same Apple ID on all devices, and won't work over a VPN — touch and pen support was only added recently. Others frame Sill as a low-friction alternative to VNC-for-iOS tools: one user reports it far faster and more seamless than VNC and says it already helped twice in a day, while another notes RealVNC failed to connect from iOS to macOS recently and that Screens 5 now costs $5/month, $30/year or $200 lifetime. A side argument broke out over whether a commenter recommending the app (and his own Cling/Clop utilities) is a spam account, with others defending him as a real developer with prior successful HN posts writing in a second language; alternatives like Astropad Workbench were also raised, and no one disputed the core claim of good latency.

**Worth reading**
- [alin23](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50113416): A user says it is much faster and more seamless than VNC and helped him twice on day one, and describes discovering the app through a short AI-made demo video.
- [isomorphic](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50114550): Argues Apple should ship a basic Screen Sharing client for iOS, reports RealVNC failing from iOS/iPadOS to macOS recently, and cites Screens 5 pricing ($5/month, $30/year, $200 lifetime).
- [defrost](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50114665): Defends the app's promoter against the spam accusation, noting years of successful personal-project submissions on HN and English as a second language.
