## 1. Goldman Sachs Is Pulling Claude Code from Its Bankers and Traders {#story-1}
*#1 on HN · [thestateofai.com](https://www.thestateofai.com/news/goldman-sachs-claude-code-traders-bankers) · 220 points · 142 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50415628)*

A State of AI write-up (aggregating a Bloomberg report) saying Goldman Sachs updated internal guidance in October to restrict Anthropic's Claude Code and Microsoft's GitHub to its ~12,000 engineers out of ~47,000 staff, cutting off traders and bankers who had used the agents to watch client flows, model positions and build decks. It notes Goldman's CIO Marco Argenti had praised trader 'vibe coding' in July, that Goldman restricted Claude in Hong Kong in April, and that Goldman is a lead underwriter on Anthropic's IPO (S-1 filed October 26, expected Nasdaq listing as ANT before end of November). The piece argues the risk logic — unreviewed code with the user's own entitlements, information barriers, token costs — was sound but that the bank went about it badly by yanking access with no replacement.

**Discussion**: The largest thread is about cost and contracts, not capability: several commenters say Anthropic caps subscription/Team plans at 150 seats and forces larger orgs onto API-rate billing, so consumption overages are structural, not misuse; one says Google's flat-rate Gemini billing with throttling is winning non-engineering staff as a result, others defend the pricing as what the models are worth. A second camp reads the timing as IPO-motivated — Goldman is underwriting Anthropic — though several push back that underwriting and internal tooling decisions sit in separate, deliberately walled-off groups, and one argues a real operational incident (an agent placing or booking an order under a trader's account) is the likelier trigger, noting strats kept access. Bank-domain commenters say the real issue is stale, unsupported trader-built tools crossing the wall: missing source control, no deployment path, entitlement and Chinese-wall violations (a script that summarizes proprietary email and serves it to the desk), and engineer teams stuck 'improving' thousands of prototypes. Others are sympathetic to the traders, arguing the right answer is a sandboxed/abstracted environment rather than a ban, with a side debate over whether such environments inevitably collapse back into 'take it or leave it' software. There's also a correction thread: the Bloomberg original and fa-mag mirror are the real sources, and State of AI is characterized as an AI-written SEO site.

**Worth reading**
- [legitster](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50416136): Claims their org is clawing back Claude access over chronic billing overages, that Anthropic won't offer a normalized rate, and that Gemini is displacing it by offering flat billing with throttling instead of overage bills.
- [steveBK123](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50416113): Walks through the concrete compliance failure modes of trader/banker-built apps: an unmonitored channel across the Chinese wall, or an 'summarize my email' app that quietly breaks data entitlements — a huge use case among finance non-engineers.

## 2. Outlook.com Is Quietly Rejecting Email from Domains Without DMARC {#story-2}
*#2 on HN · [mailroute.net](https://mailroute.net/blog/outlook-rejecting-email-without-dmarc) · 24 points · 12 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50416158)*

A post by Thomas A. Johnson, founder and CEO of the mail-relay vendor MailRoute, reporting that since November 5, 2026 Outlook.com, Hotmail, Live and MSN have been intermittently rejecting mail from domains that publish no DMARC record — including ordinary low-volume senders, not just the 5,000-messages-a-day bulk senders covered by Microsoft's May 2025 rule (550 5.7.515). Affected senders get a terse 「550 5.7.1 Message rejected: Message lacks SPF/DKIM/DMARC Auth Records」 bounce with a per-message AS number, even when SPF and DKIM are fine, and delivery is inconsistent — one message lands, the next bounces. The proposed fix is a single TXT record, 「v=DMARC1; p=none」, which changes nothing for other receivers; the piece ends in a pitch for MailRoute's own relay.

**Discussion**: Nobody disputes that the problem is real: the poster says domains he manages with valid SPF and DKIM but no DMARC started bouncing over the weekend, and several others report the same. The main complaint is the silence — Microsoft published no postmaster notice or service-health entry, and one admin grumbles about having to mitigate it for hundreds of customers on a weekend. A recurring objection is that a p=none record proves little when SPF and DKIM are already aligned, and that requiring it adds pure busywork; a sharper version argues that two corporations should not get to decide who may send email, and that unclassified mail should be marked as spam rather than refused outright. Countering that, some commenters say misconfigured email DNS is astonishingly common and that authentication is now simply the cost of doing business, and one long-time self-hoster adds that his clean setup's mail to Hotmail addresses has been vanishing with no bounce at all.

**Worth reading**
- [xmddmx](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50416159): The submitter confirms first-hand that domains with proper SPF and DKIM but no DMARC record began bouncing this weekend.
- [anonymous908213](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50416506): Argues the real issue is policy: two corporations shouldn't control who can send email, and unauthenticated mail should be spam-filtered, not refused.
- [daneel_w](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50416511): Reports weeks of mail to Hotmail recipients silently disappearing with no bounce from an otherwise clean, self-hosted setup.

## 3. Llama-modchip: 284B DeepSeek on 64GB RAM, no GPU {#story-3}
*#5 on HN · [github.com](https://github.com/synchopate/llama-modchip) · 281 points · 99 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50411961)*

llama-modchip 是一组针对 ggml/llama.cpp 的内核补丁、量化转码器和运行时工具，目标是让稀疏 MoE 模型完全跑在系统内存里。它宣称把 DeepSeek-V4-Flash（284B 总参数、13.17B 激活）从 76.87 GiB 压缩到 56.56 GiB（1.71 bpw），从而连同 256K KV cache 一起锁进 64GB Linux 机器的约 62.5 GiB 可用内存，不触发 swap，在 Ryzen 9 9955HX 上解码 9.1–14.7 tok/s。主要卖点是 AVX-512BW 四级寄存器 + L1/L2/L3 多列 tiling（prefill 25.0→44.36 tok/s，+77%）、一个 1.64 MB 的 Zobrist n-gram 自推测 drafter、/dev/shm 的 KV 快照（18.9–32.6 ms 恢复），代价是引入自定义张量类型 GGML_TYPE_Q2_0（type ID 40），未经补丁的 llama.cpp、Ollama、LM Studio 会直接拒绝加载。

**Discussion**: 最有价值的部分是几个人真的照着 README 跑了，结果都失败了：aappleby 在 Ryzen 7945HX 上测 Nemotron，说除了 prefill 更快，CPU 解码速度和原版 llama.cpp 差不多；ar-nelson 的 Xeon 被脚本判为「Tier A」，但 modchip 默认只装 Nemotron，手动装 DeepSeek 后先是张量形状不匹配报错，再用随包的二进制直接 Illegal instruction 崩溃，结论是「完全不知道怎么跑起来」。adrian17 质疑文档里的数字自相矛盾——76.87 GiB 其实是 unsloth IQ1_S 的 82.5GB，而被称作 IQ2_XXS 的量化实际有 91GB——并指出最终 GGUF 只有 unsloth IQ1_S 的 75%，还砍了注意力层精度、把每层 6 个专家降到 4 个，怀疑质量不可用。围绕 2-bit 量化吵得很开：一派引用 TUM 的量化可靠性论文称 4-bit 是性价比拐点、再低性能会显著下滑（也有人纠正该论文不覆盖当前主流的 QAT，不能一概而论），另一派说大模型吃得住激进量化，DeepSeek v4.1 Flash 在 2-bit 下仍可用，比在小模型上跑 4-bit 强。另有 kennywinker 与 senordevnyc 的长辩论：前者算出约 800 美元的 E5 工作站当量成本、质疑订阅的必要性，后者反驳说按「智能密度」折算后 Opus 5.5 级别的质量省下的人力成本远超每月 200 美元。几乎一致的共识是 README 是严重的 AI 生成「slop」，emoji 和术语堆砌到几乎不可读，反而要靠评论区才搞懂这项目到底在做什么。

**Worth reading**
- [aappleby](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50416358): 实测者报告：在 16 核 Zen 4 + 96GB DDR5 上，Nemotron 的解码速度与原版 llama.cpp 基本一致，只有 prefill 明显更快，且 setup 里根本没有 DeepSeek 选项。
- [ar-nelson](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50416444): 完整复现失败记录：readiness 脚本给出 Tier A，但 modchip 不安装 DeepSeek，手动加载报 ffn_gate_tid2eid 张量形状错误，随包的 llama-cpp 二进制直接 illegal instruction。
- [adrian17](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50415077): 拆穿文档数字：76.87GiB 实为 unsloth IQ1_S 的 82.5GB，作者疑似混淆了 IQ2_XXS 与 IQ1_S，且「MoE pruning」其实指每层只跑 4 个而非 6 个专家。

## 4. Writing as a thinking tool {#story-4}
*#6 on HN · [muratbuffalo.blogspot.com](http://muratbuffalo.blogspot.com/2026/11/writing-as-thinking-tool.html) · 191 points · 65 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50412067)*

A blog post by Murat Buffalo arguing that writing is not the record of thought but the machinery of it, built around Manuel Blum's line that the only difference between a finite automaton and a Turing machine is 「paper and pencil」. He likens drafting to out-of-core execution: reviewing a paper he thinks he understands, then finds he grasped maybe 60% until he writes the review, and a 2018 post connecting Paxos and Nakamoto consensus became the seed of a research paper. His worry is LLMs: Bezos claimed a six-page memo cannot be written without clear thinking, but a model can now produce six pages with no thinking at all, which makes writing your own prose a widening comparative advantage. His advice: freewrite in bulk without judgment, then edit ruthlessly as if a stranger wrote it, keep prose plain (Strunk and White, Vonnegut), write for yourself, and put writing on the calendar instead of waiting for inspiration.

**Discussion**: The thread is broadly sympathetic and mostly extends the thesis to code: several engineers say the real work is understanding the problem and that the volume of code they ship in a year could be typed in a week, so letting a model write it removes the step where the design gets worked out. A cluster of commenters describes middle-ground workflows — have the AI critique your own draft rather than write it, or force it to explain and prove its output — while a stricter faction argues even AI feedback is a slippery slope away from your own voice. The sharpest disagreement is over the automaton analogy: a Turing machine with a finite tape is just a finite automaton, so the real ingredient is an effectively unbounded supply of paper, which one reply turns into practical advice to buy boxes of cheap pens and notepads rather than one nice notebook. A few dissenters push back on the whole premise — one says school-forced writing made them avoid notes entirely and they have learned everything without it, and another notes writing can become pseudo-rationalizing over-analysis, linking a PubMed study on the downside. Side threads wander into stationery (Rhodia 5mm grid pads, Uni-ball Jetstream SXN-210) and colour-coded note systems.

**Worth reading**
- [raincole](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50414312): Points out the analogy is technically broken: a Turing machine with a finite tape is equivalent to a finite automaton, so the difference is unbounded space, not paper per se.
- [fcoury](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50415313): Describes a personal skill that, after an agent writes a feature, replays the meaningful diffs and has him retype them by hand — slow, but used for critical work to imprint understanding.
- [baxtr](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50414341): Counter-argument to the thread's consensus: for highly analytical people, writing can become a way to elaborate thoughts instead of testing them, leading to over-analysis and pseudo-rationalizing, with a PubMed reference.

## 5. The Reconstruction of Torn Documents {#story-5}
*#8 on HN · [bundesarchiv.de](https://www.bundesarchiv.de/en/stasi-records-archive/the-reconstruction-of-torn-documents/) · 65 points · 39 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50389923)*

A FAQ-style page from the German Federal Archives (Bundesarchiv) explaining the state of efforts to piece back together the roughly 16,000 bags of hand-torn Stasi files — 2,500–3,500 fragments per bag, an estimated 400–600 million fragments covering 40–55 million pages. The computer-assisted 「virtual reconstruction」 project run with Fraunhofer IPK since 2007 was wound down: scanning stopped in 2014, reassembly in 2016, and although the e-Puzzler software was demonstrated to work in principle in 2013 it never hit the target 80% automated reconstruction rate or became viable for mass processing, after about €6.5 million spent. The archive says the old contract is being terminated, a requirements catalogue and market survey will look for new vendors, and manual puzzle-and-glue reconstruction continues in the meantime.

**Discussion**: Much of the thread pushes back on the page's flat claim that shredded paper could not be reconstructed, citing the 2011 DARPA Shredder Challenge, the reconstructed shredded US embassy documents from Tehran, and the point that the material was only declared unrecoverable and then burned in 1991. Others defend the archive: bulk burning is genuinely hard without forced air (one commenter digs up an NSA emergency-destruction procedure layering paper with nitrate oxidiser), and scale — millions of fragments, government procurement — makes 「possible in principle」 very different from feasible in practice. Several commenters argue modern ML and cheap scanning would make short work of it and that the whole thing is a contracting or political failure rather than a technical one; a €100-per-bag digitisation estimate is shot down by people who point out each bag holds thousands of tangled scraps, and one former Pentagon staffer describes what real burn bags were like. On cost, the thread splits between calling the €6.5 million wasted and arguing it was a reasonable expected-value bet on a uniquely hard problem, with a comparison drawn to the €3.5 million, six-partner EU RePAIR cultural-heritage project. A recurring caution is legal rather than technical: the fragments contain personal data on Stasi victims, so open crowd-sourcing or publishing scans is not straightforward under the Stasi Records Act.

**Worth reading**
- [Archelaos](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50415709): Notes that the snippets contain personal data and often false statements about Stasi victims, and that the Stasi Records Act (StUG) strictly governs who may access what — a legal obstacle to any open, crowd-sourced reconstruction.
- [bradknowles](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50415591): First-hand account from someone who handled classified burn bags at the Pentagon from 1989–1995, describing their size and construction and noting they held whole sheets, not shreds.
- [esafak](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50414685): Links a 2012 BBC article with actual photographs of the fragments, and argues the bottleneck is financial or political, not technical, since the ML was adequate a decade ago.

## 7. I'm not a fan of Deno's security model (2024) {#story-7}
*#10 on HN · [goldstein.lol](https://goldstein.lol/posts/deno-security/) · 52 points · 22 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50413769)*

这是一篇博客文章，论证 Deno 的权限系统是一种伪沙箱：它过滤标准库调用，而不是拦截系统调用，因此一旦运行任何不走 Deno 标准库的代码，这层保护就会失效。文章给出两个具体漏洞：一是权限提示直接打印在同一个终端上，恶意程序可以用转义序列隐藏或操纵该提示，或者诱使终端向 stdin 写入 “y”；二是 --allow-run=uname 只按 PATH 解析，所以脚本可以传入自己的 PATH 或 LD_PRELOAD（Deno 后来通过禁止 LD_* 环境变量补上了这一个缺口），而且像用 VIMINIT 控制 $EDITOR 这类情况，任何单个命令权限都可能被绕过。文中引用了一位 Deno 开发者的说法，即该模型主要防范的是“不够聪明的恶意代码”，并建议直接用 bwrap 之类的方案进行沙箱隔离。

**Discussion**: 评论者赞同文章的技术批评，但争论 Deno 这套方案到底算不算净收益：有人说它至少是一种尝试，而 Node 长期没有提供任何东西，其他人则反驳说 Node 现在有了权限模型，而它自己的文档同样承认无法阻止恶意代码；有一条回复说，比什么都没有更糟的情况，就是让用户产生虚假的安全感。几条评论转向 Deno 的处境：官方支持只剩一年，以及它决定优先做 Node 兼容，而不是先做更细粒度的按依赖隔离，结果让 Bun 从 Anthropic 那里获得了更多关注。一条真正的外行追问引出了一些具体建议，讨论如何在已经打印了不可信输出的终端里安全地请求敏感输入：只去掉 ASCII 控制字符并不够，还需要考虑整段 ANSI 序列，以及双向覆盖和其他 Unicode 控制字符。几位评论者表示应改用操作系统层面的机制，而不是运行时层面的权限，还有一条评论纯粹是在抱怨标题里带个人观点。

**Worth reading**

## 19. Person of Interest {#story-19}
*#28 on HN · [krthr.co](https://krthr.co/person-of-interest/) · 266 points · 174 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50412318)*

**Discussion**: The thread is overwhelmingly appreciative but almost everyone repeats the same caveat: seasons 1–2 are a CBS case-of-the-week procedural in the CSI/NCIS mould, and the real show — a hard-SF story about two competing ASIs fighting a proxy war through human agents — only emerges later, so several people link watch/skip lists (a Tumblr 「good bits」 guide, the r/PersonOfInterest wiki) to shorten the slog. There is real disagreement about depth: some argue the show only ever offered a 「good」 AI and an 「evil」 one, while fans counter with long accounts of how the Machine was raised on dozens of failed variants, sandboxed behind 24-hour state resets, and escaped them, versus Samaritan being worshipped rather than taught. ## 20. Finding Xanadu: Inside a JRPG classic translation 15 years in the making {#story-20}
*#32 on HN · [readonlymemo.com](https://readonlymemo.com/legend-of-xanadu-pc-engine-fan-translation-interview-making-of/) · 65 points · 14 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50378974)*

A feature from the ROM newsletter (readonlymemo.com) on the long-delayed English fan translation of Nihon Falcom's PC Engine RPGs The Legend of Xanadu (1994) and Xanadu II (1995). Translator SamIAm started in 2011 with hacker EsperKnight but hit a wall reinserting text; retired game programmer Elmer joined in 2015 and wrote a full script decompiler and recompiler plus a more efficient SWD5 compression scheme to free memory, since Falcom interleaved code and strings and the English script ran up to 50% larger than the Japanese. The volume was roughly 240,000 Japanese characters for the first game and 95,000 for the sequel, and a planned volunteer English dub — needed because cutscenes have voiced dialogue but no subtitles — eventually gave way to inserting new code and audio directly into each cutscene.

**Discussion**: Most of the thread is a fight about localization philosophy rather than the article itself, sparked by a line in the piece where a translator turns 「Sacred Alcohol of Stamina」 into a Van Buren Coffee joke: one camp calls such rewrites fanfic-tier vandalism that permanently distorts how Western players read a game, citing the DeJap Tales of Phantasia script and Castlevania's Lord of the Rings item renames. Others argue that literal translation is a layman's fantasy — language carries tone and intent that cannot be carried one-to-one, and the classic Tina-to-Terra rename in FF6 shows fidelity to intent sometimes means changing the words. A middle position agrees intent is the standard but says adding obscure jokes nobody catches fails even that test. Away from the argument, commenters watched gameplay footage and praised the see-through UI, with some warning that the busy moving colors bother sensitive players, and one developer described using LLMs to decompile and translate Uncharted Waters IV: Porto Estado.

**Worth reading**
- [falsaberN1](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50413277): Argues that 「more interesting」 localization liberties like the Van Buren Coffee rename permanently skew the Western discourse around a game, and cites DeJap's Tales of Phantasia script as damage that outlived better official translations.
- [gnulinux](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50414885): Pushes back hard on the 「just translate the thing」 position, arguing that translation is choosing what to sacrifice and that systematically flattening idioms produces dry, unidiomatic text that loses the author's intent.
- [gavinray](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50416118): A developer describes their own LLM-assisted decompilation and translation of Uncharted Waters IV: Porto Estado, with plans to publish the patch.
