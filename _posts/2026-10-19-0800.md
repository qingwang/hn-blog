## 1. Rabbits were a medieval royal luxury in England until the Black Death {#story-1}
*#1 on HN · [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-10-rabbits-medieval-royal-luxury-england.html) · 33 points · 7 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50131315)*

A phys.org republication of a The Conversation piece (by Alex Brown) tracing how the European rabbit, not native to the British Isles, went from a rare aristocratic luxury to a countryside commonplace. Evidence cited includes a 1176 tithe dispute over rabbits in the Scilly Isles, Henry III gifting 10 live rabbits from his Guildford park to Geoffrey Despenser in 1235, and rabbit meat costing four to five times the price of chicken; after the Black Death (1346–1353) raised survivors' living standards, rabbit meat and fur spread to wealthier peasants and craftsmen, prompting complaints about crop damage and the 1390 poaching ban that followed the Peasants' Revolt. It also covers medieval marginalia — the Smithfield Decretals (illuminated in London in the 1340s) shows rabbits trying, executing and killing their human and greyhound hunters — as a forerunner of Monty Python's Killer Rabbit of Caerbannog.

**Discussion**: The thread is small and tangential rather than a debate about the article's history. The one question raised asks for rabbit dishes where the meat is the star rather than a blank canvas: the suggestions were confit or cassoulet, and paella, which is commonly made with rabbit in Spain. The most substantive contribution is a note on Australia, where rabbit was a staple food until the myxoma virus was deliberately released in the 1950s, causing myxomatosis and collapsing the population. Another commenter explains the origin of the South Sydney Rabbitohs' name — 「Rabbitoh」 was a put-down for a poor salesman hawking rabbit meat, fitting the club's poor inner-city district. There is no real disagreement anywhere in the thread.

**Worth reading**

- [lmpdev](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50149050): Explains that 「Rabbitoh」 was originally a derogatory term for someone selling rabbit meat, which became the South Sydney Rabbitohs' name.

## 2. Hybrid Reasoning Agents: LLM agents that think in latent space, act through text {#story-2}
*#2 on HN · [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18861) · 340 points · 163 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50145337)*

An arXiv paper (2610.18861) from Jiaru Zou, James Zou and colleagues at Together Computer, with code at github.com/togethercomputer/HybridAgent and weights on Hugging Face. It proposes HybridAgent: a lightweight 「HybridLink」 that switches between latent (vector) reasoning and text/tool-call generation inside one generation process, plus a continuous working memory that keeps hybrid context across turns, and two post-training stages, HybridSFT and HybridRL. Across 3 model scales and 11 benchmarks (math, science, medicine, search, agentic), the authors report a 10.4% average gain over frontier text-based and latent agent baselines, with 35.5–54.7% fewer tokens, 1.8–2.6x faster inference and 2.7–3.7x cost savings.

**Discussion**: The thread splits cleanly between capability and monitorability. One camp argues latent reasoning isn't new — looped transformers and 「continuous chain of thought」 have been researched for years, and several suspect frontier labs already ship something like this (commenters note OpenAI encrypts its thinking blobs, and infer from the blob size that they are probably still discrete tokens, not continuous vectors). The other camp, the loudest, is about safety: if the reasoning trace is no longer text, you can't audit it, and a paper-adjacent suggestion of using an external critic to assign step-level credit to latent spans gets read as reinforcement learning over a chain of thought, i.e. the 「Most Forbidden Technique」 that trains the model to hide its real reasoning — though one reply pushes back that this isn't what the paper actually does, and another corrects the claim that labs only monitor CoT to police users. Hands-on reports are the most concrete counterweight: two commenters ran the released 8B and 14B models in llama.cpp and hit infinite loops on trivial prompts (「Who is the pope?」) in 8-bit and BF16 alike, suggesting a llama.cpp/runtime incompatibility rather than quantization. Mixed in is a long, mostly off-topic subthread about whether humans think in language at all, and whether 「reasoning」 is the wrong word for what LLMs do.

**Worth reading**
- [spijdar](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50146035): Points out that the paper's own text proposes an external critic assigning step-level credit to latent reasoning spans during rollout — which, unlike mere latent thinking, is training the model against its chain of thought, the actual 「Most Forbidden Technique」.
- [SwellJoe](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50146330): First-hand replication of another commenter's failure: the 14B model in llama.cpp loops on the same paragraph for five minutes on a simple search-and-summarize task, and still loops in BF16, so the commenter blames llama.cpp support rather than quantization.
- [nullc](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50147780): An engineer who has implemented inference engines for several open models corrects the claim that KV-cache state is 「at least 50% words」: tokens are embedded once at the front and everything after is latent hidden state, which is why hidden state and latent reasoning are equally hard to observe.

## 3. William Gibson and the trademarking of cyberspace {#story-3}
*#3 on HN · [colo.to](https://colo.to/cyberspace.html) · 144 points · 45 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50146625)*

Eric_Gullichsen 的一篇第一人称回忆，讲述 1989 年 Autodesk 公司试图注册“cyberspace”商标一事。当时他在 Autodesk 研究实验室做早期 VR 工作：系统是 386 加两块 Matrox 显卡、VPL EyePhone 头盔、Polhemus 追踪器和数据手套，每秒 6–10 帧；提摩西·李瑞把实验室戏称为“Cyberia”。他写信给吉布森，结尾引用对方的话「街道自会找到用途」；吉布森让律师回信，Autodesk 置之不理。文章指出「吉布森反过来抢注 Eric Gullichsen 商标」这个流传已久的故事，其实是李瑞编造的恶作剧，经 John Perry Barlow 添油加醋、Rudy Rucker 放大后成了公认的“历史”。

**Discussion**: 讨论分成几支。一支围绕文化起源：有人称“赛博空间”诞生于 LSD 和 Grateful Dead 磁带，作者引李瑞「PC 是 1990 年代的 LSD」佐证，也有人反驳说那是过度投射，吉布森 1977–1983 年的写作里「hallucination」的用法才更相关。另一支是真正的争论：有评论者骂 Autodesk 是个百亿美元级公司却冒充反主流文化、且试图抢注作家刚发明的词；作者逐条反驳：申请并未被驳回，而是在 1991 年 VR 项目取消、开发者出走创办 Sense8 后因「未答复」被放弃，并给出 USPTO 记录，强调那份「街道」引用是他自己故意写来调侃的，不该用 Autodesk 后来的名声去评判 1989 年的实验室。有人追问：信是印在公司信笺上的吗——这算是双方都能接受的尴尬点。此外还衍生出一大串商标话题：cyberpunk 被 CD Projekt 持有、Choose Your Own Adventure、NATO strap、Cadbury 紫色，多人指出这类商标只限定在特定商品类别，不能阻止一般使用。还有人对 Neal Stephenson 造的是「metaverse」而非「meta」做了纠正。

**Worth reading**

- [Animats](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50149055): 一位当时亲眼见过 Autodesk VR 系统的评论者说他们做了 3D 乒乓演示，但并评论在空气中徒手做精密 CAD 就像「戴着烤箱手套画机械图」，当时很多人却真信这是未来。
- [pryce](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50148049): 纠正上一条评论：Neal Stephenson 造的是「metaverse」而非「meta」，「meta」是古希腊语前缀，Meta 公司对该词的占用在法律上恐怕站不住脚。

## 4. There's never been a better time to go into math (by: 3blue1brown) [video] {#story-4}
*#4 on HN · [youtube.com](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LKrwhjOI8Nc) · 303 points · 364 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50136150)*

A roughly 40-minute 3Blue1Brown video in which Grant Sanderson argues that despite AI's rapid progress on theorem-proving, mathematics is a better field to enter now than ever. The YouTube page text could not be fetched, so this is based on the title and on commenters' descriptions: Sanderson walks through several scenarios, says the field's future and funding look strong, frames math education as primarily a motivation problem, concedes around the 30-minute mark that something is genuinely lost, and cites Terence Tao's remark that math's 「age of heroes」 has ended. The video follows recent releases of machine-generated proofs (commenters mention OpenAI dumping proofs, which Sanderson reportedly says took the wind out of mathematicians' sails).

*Article not read: page had almost no readable text (probably needs JavaScript or a login)*

**Discussion**: The thread splits between professional mathematicians and software engineers who feel the same displacement, and commenters who call that reaction ego or status anxiety. A recurring objection is that the usual historical analogies — cranes, tractors, CAD draftsmen, calculators — fail because AI is a general-purpose cognitive tool whose rate of improvement is itself the anomaly, and its potential to cap out below or above human ability is what determines whether optimism makes sense. Several self-identified researchers push back on simple optimism: a math PhD student says LLM-centric math feels soulless and is not what they entered the field to do, another argues that a machine randomly solving ~40% of a field's hard problems yields -60% effective throughput as people leave rather than +40%, and a widely-upvoted analogy holds that math will go the way of biology — studying structures no one had to build. The counter-argument, from people who studied math, is that mechanizing mathematics goes back to Leibniz and Hilbert, that research has long been collaborative, and that digesting machine proofs into understanding is endless human work; one commenter notes Grant himself moved from academia to YouTube and that career advice from an established multi-millionaire deserves skepticism. Rough consensus, where there is one: the field is being shaken, a hard-to-imagine world follows if AI surpasses humans everywhere, and the social/communication side of these jobs gets more important, not less.

**Worth reading**
- [spawarotti](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50147337): Argues math will go the way of biology: researchers prize untangling mechanisms that already exist rather than being the first to build them, so nobody grieves that a brain already exists to study.
- [rubikscube09](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50148430): A self-identified math PhD student says LLM-centric math is soulless, that they wanted discoveries from their own cognition rather than directing a discovery machine, and that they expect the field to become lab-babysitting like biology.

## 5. Superlite: A Faster Alternatie to Superpowers {#story-5}
*#5 on HN · [contextfirst.blog](https://contextfirst.blog/superlite-hand-an-agent-a-spec/) · 55 points · 26 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50147492)*

这是工程经理 Iheatu Chris Wogu 为他的 Claude Code 插件 superlite 写的发布文章（GitHub 上的 iha2/superlite），定位是 superpowers 的替代品，而非竞争对手。超级功能被优化为在每一步都进行协作；而 superlite 则面向自主构建：它只收集一批最多四个问题，询问一次批准，然后构建一个包含每个任务文件所有权和接口的任务图，并行运行相互独立的任务，并对代理进行分层（Opus 只负责规划和调度，不允许接触代码；Sonnet 构建大多数任务；Haiku 只做机械性、完全明确说明的任务；Sonnet 负责审查；一个全新的 Opus 审查者只指出发现，绝不修复），并通过钩子而非提示来强制执行规则。在其无头基准测试中——一个静态站点生成器加电子表格引擎，包含 177 个隐藏测试——它可在 16.1 分钟内完成（最高峰时 18 个代理并行工作），相比 superpowers 的 52% 更少（对比原始运行），相比编排运行为 31% 更少，同时保持 177/177 的测试通过数；更早的一个 superlite 构建版平均为 26.9 分钟。该插件需要 Claude Code、git、Python 3.10+ 和 bash，可通过 /plugin marketplace add iha2/superlite 安装。

**Discussion**: 总体基调明显是质疑：多位评论者表示，像 superpowers（因而也包括 superlite）这样的技能在 2026 年已不再必要，因为像 Opus 5.5、Sonnet 5.5 和 Gemini 3.8 Flash 这样的前沿模型已经将过程内化，现在会自行编写规格说明并直接构建——其中一位称 superpowers 在 2026 年第一季度很重要，但如今已不再需要。反方（雷和丹尼·比）认为，技能是流程或程序，模型仍然无法持久且一致地产出一致的产物（例如每个功能一份设计计划和一份实施计划），因此如果这正是你想要的，技能就仍然需要——他们认为这场争论是各说各话，因为人们使用技能的目的各不相同。几位评论者补充了第一手反馈：即使是最新的 Opus，也仍然常产生不成熟的计划，而且模型的「假定我同意」行为是一种倒退，不过其他人表示，他们靠一段简短提示段落或一个审查代理对照规格签字确认就能顺利应对，评论者称这本质上就是你自己动手写的技能。有两个具体问题始终没有得到作者的回答：superlite 如何防止多个并行实现代理覆盖彼此的工作，以及额外的并行是否会使 token 成本膨胀——此外，还有一位读者在问，相比裸的 Opus 5.5，superlite 究竟增加了什么。

**Worth reading**
- [DannyBee](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50148955): 认为「技能已死」的争论是错位：如果你的目标是持久、一致的产物，那么技能仍然是必要的，但新模型已经完全不需要过程脚手架。
- [codeugo](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50147896): 提出一个具体的技术反对意见——如果多个实现代理在并行工作，有什么能阻止它们互相修改对方的文件？——文章中并未回答。
- [adastra22](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50148468): 认为模型「假定我同意」的行为是一种真正的倒退，因为即使是最新的模型，大多数时候也仍然会产出半生不熟的计划。

## 6. Laptop Based on 6502 CPU {#story-6}
*#7 on HN · [maddox.pro](https://www.maddox.pro/?p=448) · 93 points · 37 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50111537)*

A blog post by Paula Maddox (maddox.pro) presenting the finished 「b」 revision of her LT6502 laptop, a home-built 6502 machine in a translucent-orange 3D-printed case. It is half the thickness of the first version, moves to a 10.1-inch 1024×600 IPS screen (from 9-inch 800×480), runs a 65C02 at 10MHz (up from 8MHz), and adds an onboard SID emulator (nanoSwinSID) plus a WiFi modem, with a similar battery (7400mAh, USB-PD, CompactFlash storage, 46KB user RAM, EhBASIC). She links code, PCB and case files on Codeberg (codeberg.org/TechPaula/LT6502b) and lists what she would fix next: stronger heat-insert holes, a Caps Lock LED, a faster/more reliable WiFi modem, a better speaker and sound chip, faster ROM-to-RAM switching, an 18650-based battery in the hinge, USB-PD buck-boost instead of a preprogrammed 5V limit, and FAT16 support on the CF card.

**Discussion**: Commenters are impressed by the design and immediately post a spec sheet, then dig into the hard part: how a 6502 drives 1024×600 in 256 colours, with one engineer noting the 8-bit bus appears to feed the display directly and asking where the framebuffer could even live in the memory map — the thread never resolves it. Several people place the machine in context: Apple IIc and IIc Plus, the 23-pound Commodore SX-64 (with a warning that hot-plugging a monitor on one can fry its SID), the TRS-80 Model 100, HP 200LX palmtops and cheap VTech 6502-based toy laptops. A recurring tangent is why so many DIY machines stop at BASIC — most answer it is tradition and a sanity check that the hardware is a real computer, and one argues the 6502 can still do most real workloads once you strip out GUI rendering, though others counter that TLS, video and music make that a stretch and compare a 64K Z80/CP/M machine as more realistic. On power, a reader asks why such a modest chip needs charging at all; replies point out the 2–5W display dominates and that a discrete 6502 with external SRAM pays a big energy penalty versus an emulator on an ARM SoC. One dissenter says it is not a laptop at all, just a desktop in a bulky laptop-shaped case.

**Worth reading**
- [mk_stjames](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50149123): A commenter tries to reverse-engineer the video path, noting the 8-bit bus seems to drive a 1024×600 256-colour screen directly and asking where a framebuffer that large could fit in the 6502 memory map — a question the thread leaves open.
- [Dwedit](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50147731): Explains the power question: a real 6502 has no internal RAM or cache and must shuttle power to external memory chips, so a cheap ARM SoC running a 6502 emulator would draw far less.
- [MomsAVoxell](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50149045): Identifies the author as synthesizer designer Paula Maddox (founder of Modal Electronics and builder of a full CPU from discrete logic chips), giving useful background on her prior work.

## 7. Verisign .com Domain Name Registry Consumer Class Action {#story-7}
*#8 on HN · [hbsslaw.com](https://www.hbsslaw.com/cases/verisign-com-domain-name-registry-consumer-class-action) · 56 points · 13 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50148441)*

这是 Hagens Berman 律师事务所为 Kessler v. VeriSign, Inc. et al. 一案设立的招募原告的落地页，该案已提交至美国加州中区联邦地区法院，被告为 VeriSign 和 ICANN。起诉书称 VeriSign 通过合同获得了对 .com 注册机构的永久垄断（该机构拥有超过 1.6 亿个活跃注册），自 2021 年以来将价格从每个域名 7.85 美元提高到 10.26 美元——涨幅超过 30%——而运营成本估计约为每个域名 3 美元，营业利润率超过 67%，并预计到 2030 年价格将达到 13.44 美元。任何自 2022 年起注册或续费 .com 域名的人都被列为潜在集体成员；页面为律师广告，而不是新闻报道。

**Discussion**: 最常见的反应是，每个域名 3 美元的成本对于一个只需提供几百字节数据库记录的服务来说高得离谱，反驳意见则是数字服务主要由薪资驱动（工程师、支持、法律、合规）。一位评论者做了计算：VeriSign 管理约 1.79 亿个域名，员工不到 1,000 人，这意味着每个员工要承担超过 53 万美元的薪资成本——他认为这指向高管薪酬，而不是真实运营成本。几位用户从零售侧提出异议，指出他们实际支付的价格（例如通过注册商续费一个持有 20 多年的域名，每年 23 美元），并询问美国集体诉讼是否只会为美国注册人降低价格，而其他人则抱怨这些钱根本没有留在欧洲。一个更政治化的分支认为，真正的风险是美国控制的根服务器和域扣押（.org）问题，一位回复者反驳说，根据合同义务运营的 gTLD（.com、.org、.net）在任意执法方面受到限制，而许多 ccTLD 使注册人暴露于自由裁量决定之下。最讽刺的论点：集体成员会拿到一张 0.01 美元的支票，而律师拿走数百万。

**Worth reading**
