## 2. C++26 Disapproved {#story-2}
*#2 on HN · [andreasfertig.com](https://andreasfertig.com/blog/2026/11/cpp26-dis-failed-how-does-cpp-survive/) · 254 points · 143 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50360164)*

Andreas Fertig, a member of the German ISO national body in WG21, reports that the C++26 DIS ballot failed on October 23, 2026 after a twelve-week vote — the first time a C++ DIS has ever been rejected, with only ISO/IEC DIS 26926 (C++/CLI, 2006) comparable, and that project was cancelled. The two-thirds approval threshold was missed because several national bodies objected to Contracts, which they consider not ready; Fertig argues they should have accepted the repeated WG21 majority votes rather than re-litigating at the national-body stage. He lists the ISO options (new DIS ballot, back to CD, Technical Specification, or cancelling the project), notes all of them need a WG21 majority that currently favours Contracts, and puts the next meeting on November 16 as the deadlock-breaking opportunity.

**Discussion**: A large part of the thread is people who write production C++ arguing Contracts is a weak feature: jandrewrogers and others say it is little more than syntactic sugar over C asserts, that it fails to deliver the promised compile-time verification, and that concepts plus constexpr already cover real needs — while quotemstr pushes back hard that runtime invariant checks are neither legacy nor replaceable by concepts, and that the feature is not worth killing a standard over. Others are unconvinced by Fertig's framing: several commenters ask why exercising a veto that the ISO process explicitly provides should count as a betrayal of trust, with the most charitable reading (royal assent that is normally a rubber stamp) contrasted against accusations that Contracts proponents railroaded a known-flawed proposal. A subthread claims Bjarne Stroustrup backed the rejection and even used dirty tricks such as snap votes timed for other time zones; others rebut that there were no tricks, that consensus simply never formed on a fix, and that papers named 「contracts concerns」 and 「contracts fundamental flaws」 predate the vote. Side threads cover the collateral damage to reflection, std::execution and library hardening in the same draft (with a counter that compilers already ship many of these), whether SIMD can be fixed at language level, build systems/dependency management, and a long Rust-versus-C++ relevance debate.

**Worth reading**
- [AlotOfReading](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50361548): Links Lisa Lippincott's talk on contracts design, explaining why the evaluation semantics are more complex and potentially slower than plain C asserts — the argument that moved this commenter from neutral to opposed.
- [zugi](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50361561): Pushes back on the Stroustrup dirty-tricks story: WG21's consensus rule means Contracts got into the draft, consensus that it needed fixing never produced a specific fix, and voting down the whole spec was the only way to reject it.
- [Zepto8537](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50361735): Notes that other major C++26 features — reflection, std::execution and library hardening — are delayed too because they rode in the same disapproved draft.

## 3. The Internals of PostgreSQL {#story-3}
*#6 on HN · [interdb.jp](https://www.interdb.jp/pg/index.html) · 249 points · 15 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50358702)*

Hironobu Suzuki 编写的免费在线 PostgreSQL 内部原理书籍，2015 年 9 月首发，现已满 11 年，正文覆盖到 PostgreSQL 19-beta（不含新的 buffer page lock 机制），并按版本持续修订。全书按子系统分章：进程与内存架构、查询执行、HOT 与 index-only scan、WAL、在线备份与 PITR、流复制，以及 2026 年 5 月新增的 Beta 版「逻辑复制」章和 6 月新增的 8.5 节「PostgreSQL 中的异步 I/O」。页面同时包含作者求职说明与使用条款 FAQ：商用可按 20% 收入分成，或一次性买断全部版权，报价 500 万欧元（作者注明这半是玩笑），并明确不提供 PDF/RSS 版本。

**Discussion**: 评论区几乎一致认为这是理解 Postgres 的最佳免费资料，但落脚点集中在具体章节：多人推荐 HOT 更新路径那一章，认为配合 zHeap 与 TOAST 的实现笔记能建立清晰心智模型。讨论随后转向 MVCC 现状——有人指出书中没有 OrioleDB 这类新式 MVCC，另有人引用 Tom Lane 近期访谈称他看不到 Postgres 放弃 dead tuples 与 vacuum 的可能，也有人反驳说用户不必盲从上游，OrioleDB 对 SSD 更友好、MariaDB 也有替代实现。Uber 那篇 Postgres→MySQL 迁移博客被拿来当论据，随即被指当年若在邮件列表求助，多数问题本可解决，且部分问题事后已被修复。另有评论提到 Jepsen 对 Postgres SSI 的长期缺陷报告，以及 Postgres 18 中全文检索改用集群默认 collation provider、升级后需重建索引等行为变化；也有人对其中一条过于「全面」的技术评论表示怀疑，问它是不是 AI 写的。

**Worth reading**
- [getbrew](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50360879): 给出 HOT 的实用要点：只有无索引列变更且新元组能落在同一页才会触发，因此在 status、updated_at 这类高频更新列上建索引会悄悄让整表失去 HOT，可用 80–90 的 fillfactor 并用 pg_stat_user_tables 里的 n_tup_hot_upd 对比 n_tup_upd 来验证。
- [hans_castorp](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50362352): 附上 Tom Lane 谈 Postgres 三十年架构的 Snowflake 访谈链接，以及一篇讨论 MVCC 取舍的文章 boringsql.com/posts/mvcc-bad-bad/。
- [jon128](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50361655): 反驳「上游不动用户就别动」：称 OrioleDB 事实上已是 Postgres 的「另一套 MVCC」、对 SSD 更友好，MariaDB 亦有替代实现，并引 Uber 迁移博客作为规模运维的现实案例。

## 4. Giant canyon on Mars is still tectonically active {#story-4}

A Columbia University news release from the Lamont campus reporting that the solar system’s largest canyon system on Mars — the Valles Marineris chain — shows evidence of ongoing tectonic activity rather than being a fossil landscape. The page itself returned HTTP 403, so the specific measurements, fault counts and dating in the release could not be read; only its headline claim is available here.

**Discussion**: The thread barely engages with the paper itself and drifts to Mars geology wish-listing. The most substantive thread argues that Hellas Planitia, the ~7 km-deep impact basin roughly antipodal to the Tharsis volcano complex, is an underrated rover target — both because the antipodal symmetry is too neat to be coincidence and because the depth means higher atmospheric pressure, potentially crossing the triple point of water and raising planetary-protection worries, with araneiform terrain and CO2-ice bursts as possible mechanisms for pressure-driven warming and mechanical dispersal of anything living. Several commenters take the view that subsurface Mars is where life would be, so a place you can reach 7 km down without drilling is the obvious first stop. **Worth reading**

## 8. Safari introduces Adaptive Dark Mode in 27.3 {#story-8}
*#14 on HN · [webkit.org](https://webkit.org/blog/18541/introducing-adaptive-dark-mode/) · 406 points · 216 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50358562)*

A WebKit blog post announces Adaptive Dark Mode in Safari 27.3 beta: when the user has dark appearance enabled, Safari detects predominantly light pages and transforms them to dark. Sites that declare color-scheme: light dark are left alone, while color-scheme: light only provides an opt-out, though that keyword is not supported in beta 1. The engine uses a hue-preserving luminosity color matrix, targets WCAG 4.5:1 text contrast, adapts gradients, SVGs and simple black-and-white bitmap logos, leaves canvas content untouched, and hides the transformation from JavaScript to reduce fingerprinting. Apple says almost 40% of page loads come from dark-mode users but only 10% of page loads support it.

**Discussion**: Commenters largely treat this as a useful opt-in fallback but question the need: Dark Reader, Noir and Stylus have done it for years, and Chrome already has an experimental Auto Dark Mode behind the enable-force-dark flag. A long side argument about Hacker News lacking dark mode exposes the real split: some readers get eye strain, halation or migraines from light backgrounds, while others with astigmatism find white-on-black blurry or afterimage-prone, so several argue for following the system preference and offering both. Developers disagree on whether browser-forced theming helps or hurts: one reports 100+ hours of dark-mode QA and would happily drop native support, while others warn about breakage and argue that sites should author their own palettes; the opt-in design and light-only escape hatch soften that objection. Commenters also correct the implied novelty, noting prefers-color-scheme has shipped for years, and flag that WCAG 2's 4.5:1 target is weak for light-on-dark text, pointing to APCA. The privacy claim that sites cannot detect the transformation is praised as anti-fingerprinting, though some expect a side channel to appear and others object that the browser is lying to pages.

## 19. Why Don't We Live in an Art Nouveau World? {#story-19}
*#37 on HN · [asteriskmag.com](https://asteriskmag.com/issues/16/why-don-t-we-life-in-an-art-nouveau-world) · 134 points · 118 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50333332)*

An Asterisk Magazine essay (by a writer whose bio lists AI evaluation) that starts from the Sagrada Família — main spire finished this February, making it the tallest church in the world 144 years after construction began — and from the nearby Hospital de Sant Pau, a Domènech i Montaner Modernista complex built 1901–1930. The author argues the “trad critique” (humanity abandoned beauty around 1940–50) is naive: Art Nouveau was short-lived because the reaction against it began by the mid-1900s, and even in its heyday patrons fought over utility versus showpiece, as at Sant Pau, where the most ornate pavilion was the administration building and later pavilions were cheaper for lack of money. His own verdict after visiting is that the style is exhausting up close — ornament becomes cognitive load and habituation sets in within half an hour — and that there is «just the right amount» of it in the world.

**Discussion**: The thread splits along the article’s own fault lines. Several people push economics and labor: ornament vanished when craft wages rose, and commercial builders want interchangeable boxes for resale and repurposing, so style is a liability rather than an asset; others counter that beauty is not mere signaling and that we are richer than cathedral-builders ever were. Former practitioners add texture — one ex-architect says even sympathetic architects rarely get a client with the budget for a grand vision, and notes survivor bias, since the ugly slums that coexisted with beautiful buildings are gone. Maintenance is raised as the neglected killer: flat modernist surfaces are trivial to keep up compared with decoration that must be fought for against pigeons and weather, though a reply argues flat roofs, oversized glazing and thin walls are themselves expensive to maintain and fail faster than 19th-century construction. On the buildings themselves there is real disagreement: some call the Sagrada Família a masterpiece, others (including a Belgian defending Horta) call it grotesque and closer to H.R. Giger than to Art Nouveau. Commenters also correct the geography — Helsinki has roughly 600 Jugend buildings, more than Barcelona or Brussels, and Oradea, Ålesund and Riga get recommendations.

**Worth reading**
- [Oarch](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50355456): A former architect says the constraint is rarely ideology but clients: the grand visions need a perfect client with limitless budget, and adds that digitized life has eroded attention to the physical world and that survivor bias hides the slums that coexisted with beautiful buildings.
- [DubiousPusher](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50352645): Argues it is fundamentally labor cost — intricate parquet and carving disappeared exactly as trade wages rose, with Art Deco itself a compromise using flattened elements and prefabs — and calls the trad critique a tell of ignorance about how the built world is actually made.
- [robochat](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50357800): Notes the Modernista parts of Barcelona housed the very rich, and that we visit wealthy people’s houses and remember only the best of past decades, forgetting the mediocre majority.

## 20. Killing what can't be handled – putting a spaghetti demon to eternal rest {#story-20}
*#38 on HN · [themaister.net](https://themaister.net/blog/2026/10/29/killing-what-cant-be-handled-putting-a-spaghetti-demon-to-eternal-rest/) · 39 points · 9 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50337980)*

A post by Themaister (Hans-Kristian Arntzen) on the dxil-spirv project, describing how he gave up on his own hand-rolled control flow graph structurizer — a 9000-line implementation with a bus factor of one — and adopted Mesa's goto_if structurizer, the one OpenCL uses for unstructured SPIR-V. To test it he added a hack path using the fake SPV_FROG_unstructured_control_flow extension that skips structurization entirely, and built a fuzzer (structurize-test) that generates random reducible CFGs, runs them on the GPU and checks for bit-exact feedback, 4096 randomized walks per CFG. He reports finding real Mesa bugs this way, explains the level/dominance-frontier algorithm in detail, notes that he dropped PHI SSA preservation, and that switch blocks are split into chains of conditional branches. The article also contains a deliberate prompt-injection trap aimed at LLM scrapers.

**Discussion**: The thread is small and mostly appreciative rather than technical. Two commenters laughed at the anti-LLM prompt-injection line and the spec warning about barely relevant site feedback about middle-clicking images. The one substantive thread concerns the GPU infinite-loop risk the author mentions: one commenter asks whether finiteness isn't the whole point of shaders, and pjc50 answers that this should be enforced at shader compile time and that SPIR-V does not obviously prevent describing an infinite loop, pointing to section 2.11.3 of the SPIR-V spec on structured control flow; another replies that a shader can be anything you want, just like a CPU program. zamalek adds context on why shader code is so large: ubershaders from earlier generations, and Unreal's material editor producing thousands of shaders, with instruction limits loosening since DX9/DX10 and DLSS papering over complexity. cowlevel also argues the prompt injection is poorly written and unlikely to work on most models, since they are trained to ignore instructions that insult them.

**Worth reading**
- [pjc50](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50352768): Explains that loop finiteness is meant to be enforced at shader compile time and points readers to section 2.11.3 of the Khronos SPIR-V spec on structured control flow rules.
- [zamalek](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50361835): Gives practitioner context for why shaders get so big: ubershaders, Unreal's material editor generating thousands of shaders, rising instruction limits since DX9/DX10, and DLSS letting developers get away with more.
- [cowlevel](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50360780): Argues the article's prompt-injection line won't work on most models, since they have learned from human text to disregard instructions from someone insulting them.
