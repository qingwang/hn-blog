## 1. Astra can read and write weights to improve Qwen {#story-1}
*#1 on HN · [rikiyat.github.io](https://rikiyat.github.io/blog/weight-space-programming/) · 612 points · 178 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50341174)*

这是一篇关于「Weight-Space Programming」的博客文章，作者称 GPT-6 Astra 能直接在上下文中读写神经网络权重，而不需要梯度下降或优化器。实验包括：仅凭原始权重识别四个 PPO 网络各自训练的是哪个游戏、识别 DDPM 生成的形状、只看中间四层判断 7.2M 参数小语言模型的训练语言（Python/罗马字/英语），并找出 induction head。最引人注目的是改 Qwen3.5-0.8B：Astra 定位到导致推理死循环的权重方向，只改 0.016% 的权重，MATH-500 从 7.9% 升到 48.1%，IFEval 提升约 3 倍；此外还从零写出 MinAtar 三个游戏的网络，击败训练 1 亿步的 PPO。

**Discussion**: 评论区一半是震惊，一半是「意料之中」。怀疑派认为这类能力更可能是 OpenAI 为递归自我改进（RSI）专门训练出来的，且预训练语料里本就散落着大量权重文件和可解释性文献；乐观派则强调单次前向推理就胜过上亿步 PPO 实在反常理，并猜测注意力机制在权重上做了类似 PCA 的投影。多位读者给出了关键修正：Qwen 的数学提升并不是「教会它数学」，而是消除了层 15/19 MLP 写入的一个「循环方向」，与 Arditi 等人 2024 年关于拒绝方向的研究同源，投影只能移除行为、无法新增能力；同样方法用在本来就不循环、单纯数学差的 Gemma 3 270M 上则完全失败。还有人称过程其实允许了 50 轮批量迭代并在下游任务上评估，与「无环境反馈」的说法有出入。规模是最大的共识性疑虑：一个数万亿参数的模型去修 0.8B 模型，能否递归到自己身上、上下文容量是否够（有人估算 1M 上下文最多编辑约 20B 的 fp4 模型）都未经验证；不少人顺带担心权重供应链完整性和去审查（abliteration）风险。

**Worth reading**
- [l9o](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50342593): 指出 Qwen 原本就会数学，只是 96 个答案里有 85 个撞到 token 上限陷入死循环，Astra 修的是循环而非补齐知识，且对不循环的 Gemma 3 270M 无效。
- [augment_me](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50342516): 认为所谓数学飞跃本质是移除激活空间里一个导致循环而非收敛的方向，属于已知的 Arditi et al. 2024 技术，可替代 LoRA/GRPO 类后训练，但无法注入新知识。
- [Wheen](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50342060): 提醒这是数万亿参数模型在优化 8 亿参数模型，没有任何证据表明它能用同样方法改进自身。

## 2. Show HN: Subvocal – Talk to your computer without making noise {#story-2}
*#2 on HN · text post · 294 points · 202 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50340088)*

Subvocal 是由 The Subvocal Company（YC F26）的 Aarush Yadav 和 Arnav 创办的项目：它用一副眼镜向面部发射低功率射频，这种射频能穿过舌头、嘴唇和软腭，被无声的口型动作调制，再通过云端模型解码成文字，延迟通常低于 500ms。团队称原型在 20 句校准后，词错率可到约 5%，并且因为射频能看到口腔内部，闭着嘴做「内部言语」也能工作。影片里的测试者通过 500 副预购、200 美元折扣销售，目标 Q1 2027 出货，目前只支持英文，解码在配套 app 或云端完成，也要求联网。团队还说自己试过 EMG、IMU、唇读和 mmWave，最后才转向这种射频方案。

**Discussion**: 评论区整体非常热情，大量评论引用科幻作品，如《Ender's Game》《Speaker for the Dead》的 Jane、《1984》的 speakwrite 和《The Whispering Earring》，说自己等这类技术等了二十年。最集中的批评针对官网：好几个人滑了半天都不知道这是眼镜，看不到「how it works」到底怎么工作，讨厌必须滚动触发的动画，FAQ 只说不使用相机，却不说用什么；创始人逐条回应称会改，并修好了 Safari 鼠标滚轮无法滚动的问题。另一大争论是打字与说话的效率：创始人称大多数人打字 40–50 WPM，说话 120–200 WPM，说话更自然；但多位重度听写用户反驳说，口述内容充满填充词、更不精确，代码和符号仍然只能靠键盘，有人认为 prompt 质量因此下降。有人指控官网图片疑似 AI 生成，产品可能根本不存在，是骗局；dang 出面反驳，指出预购新品本来就还没有成品，技术是真的，创始人也在评论区做了大量技术答疑。还有关于 RF 长期贴着面部是否安全、是否会被用于监视、能否装在处方镜片上、为何不做成项链、发箍或耳塞的讨论；创始人说安全经过验证，射频只在主动听写时开启，也承认远程窃听难度很大。

**Worth reading**
- [aarushyadav](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50340505): 创始人解释为何放弃纯唇读方案：语音有 40 个音素，但外部可见的视位只有 12–15 个（P 和 B 在镜头里几乎一样），因此 Auto-AVSR 的 17% 词错率已经触及该模态的信息论上限，没法做到产品级。
- [aarushyadav](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50340599): 关于把硬件装在笔记本或桌上，创始人说同一频段的射频在那么远的距离会被强烈衰减，改用 60GHz mmWave 又只能看到视位，而且空间分辨率只有约 5cm×5cm，因此目前做不出能架在桌面的版本。
- [dang](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50342129): 面对「AI 生成图即骗局」的指控，dang 反驳说预购的新硬件本来就没有成品，帖子里有大量技术细节和开发中的影片，把「还没做完产品、在忙技术」说成诈骗是站不住的。

## 3. Launch HN: Momentic (YC W24) – Agents that catch bugs by using your app {#story-3}
*#3 on HN · [momentic.ai](https://momentic.ai/) · 72 points · 51 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50339913)*

Momentic, founded by Wei-Wei and Jeff (ex-Robinhood, Retool, WeWork, Qualtrics), launched 「Mo」, a hosted QA agent that autonomously explores web, iOS and Android apps to find bugs instead of generating Playwright/Selenium scripts. The post claims Mo found 269 bugs with 19.3% recall at 84% precision and $2.67 per bug in 60-minute runs against Gitea, Metabase, Twenty and Saleor (1,396 human-labeled bugs), versus Codex at 86 bugs / 6.2% recall / 86% precision / $0.80 per bug, Claude Code at 59 bugs / $4.2% and Devin at 27 bugs / 1.9%. The linked page is mostly a marketing landing page of feature announcements (CLI, auto-heal, MCP integration, plain-English YAML tests, hosted Android emulators, SOC 2 Type 2), with $250 of free Mo spend for new signups.

**Discussion**: The dominant thread is the moat question: several commenters argue a custom harness gets swallowed by the next frontier model, and two say they built comparable exploratory-testing setups in-house in weeks; the founders counter that general models still verify their own work badly, that multi-browser/mobile infra and knowledge-base maintenance are the real work, and that custom harnesses have in fact gained ground (Devin, AI code review). A second cluster is confusion over the headline metric — code commenters read $2.67 vs $0.80 per bug as 「Codex is better and cheaper」, and the team answers that recall is the metric that matters in QA since a missed bug ships broken code, conceding precision is marginally worse. Pricing drew fire: one commenter coined BYOA (bring your own agent) and complained about paying for yet another proprietary token pool, and the founders replied that no off-the-shelf model configuration met their performance bar, leaving BYOK as a possible future. On the practical side, a user reported Mo failing to install their iOS app because hosted simulators run iOS 26 while the app requires 27 (a founder said a fix was in progress), and a skeptic who has actually run parallel Playwright agents defended the difficulty of stateful exploratory testing and DOM grounding. The benchmark harness is MIT-licensed and the team invited competitors to submit results.

**Worth reading**
- [Arkada](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50341522): Argues from experience that 「just point Opus at localhost with Playwright MCP」 collapses on stateful apps — the coding agent burns its context on DOM selectors and misses bugs like a demo's 2.5h→25h inflation on refresh, and resource leases for parallel sub-agents are real orchestration work.
- [jeffan](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50340335): Co-founder Jeff explains why there is no bring-your-own-key option: vanilla models plugged into the harness were not Pareto-optimal for element grounding and visual understanding, so they built tuned custom agents — but BYOK stays on the roadmap as open models improve.
- [brahyam](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50342435): A user's firsthand failure report: Momentic could not install their iOS build on the hosted simulator (app needs iOS 27, infrastructure is on 26), a concrete limitation a founder acknowledged in reply.

## 4. US boy names that became girl names and vice versa {#story-4}
*#4 on HN · [namedaisy.com](https://namedaisy.com/blog/boy-names-that-became-girl-names) · 150 points · 153 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50339397)*

namedaisy.com 对 1936–2025 年美国社会保障局的全国婴儿姓名档案进行了分析，在 5,536 个足够常见、可供评估的名字中，有 125 个发生了显著变化，其中 54 个符合严格定义的性别转换。42 个名字从以男孩为主变成以女孩为主，仅 12 个走了相反方向。更值得注意的是转换由谁推动：42 个转向女孩的名字中有 34 个主要因为女孩使用率上升，而 12 个转向男孩的名字中有 9 个主要因为女孩使用率下降；换句话说，无论转向哪边，变化最大的通常都来自女孩。转向女孩的转换往往发生在名字人气新高时（42 个中有 32 个），而转向男孩的转换常发生在名字几近消失之后，例如 Joan 在 2001–2005 年转为男性名时只有 1,435 名婴儿使用，而它的峰值是 74,477。

**Discussion**: 评论区的主流观察是：女孩名字更“动态”，并且存在文化上的不对称。有评论者指出：“女性采用传统男性事物”已成为常态，而男性采用传统女性事物（比如男人穿裙子）仍会被当作异装或跨性别。也有用户觉得这个观察成立，但指出这正说明“女性”被当作“异常”的文化标准。另有人质疑原文“同样模式”的说法，认为在反方向上的“同样”应该是男孩使用率上升，而不是女孩使用率下降。最实质的修正来自语言背景：Jan 在荷兰语与中欧、东欧是 John 的男性形式，而 Jean 在法语中是男性名，在英语中则是女性名，因此数据中部分“性别翻转”其实是不同来源、不同发音的同拼写名字被混合在一起；有评论者进一步解释，这更像是新的、流行的女孩名叠加在一个低基数的传统名字之上，并以 Arya（《权力的游戏》）、Paris（Hilton）、Stevie（Nicks）等流行文化触发为例。此外，多名评论者批评原页面的可视化配色对色盲不友好、图表难以阅读，其中一人干脆做了替代版。

**Worth reading**
- [ecshafer](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50340940): 指出 Jan（荷兰语、中欧的 John 男性形式）与 Jean（英语女性名，也是法语男性名）是不同来源的名字，不能直接算作性别翻转。
- [casta](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50342538): 一位名叫 Daniele 的意大利男性开发者说，自己在美国因为名字被反复加入女性技术社群，甚至被以“团队女性占比超过 50%”为卖点的公司招募。
- [xnx](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50342355): 评论者认为原页面的可视化太差，于是自己重做了一个替代版本（alwaysleaveanote.com）。

## 5. Egoless Mathematics in the AI Era {#story-5}
*#5 on HN · [terrytao.wordpress.com](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2026/11/02/egoless-mathematics-in-the-ai-era/) · 27 points · 15 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50341736)*

A guest post on Terence Tao's blog, written by Karthik C. S., Amit Sahai and Jie Xue, arguing from a Platonist position that mathematical truths exist independently of the people who discover them, so AI-made discoveries should not threaten mathematicians' sense of worth. It argues that the anxiety is really a symptom of a prior mistake — measuring intellectual life by outputs, citations and prizes — and proposes 「egoless mathematics」 as an ideal that could reconcile pro-AI and anti-AI factions in the field. The post's own AI usage statement credits GPT Astra 6.0 and GPT 6.1 Sol for drafting help, and Tao notes it was converted from another file format using AI.

**Discussion**: The thread is largely skeptical. The sharpest objection, from j2kun, is that the essay reduces to 「people don't matter; all that matters is truth」 and that policy decisions should not be built on that value — he follows up by asking a Platonist commenter why his children should bother learning math rather than just prompting an AI. A counterweight comes from abstractbill, who left academia after a math PhD and says he has grown steadily more Platonist, finding it beautiful that alien mathematicians and their AIs must rediscover the same results; the reply from rubikscube09 is simply that discovering things with your own brain is fun. Several commenters attack the essay's practicality rather than its metaphysics: anarticle notes that in publish-or-perish academia credit is essentially the only reward and research budgets are already thin, so egolessness reads as an impossible luxury. The AI-usage disclosure draws the most visceral reaction, with one commenter reading the acknowledgemen as tonally close to a prayer to a machine god, and another linking a satire about a dystopian Lean-and-LLM future for mathematics that he says tenured people are literally pushing for.

**Worth reading**
- [j2kun](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50342532): j2kun reads the essay as arguing that people don't matter and only truth does, and warns about the policy conclusions that follow from treating that as a shared value.
- [anarticle](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50342509): anarticle argues egolessness is unaffordable in an academy where credit is the only currency and research budgets are already stretched, asking what researchers get instead.
- [delusional](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50342673): delusional singles out the GPT Astra 6.0 / GPT 6.1 Sol acknowledgement as reading like a prayer to a machine god, and says it is a moment to ask what is actually going on.

## 7. If you love a website, back it up {#story-7}
*#7 on HN · [blog.mistys-internet.website](https://blog.mistys-internet.website/post/2026/10/29/if-you-love-a-website-back-it-up/) · 222 points · 52 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50336518)*

A post by someone who works in web archiving (on blog.mistys-internet.website) about teapuppies.com, an Ochaken anime/toy fansite that had been online unchanged since around 2005 and then disappeared, with its domain now serving online gambling ads. The author argues personal archiving is not just for the Internet Archive and walks through ArchiveWeb.page (Chrome extension and standalone app), the Browsertrix crawler (the only paid tool, seven-day trial, open source and self-hostable) and the WARC/WACZ formats, plus ReplayWeb.page for embedding a saved site in a page — demonstrated with a full archived copy of teapuppies hosted at teapuppies.mistys-internet.website. Disclosure: most of the tools are made by the author's employer.

**Discussion**: Commenters broadly agree and trade stacks: SingleFile for one-page saves, ArchiveBox for multi-format archival, Linkding plus SingleFile, Karakeep, Hister, Obsidian Web Clipper, plain wget -mkpnp for static academic sites, and print-to-PDF. The main friction is with server-side tools: several long-time ArchiveBox users say capture queues fill with failed scans, maintenance becomes a chore, and Cloudflare/AI-bot blocking makes server-initiated crawls impossible — prompting one commenter to write a browser-side tool (recueil) precisely so captures happen in a real session. A counter-argument holds that saving whole pages mostly preserves junk, so the better goal is to extract and keep the information (ebook, text) rather than the HTML; others counter that readability-style extraction tools handle the filtering. Others raise practical obstacles the post skips: anti-AI-bot mitigations, hard drive prices for hoarders, and the fragility of trusting a single cloud folder like Dropbox. Sentiment is that link rot is inevitable and community/personal mirroring is the answer, with a long side thread arguing about whether LLMs will end up rewriting the historical record.

**Worth reading**
- [szszrk](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50339131): A multi-year ArchiveBox user reports queues full of failed scans and nothing successfully captured for several versions, and argues simpler web-clipper style captures fit real use better.
- [phendrenad2](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50340290): A strong counter-argument: the modern web is mostly CSS/JS/tracking junk, so preserve the information itself (an ebook or text) rather than schlepping hundreds of gigabytes of HTML.
- [whartung](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50342180): Raises an obstacle the article ignores: anti-AI-bot and Cloudflare-style mitigations may block CLI and app-based crawlers, even if browser extensions get through.

## 8. Ask HN: Who is hiring? (November 2026) {#story-8}

## 20. Moving our warehouse off DuckDB {#story-20}
*#25 on HN · [posthog.com](https://posthog.com/blog/moving-off-duckdb) · 118 points · 91 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50338747)*

PostHog rebuilt its customer-facing warehouse product on DuckDB plus the DuckLake lakehouse extension five months ago, replacing a shared ClickHouse cluster that couldn't handle hours-long modeling queries or size work per tenant. The post says DuckDB itself is excellent single-node software, but at PostHog's scale the deployment reached roughly 2 PB, ~1 trillion rows and 15.3 million snapshots, and DuckLake's portable catalog schema became the bottleneck: a single-table write could exceed three minutes because each commit re-read catalog statistics, and over 99% of those statistics rows belonged to dropped tables (cleaning them made writes 30–50x faster, but they grew back). The company also gave up on Postgres-wire compatibility at scale and is now rebuilding around its own Trino distribution and a homegrown open table format called HogLake, tested internally at petabyte scale before design partners.

**Discussion**: The dominant reaction is skepticism about churn rather than about DuckDB: commenters note the post announces a second migration in five months, never reports that the new stack fixed anything, and reads like recruiting or self-promotion, with several engineers asking for benchmarks, failure data and query-pattern analysis. A few commenters defend the exploration and point at real technical substance, including a suggestion that stateless ClickHouse on Iceberg would solve tenancy, and a practitioner who interviewed with PostHog describes a team short on a baseline for what a warehouse should cost. No consensus emerges; the strongest shared ground is that the post undersells its own measurements.
