## 1. IceWM {#story-1}
*#2 on HN · [ice-wm.org](https://ice-wm.org/) · 111 points · 57 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50478515)*

This is the official homepage of IceWM, a window manager for the X Window System written in C++ by Marko Maček in 1997 and now maintained on GitHub. It pitches speed, simplicity and staying out of the user's way: a taskbar with pager, global and per-window keybindings, a dynamic menu system, configurable focus models, RandR/Xinerama multi-monitor support, an optional wallpaper manager and a session manager, with over 450 themes on box-look.org, 91 optional 「Extra」 highlights, translations into three dozen languages and packaging on Debian, Ubuntu, Arch, openSUSE, Gentoo, Slackware, CentOS, antiX, NixOS and most BSDs. The latest release listed is 4.1.0 (2026-08-06), installable from a source tarball via wget and lzip.

**Discussion**: The top comment sets the tone: several readers treat IceWM as nostalgia (one submitted patches in the early 2000s, another praises how snappy it was on the lowest-end hardware), but the highest-ranked commenter insists they run it today as an all-in-one low-resource desktop for a headless remote-desktop box — panel, app menu and window manager in one unit, no configuration needed — and others chime in from audio work and from a 2004-2005 commercial Linux distribution that shipped it as the default desktop alongside Nautilus. The thread then drifts into a long X11-versus-Wayland fight: one side calls X11 thoroughly legacy and praises Wayland, PipeWire and systemd-networkd/journald as a clean break, while several reply that X11 is not legacy, that Wayland still lacks window placement and any portable wmctrl-style scripting, and that screenshots and screensharing ended up in compositor-specific extensions and portals anyway; the XLibre fork gets cited both as the torch-bearer and, dismissively, as a victim complex. Configuration is a recurring gripe — a former fluxbox user says IceWM is more sophisticated but harder to configure (could not remove unwanted workspaces) and that the lead developer ignores feature requests, so they stopped filing them. Tiling-WM users (awesome, dwm, xmonad) and an openbox fan describe them as better fits, and a side thread argues about whether software transparency was genuinely displaced by GPUs or just by sloppy programming; nobody disputes that IceWM remains a solid, tiny, still-maintained option.

**Worth reading**
- [yjftsjthsd-h](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50479019): A current daily user explains the concrete use case: IceWM as a single no-config unit (panel, app menu, WM) for a low-resource headless remote desktop.
- [kiney](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50479686): Argues Wayland will never be truly usable — it is now as old as Xorg was when Wayland began, the core protocol still lacks window placement, and screenshots/screensharing needed compositor-specific extensions and portals.
- [shevy-java](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50479662): A long practitioner comparison: IceWM is more capable than the now-nearly-dead fluxbox but harder to configure, and the maintainer's unresponsiveness to filed issues made them give up on feature requests.

## 2. Rust 1.100 {#story-2}
*#3 on HN · [blog.rust-lang.org](https://blog.rust-lang.org/2026/11/12/Rust-1.100.0/) · 627 points · 240 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50472038)*

The official release announcement for Rust 1.100.0 (12 Nov 2026). The headline item is stabilization of the never type `!` — nameable anywhere in a type, not just as a return type — after 10 years and 5 failed attempts; `Infallible` becomes an alias for it, which forced a breaking change moving never-type fallback from `()` to `!` in the 2015/2018/2021 editions. The release also stabilizes the core of `allocator_api` (the `Allocator` trait, with `Vec` and `Box`), `core::mem::DropGuard`, `std::mem::conjure_zst`, and ships Cargo changes: unused-dependency warnings, a reworked build-dir layout that applies the build-configuration hash consistently to cut cache collisions, min-publish-age 「dependency cooldowns」 (`global-min-publish-age = "3 days"`, RFC 3923), and `cargo install` now using the lockfile inside the `.crate` tarball by default.

**Discussion**: The thread is largely celebratory — people call it one of the biggest releases in years, with the never type drawing the most jokes (including one about it being named for its stabilization date). Dependency cooldowns split commenters: some argue they blunt 0-day response, others (notably Trail of Bits' woodruffw) say the answer is to cool down normal updates *and* fast-track security advisories inside the resolver itself, rather than choosing. A long subthread on compile times and 90GB+ target directories got concrete answers from Cargo maintainer epage, who listed upcoming work (less debug info in the dev profile, no rlib copies to target-dir, build-dir GC, cross-workspace cache) plus a macOS temp-file bug already fixed on nightly. The perennial stable-ABI wish came up again, and a design discussion of `bool::toggle()` — why it exists given `x = !x`, how `&mut self` works on primitives — ran to dozens of replies. Consensus: strong release, with compile time and ABI as the standing complaints.

**Worth reading**
- [JoshTriplett](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50473008): Josh Triplett explains why a stable Rust ABI is hard: generics are compiled from MIR at the consumer, so an ABI would have to be `dyn`-based, which can't express many traits and makes `Vec<T>` awkward.
- [epage](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50473366): Cargo maintainer epage lists the concrete build-time and build-dir fixes in flight — trimmed dev-profile debug info, no rlib copies into target-dir, dedup of rlib/rmeta data, then build-dir GC and cross-workspace caching.
- [woodruffw](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50477439): woodruffw argues cooldowns and fast security patching must both live in the resolver's state machine, because most tools let the cooldown block advisory-driven updates.

## 3. Brave Search API now accepts agentic payments via the x402 protocol {#story-3}
*#4 on HN · [brave.com](https://brave.com/blog/search-api-agentic-payments/) · 76 points · 37 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50477649)*

Brave's developer blog announces that its Search API endpoints can now be paid per request over x402, the open payment protocol built around the long-dormant HTTP 402 Payment Required status code. An unpaid request gets a 402 with the price; the agent signs a USDC payment on Base, retries, and gets results plus an on-chain receipt — no account or API key needed. Pricing matches Brave's published rates at $0.005 per query ($5 per 1,000) for LLM Context, web, news, image, video and local endpoints, with Base gas covered for a limited time and Stripe's Purl CLI wallet suggested as the easiest setup; Brave acts as its own facilitator, with more chains and stablecoins promised.

**Discussion**: The dominant note is skepticism about whether anyone is actually paying: one commenter who deployed x402 across a heavily crawled site says he has yet to see a single transaction, and cites similar reports on the x402 subreddit. Others argue the price is too high versus alternatives — Kagi's API at $12 per 1,000, Exa at $4, and SerpApi at roughly $15 per 1,000 for Google results — and question what happens to search economics when agents rather than ad-viewing humans do the querying. A recurring subthread debates why a blockchain is needed at all when Kagi sells a plain API key; the defence is that card networks and banks can't do sub-cent cross-border charges without per-transaction fees and customer-service overhead, and that crypto payments are irreversible, which sidesteps both. Several people revive the old argument that users hate micropayments, countered with the claim that people only hate the friction, not metered pricing — electricity and utility billing are cited as existing models — while one commenter notes Brave already tried this with BAT and earned a few cents from a site with a million monthly uniques. A privacy-focused commenter complains that a privacy browser is shipping crypto payments without Monero support, and another links a November 2026 Ars Technica story about the NYT demanding Brave shut down its paywall blocker, quipping that this is the 「pay us to skip the paywall」 service.

**Worth reading**
- [ddxv](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50479355): A commenter who implemented x402 on a heavily crawled site says no transactions have come through at all, and that the x402 subreddit is full of the same joke that only testers are paying.
- [gkbrk](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50479731): Concrete price comparison: Kagi's API is $12 per 1,000 searches, Exa is $4, and SerpApi sells 5,000 Google searches for $75, with volume discounts for company-wide use.
- [brodydung](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50479133): Argues the real gap is a budget envelope rather than the payment rail — agents re-query with rephrased questions, and removing the API key removes the monthly bill that used to expose runaway spend; asks whether Brave dedups or caches repeated queries.

## 5. Switcher (2004) {#story-5}
*#8 on HN · [folklore.org](https://folklore.org/Switcher.html) · 86 points · 23 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50477714)*

这是 folklore.org 上 Andy Hertzfeld 的一篇回忆文章，讲述 1984 年他如何为 Macintosh 写出第一个多任务环境 Switcher。他在给 Byte 杂志记者 John Markoff 演示 Thunderscan 扫描仪时，看到对方在 IBM PC 上用 Memory Shift 快速切换驻留程序，当场决定为 Mac 做同样的事。Microsoft 的 Jeff Harbers 和 Neil Konzen 也想做这个项目（因为 512K Mac 会削弱 Microsoft 小程序的内存优势，而 Lotus Jazz 已能快速切换模块），于是请他去西雅图；Bill Gates 亲自压价，用「好程序员每周写 1000 行、十周搞定、每周 4000 美元」推出 4 万美元的报价，Hertzfeld 拒绝签约，坚持独立完成后把程序随 512K Mac 捆绑发布。

**Discussion**: 讨论偏离技术细节，转向怀旧与经济换算：gwern 指出 1984 年的 4 万美元约合今天 32 万多美元，感叹当年「一个切换应用的小程序」值得如此投入，随后跟帖一路跑偏到标普 500 与苹果股票回报，以及 ChatGPT 算数是否可靠（有人建议让它写代码算而不是心算）。另一条主线是老兵回忆：曾在 Microsoft Windows Mobile 编译器团队的人说，那代工程师能从汇编调试、必要时上示波器，再在自建的地基上盖设计模式，如今这种「旧派水准」已经罕见；一位与 Hertzfeld 共事过的人补充说，他的本事是提想法并用蛮力把它实现出来，「像拿砍刀开路，后面的人再修路」。也有具体更正与考据：MultiFinder 只是 Switcher 的精神后继，代码完全无关，作者是 Erich Ringewald；文中提到的 DOS 工具 Memory Shift 被确认为真实存在，有 1983 年 12 月的 Creative Computing 文章为证，而不是 Borland Sidekick 的误记。整体氛围是敬佩与怀旧，对 Gates 的谈判手法则多帶调侃。

**Worth reading**
- [diskzero](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50479757): 亲历者澄清 MultiFinder 与 Switcher 不共享任何代码、思路也不同，并指出作者是 Erich Ringewald，两人后来分别在 Be 和 General Magic/Eazel 继续做多任务问题。
- [diskzero](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50479871): 一位曾与 Hertzfeld 共事的开发者描述他的工作风格：不套设计模式，靠点子和蛮力开路，其他人跟在后面把路修成。
- [CharlesW](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50478651): 给出 1983 年 12 月 Creative Computing 杂志的链接，证实文中 IBM PC 工具 Memory Shift 确有其物。

## 6. Edge0-145B: Frontier AI Capabilities on Your Own Computer {#story-6}
*#9 on HN · [edge0.ai](https://edge0.ai/blog/introducing-edge0-145b-frontier-ai-capabilities-on-your-own-computer) · 218 points · 57 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50475713)*

Edge0 open-sourced two on-device models, Edge0-145B and Edge0-120B, plus an inference framework, claiming 145B parameters running on a MacBook Pro-like laptop at 29GB peak memory and 80+ tokens/s at roughly 120W. The trick is architectural: groups of 64 sparse experts share two large matrices, so only about 10B parameters are touched per token; hot experts sit in GPU memory, an ~40GB expert library in system RAM, and ~48GB of vocabulary data on SSD. Reported scores: 64.6 DeepSWE 1.1, 86.52 Terminal-Bench 2.1, 90.4 GPQA-Diamond, 80.6 IFBench — ahead of Qwen3.8-Flash-Next and DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731 on several, with a claimed ~72.8 six-benchmark mean at 40% of the baseline's estimated memory.

**Discussion**: The thread largely reframes the announcement as a well-marketed fine-tune: commenters point to the GitHub repo and HuggingFace pages stating the base model is Qwen3.8-Flash-Next, and that the engine, edge0-strata, is a fork of the existing Strata project. That split the room — some call it marketing veneer over community work, others (including practitioners already running Qwen3.8-Flash-Next on 2x3090s) say fine-tunes and engine forks are normal and the real question is benchmark-maxxing versus genuine gains on general tasks. The other fight is the memory claim: the 29GB figure is read as excluding the 40GB expert library in system RAM and 48GB of vocab on SSD, implying a ~70GB footprint, which would rule out a 48GB MacBook — several commenters call this misleading or a copy error. On availability, people report the released artifacts are NVFP4 (Blackwell-only, CUDA) despite repeated macOS claims, with no Mac runtime shipped, and there is open skepticism about agent demos (Blender scenes, PCB layout) needing heavy manual cleanup afterwards.

**Worth reading**
- [daemonologist](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50478564): Argues the 29GB headline is misleading because the article itself mentions another 40GB of system memory for experts plus 48GB of SSD vocabulary data.
- [Aurornis](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50479188): Notes the repo and HuggingFace pages openly state the base model is Qwen3.8 Flash Next and the engine is a Strata fork, so the real question is whether it was fine-tuned to win benchmarks.
- [coder543](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50477240): Corrects a claim that only NVFP4 was released — a q3k quant exists — but confirms no Mac runtime shipped today despite Mac benchmarks being published.

## 8. Foundation Models framework {#story-8}

## 17. Chinese frontier AI models carry the fingerprint of export-controlled GPUs {#story-17}
*#32 on HN · [unemploymentlabs.com](https://unemploymentlabs.com/checkpoint-forensics) · 277 points · 81 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50474883)*

A forensic report from Unemployment Labs argues that six models released since April 2026 by DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, MiniMax and Ant Group were initialized on export-controlled NVIDIA Blackwell hardware. The method recovers Philox PRNG seeds and weight layouts from published weights and matches thread counts to 148-SM B200/B300 or 152-SM GB200/GB300 chips; the report says matches range from 49 to 1,385 sigma and that two were reproduced on physical Blackwell chips. It estimates the five largest runs used about 12,000 Blackwell GPUs and cost over $500 million, while conceding the weights show hardware access, not where the chips were or whether controls were broken.

**Discussion**: The dominant reaction is that this is not evidence of illegality: Chinese labs can rent or train on GPUs outside China, and commenters cite Megaspeed’s reported renting of Nvidia chips to Alibaba in Malaysia and Singapore as an arrangement consistent with current rules. Several argue export controls are therefore symbolic or merely “export friction”, while others counter that they still slow Chinese progress and that China remains behind mainly in technologies that are controlled. Critics question the report’s provenance and rigor — one says the author is an OpenAI engineer and “China hawk” with no linked code, and another notes that DeepSeek’s own DeepInfer release already advertised Blackwell SM100 kernels, making the finding less secret than framed. Methodological objections focus on whether initialization hardware must match training hardware and whether distillation could produce the fingerprint; one commenter argues distillation is token-level and unlikely to explain it. Rough consensus: the fingerprinting is technically interesting, but it supports rather than proves Blackwell training and says nothing about legality.

**Worth reading**
- [modeless](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50476539): Says the method looks sound but stresses the report does not allege illegality: training on rented GPUs outside China is legal, and disclosed chip choices matched the analysis.
- [nullc](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50476102): Notes DeepSeek’s own DeepInfer repo lists only SM100 Blackwell and Huawei Ascend 950 kernels and publishes Blackwell benchmark results, so Blackwell use was already openly disclosed.

## 20. Researchers discover rare star system that emits radio signals every 8 minutes {#story-20}
*#39 on HN · [curtin.edu.au](https://www.curtin.edu.au/news/media-release/researchers-discover-rare-star-system-that-emits-radio-signals-every-eight-minutes/) · 128 points · 63 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50465453)*

A Curtin University / ICRAR media release about a Nature Astronomy paper, “A long-period transient with a Sun-like companion”, reporting the system GPM J071507−114153: a white dwarf and a G-type star like our Sun orbiting each other, roughly 5,000 light-years away. It is the first time radio pulses have been tied to a white dwarf orbiting a Sun-like star; previous long-period transients (about a dozen known since ICRAR found the first in 2021) involved cooler red dwarfs. The discovery used the MWA survey to find it, MeerKAT to localise it, W.M. Keck optical observations to confirm the companion, and over 100 hours of processing at the Pawsey supercomputer; lead author Sam McSweeney says the pulses are clear enough to see the white dwarf moving in its orbit, so the emission comes from near or on the white dwarf itself.

**Discussion**: The biggest thread is a semantics fight: dekhn argues the headline is wrong because the object emits continuously and only sweeps its beam past us every 8 minutes, while sandworm101 counters that 「star」 and 「pulse」 have always been observer-relative terms (pulsars don't literally pulse either) and that apparent-brightness language is standard. A self-identified linguist sides with dekhn, saying 「emit」 describes an objective action while 「brightest in the night sky」 is explicitly subjective, and another commenter notes the paper's own co-author described the white dwarf as emitting pulses. Separately, commenters ask whether this is just a pulsar; the reply is that pulsars spin far faster (one cited rotates at 716 Hz, with an equatorial velocity about 24% of light speed) and more than 4,000 are known, whereas long-period radio transients have periods of minutes to hours and only around a dozen are catalogued. Others correct the assumption that binary stars are captured — most, like planets, form together from the same nebula — and a chunk of the thread is jokes about aliens, smoke-alarm batteries and the Dark Forest.

**Worth reading**
- [dekhn](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50467853): The core correction: the source emits constantly, but its beam only aligns with Earth every 8 minutes, so emission and detection are being conflated.
- [villaaston1](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50472292): Explains why this isn't just a pulsar: pulsars have much shorter periods and thousands are known, while only about 12 long-period radio transients have been found.
- [adolph](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50472890): Points out that the paper's own co-author Natasha Hurley-Walker calls them 「pulses」, undercutting the strict reading that the headline is simply wrong.
