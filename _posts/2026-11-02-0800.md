## 2. Postgres 19 is late because so many more bugs are being found now {#story-2}
*#3 on HN · [rhaas.blogspot.com](http://rhaas.blogspot.com/2026/10/postgres-19-is-late-and-its-ais-fault.html) · 326 points · 217 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50312408)*

A blog post by PostgreSQL committer Robert Haas (cross-posted from Neon) explaining why PostgreSQL 19 will likely miss its usual mid-September to mid-October window and land in November. The evidence: between feature freeze (April 8) and Sept 25, commits to REL_19_STABLE rose from 724 to 1169 year-over-year and back-patches to REL_18_STABLE rose from 248 to 433 — a 74% jump — which he attributes to far more people finding bugs with LLMs, plus three late reverts (UPDATE/DELETE FOR PORTION OF, SQL/PGQ, and ALTER TABLE MERGE/SPLIT PARTITION) between Aug 27 and Sept 15. His argument is that finding bugs got much cheaper while reviewing and committing fixes did not, so maintainer time is the new bottleneck; he calls the delay a sign of the project's quality bar, but worries about how the project will ever catch up on four decades of latent bugs.

**Discussion**: The thread is overwhelmingly positive: commenters call it technical-debt repayment, note that a rock-solid database slipping a few weeks is a good trade, and several teams report the same experience internally (one froze all feature work to burn down a security backlog, another reports over 90% of newly found bugs sitting in pre-2024 code). The main pushback is about signal quality rather than direction: people ask whether the delay comes from good reports or from triaging junk, with one commenter saying roughly half the AI bug reports he receives are invalid, and others arguing discovery is no longer the bottleneck because a small group of maintainers still has to validate and review. A stronger dissent comes from an analogy to medical over-testing: once a bug is known you incur an obligation to fix it, and some low-impact bugs may be better left unknown — rebutted on the grounds that software fixes are cheap relative to discovery, vulnerabilities chain together, and software has no terminal illness. Others push back on the article's causal story itself, arguing that late feature reverts are a process failure and that LLM use cannot make work slower since you can always not use it, and one former bug-hunter notes the Postgres maintainers handled a flood of reports with remarkable patience.

**Worth reading**
- [tibbar](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50331824): A developer who spent the summer doing AI-assisted bug hunting in Postgres lists the haul with mailing-list links: serialization anomalies (write skew, duplicate primary keys under SERIALIZABLE), foreign-key integrity holes, a partition-pruning planner bug, and an unfixable timestamptz-vs-timestamp ordering bug that can make index scans return wrong answers.
- [shalmanese](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50333813): Argues the medical-screening paradox applies: once you know about a defect you face a duty to fix it, every fix has an opportunity cost, and the list of defects is effectively infinite — so more knowledge is not always better.
- [0c3ca83](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50332343): Concrete triage data from the other side: about half the AI-generated bug reports he receives are invalid and many more are questionable, making verification a large time sink (links a VulnCheck writeup).

## 3. F/OSS Comics #13: BSD Unix, Part 3 – The Beginning of BSD Unix {#story-3}
*#4 on HN · [fosscomics.com](https://fosscomics.com/posts/13.%20BSD%20Unix%20Part%203%20-%20The%20Beginning%20of%20BSD%20Unix/) · 92 points · 9 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50332163)*

这是 F/OSS Comics 系列漫画的第 13 幅，这一期用漫画叙述 BSD Unix 的起源。1976 年 Ken Thompson 回到贝尔实验室后，Bill Joy 和 Chuck Haley 自行探索 Unix 内核；1977 年初 Joy 把 Pascal 系统与从 em 发展而来的行编辑器 ex 打包，形成了 Berkeley Software Distribution，第一年寄出约 30 份磁带。随后 2BSD 加入 vi 和 C shell，发货近 75 份；1978 年 Joy 帮助研究生 Ozalp Babaoglu 在 VAX 的 32/V 上实现虚拟内存，以满足 Fateman 运行 Macsyma 所需的地址空间，并最终于 1979 年 12 月推出 3BSD，引起 DARPA 关注。当时 Berkeley 只收取磁带和邮费，用户仍需 AT&T 的 Unix 源码许可。

**Discussion**: 评论区整体是好评，有人表示正在补完全系列，还贴出自己最喜欢的「What is a real programmer?」一格。随后讨论转向勘误：一位读者认为第 3 章开头称电子计算机出现于二战并不准确，因为当时用的是继电器，ENIAC 是战后才完成的；另一位读者指出 Colossus 确实使用电子管，对上述说法提出部分反驳。也有技术性提问：文中说 ex 源自 em，而不是常见的 ed。有人给出 Coulouris 讲述 em 历史的链接，并引述 em 是「editor for mortals」的说法，以及 Ken Thompson 声称不想在编辑时看到文件状态的名言。几条回复围绕这种编辑哲学辩论：一方承认自己仍用 ed 改熟悉的配置文件，另一方则认为在没有 LSP、语法高亮和 lint 的情况下写内核代码还刻意不看上下文，属于固执。

**Worth reading**
- [nagaiaida](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50332729): 给出 Coulouris 的 em 编辑器历史页面，说明文中「em」这一不常见来源的来历。
- [stackghost](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50333356): 引用 Ken Thompson 关于「编辑时不想看到文件状态」的原话，成为整串 ed/vi 之争的引子。
- [mixmastamyk](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50333667): 指出第 3 章把早期电子计算机全部归入二战的说法有误，继电器机器与电子管机器应区分开。

## 4. Ask HN: Which TV do you recommend for privacy? {#story-4}
*#6 on HN · text post · 310 points · 460 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50328896)*

An Ask HN post from someone who hasn't owned a TV in years, wanting a 60–80" 4K set at 60Hz+ that respects privacy, is willing to pay a premium, and plans to use an Apple TV as the only source. The post drew 460 comments, but there is no article — the thread itself is the content, and its answer is a near-consensus procedure rather than a product: pick a TV on picture quality, never give it WiFi or Ethernet, and drive it over HDMI from a device you trust.

**Discussion**: The dominant answer is 「any TV, just never let it on the network」, with an Apple TV, an Nvidia Shield, a Jellyfin box or a Linux mini PC on the other end of the HDMI cable — several people run exactly this with LG OLEDs, Sony Bravias, Hisense and TCL sets and report it works fine. A vocal minority pushes back on the set-top box: swapping LG or Google for Apple or Amazon is no privacy gain, so they recommend a cheap x86 mini PC running Kodi, Plasma Bigscreen or a self-compiled degoogled LineageOS Android TV. A second disagreement is whether offline is really offline: some warn about TVs joining neighbours' open networks, Amazon Sidewalk, Bluetooth and even HDMI Ethernet Channel (HEC), and insist on physically pulling the WiFi module; others say there is no documented evidence of TVs phoning home with no credentials and call the fear overblown. Concrete alternatives named repeatedly are commercial/digital-signage displays sold as 「dumb」 panels (Samsung QB55C-N, ViewSonic CDE65G3, Sceptre), very large monitors — with the caveat that some new monitors now ship a smart TV OS — and projectors, which are almost always dumb but struggle in daylight. For the technically inclined, rooting LG webOS (tv.slada.sk guide, webosbrew, Glasshouse) and dropping the set on a no-WAN VLAN is the power-user route, though LG patched the root exploit in firmware from 2026-10-10 onward.

**Worth reading**
- [MegaDeKay](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50332515): Detailed recipe for an LG OLED: root webOS, install Glasshouse to kill telemetry, then use Adfree YouTube and homebrew apps — with the warning that LG closed the rooting hole, so avoid firmware 43.21.84 or anything newer than 2026-10-10.
- [mrandish](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50331474): First-hand report that Vizio now punishes offline use: after about ten power-ons it forces a WiFi setup workflow that must be dismissed with three remote clicks, and it refuses to remember an external HDMI input across reboots.
- [drnick1](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50330815): Argues that replacing LG/Google with Apple or Amazon achieves nothing, and describes compiling LineageOS (Android TV 17) for generic x86 with Claude's help as the only real 10-foot, degoogled, APK-compatible option.

## 5. Mystery of 60-Second Fluctuations in Power Grids Solved {#story-5}
*#7 on HN · [fz-juelich.de](https://www.fz-juelich.de/en/news/archive/press-release/2026/mystery-of-60-second-fluctuations-in-power-grids-solved) · 99 points · 40 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50315217)*

A team led by Dirk Witthaut at Forschungszentrum Jülich, with researchers from the University of Cologne and KIT, traced the brief 60-second dips in European grid frequency observed by Tennet Germany to large battery storage and UPS systems that check their state of charge at the top of every minute. Writing in Nature Energy, the researchers found the effect at 80 measurement points on the Jülich Living Lab Energy Campus and say it is global, not just European: in Ireland the one-minute signal’s amplitude quadrupled between 2016 and 2021 as data-centre battery capacity grew. The proposed fix is to randomize the timing of these checks instead of synchronizing them on the minute.

**Discussion**: Several commenters pressed for details the press release omits: which manufacturer and model, and whether firmware could be changed. One reader found in the paper that a manufacturer did issue a software update removing the strict periodicity in one battery block of the investigated UPS system, and others expect many vendors use similar schemes. Skeptics questioned the 「years of detective work」 framing because a clock-controlled cause seems obvious, while defenders note the effect is tiny, buried among millions of devices, and required Fourier analysis over many minutes to detect. There was broad agreement that this is a thundering-herd problem familiar from cron jobs, with jitter, staggered offsets, systemd timers and Google’s scheduling syntax offered as software analogues; commenters also explained the turbine wear through inertia loss and repeated stress cycles. Some remained puzzled that a state-of-charge check draws enough power to register on the grid at all, with replies suggesting cells are briefly disconnected for voltage measurement or capacitors are charging.

**Worth reading**
- [manarth](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50333997): Quotes the paper reporting that a manufacturer issued a software update removing the strict periodicity in one battery block of the investigated UPS system, and notes the practice is probably widespread across manufacturers.
- [Animats](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50334087): Explains why detection was hard: the effect is small enough that it only becomes visible through Fourier analysis over many minutes of noisy grid data.
- [0xbadcafebee](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50333319): Frames the issue as request smoothing and describes how anti-islanding standards already require jittered five-minute reconnect checks so rooftop solar and battery inverters do not all reconnect at once.

## 7. Nibnib: Image compression with extreme decompression speed {#story-7}
*#10 on HN · [github.com](https://github.com/raphlinus/nibnib) · 132 points · 23 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50299811)*

这是 Raph Levien（raphlinus）发布的一个实验性图像格式及其 Rust 实现，最初是为了在 RP2350 单片机上做 DVI 输出：每像素约 6 个周期的预算只够取像素、查调色板、写行缓冲。格式以 4bpp 存储，解码时一次从 1 字节里出两个 RGB565 像素，做法是把上一像素加倍后加上一个 256 项调色板中的 32 位「差值」条目，内层循环只有一条 wrapping_add；编码端则用 Floyd-Steinberg 误差扩散在调色板条目中搜索，速度比解码慢好几个数量级，调色板目前还不是自适应生成的。作者称在 Adafruit Fruit Jam 板上可从 SD 卡以 60fps 播放 640x480 视频，并提醒该格式不面向归档、也不带元数据。

**Discussion**: 最常见的抱怨是缺少基准数字：有人指出既然主打「极致解压速度」，至少该给一个 JPEG 的对照点和码率/质量/速度曲线；反方则认为 6 条指令一像素已经说明问题——JPEG 不可能做到，一位评论者直接说它「以 HDMI 行速解码」。多位老硬件玩家把它比作 Amiga 的 HAM 模式（6 位输入出 12 位色，水平方向有拖影），认为这是同一类思路。也有人质疑这算不算「压缩」——不过是 delta 滤波加调色板——但随即有人解释 Floyd-Steinberg 会把 100% 的量化误差推给邻居，所以不会漂移，作者的巧妙之处全在编码端。Retr0id 提醒针对 RP2350 优化几乎肯定不是现代桌面核心上的最优解，作者完全同意，说可以改 README 说明。另有评论者追问项目是否「vibe-coded」，作者坦承两者最初都是手写、后来用 Claude 推进，并说自己对 LLM 心情复杂、正努力「用好它、抵抗垃圾输出」。

**Worth reading**
- [raphlinus](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50333572): 作者回应 LLM 质疑：图像压缩和 symbolasm 起初全是手写，Claude 只是加速了进展，他甚至说不用 LLM 也能做出同样结果、只是会更慢，并坚持自己写文字部分。
- [Retr0id](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50330077): Retr0id 指出为 RP2350 这类资源受限设备优化的代码，在深流水线、宽 ILP/SIMD 的现代桌面核心上几乎肯定不是最优；作者在回复中承认并考虑在 README 里澄清。
- [schiffern](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50332306): 解释为什么误差不会累积：Floyd-Steinberg 保留了 100% 的量化误差并推给邻近像素（对比 Atkinson 只保留 75%），并附了一篇抖动算法博客链接。

## 8. Tips for more effective fuzz testing with AFL++ {#story-8}
*#11 on HN · [nullprogram.com](https://nullprogram.com/blog/2025/02/05/) · 111 points · 17 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50271106)*

Chris Wellons (nullprogram.com) publishes an eight-point checklist for fuzzing C and C++ targets with AFL++ on glibc Linux, distilled from fuzz testing roughly 1,000 projects over six years. Beyond the usual ASan+UBSan setup, he argues for non-default ASan options — detect_stack_use_after_return=1 and max_malloc_fill_size raised from the default 4KiB to 1GiB, since the 4KiB cap hid real bugs from him — plus persistent mode, compiling the needed sources straight into a single translation unit (even static functions), a size-fitted copy of the fuzz buffer so read overflows are detectable, memfd_create for APIs that insist on a path, and shrinking generous fixed buffers like 65536 down to 16 to reach overflow paths faster.

**Discussion**: Almost the entire thread drifts away from the article into a debate over whether memory-safe C/C++ is a question of developer skill. jonhohle opened it by saying AFL found bugs even in FAANG code written by engineers with the top title, which changed his mind; others push back that skill still reduces bug rate, that the best programmers simply work on harder problems, and that deliberately rough prototype code can be the competent choice.
