## 2. The importance of communities around software {#story-2}
*#3 on HN · [lumi.codeberg.page](https://lumi.codeberg.page/blog/software-community.html) · 17 points · 5 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50294507)*

A blog post by lumi on codeberg.page arguing that copyleft licenses like the AGPL only signal software-freedom values without guaranteeing them, and that the community around a project is what actually determines its ethics. lumi cites the example of an AGPL-3.0 project that is dual-licensed commercially and requires a contributor license agreement — free in only a narrow sense — and suggests judging projects instead by their code of conduct, whether they ban generative-model contributions, whether a corporation controls them, and whether they push users onto Discord or GitHub. The post ends by urging readers to join or found communities and software cooperatives such as The Starlight Network and Polyphony.

**Discussion**: The thread is small and mostly disagrees with the thesis. The top commenter argues the license comes first and logically precedes community: publishing under an open license lets anyone build whatever community they want, while proprietary code leaves you stuck with the vendor's community. A second commenter takes a purist line that truly free software should be Apache 2.0. The rest of the thread is a hostile exchange about AI: one commenter reads the post as excluding AI users and tells them to get lost, another sarcastically contrasts supposedly oppressed AI users with Codeberg's funding, and another says AI-generated contributions are displacing human ones, citing Claude's role in Ruby development and asking whether DHH's omarchy Linux distribution has any real community left.

**Worth reading**
- [nickelpro](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50294865): Argues the causal order is reversed: the license determines what communities are even possible, so it precedes community rather than the other way round.
- [germanrabbit](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50294719): Pushes back from the opposite direction, saying that if the goal is真 freedom the license should be Apache 2.0 rather than copyleft.
- [shevy-java](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50294847): Claims AI-generated code is already crowding out human contributors, citing Claude's involvement in Ruby and questioning whether omarchy has a community beyond paid boosters.

## 3. Show HN: Auto Dismiss Mac OS "Allow <app> to find devices on local networks?" {#story-3}
*#5 on HN · [github.com](https://github.com/johncassil/dontallow) · 23 points · 11 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50294506)*

这是一个由 johncassil 开发的单文件式 Swift 小工具 dontallow，通过 Homebrew tap 安装，运行一个按用户启动的 launchd 代理，监听系统进程 UserNotificationCenter 创建的窗口，读取对话框文字，确认是「本地网络」提示后按下「不允许」按钮，默认在 0.25 秒内完成，空闲时零 CPU 占用，重启后仍生效。README 解释了弹窗反复出现的原因：Chrome、Brave、Edge、Arc 等 Chromium 浏览器每次启动会把应用包复制到随机临时目录以保住签名，macOS 把该权限绑定到那个随机路径，于是每次启动都像新应用；使用带版本号的安装路径（.../versions/1.2.3/）的应用则每次更新都会新增一条记录。它还提供暂停、allowlist、宽限时间、只记录不操作的前台模式，以及解读 Local Network 规则库的功能；但仅支持英文系统，构建需要 macOS 13 以上，弹窗本身只在 macOS 15 Sequoia 及以后存在，目前只在 Intel、macOS 26.6 上测试过。

**Discussion**: 评论不多，主要分两拨。一拨关心安全：有人问普通人如何判断这种小型 brew 包是否恶意，回复认为这本质上是在权衡「去掉一个烦人弹窗」的收益和「经由极易被投毒的渠道分发」的风险，并指出这问题适用于所有未经独立审查的可执行代码。另一拨则称赞这类工具的价值，认为这正是 LLM 时代个人可以低成本解决小烦恼的典型例子，还有人贴出自己用 Claude 写的类似工具 Squeegee（按窗口而非按应用关闭残留窗口）。也有技术性纠正与归因分歧：有人指出宣称「一个 Swift 文件」实际是两个；有人把缺少全局开关归咎于 Apple 产品经理的决定，另一位评论者反驳说这更可能是权限键选择带来的意外后果，而非有意设计。整体没有形成共识，讨论很快转向了泛泛的信任与 AI 话题。

**Worth reading**
- [HughParry](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50294763): 提出核心疑问：面对这种小型 Homebrew 包，除了自己读源码或让 AI 审一遍，普通人没有任何可靠办法判断它是否恶意。
- [sigmoid10](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50294803): 给出务实框架：这是所有未经独立审查的可执行代码共有的问题，要问的是「消除一个烦人弹窗」的收益是否值得承担一个极易被投毒的分发渠道带来的风险。

The thread is mostly recommendations and scene news, with no real dispute beyond terminology; one commenter also links a previous HN discussion from 2025.

## 7. Tomáš Mikolov awarded the Medal of Merit by the President of the Czech Republic {#story-7}
*#17 on HN · [vut.cz](https://www.vut.cz/en/but/news-f19528/two-but-graduates-received-state-honors-from-the-president-of-the-republic-d378468) · 124 points · 14 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50292233)*

A Brno University of Technology (BUT) news item reporting that two of its graduates received state honors from the Czech president at Prague Castle on 28 October: Tomáš Mikolov, co-creator of word2vec and founder of BottleCap AI, got the Medal of Merit for contributions to science, and Filip Maleňák got it for technology as creator of the Záchranka emergency app, which lets callers transmit precise location, request help when unable to speak, and find the nearest defibrillator. It is one of 51 state honors awarded that day, and the piece is largely a short institutional profile of the two alumni rather than a technical account.

**Discussion**: The thread is almost entirely appreciation: commenters call word2vec a beautiful piece of science and argue its real contribution was showing that a stripped-down linear model — trainable on multi-core CPUs with Hogwild — could learn meaning as geometry at scale, dominating the field for roughly five years before more complex architectures took over. That enthusiasm comes with pushback on originality: one commenter notes that TF-IDF, PPMI matrices reduced by SVD, and Bengio et al.'s neural language models predate it, and cites Levy & Goldberg 2014 showing skip-gram with negative sampling implicitly factorizes a PPMI word-context matrix. Others supply nostalgia (undergrads in 2016–2018 who never imagined these systems would scale to real-world interaction) and a memory that the original paper initially struggled to get accepted. The thread also answers the 「what has he been up to」 question: BottleCap AI, where his team works on making open-weight models — Chinese ones in particular — less verbose and less trigger-happy with tool calls.

**Worth reading**
- [armcat](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50294717): Points to BottleCap AI's current work, with links to a post about an agent running the same query 254 times and to their ThinkingCap work making Qwen3-8B more efficient and on-point.
- [microtonal](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50294358): The key correction on prior art: vector-space retrieval (TF-IDF, PPMI + SVD) and neural LMs came earlier, and Levy & Goldberg 2014 showed skip-gram with negative sampling implicitly factorizes a PPMI matrix — Mikolov's real win was a simple linear model amenable to fast Hogwild CPU training.
- [microtonal](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50294218): A practitioner's history of the era: cutting embedding training down from RNNs and convnets to a linear model is what the available compute demanded, plus a recollection that the word2vec paper was initially hard to get accepted.

## 8. The tragedy of losing your Wikipedia page {#story-8}
*#29 on HN · [wired.com](https://www.wired.com/story/the-tragedy-of-losing-your-wikipedia-page/) · 38 points · 49 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50247342)*

A Wired feature on Wikipedia’s deletion of biographies of living people, built around Martina Dimoska, a NASA Johnson Space Center “analog astronaut” whose new page was nominated for deletion the day after she celebrated it on Facebook. It explains that editors judge verifiability rather than truth, and that “notability” means enough independent reliable coverage, then shows what a page is worth to its subjects: a Michigan orthopedist used his as an expert-witness credential, a French-Iranian adviser says it helped win media invitations, and both lost their pages. The Dimoska case escalates through editor Kiril Simeonovski’s dismantling of 32 new sources, a Telegraph citation that turned out to be Telegrafi, and suspicious new accounts including one called “Kiril’s mom.”

**Discussion**: The thread is mostly unsympathetic to the article’s framing: commenters argue Wikipedia is an encyclopedia rather than a database, and that the scarce resource is editor attention and quality control, not storage. Several stress that a page requires substantial independent coverage, not personal merit, and point to self-written or publicist-written biographies as the real problem. A counter-current says Wikipedia lost its early ambition to be a reference for all knowledge by imitating Britannica, and that “consensus” can mean a clique outlasting dissent; one commenter cites a Chinese singer’s English article being speedy-deleted seconds after creation. Editors push back that deletion debates often run for weeks and that the Wired piece ignores cases where deletion review improved an article. A side discussion on correcting errors concludes that firsthand knowledge is not enough: you need citable reliable sources, and if the mainstream record is wrong, better scholarship or a neutrally presented dispute.

**Worth reading**
- [internetter](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50281058): An experienced AfD participant argues the piece cherry-picks, citing the Savannah Burton deletion debate as an example where the process improved an article and established notability, and links several discussions he says contradict Wired’s framing.
- [beedeebeedee](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50281032): A commenter describes being ordered in their first salaried job to create a Wikipedia page for their boss and battling editors for weeks until it stuck, calling the work unethical and saying they later quit.
- [jacobolus](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50282591): An editor explains the practical route for fixing factual errors: cite better reliable sources, and when mainstream sources are wrong, wait for scholarly consensus and present the dispute neutrally.

## 9. Keplearn: Extract Equations from Data in Seconds {#story-9}
*#30 on HN · [github.com](https://github.com/owls-on-wires/keplearn) · 71 points · 5 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50289590)*

Keplearn 是一个用 Rust 编写的确定性符号回归引擎，由 Chandler Freeman 开发，采用 MIT 许可证。它从 stdin 读取 CSV 或 TSV，并输出 JSON，其中包含按优劣排序的闭式方程：该引擎对目标列施加一系列可逆变换（log、sqrt、arcsin 等），依据带 MDL 复杂度惩罚的、占总数据 25% 的留出集进行排序，并把拟合常数映射到邻近的有理数以及 π、e、√2 的倍数，随后对这些结果逐一进行验证。作者报告称，在 SRBench 的 Feynman 数据集上，其符号求解率达到 64.7%，而此前公布的最高成绩是 AIFeynman 的 55.8%；但在 138 个没有闭式真值的黑盒数据集上，中位测试 R² 约为 -0.003，作者将这一点视为以精确形式为目标、而非通用曲线拟合所付出的代价。

**Discussion**: 这个帖子规模很小，且大多是历史背景，而非对工具本身的评估。最高票评论深入讲述了 Kepler 的故事：他从嵌套正多面体的球体理论出发，在 Tycho Brahe 的数据使该理论的预测变得不可信之后，放弃了这一理论；该评论把这段经历当作一次重大的智识谦逊之举。有回复补充说，Kepler 既没有计算器，也没有笛卡尔坐标系。只有一位评论者表示自己实际查看过这个仓库，称赞了其中的确定性符号回归算法，并链接到最近另一个相关 HN 帖子，指出符号回归「似乎正迎来一个小高潮」；另一条简短评论则称赞这份代码看起来像是人写的。没有人质疑 SRBench 的这些数字，但也没有人报告自己实际使用过该工具，因此这算不上背书。

**Worth reading**
- [seanhunter](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50293750): 详细讲述了 Kepler 如何放弃其优雅的嵌套正多面体理论，因为数据已经推翻了它，并称这是该工具名称所承载的真正教训。
- [likoya](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50294018): 补充了一个促使 Kepler 的成就更加令人惊叹的细节：他既没有计算器，也没有笛卡尔坐标可用来辅助工作。
- [verdverm](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50294750): 唯一一位表示自己查看过该仓库的评论者，称赞了其中的确定性方法，并链接到一条较早的 HN 符号回归帖子，认为这一领域正在获得关注。

## 10. Utah charges Kalshi and its CEO with illegally promoting online gambling {#story-10}

**Discussion**: The thread's sharpest correction is that the 9-bit headline understates the problem: the keys were never cryptographically random in the first place, so debating key-space size is beside the point, and several commenters note that broken crypto is nearly indistinguishable from working crypto at the surface — it looks like it encrypts, and almost nobody inspects further. ## 20. Forging a Microsoft Edge Full Chain with Logic Bugs Only {#story-20}
*#50 on HN · [blog.orange.tw](https://blog.orange.tw/posts/2026-10-the-last-proof-of-human-craft-browser-full-chain/) · 224 points · 38 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50285319)*

Orange Tsai of DEVCORE wrote up the chain he used to win Pwn2Own Berlin 2026 against Microsoft Edge: five bugs plus two Windows features, no memory corruption and, he stresses, no AI assistance, earning $175,000 and 17.5 Master of Pwn points, and later a Pwnie Epic Achievement award. The entry point is EdgeCAGuidanceNavigationThrottle, an Entra ID Conditional Access throttle that fires only from Microsoft origins but re-navigates to an attacker-supplied switch_profile URL through the browser's Navigation API; because the call passes no source_contents, Chromium binds the navigation to whichever tab is currently active, giving a UXSS. He then bypasses the origin check with a cross-window window.open, the popup blocker via a hardcoded Microsoft whitelist, the sign-in requirement via OneAuth auto-login, and the email requirement via a CORS misconfiguration on assets.msn.com; the escape comes from Edge's read:// Immersive Reader scheme being left off Chromium's not_allowing_javascript_urls_schemes blacklist, leading to arbitrary file write and sandbox breakout.

**Discussion**: The thread is almost uniformly admiring, with several commenters (including security-adjacent ones) saying the popup-blocker bypass, the UXSS and the eventual ACE were each a shock, and praising the writing for being dense and free of filler. The loudest substantive complaint is about silent sign-in behaviour: one commenter says this explains a time he found himself mysteriously logged into Edge, another calls the automatic profile creation 「disgusting behaviour, RCE chain or not」, and a third notes Google pushes similar defaults. A smaller subthread turns on Microsoft's maintenance of forked Chromium code — some argue the special-casing of Microsoft origins is inherently how such holes get built, others that the code was clearly low-priority and nobody cared. A separate argument breaks out over AI: two readers stopped at the author's joke about asking your agents to fill in the details, and were corrected by others that the post does walk through the full reasoning and states outright that no AI was used; one of the two conceded.

**Worth reading**
- [darkamaul](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50286550): Points to a bonus 「reverse chain」 video in which the team pops Edge starting from calc, which several replies find baffling and want a writeup for.
