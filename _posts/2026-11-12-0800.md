## 2. 93% of paint splatters are valid Perl programs (2019) {#story-2}
*#2 on HN · [mcmillen.dev](https://www.mcmillen.dev/sigbovik/) · 383 points · 50 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50459745)*

A short April Fools–style paper by Colin McMillen, published at SIGBOVIK 2019 (Pittsburgh, April 1), which asks whether it is possible to smear paint on a wall without producing a valid Perl program. Using OCR on a dataset of 100 paint-splatter images, it finds that only 93% parse as valid Perl, and it presents seven splatters that do not, plus a gallery pairing each image with the Perl source OCR extracted from it. The paper won SIGBOVIK's 「Unwitting Participation Ribbon」; the author also notes amusing late finds, such as a splatter OCR'd as lerzfijglpFiji-j and another as -*?, both of which evaluate to 0 in Perl, and ;E,'__' which evaluates to E__.

**Discussion**: The thread splits between people enjoying the joke and people objecting to the method. The main critique, from SoraTakai, is that the result depends entirely on which OCR is used and that the paper gives no baseline — no measurement of what fraction of random strings or random OCR output parse as Perl — so 93% is meaningless; charcircuit and notorandit agree it is really a story about bad OCR and cherry-picked settings. Defenders reply that this misses the point: it is a joke about Perl, and to the extent it is serious it is about how Perl accepts absurd inputs, with OCR merely a funny generator of arbitrary strings, and they note the author did try multiple OCR settings and picked the most 「interesting」 valid program when several appeared. Several commenters go off on a tangent about other formats that accept nearly anything — Java properties files (invalid only on a malformed \u escape) and CSV, with one first-hand tale of an XML error page being silently ingested as CSV and cascading into null-filled database records. Others note this is at least the fourth or fifth time the paper has hit the front page (2019, 2021, 2024 threads are linked), and a nostalgic subthread mourns and celebrates Perl, including a report of a ~200k-line Perl monitoring stack from 2003–2004 that is apparently still running.

**Worth reading**
- [0xbadcafebee](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50460966): Quotes the paper's explanation that most of the valid programs work because of Perl's 「unquoted strings」 feature, where bare alphanumerics parse as quoted strings — and notes that perl -w warns the bareword may clash with a future reserved word.
- [omoikane](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50462491): Points out a footnote gem: a Perl error quine, where writing the line 「Illegal division by zero at /tmp/quine.pl line 1.」 into a file makes Perl reproduce it, verified on perl 5.42.0 with any absolute path.
- [SoraTakai](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50462724): The strongest methodological objection: the result depends on which OCR is chosen and the paper establishes no baseline pass rate for random input, making the 93% figure meaningless.

## 3. Critical RCE Vulnerability in Apache HTTP Server {#story-3}
*#4 on HN · [wiz.io](https://www.wiz.io/blog/cve-2026-119618-rce-apache-http-server-vulnerability) · 149 points · 68 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50462177)*

A Wiz Research blog post about CVE-2026-119618, a race condition in the assign_headers() function of Apache's mod_http2 that lets an unauthenticated remote attacker corrupt heap memory and potentially execute code as the web server process. It affects httpd 2.4.55 through 2.4.70 (patched in 2.4.71) and standalone mod_h2 builds, and was found independently by Anthropic and Google Cloud's vulnerability research team. Wiz claims 64% of cloud environments contain vulnerable resources and 51% have publicly exposed instances, while stating no exploit exists and no in-the-wild exploitation has been reported; the mitigation is to patch or disable h2/h2c and stay on HTTP/1.1.

**Discussion**: The most concrete contribution is a commenter who says he reproduced the h2 race against a vulnerable build using DeepSeek for about $0.02 in 7 minutes, and confirmed the patched version resisted the same attempt. Much of the thread argues over the RCE label: several commenters say memory corruption is not RCE absent a working exploit, while others point to the Apache advisory and Red Hat's claim that public research demonstrated a reliable exploit against a default-configured mod_http2 server, plus the independent discovery dates from Anthropic and Google, as reason to believe it. Commenters also pick apart the post's quality — a leftover CVE-2026-111497 identifier from a Brotli writeup, GitHub listed as the vendor of mod_h2, and a patched standalone version (2.0.24) that contradicts the stated affected range up to 2.0.42 — with several calling it LLM-generated slop and pointing to the official httpd security page instead. Sysadmins note Apache is still widely deployed (roughly a fifth of sites, often behind nginx, the default WordPress Docker image, and wherever mod_shib or mod_md is needed), complain that no patched distro packages or containers existed yet, and question why disclosure preceded fixes. A side thread debates AI-discovered vulnerabilities, with most agreeing that finding bugs with AI is no different from fuzzing or static analysis, while one commenter wants projects that reject AI to refuse AI-found fixes and others mock that position.

**Worth reading**
- [jerrythegerbil](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50463365): Claims a quick reproduction: a directed prompt against a vulnerable version found an h2 race in 7 minutes for about $0.02 of DeepSeek usage, the same attempt failed on the patched build, and disabling h2 is the effective mitigation.
- [woodruffw](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50462662): Argues there is a strong norm against calling something RCE without a demonstrated exploit or at least a clean write-what-where path, so the RCE framing is overstated — and notes an LLM should easily prove RCE if it exists.
- [perching_aix](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50462841): Cites Red Hat's advisory describing a reliable public exploit against a default-configured mod_http2 server, plus Apache's timeline showing independent discovery by Anthropic (Oct 26) and Google (Nov 3).

## 4. Jane Street: Socratic Tutor {#story-4}
*#6 on HN · [github.com](https://github.com/janestreet/agent_skills/blob/master/socratic-tutor/SKILL.md) · 48 points · 12 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50438261)*

这是 Jane Street 的 agent_skills 仓库中的一份 SKILL.md 提示词规范，用户明确说出「教我」「考我」「练我到熟练」等触发语时才会启动，一次性的「解释 X」问题不会触发。流程是：先做 3-5 道题的诊断性摸底，把话题拆成 4-10 个子技能并各自评为 0-5 级（0 完全没听过，5 能教别人、能处理边界情况），此后一次只问一个问题、答错不直接给答案而是追问更窄的问题、按 2 次以上独立检验才升级，并穿插无预警的间隔复习，全部子技能达标后做一次综合终考；还支持「show progress」「harder/easier」「just tell me」「save state / resume from」等命令。

**Discussion**: 评论分成两派。一派认为这种把结构化思维固化成提示词的做法有价值，有人观察到职场邮件质量整体提升，认为 AI 正把「结构、精简、次序」这些表达习惯传导给人类。质疑方的火力集中在两处：一是这种文件是否真的有用——有人直言这像一年前的「AI 顾问式垃圾」，说自己客户刚开除过一个往代码库里塞这类文件的人，并好奇 Jane Street 的 AI 负责人是谁；二是原创性——有人怀疑 Jane Street 抄了 bevibing/socrates-skill，但立刻被反驳说两者复杂度完全不同，GitHub 上「socratic tutor skill」能搜出 92 个仓库，苏格拉底式提问本是公开技术，多人各自独立想到很正常，也有人晒出自己 2024 年 12 月就做过类似的。另有实践派表示根本不需要 skill：直接让 AI 生成 Lean 教程加练习，一次成型效果就很好，关键是可以随时说「我卡住了，给个提示」。共识大致是：技巧本身没问题，但把它包装成仓库文件是否比一句提示更有用，仍无定论。

**Worth reading**
- [beans6543](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50463915): 用 GitHub 搜索反驳抄袭指控：苏格拉底式提问是公开的经典教学法，「socratic tutor skill」能搜到 92 个仓库，Jane Street 的版本只是更复杂，属平行发明而非抄袭。
- [bob1029](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50463980): 最强反方观点：认为这类文件就是「AI 顾问式垃圾」，自己的客户刚因为有人往代码库里堆这种东西而将其解雇，并调侃 AI 革命在不同地方推进速度差异巨大。
- [IshKebab](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50464115): 第一手经验：不用任何 skill，直接让 AI 生成带练习的 Lean 教程，第四次学 Lean 终于有进展，最大价值是能随时要求「我卡住了，给个提示」。

## 5. I gave an agent a lakehouse and control of my garden {#story-5}
*#7 on HN · [jatorre.github.io](https://jatorre.github.io/garden/) · 33 points · 10 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50415162)*

Jatorre, who is building a data product called LakehouseBox, instrumented a 2 m² raised vegetable bed with about €300 of Ecowitt gear — eight soil probes, a weather station, leaf-wetness, rain, wind and solar sensors, plus a Reolink camera — and routed it all into Apache Iceberg tables via a device sink (the Ecowitt gateway POSTs 62 rows per post directly, no middleware). Since 18 September the garden has logged just over four million readings, which an agent queries instead of dashboards: e.g. a before/after SQL check showed lawn probes jumping from 29% to 72% moisture after watering while soil-ch5 stayed dry. The key claim is that the interesting work is giving the agent better senses and instructions rather than more applications — FTPS upload landed camera JPEGs in the catalog, and when the wide shots missed caterpillar damage (frass on leaf undersides), he changed the agent's prompt to examine full-resolution crops for holes, chewing and pests, comparing against previous days because damage is a rate, not a state.

**Discussion**: The top comment accuses the author of stripping the human element out of gardening — keeping his kids and wife out of it, letting agents invade a natural domain, and calling a sensor-soaked 2 m² bed 「vulgarity」 done because it can be done; replies brush this off (「Zeurpiet」) or joke that kids have other interests. A middle view holds that automating watering 「optimises the fun out of」 gardening — you stop developing the intuition for what each plant needs — but others argue the pleasure of tinkering is itself legitimate and that people don't have to agree on what the point is. The author shows up to describe what actually motivated the agent: a fixed rules engine for watering kept being wrong in ways that needed context (forecast, last week's response, leaf appearance), so an agent now runs each morning, reads the whole history, adjusts watering within hard limits, and logs its predictions to check against the next day; he discloses that the data layer is LakehouseBox, his own product, with code at github.com/jatorre/garden-lake. The most concretely useful thread is about safety: give agents named actions with hard caps (「water zone 2 for at most 10 minutes」) plus a log of past actions and a daily rate limit, because models misread sensors or repeat actions — 「a plant can survive one bad guess but not a stuck valve.」

**Worth reading**
- [jatorre](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50415168): The author explains why rules gave way to an agent (context-dependent watering decisions within hard limits, with self-checked predictions) and discloses that the lakehouse is his own product, linking the code.
- [krishanjarvis](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50415270): Practical hardening advice for agents that touch physical devices: named actions with hard limits, an action log read before each decision, and per-day caps so mistakes become no-ops.
- [zkmon](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50464205): The strongest objection: that the project removes family and the human element from gardening, and that imprecision is natural rather than a problem worth €300 of sensors to fix.

## 7. Gemini Decisions API {#story-7}
*#10 on HN · [blog.google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/gemini-decisions-api/) · 398 points · 122 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50456791)*

这是 Google DeepMind 的产品博客，宣布 Gemini Decisions API 与配套模型 gemini-decision-1：不再逐 token 生成文本，而是在一次前向传播中对候选选项打分，返回 0 到 1 的置信度。单次请求可输入最多 256K token 的多模态内容（文本、图像、音频、视频、PDF），并最多附带 1,000 个问题，支持是/否（noul）、2 到 255 选一（choice）和有序评分标准（score）三种题型。官方称端到端延迟 150-250 毫秒、每百万输入 token 收费 0.10 美元且输出 token 不计费，比 Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite 搭配 Interactions API 便宜近 5 倍，并称在路由、排序、多模态理解等基准上校准误差最低；文中还列举了 Gemini Notebook 用它做模型路由（日常查询延迟降低最多 40%）、Stitch 快速画布、Google Labs 的 CC 表情选择以及 Search 的 MCP 工具安全层等内部用例。

**Discussion**: 讨论很快从产品本身转向“概率是怎么来的”：有人认为对 yes/no 的 logprobs 取比值即可，但立刻被反驳说经过 RLHF/RLVR 后 logprobs 与真实概率脱钩，且校准需要针对具体任务的验证集，而决策模型恰恰事先不知道任务。围绕校准的争论最技术化：一位评论者指出，传统分类器只需在 p(x,y) 上校准，而这类模型把任务放进输入，分布变成 p(task,x,y)，任务措辞的细微变化就可能破坏校准，且平均意义下的校准会掩盖单个任务的高估或低估。实际使用者给出的场景集中在路由、护栏/是否需人工复核、打标与评测、把评分标准当作检索的 cross-encoder；也有人泼冷水，认为这只是免训练分类器的原型工具，进入生产后会遇到成本暴涨、难以维护的问题，甚至已有回滚到普通 LLM 的案例。价格是最集中的分歧点：有人抱怨 0.10 美元/百万 token 比 Liquid 的 0.02 贵得多、也约为 Jev 的两倍，但一位做过快速评测的用户报告 Brier 分数比 Jev 低约 4 倍（视任务 2-6 倍）、p50 延迟低 100 毫秒以上、端到端成本约两倍，称这是首次有模型大幅超过 Jev。另有一大支线是谷歌产品信任问题：不少人拿 Jules 被关停和 Antigravity 的起伏说事，反驳者则强调这是面向企业的模型与 API，有付费客户，逻辑不同于消费级实验品。

**Worth reading**
- [Topfi](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50461867): 做过快速评测的用户称该模型在多数场景下 Brier 分数比 Jev 低约 4 倍（2-6 倍视任务而定），p50 延迟也低 100 毫秒以上，但端到端成本约为两倍，并认为这是 Gemini 团队相当出色的一次发布。
- [sdl](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50464041): 给出最清晰的理论批评：传统分类器只需在 p(x,y) 上校准，而这类模型把任务也作为输入，分布变成 p(task,x,y)，任务略微换措辞就可能落在分布外，且平均校准良好不代表单个任务不失准。
- [nlpnerd](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50463896): 从工程角度唱反调：这类接口让没有 ML 背景的开发者能快速做出原型，但上生产后成本从 20 美元涨到每月 3 万美元、幻觉从 200 个测试样本里零次变成每天 600 次，且已有决策模型回滚到普通 LLM 的案例。

## 8. United Airlines bans AI agents from booking flights on behalf of travelers {#story-8}

A short how-to from Hugo van Kemenade on GitHub's new Actions workflow lockfiles (announced GA Oct 28, 2026). **Discussion**: The thread's most substantive contribution is from zizmor's maintainer, who lists what lockfiles still don't cover: reusable workflows must stay hash-pinned by hand, composite actions are only locked transitively through their callers (and `gh actions-lock` strips the action's own hash-pin by default, which weakens pinning for any external repo calling it), and Docker-image `uses:` refs aren't locked at all. **Worth reading**
- [woodruffw](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50459919): zizmor's maintainer enumerates the current gaps — reusable workflows, composite actions, and Docker-image refs aren't locked, and the tool's default behavior can strip hash-pins from composite actions, endangering external callers.

## 14. Did Life Begin in an RNA World? {#story-14}

## 18. How to make a CNC plasma cutter with high-frequency start {#story-18}

The frame is box-section steel with 3D-printed ABS-GF parts, 608 bearings as linear rails and Leadshine easy servos run at 12V despite an 18V spec; it cuts 3mm steel, but 4mm defeats him, cuts are bevelled on one axis, and he suspects the used machine may be faulty.

**Discussion**: The thread is largely additive rather than contentious. One commenter points to the parallel desktop wire-EDM scene (picoEDM, Gedm, openEDM, baxEDM) and asks whether kerf-width and taper tuning is worth it at this scale, since precision is plasma's weak spot. The only real disagreement is over USB fixes: one experienced commenter recommends measuring cable shield continuity with a DMM, which draws a rebuttal that tying grounds together creates ground loops and that braids should be grounded at one end — answered in turn by the claim that broken shields are worse than loops, braids are essentially never single-ended in sensitive designs, and ferrites do nothing for the low-frequency impulse noise at issue, though opto-isolated USB cables might (rarely at USB 2.0 HS).
