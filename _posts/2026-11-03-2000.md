## 1. Postman silently auto-installs AI plugins and attempts to share all API data {#story-1}
*#1 on HN · [github.com](https://github.com/postmanlabs/postman-app-support/issues/14370) · 626 points · 202 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50354960)*

这是一份 GitHub issue 报告（严重级别），针对 Postman Desktop 12.33.4：一位用户在 2026 年 10 月 24 日打开该应用后，Postman 的日志显示它运行了 “auto-existing-upgrade” 安装器，把 Codex 插件（1.1.0）和 Claude Code 插件（2.0.1）装进了他的编码代理，并另外装了一个 Postman CLI 到 ~/.local/bin 下。该安装器改写了 ~/.codex/config.toml，注册了一个 SessionStart hook，只要遇到任何 “非平凡的 API 工程任务”，就指示代理加载 postman:api-engineer skill；安装器源码还调用 config/batchWrite 来给自己的 hook 写入 trusted_hash（即自我批准）。它还启用了一个远程 MCP 端点（mcp.postman.com），该端点始终没能通过身份验证；报告者明确指出，这份报告只说明存在不受欢迎的集成，不主张存在数据外泄。提出的诉求包括：停止自动安装、移除 hook 自我批准、给出完整回滚路径，并把启动指令收窄。

**Discussion**: 评论几乎一致地感到愤怒，把这件事等同于恶意软件，并把它放进一篇更长的、关于 VC 驱动的产品腐化（“enshittification”）的叙事里：Postman 起初只是一个 HTTP 客户端，后来融资超过 4 亿美元、员工超过 1,500 人，如今需要靠强制登录、云端同步和 AI 功能来支撑 2021 年的 56 亿美元估值。讨论很快转向替代方案：Bruno 被提及的次数最多，此外还有 Yaak、Kreya、Hurl、Insomnia、IntelliJ/VS Code 的 .http 客户端、Emacs 的 restclient，以及 httpie/xh；好几位评论者指出，Bruno 和 Insomnia 其实也在走同一条路——Insomnia 8.0 及以后版本要求有云端账号，Bruno 也有付费层，其讨论帖则被描述成类似 “旅程”。有一条针对 “只是用 curl” 的反驳说，Bash 的转义很糟糕，而 GUI 对许多开发者来说仍然更容易，这就是 Postman 当初能流行起来的原因；另有一些人回应说，这个问题如今可以用代理生成的工具或 IDE 客户端解决。若干评论者给出第一手的企业案例（某家医疗机构的网络安全团队取消了 Postman 的认证），有人质疑 56 亿美元这个数字（一次 2021 年 ZIRP 时代的融资，此后没有新融资），也有人从生产使用的角度描述了同样的行为。只有一条帖子和 issue 本身一样，声称的规模比证据所能支持的更大。

**Worth reading**
- [bottombutton](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50356050): 一位在医疗行业工作的评论者说，他们的网络安全团队因这件事取消了 Postman 的认证，团队一夜之间换成了 Bruno，而与此同时 Postman 的销售仍在不断发来推销。
- [neya](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50355797): 一位评论者声称 Postman 在未经许可的情况下代理他的请求（敏感数据经由 Postman 的服务器转发，且无视防火墙里设置的排除规则），还说现在单个请求的导出功能被锁在 “Share” 和订阅后面——这两点当事人均未提供证据。
- [justincormack](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50356588): 对估值给出的更正：56 亿美元是 2021 年 ZIRP 高峰时的数字，此后 Postman 再未融资，如今不太可能以接近这一估值再融到钱。

## 2. Qwen 3.8 27B on my laptop can interpret model weights, too {#story-2}
*#2 on HN · [simonwillison.net](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/3/qwen-can-interpret-model-weights/) · 267 points · 75 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50355251)*

Simon Willison reproduces the no-tools experiment from 「Sparks of Weight-Space Programming」 by Rikiya Takehi, Jayden Teoh, Alex Zhang and Omar Khattab, feeding raw policy-network weights (as floating-point text) to a local model and asking which Atari-style game on a 10x10 grid the network plays. Qwen 3.8 27B — a 17GB model run in LM Studio via the llm-lmstudio plugin, after raising the context window past the default 8192 and bumping the timeout — correctly named Pac-Man (46m12s, 43,506 reasoning tokens), Pong (1h4m54s) and Breakout (52m25s), and failed only on the obscure 1981 game Freeway. Willison also flags the pushback: spaCy's Matthew Honnibal says the paper conflates tool-free weight reading with code-assisted weight editing, and Sean Goedecke argued the headline Qwen3.5-0.8B math fix (MATH-500 7.9%→48.1% by editing 0.016% of weights) is just steps a human ML researcher would take — Willison concludes it is 「a cool paper」 but not the end of source code.

**Discussion**: The thread splits into three arguments. Several commenters (zkmon, latentsea) treat this as the natural next step — weights are just another token sequence for a linear-algebra machine, and text-to-weights is a learnable function — while others dismiss it as unsurprising and uninteresting, prompting a sharp rebuttal that weights are not tokens and anyone making that claim should learn the linear algebra (defended at length by a reply arguing models simply have more fidelity in a KV cache than human working memory). A separate cluster of practitioners is more interested in direct weight editing as a practical tool than in the demo: one reports using a bigger model to strip undesirable pathways from a hand-trained LoRA checkpoint that further training could not fix, and others want quantized IQ3 quants made less loop-prone. On the paper itself, the sharpest correction is that the impressive Qwen3.5-0.8B example is an abliteration-style edit of a known thinking-loop pathology, not evidence of general weight comprehension — and the thread spends hundreds of words on whether a non-peer-reviewed blog post may be called a 「paper」 at all, ending with Willison conceding the authors' own BibTeX says blog post and editing his post to 「paper-shaped」.

**Worth reading**
- [badsectoracula](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50355997): First-hand reproduction: a commenter gave Qwen 3.8 27B his own tiny 5x5-to-2x2 upscaler network's source and setup code, and it deduced the architecture and pixel role without being told, then improved the weights while staying bit-compatible with his existing C program.
- [stymaar](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50356129): Strong correction: the paper's 0.8B math result is a fairly straightforward abliteration of a documented thinking-loop pathology, unrelated to the tool-free weight-reading experiment that Willison actually reproduced.
- [simonw](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50356603): Willison explains his 「paper」 wording was partly a deliberate dig at the ceremony and PDF format of formal publications, after conceding the authors themselves label it a blog post.

## 3. Jailbreaking my car's navigation system just so I can change the time {#story-3}
*#3 on HN · [blog.nns.ee](https://blog.nns.ee/2026/11/02/renault-hack/) · 381 points · 137 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50339288)*

A long reverse-engineering write-up about a 2014 Renault Latitude whose TomTom Carminat LIVE head unit (codename beaumont, Samsung S5P6440 SoC, Linux 2.6.28.10) refuses to let you set the clock unless a genuine navigation SD card is inserted — the menu definition that reaches the Settings applet is itself read from the card, and the system validates the card's burned-in CID against signed files. The author fetched the firmware, found the ttsystem update to be an ext3 root filesystem, built a QEMU emulator with a fake CAN bus to test attacks, and worked through the master.script interpreter (ttn), a single-token SHEX exec primitive, and a path-traversal LOG file-write bug, only to be blocked repeatedly by read-only and noexec mounts. The fetched copy is truncated mid-section, so the final exploit chain is not visible here; the author says in the thread that a kernel module still blocks executing anything from unsigned partitions.

**Discussion**: Most of the thread is a pile-on of first-hand infotainment horror stories: Subaru owners report head units replaced under warranty, CarPlay that won't reconnect, drifting and mutually inconsistent clocks, and a top display that lags or shows the wrong station; others cite a 2001 luxury car that only let you pick time zones, a 2025 Toyota with two disagreeing clocks, and a Nissan Leaf that re-asks for data-collection consent on every start. A second thread argues over replaceability — the old 2-DIN slot let you swap in an aftermarket unit, and several people say that's the real loss in modern sealed dashboards; a Tesla owner counters that OTA-updated cars avoid this fate, and is rebutted with HW3/Intel Atom cars already getting lesser updates and with the privacy cost of connected cars. A recurring consensus is that a plain manual clock-set button (or a DST switch, as on some Skodas) is all anyone wanted, with CarPlay as the pragmatic workaround. There are also good corrections: one commenter dismantles the folklore that power companies shifted grid frequency by 1 Hz at DST, noting it would take about 2.5 days to move an hour and would wreck every grid-tied machine; others pin a dead car clock on the 2G/3G sunsets and the 2019 GPS week rollover. The author himself drops in to explain that the SD card had already been thrown in the trash before the sale, which is why he declined it.

**Worth reading**
- [xx_ns](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50354216): The author reveals the previous owner had already accidentally binned the SD card and was offering to dig through the trash for it, which is why he said not to bother — deflating the thread's main 「just take the card」 criticism.
- [Majromax](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50356992): A detailed correction of the popular claim that power companies shifted grid frequency to 59/61 Hz for DST: synchronous clocks are only good for holding time, a 1 Hz offset would need ~2.5 days to move an hour, and the frequency is coupled to turbine inertia anyway.
- [xx_ns](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50354618): The author says he'll keep going for a better UI than the 「No maps found」 screen, but a kernel module blocks mounting non-signed partitions as executable, so a follow-up post may come later.

## 4. Stringman, an $800 open-source room scale gripper robot {#story-4}
*#4 on HN · [neufangled.com](https://neufangled.com/) · 102 points · 46 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50354358)*

Neufangled Robotics 发布 Stringman，一台约 800 美元、OSHWA 认证的开源缆索驱动机器人：四个锚点固定在天花板四角，用绳索吊起一只带电爪的滑块，从而覆盖整间屋子，而不是像桌面机械臂那样局限在固定半径内。在 7×7×3 米房间、绳索张力上限 28 牛顿的条件下，它在靠近地面处可举 2.2 公斤、全屋平均约 1.9 公斤，号称同等 2 公斤负载下单位成本工作容积约为常规方案（对比一台 0.75 米串联臂）的 500 倍。硬件为 PET-GF/PLA 3D 打印件，抓取端用 Raspberry Pi Zero 2W、锚点用 Pi 3A+，开放 CAD、PCB 与控制软件，配套 Python API、MCP server、LeRobot 兼容和 MuJoCo 仿真，团队称已在真实家庭累计测试约 1000 小时。

**Discussion**: 讨论被大量关于人形机器人杀人和被绳索勒死的玩笑占据，真正有内容的争论集中在机械设计上：一位评论者提出用三个电机、两个角落线缆合并成 X+Y 长度、再由抓取端第三条线放下的方案，声称能省一个执行器并避免线缆全屋扫过；其他人立刻指出那样悬吊点变成单点、摆动更难控制，侧向张力正是当前设计保持稳定的原因。还有人从运动学角度解释三条缆索本可解出一点，但活动范围被限制在三个锚点构成的三角形内，所以四条才是为了方形房间的一半以上空间。实用性上，多位评论者怀疑家庭环境里线缆是硬伤，怀疑它能搬被子、折毛巾或处理堆在玩具上的杂物，但有人反驳说儿童房恰恰是唯一真实场景。项目本身获得普遍好评，Hangprinter、Maslow CNC 和 Red Cable Robots 被反复引用为先行技术与先例，也有人批评页面图片的完成度和外观太像「小孩用胶带和乐高拼的」。

**Worth reading**
- [swid](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50357161): 提出用三执行器替代四电机的具体机械方案：两个角落单元各把两条线合并为 X+Y 长度线缆，第三条线装在抓取端负责下降，从而让大部分绳索留在天花板附近。
- [wave_function](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50356938): 从运动学解释三缆方案为何可行但受限——每条缆长定义一个球面、三球交点即定位点，活动范围会被限制在三个锚点构成的三角形内，因此才需要第四根线。
- [monkmartinez](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50356706): 指出这类缆索驱动机器人有长期先行者 Hangprinter，一个早已存在的开源三/四线缆 3D 打印平台，值得直接参考其运动学积累。

## 5. Fujifilm to boost tape production 5x as AI fuels archiving demand {#story-5}
*#5 on HN · [arstechnica.com](https://arstechnica.com/gadgets/2026/10/fujifilm-to-boost-tape-production-5x-as-ai-fuels-archiving-demand/) · 284 points · 113 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50336918)*

Fujifilm宣布投资约100亿日元（约6300万美元），在神奈川工厂小田原厂区把LTO磁带产能提升到2025财年的5倍，项目跨2026至2028财年，重点是去年发布的FUJIFILM LTO Ultrium 10。公司把需求增长归因于生成式AI带来的数据爆炸，以及磁带可物理离线（air-gap）存放、抵御勒索软件的特性。文中回顾了Fujifilm自1950年代起的磁带历史、2011年首个商用钡铁氧体磁带，以及Gen 10采用的钡铁氧体+锶铁氧体「混合磁性颗粒」。

**Discussion**: 大量一线经验集中在「磁带能用但会咬人」：写完后必须真正做一次完整还原测试，同一份数据最好同时写两三盘、用不同驱动器写，并定期清洗磁头，因为驱动器之间甚至同一台驱动器读写自己写的带子都可能失败。可靠性上评论者明显分裂，有人说15年经手约千盘磁带从未出错，也有人列举增量备份无法还原、Iron Mountain丢失磁带、环境温湿度导致故障等案例，折中看法是机房的恒定环境与专业磁带库才是正面体验的关键。价格讨论很具体：LTO-10 30TB带约259–300美元（约8.6–10美元/TB），但单台LTO-10驱动器约2.5万美元，于是有人推荐淘二手LTO-7/8，随即被纠正——近年标准把向后兼容从两代缩减到一代，LTO-10本身也不兼容旧带。关于「AI驱动」的说法不少人存疑，认为主因是勒索软件、从不删数据的囤积习惯和HPC的分层存储（HSM），AI只是叙事包装；对消费级磁带驱动器的期待则被市场规模和蓝光光盘的前车之鉴浇了冷水。

**Worth reading**
- [dylan604](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50353433): 一位运维老手列出实战教训：写完必须做真实还原而非只确认可读，同一份数据用不同驱动器并行写多份，清洗磁头，并注意不同归档软件（如直接 tar 写带）互不兼容。
- [doughnutstracks](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50352954): 给出实际报价：LTO-10 30TB带约259–300美元（8.6–10美元/TB）、40TB约491–555美元、LTO-9 18TB约92–129美元，但单台LTO-10驱动器报价约2.5万美元，才是真正门槛。
- [cyberax](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50356490): 建议现在不要买LTO-10：容量提升不值价格涨幅，驱动器太新，磁带库用的half-height型号还没上市。

## 6. Mac at Any Scale {#story-6}
*#7 on HN · [namespace.so](https://namespace.so/blog/mac-at-any-scale) · 220 points · 115 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50352758)*

A Namespace engineering post explaining why the macOS cloud provider fills racks with MacBook Pros instead of Mac Minis: a January order of Minis arrived in July, and Apple updates CPU models unevenly across devices. It details custom shelves, IP KVM, 10 Gb Ethernet and four cables per laptop, plus workarounds for unreliable HDMI, FileVault unlock prompts that time out, and a USB NIC bug. Namespace runs macOS VMs on Apple's Virtualization framework, is limited to two VMs per Mac, uses a gVisor-based userspace network stack to reach 4 Gbps, and distributes 500 GB to 2 TB images peer-to-peer.

**Discussion**: The thread splits between admiration for the engineering and frustration that Apple forces it. Many argue Apple should sell a proper rack server or Xserve-like product, while others say the server market is too small next to iPhone revenue and Apple has no incentive; a few note Apple already has custom 2U machines for its own cloud. The biggest practical objection is fire risk from roughly 100 lithium-ion laptop batteries per rack; a battery engineer replies that cooling and charge management matter more and recommends holding packs at 50% state of charge, while another says the datacenter operator still needs to accept the risk. Commenters also debate whether renting makes sense: one calculates a $900 Mac Mini pays for itself in nine months versus per-minute cloud Macs, but others say low-volume or fully remote teams have no place to put owned hardware and that cloud pricing buys away datacenter operations. On Apple's rules, commenters dispute the two-VM and 24-hour lease limits, and several defend Xcode Cloud for small projects while others call iOS code signing and tooling far worse than Android's; the author answers questions about the VM cap, xtool's lack of an iOS Simulator, MDM settings, and a BitTorrent-inspired image distribution system.

**Worth reading**
- [js2](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50355158): An engineer describes AWS's bare-metal Mac instances: FileVault is disabled, wiping takes about an hour, launches take 5–10 minutes, billing has a 24-hour minimum, and capacity often returns 「None available」, making elastic Mac CI impractical.
- [tgtweak](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50356410): A battery engineer argues the laptop packs are less risky than they look in stable, cooled airflow, but says Apple should hold them at 50% state of charge rather than 100% to limit degradation and swelling.
- [solatic](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50354020): A commenter runs the cloud-Mac economics and finds a $900 Mac Mini breaks even in nine months against per-minute pricing, questioning whether buyers compare costs or are mainly outsourcing datacenter operations.

## 7. OpenAI seems to be silently re-enabling model training on Codex chats {#story-7}
*#9 on HN · text post · 320 points · 95 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50352757)*

A text-only HN post, not a linked article: the author says their company deliberately turns off data sharing in Codex/Claude Code to avoid leaking IP, but they and several coworkers found the 「Improve the model for everyone」 setting silently switched back on at chatgpt.com/settings/data-controls. They ask whether this is isolated, and add edits noting an earlier HN thread (49643556) where others had seen it, plus a second opt-out at privacy.openai.com. No cause is established — whether it is a bug, a UI migration, or deliberate is unresolved.

**Discussion**: Reports are split, which is the main fault line: several people (chriskanan, kylehotchkiss, kian, the OP) say their setting came back on — chriskanan says they have two previously granted disable requests on record — while others checked and found it still off, and one commenter flatly challenges the OP to prove they ever toggled it off. The most-cited precedent is Mistral, where a commenter says switching from opt-in to opt-out and briefly removing the org-wide toggle reset every employee's slider to on, later reversed after about 48 hours — read by most as incompetence rather than malice, a framing others reject given the financial incentive. Practical advice dominates the rest: sign a ZDR contract, use gated models on Bedrock/Azure/GCP, or self-host open weights, with pushback that self-hosting a frontier-class model is unaffordable for small teams. Adjacent complaints surface about Google labelling chat retention as 「Gemini Apps Activity」 and about Claude Code triggering /feedback despite org settings.
