## 1. Payments Canada announces launch of Canada's Real-Time Rail (RTR) payment system {#story-1}
*#1 on HN · [payments.ca](https://www.payments.ca/canada-launches-real-time-rail-bringing-247-payment-infrastructure-national-economy) · 332 points · 181 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50427362)*

Payments Canada's press release announces the launch of the Real-Time Rail (RTR), a national system letting participating banks, credit unions and payment service providers clear and settle payments in real time, 24/7, using ISO 20022 data-rich messaging. It cites C.D. Howe Institute projections of up to $14.5B in cost savings over a decade (lower processing costs, less cash and cheque use, less float) plus $16B in economic gains rising to $27B as features are added, and claims Canada is the first G7 country to require centralized fraud services at launch — confirmation of payee, a transaction fraud score, a risk list and a fraud reporting platform. The launch cohort is notably not the Big Five banks but Wealthsimple, Questbank, Peoples Trust, Paramount Commerce, KOHO, DoBusiness.com, Digital Commerce Bank and Bank of America; RBC is expected in Q2 2027, all institutions by mid-2027, reaching 90% of Canadians within a year. Built with CGI, Deloitte Canada, IBM Canada and Interac, which acts as technical operator and first fraud services provider, with Interac e-Transfers beginning to clear and settle on RTR.

**Discussion**: A large share of the thread is a proxy argument about the US: several commenters claim America has no real-time payments and ask why, and are corrected — The Clearing House's RTP network has run since 2017, FedNow launched in 2023, and combined they reach roughly 70% of deposit accounts, with one commenter citing about $800B/year in FedNow volume. Others defend the US status quo on netting and consumer protection grounds, arguing debit/credit chargebacks and end-of-day settlement are features, not bugs, and that instant rails like Zelle have no reversal path for scams. On Canada itself the scepticism is about reach: no major bank is in the first wave, so commenters compare it to SEPA Instant, which sat mostly unused for a decade until the EU made bank participation mandatory, and ask what RTR offers over Interac e-Transfer, which is already free-ish and near-instant for most people. Practitioners push back that RTR is bank-to-bank plumbing, not a consumer app — it cuts Interac settlement from tens of minutes to seconds, and the public-ish ownership (Payments Canada, overseen by the Bank of Canada and Finance) is the point, with debate over whether it is technically a Crown corporation. Side threads correct a claim of a 6% effective Canadian corporate tax rate, complain that 「Rail」 reads as trains, and worry about a centralized fraud layer plus the precedent of the 2022 Emergencies Act account freezes.

**Worth reading**
- [cdnsteve](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50430868): Corrects the top comment's tax claim: the 6.4% is the marginal effective tax rate on new investment, not the corporate rate, which stays at 26.5% combined federal/Ontario or 11.2% for CCPC small-business income — and it is still draft legislation.
- [a-priori](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50429193): Explains the phased rollout: the big banks sit out phase one to control volume, join in phases two and three, with the Globe and Mail reporting every Canadian institution on the rail by mid-2027.
- [toomuchtodo](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50427894): A commenter working on FedNow policy gives concrete numbers — roughly $800B/year run rate, $10M per message for under five cents, 20-second SLA — and notes Walmart's pay-by-bank rollout as the first real consumer use case.

## 2. Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2 {#story-2}

Anthropic's launch post for Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Mythos 5.2, which it says are the same underlying model split by safeguards: Fable ships to all users with cyber/bio guardrails, Mythos goes to vetted organizations via the Life Sciences and Cyber Verification Programs. Claims include up to 30% faster output than 5.1, a price cut to $8/$40 per million input/output tokens (optional fast mode at 2.5x speed for $16/$80), and stunts like 1,000 coordinated subagents building a bootable OS from an empty folder in eight days, a Python-to-Rust production migration with 26x faster startup, a 30-hour formalization of Fermat's Last Theorem, two open black-hole physics problems, and three working electronic devices designed from scratch. Anthropic still recommends Opus 5.5 as the default daily driver, positioning Fable/Mythos as advisor, orchestrator, or heavyweight for the hardest open-ended work.

**Discussion**: Most of the thread is not about 5.2 at all but about the recurring claim that Anthropic quietly degrades the previous flagship before a launch — several users say Opus 5.5 got noticeably worse in the past week or two, and others report the same for OpenAI's GPT Astra and Sol 6.1, so the suspicion is aimed at labs generally rather than one. The skeptics argue this is apophenia plus confounded variables: thinking-effort defaults, prompt/harness differences and traffic routing change output quality without any weights changing, and one commenter reran a pre-Nov-3 eval and found quality flat-to-better with faster time-to-first-token and slightly fewer tokens. A second cluster attacks the benchmark presentation — Fable 5.2 at xhigh compared against Gemini 4 Carbon at medium, Carbon left off the cyber comparison, and older models suddenly scoring 20-30% where they used to score 60%. Hands-on reports are mixed but warmer: faster and fewer max-token overflows than 5.1, strong vision-to-HTML results, but at least one user burned a full five-hour quota in 20 minutes because it spawned nested subagents despite an explicit cap. Side threads cover the odd version numbering (flagship 5.2 below Opus 5.5), Claude's prose style versus Gemini, launch-week subscription reset offers, and the economics of $200/month plans.

**Worth reading**
- [ariwilson](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50430746): Reran a 30-task bug-fix eval on Opus 5.5 medium before and after the suspected nerf: 19/20 vs 36/40 solved, 0.95x thinking tokens, and median time-to-first-token halved from 3.0s to 1.7s — evidence of no quality regression.
- [spwa4](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50430419): Argues the timing is not coincidental, tying the release to Qwen4 previews and claiming Anthropic is sitting on better non-public models (Model 2/3/4) and is now metering capability out deliberately.

## 3. Our Smallest, Most Efficient Model: Muse Ember {#story-3}
*#4 on HN · [research.meta.ai](https://research.meta.ai/blog/muse-ember) · 115 points · 49 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50429024)*

Meta's research blog post announcing Muse Ember (muse-ember-1.0), a small, cheap model aimed at the high-volume work agents repeat: tool calls, coding subtasks, routing, extraction and long-document parsing. It claims to match Claude Haiku 5.5's best score at roughly a third of the cost and to beat every GPT-6 Luna setting, at $0.10 per million input tokens, $0.004 per million cached input tokens and $0.50 per million output tokens, with a 1M-token context window and up to 512K output tokens, natively handling text, images, audio and video. Meta says it is the first model trained using Muse Wonder Full, was evaluated under its Superintelligence Scaling Framework (staying below high-risk thresholds), and costs about $0.14 per Artificial Analysis task.

**Discussion**: Almost nobody in the thread discusses the model itself; it is dominated by Simon Willison running his standard 'pelican riding a bicycle' SVG test at the six new effort levels (minimal, low, medium, high, xhigh, max), finding that more reasoning tokens did not produce an obviously better drawing and that several people actually preferred the minimal output. swatcoder explains why: models build SVGs concept-by-concept (bicycle, then pelican, then fish basket) instead of interleaving parts, so details like legs straddling the frame are wrong across the board, and short prompts make models fill gaps from learned associations — the same reason every drawing has a yellow sun, motion lines and a fish basket. On substance, commenters note the unusually low cache price (4% of input versus roughly 10% for Luna/Haiku), ask why no DeepSeek comparison and why there is no parameter count to justify 'small', and complain the weights are not open; one suggests it is the stealth OpenRouter model 'crouton-alpha', another that refusals should score negatively rather than zero on benchmarks. There is also a side argument about whether Meta is still relevant in the LLM race, with several defending its post-Llama-4 reboot and the Spark series, and a tangent predicting (and disputing) a future of hyper-specialized per-profession models.

**Worth reading**
- [swatcoder](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50430884): Explains the 'ley lines' phenomenon: vague prompts make models fill gaps with the same trained-in defaults (yellow sun, three motion lines, fish basket), which is why creative AI output reads as lazy at scale.
- [minimaxir](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50430835): Flags the concrete pricing edge: cached input costs 4% of normal input versus about 10% for Luna and Haiku.
- [open-paren](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50429645): Claims Muse Ember is the model behind the 'crouton-alpha' stealth listing on OpenRouter, which another commenter immediately asks them to justify.

## 7. Show HN: MeowDisplay Is iPhone Mirroring, but in Reverse {#story-7}
*#9 on HN · [meowdisplay.app](https://meowdisplay.app/) · 31 points · 13 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50429636)*

MeowDisplay is a free, open-source app that turns an iPhone, iPad, Android device or another Mac into a display and remote-control surface for a Mac — the inverse of Apple’s iPhone Mirroring. Its landing page pitches Window view with tabs and split panes, an edge-held Stage Peek window switcher, several touch modes (Direct Touch, Smart Touch and Trackpad), Apple Pencil input, clamshell operation through a software virtual display, and USB, local Wi-Fi or Tailscale connections. It is iOS 2.0 on the App Store, a macOS 14+ companion app (2.0.0), and Android 10+ in closed testing; the author says it began as a fork of OpenDisplay and is built in Swift and Kotlin.

**Discussion**: The thread is broadly positive — one commenter said they had vibe-built a similar window-sharing system over a tailnet, and another praised the site as refreshingly free of marketing slop — but it presses on provenance and latency. A commenter challenged the lack of fork disclosure, and the author responded by adding it to the post and explaining that MeowDisplay builds on OpenDisplay’s capture, virtual-display, encoding and receiver foundations while taking interaction, streaming and security in a phone-first direction. Latency is the main skepticism: one commenter said nearly all remote-display tools feel mediocre and only RDP has felt truly responsive; the author replied that Wi-Fi testing showed a 14–17 ms median from capture to receiver handoff, not end-to-end, with occasional 50–90 ms spikes and stalled frames, and that he deliberately shows the Mac’s real cursor rather than a locally rendered one that might feel smoother but misrepresent its position. Asked about color, the author confirmed there is no HDR support yet and that iPhone color-profile accuracy has not been verified. Commenters also corrected the assumption that Apple’s built-in Sidecar already does this: Sidecar extends a Mac to an iPad, but not an iPhone.

**Worth reading**
- [mpolichette](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50430187): mpolichette asks what the OpenDisplay fork actually changes and argues the provenance should have been declared in the announcement — the challenge that prompted the author to add the disclosure.
- [raiseCatError](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50430121): The author gives concrete latency figures: 14–17 ms median capture-to-receiver handoff on Wi-Fi, not end-to-end, with 50–90 ms spikes and visible stalls.
- [freeli](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50430178): freeli corrects the Sidecar comparison: Apple’s Sidecar extends a Mac’s display to an iPad, but it does not work on iPhones.
