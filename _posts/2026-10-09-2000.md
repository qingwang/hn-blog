## 1. Cloudflare acquires Deno {#story-1}
*#1 on HN · [deno.com](https://deno.com/blog/cloudflare) · 846 points · 464 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50019911)*

Deno 官方博客宣布整个 Deno 团队加入 Cloudflare，转型去把 celld（自托管 Durable Objects/Workers 实现）合并进 workerd，目标是让 Workers 编程模型在 Cloudflare 网络和自建基础设施上都能一等公民地运行。具体时间表：Deno 运行时再支持一年（每月发布 bug 与安全修复），之后停止开发，代码继续开源但由外部接手；Deno Deploy 六个月内关停并为付费客户提供迁移到 Workers 的支持；JSR 继续运营但基础设施迁到 Cloudflare；rusty_v8 继续维护并逐步并入 workerd。文章把这描述为从 Deno 到 Deno Deploy 到 celld 的延续，并强调 Durable Objects 对 AI agent harness 的价值。

**Discussion**: 讨论几乎全部聚焦在被埋在文末的那句：Deno 运行时一年后停止开发。许多人为自己 production 项目上的 Deno 代码发愁，也有人认为迁移回 Node/Bun 只需一两天，不算大事。主流解读是这是一次 acquihire——Cloudflare 要的是团队和 celld，不是 Deno 本身，一位评论者称之为「买下自托管版 Workers 以商品化互补品」，也有人担心 celld 被买来扼杀，Kenton Varda 亲自出面澄清 workerd 与 celld 都会继续开源、且语义不限于 Cloudflare 自家基础设施。反复出现的解释是 VC 融资：一旦拿了风投，要么赢者通吃要么被收购关闭，因此结论是「选无聊技术」，Node 依然是默认推荐。有少数人持正面看法，认为 Cloudflare 的 serverless 原语简洁廉价、团队有了稳定归宿，celld 模型并入 workerd 比 Deno 本身更有意思。

**Worth reading**
- [tech234a](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50022900): yt-dlp 把 Deno 作为下载 YouTube 视频时的默认首选 JS 运行时（取代自研解释器），虽然也支持 Node 和 QuickJS，但作者说明该项目选择 Deno 有可移植性、安全和速度上的理由——这让 Deno 停更的影响远超一般 Web 项目。
- [kentonv](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50025648): Kenton Varda 反驳「workerd 语义只能在 Cloudflare 基础设施上跑」的说法，称已有生产用户完全不用 Cloudflare 自托管 workerd，并要求大家读 Cloudflare 博客中关于自托管的一节。
- [sholladay](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50021907): 长期 Deno 生态用户 sholladay 的亲历说法：Deno 从「漂亮简洁」变成臃肿，是从把 npm 兼容列为优先事项那天开始的，他认为这源于风投压力，等于放弃了从第一性原理重建 Node——这一判断在评论里被多人复述但也被反驳。

## 2. Show HN: Carrier-Explode: iPhone, Pixel and Galaxy carrier settings decoded {#story-2}
*#2 on HN · [carrierexplode.com](https://carrierexplode.com/) · 68 points · 9 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50024499)*

carrier-explode 是一个副项目，持续归档 iPhone、Pixel 和 Galaxy 固件中的运营商设置，并以解码、可比较的形式呈现。其 2.0 版本带来了重做的后端，你可以查询某个运营商在各手机上的 APN、VoLTE、Wi-Fi 通话和 5G 支持情况，对比任意两个版本或运营商，并通过 JSON API 或每日更新的 CC0 数据集获取全部数据。作者表示部分假设仍在核实中，但该工具已被若干爱好者群体证明有用。

## 3. Our $445M Series D {#story-3}
*#3 on HN · [oxide.computer](https://oxide.computer/blog/our-445m-series-d) · 472 points · 194 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50020014)*

这是 Oxide Computer 官宣 4.45 亿美元 Series D 的博文。公司称其普通运营（卖服务器）已产生应税收入，春季甚至缴纳了所得税——这在烧钱的初创公司里罕见；主要原因是订单积压量巨大、需求远超供给，卖硬件需要提前把大笔现金压在零部件和制造上，因此融资用于保障库存、满足积压订单并扩张产能。本轮由 Eclipse 领投，老股东 USIT、Jane Street、Counterpart 跟投，新投资人包括 Atreides Management，以及作为战略投资人的 AMD。

**Discussion**: 讨论被“招聘流程”抢了风头：Oxide 要求进入面试的候选人预留约 9 小时（三段 3 小时），且申请阶段要提交大量书面材料（工作样本、写作样本和 8 道长问答题）。一派认为这体现了高标准和对匹配度的重视，能带来更一致的团队文化；另一派（包括多位求职者）批评它不尊重候选人时间，尤其在同时投递几十家时不可行，而且有人按要求付出大量时间后根本没收到承诺的回复。Oxide 方辩解说书面材料才是主要筛选手段，进入面试环节时通常已是从数百人中筛出的最后一到两名候选人，因此实际投入远小于最坏情况。另有少部分讨论围绕三个问题：为什么盈利后还要发股而不是举债（有人主张订单融资用债务更合理，反驳者认为债务会放大风险且当前股权条件极好）、公司到底卖什么（一体化机架 + 全栈开源软件，本地部署的“云计算机”，目前不支持 GPU），以及大规模稀释是否伤害老股东。整体氛围以祝贺和“产品很酷”为主，质疑主要集中在面试成本和融资结构。

**Worth reading**
- [dcre](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50021016): Oxide 的 dcre 解释流程：面试官已读过候选人的详细书面材料、候选人也拿到面试官当初的申请材料，所以没有电梯陈述和简历走查，只有对话，且书面材料才是主要筛选关卡。
- [bcantrill](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50022795): Bryan Cantrill 反驳“长时间面试是新现象”：他 1996 年去 Sun 就被飞去做了整整一天 9 小时面试外加午餐晚餐，并建议真想抱怨的话该抱怨他们书面化的招聘流程。
- [JRandomHacker42](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50020695): 一位求职者指出 Oxide 承诺会回复每一份申请，但他投递后 6 个月石沉大海；另一条回复补充申请材料体量极大，怀疑评估者根本读不完。

## 4. Triple-A Minesweeper {#story-4}

Triple-A Minesweeper is a browser-based joke game (minesweeper.mikelacher.com) that wraps an ordinary game of Minesweeper in the trappings of a big-budget modern release: a run of unskippable publisher/engine logo stings, an over-dramatic opening cutscene with voiceover dialogue, a mission-style HUD indicator pointing at a square, and a long credits roll. Its whole joke is that none of the ceremony affects the actual puzzle — commenters note you can hit a mine and the scripted ride continues regardless. The page itself had no readable text when fetched, so everything here comes from the title and the thread.

**Discussion**: The thread is almost entirely affectionate riffing, with people inventing additional AAA absurdities: a launcher, a forced 42GiB update that gives you nothing if you were in a hurry, EULA scrollboxes, an epilepsy warning, a save-icon warning, production babies and credited pets, lootboxes, season passes, operator skins and a kernel-level rootkit. ## 5. Typesafe AI raises $870M at $7.5B {#story-5}
*#5 on HN · [typesafe.ai](https://typesafe.ai/blog/series-ai) · 109 points · 84 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50023450)*

A fundraising post on Typesafe AI's blog announcing an $870M Series A at a $7.5B valuation, led by Andreessen Horowitz with Sequoia Capital, DCVC and assorted angels, and Martin Casado joining the board (the headline puns 「Series AI」). The post promises to go all-in on Jev, its 「machine-native」 decision models, claims a third of the Fortune 500 already use it and that it has saved customers millions in production, and says enterprise features are coming. It is mostly promotional copy with little technical detail.

**Discussion**: Commenters are split on whether the valuation makes sense. Skeptics note a dozen decision models appeared within two days of Jev's release and several dozen within a week — mostly open source — plus OpenAI's own Decisions API, so Jev looks easily cloned; one argues the core method was published on arXiv in 2025 and Typesafe mainly marketed it, though others object that the cited paper solves a specific sales-conversion problem, not the general-purpose task Jev targets. Defenders counter that small fine-tuned LLMs are a different beast from Jev on cost, latency, native output format and (allegedly) prompt-injection resistance, and that strong execution and marketing are real assets. Others argue moat is beside the point: one long comment frames the round as a self-reinforcing bet where writing the check itself attracts talent and customers, and simonw notes VC is a hits business where Anthropic's ex-OpenAI founders already paid off. Practitioners add concrete data points — a commenter says Jev can't model coin-flip probabilities correctly, another says Jev trails several hastily built alternatives on public leaderboards, and a developer runs the open-source Laya model locally in about half a second on a tiny VM. Many also admit confusion with the old Scala company Typesafe (now Lightbend).

**Worth reading**
- [devin](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50025159): Disputes that Jev took years to develop, claiming the technique was published on arXiv in 2025 and Typesafe simply marketed it — a dispute others argue is a misreading of that specific-purpose paper.
- [verdverm](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50025469): A first-hand test claims Jev fails at basic probabilities (coin flips, dice rolls), and that the 「not real probability」 disclaimer only appears after you get a result.
- [pickle-wizard](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50025374): A developer spun up the open-source Laya decision model on a 2-vCPU/6GB VM with ~0.5s responses, arguing local deployment beats paying for a SaaS and keeps customer data in-house.

## 6. Sorry, I'm in a meeting {#story-6}
*#6 on HN · [iminafleeting.com](https://iminafleeting.com/) · 593 points · 195 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50018088)*

iminafleeting.com is a joke-but-usable tool by John Carroll that plays ~12-minute synthetic recordings of made-up workplace meetings — e.g. standups and API design reviews — with all-AI voices, AI-generated or stock faces, and invented names (Carroll himself is the only real one). You pick a meeting and press play on speaker so anyone nearby assumes you are booked; it starts at a random point so the opening line doesn't repeat, and with 「Back-to-back meetings」 ticked it rolls into another meeting suited to the time of day. It also offers a fake calendar invite linking back to the site, plus 「Leave」/「I have to drop」 buttons whose participants say goodbye before hanging up.

**Discussion**: The strongest thread is managers and ICs trading real tactics for defending focus time: creating a manager-owned 8am–11am Friday 「team meeting」 so the block carries authority, booking fake meetings with names like 「[IMPORTANT] CUSTOMER OUTAGE」 to survive a publicly viewable calendar, and even inventing virtual HR employees (「Team On-call Primary/Secondary」) so no one could page individual SREs. A prominent counter-argument says this doesn't fix the root cause — the meetings just get squeezed into the afternoon, and some people treat the block as their only work time, so what's really needed is a manager who blocks interruptions himself and backs the team when they decline meetings. Several people report the audio is broken outside the British track, with HTTP 404s on media like standup-a.mp4 across Firefox, Chrome, Safari and Brave (the author acknowledges and is fixing it), and there's a long argument over whether the synthetic voices are convincingly dull or obviously uncanny AI speech. A large tangent triggered by one commenter's warning about an unblocked YouTube ad ruining the illusion sprawls into adblockers vs. YouTube Premium, and others compare the site to old boss keys (Leisure Suit Larry's F11, Space Quest III) and hackertyper. Requests repeat for a Zoom/Teams/Meet UI and more accents.

**Worth reading**
- [alexpotato](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50020900): An SRE manager describes creating a weekly 8am–11am Friday 「team meeting」 for his team, who loved it precisely because a manager-owned block carries more weight when someone tries to schedule over it.
- [xyzzy_plugh](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50024814): A first-hand account of SREs so overloaded that HR created virtual employees 「Team On-call Primary/Secondary」 with their own laptops and phones, making the team effectively invisible to the rest of the org.
- [Aurornis](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50025209): A detailed counter-argument that blocking out focus time treats the symptom: meetings get compressed into the afternoon, some people treat the block as their only work time, and the real fix is an active manager who absorbs communications.

## 7. Pointing AI at archives found a forgotten meteorite, lost rhinos, and more {#story-7}
*#8 on HN · [jessewaites.com](https://jessewaites.com/blog/post/i-pointed-ai-at-400-years-of-archives/) · 41 points · 23 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50019056)*

软件工程师 Jesse Waites 受历史学家 Benjamin Breen 用 Opus 5.5 在荷兰东印度公司（VOC）档案中发现 1615 年渡渡鸟目击记录的启发，搭建了一条多模型 AI 研究流水线。素材包括 GLOBALISE 转录的 435 万页 VOC 档案（1600s–1790s）、荷兰国家图书馆报纸和两百年美国报纸；他用廉价决策模型 Jev 做初筛（读完 59,000 条大象记录仅花约 3 美元），再让 Claude Haiku 精读被标记的段落，最后人工核对原始手稿扫描。最具体的成果是 1812 年《Java Government Gazette》转载的一封孟买来信，记载 8 月 6 日在今马哈拉施特拉邦 Cokurrgaum 村附近坠落的一块约四磅、富含铁、带黑色熔壳的石头——他查遍六份陨石目录都无记录，若被确认将是该邦最早的陨石坠落记录，比现有记录早 26 年；此外还发现 1738–1740 年荷兰人两度运送爪哇犀牛给康提国王、犀牛均在途中或马厩死亡的信件。作者还开源了他的工作流工具 Antiquity（github.com/jessewaites/antiquity）。

**Discussion**: 评论区分成两条主线：一是对页面本身特效的抱怨，二是对方法论和动机的质疑。多位评论者（jvanderbot、Telemakhos、acgourley）认为旋转犀牛、火山和动画流程图等特效既滑稽又遮挡正文，Telemakhos 直言网页的功能是供人阅读，特效盖过文字是「形式不随功能」；也有人（qarl、xenospn、fragmede）表示觉得有趣。作者 piratebroadcast 后来在页顶加了一个关闭特效的按钮。方法论上，yannis 追问翻译可靠性并指出开普敦和邱园还藏有大量 VOC 档案（部分被私掠船劫掠），作者与 acgourley 讨论了 OCR 残迹、1650 年前迥异书法的难点，acgourley 还建议对历史文档做一次昂贵的结构化预解析以降低后续查询成本。sorokod 尖锐质疑作者本人对东印度公司其实了解甚少，称这类项目像「垃圾食品」，作者反击其缺乏乐趣，hypfer 则指出这类兔子洞确有价值但风险值得点出，气氛一度紧张。dang 补充了 Breen 那篇关于渡渡鸟的原文，并指出 benbreen 本人就是 HN 用户。

**Worth reading**
- [yannis](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50025154): yannis 指出除荷兰外，开普敦和邱园也藏有大量 VOC 档案（部分系私掠船劫掠所得），并质疑字形难辨的高地荷兰语/德语文本经过 AI 翻译后可靠性如何。
- [acgourley](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50025107): acgourley 正在做类似的政治舆论语料解析项目，建议对历史文档也做一次昂贵的结构化预解析，之后提问成本会更低，并指出现代转录与历史扫描面临同样的拼写推断问题。
- [whythismatters](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50025015): whythismatters 摘出作者开源工作流工具 Antiquity 的 GitHub 链接，任何有档案问题和编码 agent 的人都能复用这套流程。

## 15. The reciprocal sum of the prime-prefix-free numbers converges [pdf] {#story-15}
*#18 on HN · [jdb19937.github.io](https://jdb19937.github.io/prime-prefix-free/ppf.pdf) · 3 points · 5 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49961538)*

这是微软的一篇产品发布文章，推出 Microsoft-Decision-1，一个「决策评分」模型，专门为路由、分类、优先排序、验证和工作流控制输出软件可直接采用的结构化结果。该模型是在 Qwen3.5-9B 上后训练的，先在 Microsoft Foundry 上线、随后登陆 OpenRouter；微软称它在 36 项基准测试（约 15 万个盲测问题）中准确率最高，P50 延迟比 GPT-6 Sol 快约 35 倍、比亚军 Quyet-1.0-Large 快 4.5 倍，输入定价每百万 token 0.042 美元、输出免费。文章还给出内部用例：Xbox Research 用它处理 1 万多条反馈（比 GPT-6 Sol 快 14 倍、便宜 200 倍），Copilot 团队测得比 GPT5.6 Luna 快 100 倍，Microsoft Discovery 的自适应重规划结果一致性是 LLM 的 46 倍。

## 18. MXC - a sandboxed code execution system {#story-18}
*#24 on HN · [github.com](https://github.com/microsoft/mxc) · 154 points · 73 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50016489)*

Microsoft's eXecution Container (MXC) is an SDK for running untrusted code — agent output, plugins, tools — on Windows, Linux and macOS. It exposes a unified policy model (JSON container-creation requests, filesystem read/write/deny lists, egress control, UI/clipboard policy) over pluggable backends including ProcessContainer, Windows Sandbox, LXC, Bubblewrap, Seatbelt, MicroVM (Nanvix), Hyperlight, IsolationSession and WSLC, with Rust, .NET and Node SDKs, plus a diagnostic 'audit' mode that records denied accesses to help author policies. Distributed via crates.io, NuGet and npm; Rust pinned to 1.93; optional Windows-only telemetry is off unless several consent conditions are met.

**Discussion**: The thread is broadly positive on the concept — several commenters who have hand-rolled bubblewrap setups (dannyw, gandreani, saghm) say consistent configuration is genuinely hard and error-prone, and they single out the audit/debug mode, MIT license and readable docs as the real value. The biggest criticism is that MXC sits on OS primitives and shares the host kernel (seatbelt on macOS, bubblewrap on Linux), whereas projects like smolvm/microsandbox give each task its own VM and kernel; simonw specifically flags that fine-grained network policy (allow/deny by hostname, IP, CIDR, port or protocol) is missing on macOS, and points to Anthropic's sandbox-runtime, which he says does exactly that on the same primitives. Others argue the landscape is overcrowded and speculative — dozens of harnesses and sandboxes shipping in six months — with skeptics doubting Microsoft's cross-platform quality and defenders noting MXC is really a policy-orchestration layer, not a new sandbox. There is a heated side-thread after one commenter claimed MXC contains a sandbox escape and that bubblewrap's setuid/capability handling is wrong; another demanded issue reports or code references as evidence.

**Worth reading**
- [simonw](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50019972): simonw: the feature he cares about — hostname/IP/CIDR/port-level egress rules — is present on Windows and Linux but missing on the macOS Seatbelt backend.
- [dannyw](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50018364): dannyw: debunks a 350k-SLOC figure by showing the counter includes comments and tests, and lists concrete bugs (a no-op seccomp filter, .env treated as shellcode) in a rival bash-based sandbox the critic was promoting.
- [rfgplk](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50018723): rfgplk: claims MXC contains a sandbox escape and that its bubblewrap wrapper gets setuid and capability dropping wrong, with no links — a claim another commenter immediately challenges for lack of evidence.

## 19. Programming Isn't Special {#story-19}
*#25 on HN · [blog.glyph.im](https://blog.glyph.im/2026/10/programming-isnt-special.html) · 131 points · 145 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50017357)*

A blog essay by Glyph, creator of Twisted's Deferred, arguing that programming is not a special case: it is Art, so the AI resistance seen in music, film and publishing should apply to software too. Drawing on John Berger's 「Ways of Seeing」 and the concept of mystification, he contends that most code (like most writing) is mundane copywriting, but that this undignified work is the practice ground where the roughly 0.1% chance of greatness comes from — doing every project with AI takes that chance from 「definitely sometimes」 to 「never」. He offers Deferred, an aesthetic reaction to passing callback/errback boilerplate, as his own example of a code poem, and closes with 「Slop is slop, no matter the medium.」

**Discussion**: The thread splits on the thesis itself. Many engineers reject the art framing: programming is an industrial, utilitarian process, nobody reads the code, only the results matter, and the tenth internal REST API is not a museum piece — though others note the essay pre-empts exactly this by saying most writing and painting is mundane too. Defenders give examples: jaekwon calls his CoffeeScript parser and GnoVM art, a non-programmer points to the Vitruvian Man as a technical document that became art, and several cite the demoscene, Black Perl and the Obfuscated C Contest. A recurring meta-complaint is that many commenters clearly didn't read the piece. On AI, the thread turns confessional: one commenter likens it to drum machines and backhoes and asks to keep his tools, another says AI finally lets him write code to his own standards, while a third is bleak that nobody reads source code, so hand-writers won't even keep the bespoke luxury market displaced weavers had. One notable correction: a commenter points out the author mourns low software literacy right after abstracting the descriptive 「callback」/「errback」 terms into 「deferred」/「promise」 for aesthetics. The story was flagged by users and later unflagged by moderator dang.

**Worth reading**

## 20. Training Text-to-Image Models Without a VAE {#story-20}
*#26 on HN · [linum.ai](https://www.linum.ai/field-notes/pyramid-jit) · 29 points · 9 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49983582)*

Linum, a two-person generative video lab, presents Pyramid-JiT (P-JiT), a decoder-only pixel-space text-to-image architecture that drops the VAE entirely. It claims to hit Linum v2's FD-DINOv2 quality with 11.3× fewer training samples and 4.3× fewer GPU-hours at 4× the pixels, using 32x32 token compression versus the ~16x16 ceiling the authors attribute to latent diffusion autoencoders. Key techniques: three OutputHeads along the DiT trunk regressing images at 128², 256² and 512² (K-Readout), a widened linear patchification bottleneck (256→1024), and Gated Residuals adapted from Qwen 3.8-Next. Code and weights are released under Apache 2.0 as a research artifact, not a full model release.

**Discussion**: The top thread is a substantive counter-argument from joefourier, who suspects the VAE 'ceiling' reflects a suboptimal VAE rather than a fundamental limit, and argues a custom-retrained VAE with translation/rotation invariance plus low-res latent drafting could give the same benefits and reach 4096x4096 output for equivalent compute; they also flag that for video they would be very hesitant to abandon interframe compression. The author engages other technical suggestions directly rather than defending the paper. E-Reverance describes an alternative unpatching scheme — 1x1 'patches' with overlapping 3x3/5x5 local pixel-wise attention, essentially a dynamic convolution, and with hidden states at pixel positions rather than RGB — which they say beat both linear and conv unpatching, and the author says they may try it. A ComfyUI question gets a practical answer: no integration exists, but weights and inference code are downloadable and easy to wire up.

**Worth reading**
- [joefourier](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50025531): A skeptical practitioner argues the VAE compression ceiling may just mean a poorly trained VAE, and that latent-space drafting plus interframe compression would be better for video.
- [E-Reverance](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49987358): E-Reverance details a pixel-wise local-attention unpatching scheme (stride-1 3x3/5x5 windows over per-position hidden states) that offloads spatial decoding from the patch tokens.
- [schopra909](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49983668): The author lays out the lab's motivation: a 720p 5-second video costs 110K tokens versus LLM training contexts of 8K, so condensing the context window 4x is the path to affordable models.
