## 1. RepRapMicron {#story-1}
*#1 on HN · [github.com](https://github.com/VikOlliver/RepRapMicron/wiki) · 248 points · 37 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50141076)*

这是 Vik Olliver 的 µRepRap 项目 wiki，目标是造出一台 RepRap（可自我复制的 3D 打印机），用常见易得的材料实现微米（1µm）和亚微米级制造，思路是用 OpenFlexure 项目的 3D 打印显微镜平台做精密定位，本质是一台极精确的小型 CNC。页面称 PIKA V0.02 硬件可在约 8mm 立方体积内实现约 1µm 的重复定位精度和 10µm 或更好的可靠精度，用自制蚀刻探针沉积出小至 4µm 的 UV 树脂液滴，并用 125/9 光纤探针固化了 9µm 宽的树脂线；已用 4µm 层高打印出 0.5mm 的 Benchy，另有一个 1.3mm 的「Hello World」是直接用探针在玻璃片上的 Sharpie 墨迹上刻出来的。硬件用 400 步 NEMA17 步进电机（200 步也接近 1µm）、GRBL 固件、0.5mm 针头或 0.25mm 针灸针作探针，树脂用的是 UV 美甲胶，加一个 3W UV LED 作打印头。

**Discussion**: 讨论整体非常兴奋，但主要争议在「自我复制」和「小尺寸就等于更高精度」这两点。有评论者把这件事类比 1790 年代的精密机械浪潮（Ramsden 的分度、Maudslay 的螺纹车床、Wilkinson 的镗床），认为如今业余爱好者已能操纵单个原子；另一派则指出所有 RepRap 从来都不能完整自我复制，仍需要电机和控制器，而且把树脂打印缩小三个数量级不太可能，也许只能做成微型 CNC 或用来打印 MEMS 部件。有人用平方-立方定律和材料属性随尺度变化论证机械结构无法简单缩小，并强调「做得更小」并不等于「更精确」——真正的高精度机床通常反而更大。还有人给精度泼冷水：之所以能做到，是因为工作范围只有 8mm 立方，而廉价的蚀刻玻璃光栅编码器现在不到 50 美元就能给到 1µm 精度，直接用带内置编码器的直线电机更省事。支线讨论集中在家庭芯片制造：Sam Zeloof（AtomicSemi/fab2）、Jeri Ellsworth、Hacker Fab 以及那个「花园棚屋里的晶圆厂」——有人纠正说棚屋的主人本身是半导体公司创始人和 CTO，用家庭实验室跑业务，从车库一次性演示到可靠 mini-fab 的工作量差着好几个数量级。此外有人吐槽 USB 显微镜质量糟糕，回复澄清它们只用于回零和定位，观察成品要用 100x–200x 的台式显微镜。应用设想包括导电树脂打印 MEMS（加速度计、陀螺仪、微型扬声器）、磁芯存储器、黑胶唱片和 DIY 喷墨打印机。

**Worth reading**
- [generuso](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50142402): 对「缩小即提升精度」的正面反驳：精密机械史是靠旧机器造出更精确的新机器逐级爬升的，而超高精度机床通常比低精度机床更大。
- [DannyBee](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50142806): 指出这份精度来自仅 8mm 的立方工作体积，并提醒廉价的蚀刻玻璃光栅编码器如今不到 50 美元就能达到 1µm，带内置编码器的直线电机可能是更简单的路线。
- [generuso](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50143443): 用 Sam Zeloof 从 CMU 电气工程学生、业余做半导体的爱好者到运营资金充裕的 mini-fab 设备团队的经历，说明车库一次性演示与可靠 mini-fab 之间的工作量差着好几个数量级。

## 2. Someone behind the software {#story-2}
*#2 on HN · [karrisaarinen.com](https://karrisaarinen.com/posts/someone-behind-the-software/) · 100 points · 36 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50142573)*

A post by Karri Saarinen, co-founder and CEO of Linear, arguing that good software carries the tastes, experience and judgment of the people who make it — which he calls the product's 「soul」. He says Linear deliberately values intuition over process and data, and that intuition means a developed point of view rather than personal preference. His main complaint: many products, including ones from very successful companies, now feel like collections of added capabilities — 「Frankenstein's monster rather than a carefully shaped sculpture」 — and he suspects AI agents, each acting as a one-person software factory, dilute the shared perspective that once gave products character. There is no fix offered beyond hoping easier software making leaves more room to develop and follow a perspective.

**Discussion**: The thread largely agrees with the sentiment and reaches for older frameworks to sharpen it: one commenter quotes Dorothy Sayers' 「The Mind of the Maker」 via Fred Brooks' Mythical Man-Month, describing creation as idea, implementation and interaction, and another cites Peter Naur's 「Programming as Theory Building」, warning that when you delegate work to an AI the theory of the program is no longer yours. The sharpest disagreement is about whether AI is even the culprit: one commenter argues the additive-feature problem is the classic Innovator's Dilemma — removing things upsets users and is politically hard inside a company — and that AI factories just make it worse on the margins; another agrees AI only reinforces forces already at work, but thinks vibe coding could disrupt from the bottom up. Several people report concrete first-hand observations that taste still tracks the prompter, not the tool, with one claiming Claude produces more pleasant interfaces because it validates visually while Codex is 「rigidly literal」 and adds clutter like a 1990s ERP system. Two side debates: whether solo developers now have an advantage in shipping opinionated software (broadly yes, if they don't hand too many decisions to agents), and whether intuition scales past small teams, since there is no way to weigh 50 people's intuitions against each other. Enthusiasts drew the analogy outside software — handmade food, clothing, goods lost to factories since the Industrial Revolution — while one commenter pushed back on the nostalgic framing with an ironic 「artisanal, hand-crafted lines of code」 pastiche.

**Worth reading**
- [solatic](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50143655): Argues the post's core complaint is not about AI at all but the Innovator's Dilemma: removing or reworking features always angers some users, so good product leadership requires the courage to 「fuck with the user」 and accept churn.
- [jdw64](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50143545): Cites Peter Naur's 「Programming as Theory Building」 — a program dies when its team disbands or knowledge transfer fails — and asks whether that theory survives at all when you delegate the work to an AI.
- [kristianc](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50143926): Concrete tool comparison from use: Claude validates its work visually and has better interface instincts, while Codex is rigidly literal about the spec yet piles on explanatory text and visual clutter, and the product-manager translation instinct is what AI lacks.

## 3. The Intel 8087 chip: floating-point addition and subtraction {#story-3}
*#4 on HN · [righto.com](https://www.righto.com/2026/10/8087-addition-subtraction-microcode.html) · 50 points · 7 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50142449)*

Ken Shirriff's righto.com article tears down the Intel 8087, the 1980 x87 coprocessor that sped up floating-point math on the IBM PC by roughly 100x for addition. After decapping a chip with a chisel and microscope, he maps the die: a 1648-micro-instruction microcode ROM, an 80-bit 「temporary real」 internal format (sign, 15-bit exponent with bias 16383, 64-bit significand), a 69-bit integer adder, a barrel shifter, leading-zero and exponent-converter circuits, and eight tagged stack registers. He then walks the actual add/subtract microcode starting at address #0232 — exponent compare and argument swap, the CREATE_DENORM call that aligns significands, sign-latch handling, and the normalize/round fixes for overflow, zero and denormal results.

**Discussion**: The thread is small and almost uniformly appreciative, with the author himself replying to nearly every comment. A commenter flags a typo (1.1 binary × 2^8 is 384, not 304) and Shirriff accepts the correction, so there is no real disagreement anywhere. bonzini asks whether Shirriff has covered how 8087 instruction decoding is shared with the 8086, and Shirriff links his own February 2026 article on 8087 instruction decoding. WalterBright contributes the strongest first-hand anecdote: early Win32 did not save 8087 registers on context switches, and he phoned Microsoft to stop them destroying FP state, after which they relented. The remaining comments are nostalgia about saving up for a 486DX and praise for righto.com.

**Worth reading**
- [WalterBright](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50143956): WalterBright says early Win32 context switches discarded the 8087 register state until he called Microsoft to object, and that he still doesn't understand why numerics people ignore the 8087.
- [n923263](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50143070): Flags an arithmetic slip in the article — binary 1.1 × 2^8 is 384, not 304 — which the author acknowledges.
- [bonzini](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50143414): Asks how 8087 instruction decoding is shared with the 8086, prompting Shirriff to link his earlier decoding article.

## 4. Java sealed classes and exhaustive pattern matching {#story-4}
*#5 on HN · [neilmadden.blog](https://neilmadden.blog/2026/04/24/java-sealed-classes-and-exhaustive-pattern-matching/) · 82 points · 46 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50141579)*

Neil Madden's blog post explains that sealed interfaces (Java 17) plus switch pattern matching let the compiler check exhaustiveness, so no default branch is needed — but only within one compilation unit. If a library adds a new permitted subtype and a consumer is not recompiled, the old consumer throws a runtime MatchException instead of failing to build, so adding a case to an exported sealed type is effectively a SemVer-major breaking change. His conclusion: sealed types are excellent inside a component, risky in a public API — a mistake he says the preview API for PEM-encoded cryptographic objects makes by recommending clients pattern-match on a sealed interface.

**Discussion**: Almost nobody argues with the article's actual point; the thread turns into a Java-versus-Kotlin/Scala language war. Kotlin partisans call sealed classes and pattern matching catch-up, and are corrected on the timeline: Kotlin's `when` has been exhaustive since 1.0, while Java's pattern matching only arrived in Java 21 (2023), and sealed classes in Java 15 (2020), not 17. A long exchange pits a defender of Java's deliberate, minimal, late-mover design — who says the team has deliberately adopted no Kotlin feature, that ~90% of Java platform developers stay on the Java language, and that 95% of the JDK is runtime and tooling — against critics who list local type inference, switch expressions, records without `with`-ers/copy, the withdrawn string templates and a stripped-down smart casting as features arriving 5–10 years late. Others defend Java as consistent with few sharp edges, a claim challenged by commenters who cite generics, erasure and variance as exactly such edges and prefer C#'s design. Side threads cover whether Kotlin exists without the JVM (Kotlin/Native is a subset; JetBrains still ships JVM-based IDEs), Java's missing nullability types, and whether string interpolation still matters when LLMs write the code.

**Worth reading**
- [Sharlin](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50143419): Correction to the premise: sealed classes shipped in Java 15 back in 2020 — pattern matching is what arrived later and made them useful.
- [pron](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50142728): A Java-side insider argues the minimalism is intentional: no Kotlin feature has been copied wholesale, features are held back for years until their value justifies the complexity, and Java's ~90% share among platform developers has held for 15+ years.
- [dtech](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50143624): A pointed counter-list from a user: local type inference, switch expressions, records lacking copy()/with-ers, the abandoned string templates and reduced smart casting all landed 5–10+ years after Kotlin, which he calls 「weaksauce」.

## 5. Sigsaly: Churchill's top secret hotline to the President {#story-5}
*#6 on HN · [iwm.org.uk](https://www.iwm.org.uk/history/iwm/cwr/telephone) · 62 points · 6 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50096110)*

An Imperial War Museums history page on SIGSALY, the encrypted transatlantic voice link between London and Washington. Bell Telephone Laboratories began the project in October 1940 (then called “Project X”), digitising speech and mixing it with random noise from identical 12-minute records played in sync at both ends, then reconstructing the voice with a vocoder; each terminal cost $1 million in 1943 money and weighed over 50 tonnes.

**Discussion**: The thread is mostly appreciative, adding technical and historical colour to the article rather than disputing it. The top comment reproduces a 1983 IEEE review crediting SIGSALY with eight “firsts”, including the first enciphered telephony, first transmission of speech by PCM, first companded PCM and first useful speech bandwidth compression, and notes the relevant patents stayed classified until 1975. Others link an NSA publication with an illustration of the encryption process (framing it as a one-time pad) and point to Alan Turing’s own speech-scrambling system, Delilah, as related reading. One commenter recalls from a Turing biography that Turing was sent to the US to work on the project but contributed little because the Americans did not fully trust him, and a minor pedantic subthread argues the headline should say “the President of the United States”.

**Worth reading**
- [Merrill](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50143612): Quotes a 1983 IEEE review listing eight technical “firsts” for SIGSALY (enciphered telephony, PCM speech, companded PCM, speech bandwidth compression and more) and notes the patents were classified until 1975.
- [prathje](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50143222): Links the NSA’s own SIGSALY publication, which includes a diagram of the encryption process, and describes the scheme as a one-time pad.

## 8. Legal experts warn Meta's new terms still grant broad scraping permissions {#story-8}
*#9 on HN · [niemanlab.org](https://www.niemanlab.org/2026/10/legal-experts-warn-metas-new-terms-still-grant-broad-scraping-permissions-despite-language-changes/) · 196 points · 50 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50139692)*

A Nieman Lab report by Andrew Deck and Hanaa' Tameez says Meta quietly revised its upcoming commercial terms after the outlet's earlier story exposed broad scraping language. Meta removed the word 「train」 from the allowed use cases and narrowed them to 「advertising and business products」, but the terms still authorize automated crawling of any domains 「associated with your products or services」 and let Meta 「create test credentials」. Attorneys Matt Topic (Loevy & Loevy), James Grimmelmann (Cornell) and Benjamin Sobel (Wisconsin) all call the fix inadequate — Grimmelmann says the language 「allows Meta to drive a truck through」 and Sobel notes robots.txt is a voluntary preference, not an enforceable right. The terms take effect October 30 with no opt-out, drawing condemnation from the European Publishers Council, the UK News Media Association (CEO Theo Bamber) and the News/Media Alliance, whose Danielle Coffey calls it 「outrageous and probably illegal」.

**Discussion**: The thread's dominant consensus is that robots.txt is not a control but 「asking nicely」, and several commenters note that anyone who has written scrapers professionally for years has never once been asked whether a target's robots.txt permits it. A large subthread debates whether feeding scrapers poisoned or fabricated content is a better defense; the idea is popular, but one commenter argues real data correlates while fake data is unique and therefore probably fails to poison a model. Practically, one commenter says UA-based blocking is now pointless because Meta's agent rotates addresses and partly comes from non-Meta ASNs, and reports that matching on ASN plus TLS JA4 fingerprinting actually held. A dissenting pair of commenters ask why people are alarmed at all, since Meta already crawls their sites despite robots.txt, and the most-echoed advice is simply to remove your business from Meta's platforms entirely.

**Worth reading**
- [xmcp123](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50141514): Someone who has written bots and scrapers for nearly 20 years says no employer has ever once asked about robots.txt.
- [Heronsunny](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50140676): First-hand report that UA blocking no longer works against meta-externalagent because it rotates and partly runs off non-Meta ASNs, while ASN plus TLS JA4 matching did stick.
- [post-it](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50141779): A counter-argument to the popular 「poison the scraper」 idea: real data correlates, fake data is all unique, so poisoning AI training data may be a waste of time.

**Discussion**: 讨论很快分成两条线。第一条是同类项目辨析：有人指出这与 Terra 不是一回事，更接近「给 Lua 加类型的 TypeScript」；有人问与 Luau 的比较，回答是 Luau 是另一门语言和运行时，而 Teal、Fennel、MoonScript 属于 Lua 语法三大流派；还有人纠正 Fennel 由其创建者 Calvin Rose 所写、现在的维护者是 technomancy。第二条线几乎占了一半篇幅，是「LLM 时代还需要新语言吗」的争论：有人质疑这类实验除艺术价值外还有什么用，反驳者则说 Teal 早于 LLM 且静态类型检查器对 AI 生成的代码恰恰有帮助，因为编译期错误是廉价而明确的反馈。此外还有老生常谈的 1-based 索引之争，以及 Lua 是否适合独立写大型程序的分歧——有人称自己用 LuaJIT 交付并维护多年、反驳「Lua 写非平凡程序是徒劳」的说法。

**Worth reading**
- [ufo](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50140999): 给出一个大规模生产用例：Lua 包管理器 Luarocks 的 src/luarocks 目录本身就是用 Teal 写的。
- [saghm](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50139788): 第一手经验：作者为给一个 mod 加功能而把代码移植到 Teal，并观察到 LLM 写的 Lua 质量很差（滥用 pcall 当控制流），而 Teal 能静态约束类型。
- [veqq](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50142918): 纠正 Fennel 的归属：Calvin Rose 创建了 Fennel 和 Janet，technomancy 只是现在的维护者。

## 18. The Brain Sandwich {#story-18}
*#26 on HN · [terriblesoftware.org](https://terriblesoftware.org/2026/10/02/the-brain-sandwich/) · 48 points · 21 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50139998)*

A short post on the Terrible Software blog by the author of a conversation with Emily, one of his strongest engineers, laying out her 「Brain Sandwich」 framework for using AI without letting engineering skill atrophy: read and understand the area yourself first, then delegate the building to an agent with no shame, then come back with your own brain to read the diff, judge whether the complexity is warranted and write a reviewable PR description. The stated rule of thumb is that if you cannot explain what is changing and why, the work is not ready for anyone else to look at. It is framed as a reaction to engineers handing over their entire job to AI — 「Meat Proxy」, as the author says the cool kids call it — and to the sheer volume of code reviews.

**Discussion**: The top-voted comment is dismissive: coldtea argues AI's real utility is shipping without understanding the work, so this advice will change as much as 「trade, but be ethical」 did on a late-80s trading floor — maybe 1 in 100 will follow it. Others are more receptive: one commenter calls personal responsibility for what goes into and out of the LLM obvious but critical and plans to use the term with less technical colleagues, another pedantically notes it is an AI sandwich, not a brain sandwich, and fwipsy draws the boundary that kernels need every edge case considered while web games do not. The most substantive pushback comes from jonathanlydall, who rejects reading everything before prompting: he keeps several AI conversations running, has the agent draft a plan while he reviews other work, then interrogates the AI until the system-design implications are clear, calling that judgment the difference between vibe coding and accelerated agentic development. A sub-thread between StilesCrisis and 0c3ca83 argues over whether AI can beat your own design and over AI's code quality, with 0c3ca83 claiming AI review has a 50-60% false-positive rate and that its code is usually an overcomplicated mess trimmable to a tenth of the size. The thread also questions the workplace economics: jmarlow47 says the discipline may not pay off where management only measures delivery, while apical_dendrite reports execs souring on AI-generated work after customer-facing mistakes and fatigue with 「Claude-style」 writing.

**Worth reading**
- [coldtea](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50143555): The top comment rejects the premise outright: AI's actual utility is shipping without understanding, so an advice post like this will be ignored by all but about 1 in 100.
- [jonathanlydall](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50141590): A senior developer argues against reading everything up front, describing his workflow of running multiple agent conversations in parallel and scrutinizing the AI's plan until the system design is sound.
- [0c3ca83](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50143050): Concrete numbers from a sceptic: AI review has a 50-60% false-positive rate, AI code is 「pretty shitty」 but its persistence broadens scope usefully, and its output can usually be cut to a tenth the size while keeping the idea.

## 19. Hatching (Heraldry) {#story-19}
*#27 on HN · [en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatching_(heraldry)) · 61 points · 18 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50093081)*

It's the Wikipedia article on hatching, the system in heraldry where tinctures (colours) are represented by dots and lines so arms can be shown in monochrome on woodcuts, engravings, seals and coins. It traces the present-day system to the 1630s and to Silvester Petra Sancta and Marcus Vulson de la Colombière, while arguing the primacy actually belongs to the Leuven engraver Jan Baptist Zangrius, whose 1600 system they only altered in the hatching for Sable. The article also credits the Low Countries' copperplate engravers — Franquart (1623), Butkens (1626), de Rouck (1645) — as co-developers, since hatchings required finer line spacing than woodcuts could produce.

**Discussion**: Almost nobody argues about the article itself; the thread is a riff session on monochrome ways of encoding colour. The top comment ties hatching to the notorious Android 4.4 hairy-heart emoji, allegedly a black-and-white hatched heart whose dot patterns were misread as yellow, which others say is how the story reached a wide audience. Several commenters trace the same idea through graphic design and games: one points to MacPaint/Susan Kare textures and to Henry Dreyfuss's Symbol Sourcebook (1972) for architectural material patterns, another notes hatching in rifle-stock checkering and in technical-drawing conventions for representing metals. A subthread asks why heraldry belongs on HN at all, and the answers converge on hackers' disproportionate interest in dithering, halftones, fonts and general visual detail — with one commenter confirming the audience's fondness for aesthetics by joking about Tektronix accepting a patch to PLOT 10. A separate tangent criticises Google Maps for devoting a third of the screen to a button that recolours the vehicle arrow, citing a former employee's essay about Google's 「Don't complain, fix things」 culture breeding learned indifference to problems.

**Worth reading**
- [NamTaf](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50140982): Points out the real-world payoff: the Android 4.4 hairy-heart emoji is said to come from misinterpreting a hatched heart whose dots denoted yellow.
- [goodmythical](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50141855): Argues early graphic designers faced the same problem as Renaissance engravers, and locates MacPaint's likely reference in Henry Dreyfuss's Symbol Sourcebook (1972) with its architectural fill patterns.
- [bovermyer](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50141503): A commenter who writes about heraldry says they're glad to see the topic but are surprised it interests the HN crowd, prompting a thread on why hackers care about visual-codings.

## 20. Regex visualized as 3D marble run {#story-20}
*#28 on HN · [regexmarblerun.com](https://www.regexmarblerun.com/) · 76 points · 27 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50122671)*

这是用户 seizethecheese 提交的一个网站（regexmarblerun.com），链接页面几乎没有可抓取的文本，很可能是需要 JavaScript 渲染的交互式应用，因此这里无法确认其具体实现。按作者在评论中的说明，这是他的同事为教非技术人员（以及觉得正则很难的技术人员）理解任意正则表达式而做的可视化工具：把正则的匹配过程表现成一条 3D 弹珠轨道。作者同时披露该工具是用他公司正在开发的本地编码 agent 竞技系统 Agent Duel（github.com/bottomless/agent-duel）构建的，属于带推广性质的展示。

**Discussion**: 讨论基本没有围绕这个 3D 演示本身，而是迅速演变成一场正则表达式到底难在哪里的辩论。一派（ogogmad、atoav）认为正则的核心其实极小——量词 . | ? * +、捕获组 () 和懒惰变体 ?? *? +?，其余都是语法糖，难的是教学材料一上来就展示全部复杂度；另一派（StilesCrisis、nick__m、crazygringo）反驳说这远不是全部，还有反向引用、前后向断言、递归、内联标志等，PCRE2 兼容的正则可能极其复杂。围绕反向引用出现了最有技术含量的一轮交锋：有人称其为有争议的特性，随后被纠正为它并非「有争议」，而是超出了正则语言的表达力（对应有限自动机无法表达），支持它会让最坏匹配时间从 O(n) 升到指数级、问题变成 NP 完全，因此多数引擎用回慢速算法。另一个分支是 crazygringo 对正则语法的控诉（全是标点、没有注释和换行、转义地狱），多人指出大多数引擎的扩展/verbose 模式（如 x 标志）本就允许空白和注释，还有人推荐 pomsky-lang.org 和 Raku grammar 作为可读替代方案，也有人主张直接为复杂正则写单元测试。此外有零星实测反馈：输入框似乎限制在 301 个字符，有人表示希望加上音效和跟随镜头。

**Worth reading**
