## 1. DiscImageStudio: burn visible artwork onto CDs, DVDs and Blu-ray discs {#story-1}
*#3 on HN · [github.com](https://github.com/JiaFeiMiao-K-Cat/DiscImageStudio) · 158 points · 35 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50346849)*

DiscImageStudio 是一个以 GPL-3.0-or-later 授权的开源项目（.NET 10），把一张普通图片转换成符合 CD、DVD 和单层 BD-R 25 GB 物理层约束的数据，刻录后光盘数据面会呈现肉眼可见的图案。Windows 端提供 WPF 图形界面与一键刻录（基于系统自带 IMAPI2，仅接受空白盘），Linux 和 macOS 只有实验性 CLI，可离线导出 ISO / RAW / WAV+CUE；实现参照 ECMA-394、ECMA-267、ECMA-359 与 ISO/IEC 30190，CD 交织部分移植自 arduinocelentano 的 CDImage。项目还附了一套盘片标定流程，声称把可确定的盘面物理参数精度从 10^-6 提升到 10^-14，并用三次轨距拟合修正十字线弯曲。作者明确警告即时刻录为实验功能，只应用可报废的盘片。

**Discussion**: 讨论很快变成了光盘盘面打印技术的怀旧串：LightScribe、Yamaha 的 DiscT@2、2007 年的 Labelflash 以及 Neato 贴纸标签都被翻出来。对 LightScribe 的实际效果存在分歧——有人回忆每次要 20–30 分钟、成品在正常光线下偏淡、还不如记号笔；也有人反驳说慢归慢，纯黑白文字和线稿只要调好对比度就很清楚。一位做过 BSidesSF CTF 题目的人分享了第一手经验：他让人从 CDDA 原始 PCM 反推刻录后的盘面图像，花了数周才搞定，因为测定 pre-groove 几何极其敏感，他始终没能做出足够可靠的标定流程，因此对有人做成这件事表示佩服。关于图案与数据能否共存，有人指出同一区域不能兼作两者，但盘内圈放数据、外圈闲置区放图案是可行的。另有一支支线在争论项目里随处可见的动漫少女立绘是否令人反感，以及现在还有没有厂商在生产光驱。

**Worth reading**
- [bmenrigh](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50349134): 作者做过同类 CTF 题：从原始 PCM 模拟刻录盘面，花了数周，卡在盘片 pre-groove 几何标定的敏感度上，最终没能发布可复用的标定方案。
- [sourweasel](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50347437): 纠正「图与数据能否兼得」的疑问：同一区域不能既存数据又成像，但内圈数据加外圈闲置区画图是可行的。
- [sam1714](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50347374): 对 LightScribe 的反面一手评价：每遍 20–30 分钟、盘片更贵，烧两遍后在实际光线下仍偏淡，家用还不如记号笔。

## 3. European RISC-V Industrial Computing Initiative {#story-3}
*#5 on HN · [erici.eu](https://erici.eu) · 61 points · 24 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50334523)*

ERICI is a newly founded, non-commercial European initiative (initiated by Yuri Zaporozhets in Ravensburg) proposing a reference software stack for RISC-V industrial computers, arguing that RVA23 silicon — the profile ratified in October 2024 that makes hypervisor and vector extensions mandatory — now exists but the industrial machine built around it does not. It specifies layers: an HFI BIOS (GPL-2.0, based on U-Boot), the mr-bml bootloader (GPL-3.0), the gRouvr partitioning hypervisor and QSOE (Apache 2.0), with real-time and Linux partitions isolated via IOMMU on a Skimmer microkernel or seL4. The site lists two existing RVA23 boards as examples — T-Chip's edge mini PC and Milk-V's board, both using SpacemiT K3 with 8× X100 cores up to 2.4 GHz, up to 32 GB LPDDR5 — and says the partitioned stack has been demonstrated on a K3 PicoITX board but not yet on those products.

**Discussion**: The thread is mostly skeptical about the framing rather than the technology. Several commenters ask what problem this actually solves, noting industrial machines already run for decades; the answer offered is that modern software churn plus the risk of political sanctions on ISA licensing makes vendor-neutral openness newly valuable. Others point out the awkward contradiction that the only RVA23 hardware cited is Chinese (SpacemiT K3, in T-Chip and Milk-V boards), making a European initiative read as promotion of Chinese appliances — a commenter notes the reference stack is verified on exactly that silicon. The most concrete counter-argument is the European Processor Initiative as precedent for an EU chip effort that amounted to little, though a reply cites EPI's SiPearl Rhea1 (80 Neoverse V1 cores, selected for the Jupiter exascale machine) as real output. A separate thread mocks the site's AI-generated copy and layout, with some defending it as harmless for a technical landing page and others finding the repeated 「the silicon is here, the industrial computer isn't」 construction tiresome; one early comment's reference to 「CPEU」 was never explained.

**Worth reading**
- [ShadowOfThePit](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50348987): Asks the central question plainly: industrial machines already run 30+ years, so what is this initiative actually fixing beyond generic FOSS advocacy?
- [hollowonepl](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50348997): Argues it is ironic for a European initiative to promote Chinese computer appliances, given that Chinese vendors are the only ones making this silicon today.
- [0l](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50348784): Cites the European Processor Initiative as a precedent for an EU chip initiative that went nowhere, linking its Wikipedia page.

## 4. Resisting Cognitive Surrender {#story-4}
*#8 on HN · [knlb.dev](https://knlb.dev/essays/surrender) · 127 points · 43 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50323123)*

A working-notes essay by knlb (October 2026) arguing against 「cognitive surrender」 — accepting agent output without thinking — which he calls fast, low-effort and seductive but prone to suboptimal decisions and quiet technical debt. He describes Gell-Mann amnesia with agents (in awe where he lacks expertise, then watching the agent fail to create a directory), complexity explosions in projects that shipped hundreds of thousands of lines, and the erosion of mechanical sympathy, citing The Ironies of Automation (1983). His remedies: a personal 「vibe limit」 for change complexity, conscious triage of what to delegate, a four-column PR view (diff, agent commentary, his own notes), stacked 「make the change easy, then make the easy change」 diffs, an AGENTS.md rule forbidding direct edits, debugger sessions, and shell harnesses that explain obscure flags.

**Discussion**: Several commenters largely agree but push further: one proposes a hard architectural boundary between agent-written and human-written code, like Shopify's platform APIs that let third-party code be a big ball of mud without corrupting the core, and others describe review collapsing into rubber-stamped LGTMs, verbose machine-written codebases, and contributors quitting rather than work alongside 「mindless robots」. The sharpest exchange is about review drift — that no single PR is ever rejected for making code 1% less understandable, so the loss compounds, with one commenter correcting the arithmetic to 0.99^100, a 63.4% decline. Against this, a self-described FAANG-ish commenter argues quality simply doesn't matter in the market: production issues up ~40% year over year while engagement and user numbers keep growing, a claim another commenter rejects as both bad engineering and commercially self-defeating. A recurring counter-argument is that the essay is copium that will evaporate as models improve — one predicts the author will 「just send it」 within six months and lists Karpathy and Primeagen as following the same trajectory, though a reply insists Primeagen actually walked back toward a middle ground. Others report the pressure is managerial rather than enthusiastic, note real improvement in model reliability since August, and suggest vibe-coded prototypes that get thrown away are a legitimate use.

**Worth reading**
- [cowlevel](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50347920): Argues the real damage is bias, not error: every merge that degrades understandability by 1% still gets merged, and after 100 such merges the codebase is incomprehensible even though no reviewer made a mistake.
- [nurettin](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50348404): First-hand account of the model-improvement curve: statistical errors persisted into January, were gone after August, and instruction-following is now the main remaining problem.
- [willtemperley](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50349210): A GIS practitioner describes foundational, intricate libraries being copied and relicensed by vibe-coders, with the key maintainer now on indefinite strike — a concrete cost of the quality-indifference argument.

## 5. Toit v2.0.0 {#story-5}
*#9 on HN · [toit.io](https://toit.io/blog/toit-v2-0-0/) · 13 points · 5 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50321605)*

The Toit blog announces v2.0.0 of Toit, a language for writing scripts on a computer and applications on microcontrollers such as the ESP32, after more than four years of alpha, 2,000+ commits, 500,000+ lines changed and 200+ alpha releases. Toit started closed-source at Toitware, was open-sourced, and has been carried on by volunteers since Toitware shut down in 2024. The release was unblocked by the last planned breaking change — peripheral APIs now accept pin numbers directly instead of requiring gpio.Pin objects — while a formatter, compiler resolution improvements and more architecture support remain as open PRs for later releases; other additions include Arm EC618 and most ESP32 variants, a unified toit command, a rewritten package manager, mixins and firmware envelopes.

**Discussion**: The thread is tiny, so there is no real debate — mostly clarification. One commenter quotes the project's own one-liner (a readable syntax, an efficient runtime, good tooling, free and open source, volunteer-maintained), which prompted another to say that even after reading that blurb and more of the homepage he still had no idea what Toit actually is — a complaint about positioning rather than the release itself. The most substantive reply supplies credibility instead of explanation: the people behind Toit are the original V8 VM team from Chrome, so they know their stuff. Nobody in the thread reports running v2.0.0 or pushes back on the release.

**Worth reading**
- [skrebbel](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50349258): Says the project's own description and homepage leave him unable to tell what Toit is — an implicit critique of how the project explains itself.
- [jacobgorm](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50349259): Notes that Toit's creators were the original V8 VM team at Google, vouching for the technical quality behind the language and runtime.

## 6. Scaling Git at OpenAI {#story-6}
*#12 on HN · [openai.com](https://openai.com/index/scaling-git-at-openai/) · 192 points · 108 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50345924)*

OpenAI's engineering post introduces Pando, a new Git backend for its monorepo that keeps the standard Git protocol and client but replaces the storage layer. Pando gives up repository-wide point-in-time consistency (using causal consistency, with only per-ref serializability) and stops generating a unique packfile per client, instead reusing immutable packfiles recorded as ref versions in blob and metadata stores. OpenAI says this cut median push latency by nearly 5x and now serves close to a petabyte of Git data per day, after reliability had at times dropped below 90%.

**Discussion**: A large camp treats the post as evidence of agent-driven slop rather than engineering progress: commenters ask what work could justify petabyte-scale Git traffic and argue that agents produce bloated code, endless fix commits and redundant documentation. Others push back that cheap code expands the set of worthwhile projects, that agents benefit from version history as much as humans do, and that Git being unusable for Google- and Meta-scale monorepos predates the AI era entirely. Several note this is roughly the fifth to seventh such post in a year (Cursor, GitHub twice, Anthropic, GitLab, Entire), and wonder how much the designs converge on ideas Google and Meta have used for a decade. Concrete criticism targets the post itself: one commenter building a code forge says there are no throughput or concurrency benchmarks, and another asks how streaming packfiles to blob storage without unpacking them can be safe against misbehaving clients and agents. A recurring side thread praises the write-up for being detailed and free of marketing hype, and skeptics and defenders split over whether LLM-written code volume is an asset or a liability for future maintenance.

**Worth reading**
- [pksunkara](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50347773): Points out the post has no benchmarks — no pushes per second or concurrent pusher counts — from the perspective of someone building a competing code forge.
- [cadamsdotcom](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50349090): Asks how Pando can validate a pushed packfile while streaming it to blob storage without unpacking, and whether that amounts to trusting the client, especially with agent-driven pushes.
- [skydhash](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50348449): Argues Git is sufficient for the Linux kernel, FreeBSD, QEMU and Firefox; it broke at Google and Meta because of org-wide monorepos, and now a different assumption — that you want version control rather than a rolling snapshot log — is breaking.

## 7. Self-Employed? Solopreneur? NIST Has a Cybersecurity Guide Just for You {#story-7}
*#14 on HN · [nist.gov](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2026/11/self-employed-solopreneur-nist-has-cybersecurity-guide-just-you) · 199 points · 54 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50340619)*

NIST has published Cybersecurity White Paper 50 (CSWP 50), Small Business Cybersecurity: Non-Employer Firms, a lay-language rewrite of NISTIR 7621 aimed at the more than 28 million U.S. businesses with no paid employees. It maps selected actions from CSF 2.0 — backups, patching, changing default router passwords — to a rationale, first steps, and later-stage practices, plus worksheets and three hypothetical use cases involving a lawyer, an artisan and a consultant. NIST says no prior cybersecurity knowledge is assumed.

**Discussion**: The thread is split between readers who say the paper is still not written for novices — starting with the title 「Non-Employer Firms」, which no small business owner uses — and defenders who say the PDF defines phishing and MFA, links to deeper CISA resources, and avoids talking down to readers. Critics also complain the guide is buried behind the news page and an academic-looking landing page, requiring extra clicks busy owners will not make; one defender counters that the download is two clicks away. A separate and much louder argument erupts over the document's use of 「SI」, which several commenters read as 「Super Intelligence」: some call it a political shibboleth or federal mandate that undermines the paper's credibility, while others defend NIST as one of the few trustworthy technical publishers. Practitioners add concrete advice — inventory what is valuable, delete data you do not need, keep secrets out of email, use Signal — and one commenter argues solo operators inevitably need a trusted IT consultant, lawyer and accountant rather than a document. The rough consensus, if any, is that NIST's material is solid and worth reading, but that this package does not reliably reach the non-technical audience it claims to serve.

**Worth reading**
- [nomilk](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50348580): Argues the title and framing defeat the lay-language goal: small businesses do not call themselves 「Non-Employer Firms」 and most do not know what NIST or a white paper is.
- [philosopherNoob](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50344808): Objects to the document's use of 「SI」 as misplaced jargon that puts a fancy algorithm on a pedestal instead of grounding it for ordinary readers.
- [mmooss](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50346248): Offers detailed practical advice for non-technical owners: inventory valuables and threats, avoid storing what you do not need, keep passwords and client data out of email, and use Signal.

## 9. Shrinking NixOS RAM footprint and resource requirements to the bare minimum {#story-9}

syscall.sh 是一个按架构整理的 Linux 系统调用参考网站兼 API，作者 guitmz 在 2020 年首次发布，这次大幅更新。网站现在覆盖 27 种架构，带有内核版本选择器（Linux 3.3 到 7.3），列出系统调用号、参数、寄存器和进入内核的指令，并标注每条系统调用首次出现和何时被移除；每种架构的数据还以纯 JSON 形式提供，例如 /x64/syscalls.json，另有 API 位于 api.syscall.sh/v2。所有数据都由生成器直接扫描内核源码中的系统调用表和 SYSCALL_DEFINE 宏得出，简短描述取自 man-pages 项目和 liburing，前端每周通过 cronjob 从 API 重建。

**Discussion**: 评论区整体很正面，有人正在学 aarch64 汇编，称其“很方便”，也有人反馈网站在自己机器上会打乱显示效果。最实质的纠正是：x86_64/x32 上系统调用号放在 eax 而非 rax，高位被忽略；作者承认这个错误并说自 5.14 起内核只读 eax，会补上说明，而纠正者又贴出 lore.kernel.org 的补丁链接指出内核在此问题上反复过几次。不少人对“为什么同一套 Linux API 的系统调用号各架构不同”感兴趣，作者和他人解释系统调用 ABI 是架构相关的、号码是 90 年代各移植维护者各自分配的、如今已成冻结 ABI，riscv 等新架构干脆不实现 open/fork，libc 会转调 openat/clone。还有一段争论围绕“约 400 个系统调用是否真的算 400 个不同调用”：一方认为 read/pread、pipe/pipe2 等只是同一功能的变体，另一方坚持系统调用号是客观且唯一有意义的计数，并用 ioctl 子命令上千、io_uring 把许多调用合并成一个入口、以及 Windows ntos 约 490 个系统调用来反驳；adrian_b 估计 x86-64 上有 450 多个，其中约三分之一已过时、另三分之一极少使用。有人建议给已废弃的调用加提示横幅（如用 pipe2 替代 pipe），作者则提到可以增加“相似系统调用”区块，并透露打算整理公开自己写的 CLI 工具。

**Worth reading**
- [bkallus](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50335608): 指出 x86_64 和 x32 的系统调用号实际放在 eax 而不是 rax，rax 高 32 位会被忽略，作者确认并更新文档。
- [ozgrakkurt](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50337485): 实用建议：用 echo '#include <sys/syscall.h>' | gcc -E -dM -xc - 在本机核对真实系统调用号，因为 LLM 给出过错误常量；同时提到 Chromium 的旧系统调用表。
- [guitmz](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50336336): 解释各架构系统调用号不同的历史原因：90 年代各移植开发者自行按添加顺序编号，随后冻结成不可更改的 ABI，且 32/64 位类型差异本就要求不同变体。

## 19. Too Expensive to Exist {#story-19}
*#40 on HN · [mapwriting.substack.com](https://mapwriting.substack.com/p/too-expensive-to-exist) · 37 points · 5 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50347784)*

A personal essay on the Substack Mapwriting about a $12-a-night private campground in Texas, an hour and a half from the author's home, that is ending individual camping and day-use trail access in October 2026 because the owner can no longer afford rising liability insurance. From there it widens into an angry sweep: state park campsites booked out past Christmas, over 95% of Texas land in private hands, a 25-acre wood near his childhood home fenced and clear-cut by an owner afraid of wildfire liability, pushes to transfer federal land to states, the 211-mile Ambler Road mining corridor in Alaska, and the argument that growth, Jevons paradox and cost disease make every such loss permanent.

**Discussion**: The thread is small and mostly sympathetic. One commenter sorts the complaints into three buckets — insurance-industry rent-seeking, ennui, and symptoms of rising population density — and argues supply and demand apply to space as much as anything, though he insists he is not relitigating Malthusianism. Another immediately rejects that framing, defining Malthusianism as the claim that some people must be poor because reproduction outruns productivity and that gains get absorbed by population, then counters that food production has outpaced population and birth rates are falling, recommending Henry George's Progress and Poverty as the better account, where land scarcity rather than reproductive instinct creates poverty. A third observation is that the essay's repeated word is 「impotent」: the sentiment is widespread among ordinary people but absent from leaders, whose networks are correlated with the wealthy and insulated — they are the last to hear about campground insurance rates. The most substantive reply comes from a Texas native who says the state has no interest in funding community resources; he cites Fairfield Lake State Park, where the state could have bought land it already leased cheaply from a power company that closed its plant, complete with staff and infrastructure in place, but let it fall into private hands. He also corrects a common assumption that the Rockies' and Sierras' public land is geographically inevitable, noting it came from deliberate local and municipal campaigns. One commenter found the piece more stream-of-consciousness than expected from the title but agreed with it anyway.

**Worth reading**
- [hingler36](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50348796): A Texan recounts how the state declined to buy Fairfield Lake State Park from the power company that leased it the land, despite having staff and infrastructure ready, and argues public land in the Rockies and Sierras was won by concerted local campaigns, not geography.
- [skew-aberration](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50348797): Pushes back on the Malthusian framing, arguing productivity growth has outpaced population and fertility is falling, and points to Henry George's Progress and Poverty for a land-scarcity explanation of poverty.
- [rafterydj](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50348005): Notes that the essay's recurring word is 「impotent」 and that leaders are structurally the last to hear about costs like campground insurance because their networks correlate with wealth.

## 20. Ask HN: Does anyone have a contact with Cleveland-area Ham club? {#story-20}
*#41 on HN · text post · 131 points · 21 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50346164)*

这是一条 HN 求助帖，不是链接文章。发帖人 stockresearcher 说，自己家里有一大批无线电设备需要搬走，否则就会被扔进垃圾箱，其中很多还是全新的；他填写了 Cuyahoga Amateur Radio Club 网站的表单，但怀疑没有人会看，于是来 HN 找人牵线。发帖人后来更新说，通过这个帖子已经联系上俱乐部的人，虽然不能保证结果，但「避免进垃圾填埋场的最好机会已经出现了」。

**Discussion**: 回应的重点几乎全是帮忙：有当地火腿（Radio2034）建议同时联系 Case Western 的 W8EDU 俱乐部，并留下邮箱；也有人表示会把帖子转给认识的无线电爱好者。Archive.org 的 jonah-archive 借机提醒，他们运营的 Digital Library of Amateur Radio Communications 已经收录超过 25,000 件资料，欢迎捐出纸质文献和 ephemera，但不收硬件。讨论还延伸到「这种帖子为什么能上首页」：有人认为 HN 本来就偏爱火腿相关的极客历史，而且发帖时间对美国用户有利；也有人调侃火腿像「精灵森林里的德鲁伊」，稀有、有文化共鸣，还容易拿到免费赞同。另有枝节讨论如何追查一位祖父在 1940–80 年代的呼号历史，建议查学校图书馆和县图书馆的旧缩微胶片。

**Worth reading**
- [Radio2034](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50346619): 当地火腿 Radio2034 建议联系 Case Western 的 W8EDU 俱乐部，并表示愿意直接帮忙牵线。
- [jonah-archive](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50347950): Internet Archive 的 jonah-archive 介绍 Digital Library of Amateur Radio Communications，称其已收录逾 25,000 件资料，可接收纸质无线电文献。
- [Radio2034](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50348043): 给追查祖父呼号的人提供具体方法：查学校俱乐部档案、校图书馆和县图书馆数字化的旧缩微胶片。
