## 1. “Math 2.0” will need to value mathematical progress more holistically {#story-1}
*#1 on HN · [mathstodon.xyz](https://mathstodon.xyz/@tao/117395269325940185) · 175 points · 102 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50002008)*

A Mathstodon post by Terence Tao arguing that now that AI systems are autonomously solving open problems — an effort commenters link to OpenAI’s October 6 release of mathematical documents — the field must redefine progress more holistically, rewarding understanding, exposition and integration of results rather than flags planted on solved problems. The linked page could not be fetched (needs JavaScript/login), so the summary rests on the title and on passages commenters quote from it, notably Tao’s warning that merely knowing a solution exists “contaminates” efforts by humans and AI to find alternate routes that reveal additional insight. Tao’s core complaint, as quoted, is that AI prompters solve problems without interest in the broader field and cannot answer questions, give talks, or otherwise engage with it.

*Article not read: page had almost no readable text (probably needs JavaScript or a login)*

**Discussion**: Broad agreement that Tao’s take is balanced, but sharp disagreement over whether it underestimates future models: skeptics (XorNot, vasco, bluepeter) argue there is no evidence that distilling insight or writing exposition stays beyond AI, since models could not do any of this a few years ago, while others (rtpg, JumpCrisscross) counter that without an author to answer questions and supply the hidden insight, we end up with intellectually unsatisfying proofs nobody builds on or simplifies — “no shoulders to stand on.” Practitioners add concrete institutional fallout: commenters describe a doctoral-training crisis since standard thesis problems are now solvable by Claude Pro, PIs redirecting grant money to AI compute, and wealthier universities and countries gaining an edge from resource-intensive AI. The thread also diverges into funding politics, with one camp calling publicly funded pure math a laundered “paid hobby” and fraud and defenders citing number theory, imaginary numbers, group theory and Boolean algebra as useless-seeming work that paid off centuries later. Recurring reference points include Thurston’s 1994 essay as the same argument restated, a Ramanujan analogy that opponents say fails because Ramanujan held the insight himself, and speculation about humans retreating to “Math 1.0” hobby work while machines run the real frontier.

**Worth reading**
- [sanxiyn](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50002418): Notes that Tao’s argument is essentially William Thurston’s 1994 essay “On proof and progress in mathematics” restated, and links arXiv math/9404236.
- [contubernio](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50002269): A math-adjacent researcher details the concrete damage: conventional PhD thesis problems are now easily solved by Claude Pro, AI compute puts a premium on resources favoring rich universities, and AI already writes better undergraduate lecture notes and research exposition than most mathematicians.

## 4. Margaret Hamilton has died {#story-4}
*#6 on HN · [news.mit.edu](https://news.mit.edu/2026/margaret-hamilton-computing-pioneer-dies-1007) · 1,256 points · 138 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49998895)*

MIT News obituary for Margaret Hamilton, who died Sep. 30 at 90. She joined MIT's Instrumentation Lab in 1965 as the first programmer on the Apollo project and rose to lead the team of 400+ writing the onboard flight software for the Command and Lunar Modules, popularizing the term 「software engineering」 and receiving the 2016 Presidential Medal of Freedom. The piece credits her defensive, priority-driven software with saving the Apollo 11 landing when the guidance computer threw 1202 alarms from overload, and notes her later companies Higher Order Software (1976) and Hamilton Technologies (USL).

**Discussion**: The thread is dominated by tributes — the 1969 photo beside stacks of listings, her Lego minifigure, calls for a black bar — plus links to Apollo code on GitHub and the Computer History Museum oral history. A minority pushed back hard on the hero narrative: citing Don Eyles' book, they argue Hamilton only got a leadership role in the LM software in early 1970, after Apollo 11, and that Hal Laning originated asynchronous software and priority scheduling, for which she was honored; others accused those commenters (some flagged or killed) of misogynist downplaying, and the exchange devolved into an argument about whether such criticism is fair. A recurring technical defense notes the AGC's 4KB RAM / 16KB ROM and ~0.043 MIPS, making the priority scheduler genuinely hard. Smaller subthreads correct that the famous photo shows simulator printouts rather than AGC source, credit her with coining 「software engineering」, and argue over whether that term is now over-applied to web work.

**Worth reading**
- [Jach](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50000913): Quotes a footnote from Don Eyles' Apollo memoir claiming Hamilton's leadership of LM software came only in early 1970, after the main goal, and that Hal Laning originated asynchronous software and priority scheduling.
- [jshier](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50000528): Corrects the iconic photo: she is standing beside printed output from an Apollo simulator run, not the actual AGC or lunar-module source code.
- [catskull](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50000350): Commenter says he tracked down and republished the obscure textbook interview in which Hamilton first used the phrase 「software engineering」.

## 5. How did Rosalind Franklin miss the helix in her iconic DNA image? She didn't {#story-5}
*#7 on HN · [science.org](https://www.science.org/content/article/how-did-rosalind-franklin-miss-helix-her-iconic-dna-image-she-didn-t) · 144 points · 53 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49969073)*

The linked piece (which returned HTTP 403 and could not be read directly) is a Science news article about a new Journal of the History of Biology paper (10.1007/s10739-026-09866-7) by science historian Alistair Sponsel of the Science History Institute and crystallographer Brian Sutton of King's College London. Their argument, per the HN title and commenters, is that Franklin did not overlook the helix: she had already read a helix out of an earlier, cruder diffraction photo, and that is precisely why she set out to make the sharper Photograph 51. Commenters note the paper does not overturn Watson and Crick, whose model added the two antiparallel strands and complementary base pairing.

*Article not read: HTTP 403*

**Discussion**: The thread spends most of its energy on the narrower question of how much Franklin actually wrote down, and disagrees about it. dekhn and masfuerte dig into her notebook pages, citing entries like 「evidence for [a] 2-chain helix」 (10 Feb 1953) and 「diads in A. Chargaff data」 (19 Jan 1953), which one philosophy-of-science paper reads as her inferring complementary base pairing eleven days before Watson saw Photo 51; masfuerte transcribes the crossed-out line 「indistinguishable from double helix with residues on」 as evidence she was actively thinking in double-helix terms. On the other side, titanomachy quotes Judson's The Eighth Day of Creation and Aaron Klug saying she never made the inductive leap — she never saw the dyad symmetry or the base pairing, and never modelled the backbones running in opposite directions — and skew-aberration argues the headline is clickbait, since helical DNA was already the leading candidate for years and Franklin herself famously staged a 「funeral notice」 for helical A-DNA. Several physicists/chemists push back on each other's technical claims (whether fiber diffraction implies helicity, whether beta keratin counts as helical), while colingauvin corrects a commenter who said Franklin 「wasn't a chemist」: she was in fact the only chemist of the group, Watson being a biologist and Crick and Wilkins physicists. The rough consensus is that the discovery was cumulative — helix, then double helix, then the copying mechanism — and that the Nobel's three-person, living-recipient rules and Franklin's early death explain the credit gap more than any single missed insight.

**Worth reading**
- [masfuerte](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49999573): Transcribes Franklin's actual notebook text, including the crossed-out line 「indistinguishable from double helix with residues on」 — the closest thing in the thread to primary evidence.
- [titanomachy](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50000579): Quotes Judson's The Eighth Day of Creation and Aaron Klug directly arguing Franklin never reached the dyad symmetry or base pairing, the strongest counterweight to the 「she knew」 reading.
- [skew-aberration](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50001059): Argues the headline misframes the history: helical DNA was the working hypothesis for over a decade, Franklin was trying to prove or disprove it, and the open questions were strand number and molecular details.

## 7. 'Jonathan' is the oldest land animal on Earth {#story-7}
*#10 on HN · [404media.co](https://www.404media.co/oldest-living-land-animal-jonathan-the-tortoise/) · 127 points · 55 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49998066)*

404 Media 报道了 Jonathan，一只约 194 岁的亚达伯拉象龟（Aldabrachelys gigantea），大约 1832 年孵化于塞舌尔，1880 年代被作为礼物送到圣赫勒拿岛，此后一直住在总督官邸 Plantation House。研究人员用脸颊拭子首次对他的基因组进行测序，并与包括一只 36 岁的象龟 Tank 在内的其他龟类比对，结果发表在 Science Advances 上，显示他在 DNA 修复、胰岛素调控、线粒体功能和端粒维护、自噬等「大多数衰老通路」上带有独特变异。最引人注目的是他的甲基化组：线粒体相关部分保持「低熵」，与一只五岁象龟相当，尽管他的其余基因组熵随年龄增长。论文作者、Kallel Foundation 的 Stephen Clark 表示目标是影响人类健康寿命，但强调要厘清这些线索还需更多研究；样本最终由美国太空军从圣赫勒拿运到佛罗里达。

## 8. Cleo (Mathematician) {#story-8}
*#11 on HN · [en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleo_(mathematician)) · 120 points · 21 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49982445)*

A Wikipedia article on Cleo, a pseudonymous Math StackExchange account that between November 2013 and December 2015 posted 39 correct answers to advanced complex-integration problems — often within hours and with no derivation — while its profile claimed to be a woman limited by an unspecified medical condition. After years of speculation (including a denial from Terence Tao), YouTuber Joe McCann and a viewer traced it via Gmail password-recovery backup addresses, and Vladimir Reshetnikov, an Uzbekistani-American developer who worked at Microsoft, confirmed in January 2025 that he created Cleo and the related accounts 「Laila Podlesny」 and 「Oksana Gimmel」; he later posted a Base64 「Creator of Cleo」 message on his profile. He said the persona and terse answers were meant to draw attention to neglected problems and push others to develop their own methods.

**Discussion**: DavidSJ, author of one of the April 2023 X threads the article leans on, objects that Wikipedia credits a 「late 2023」 Reddit user for the sleuthing that he and others had already done, and says he won't edit the page himself because of Wikipedia's conflict-of-interest rule; several commenters tell him just to add it or have someone cite his HN comment. The biggest technical thread asks whether any of the 39 answers responded to questions that weren't Reshetnikov's own sock puppets — epcoa calls this omitted detail relevant, and tibbar argues it makes the solutions much less impressive, since inverting a known integral is far easier than solving one; creatonez counters that Reshetnikov claimed he only conjectured answers by tweaking similar integrals in symbolic software and that every post was a gamble, which tibbar disputes because symbolic differentiation is deterministic. A parallel debate draws analogies to LLM-generated proofs: one commenter says all the value is in understanding the derivation and that AI is just a search engine, while another replies that results aren't 「self-evident」 even if they follow from axioms. A popular reframing casts the whole stunt as a real-world Cunningham's Law experiment that baited the forum into scrutinizing problems harder.

**Worth reading**
- [DavidSJ](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50001193): DavidSJ, whose April 2023 investigation predates the late-2023 Reddit analysis the article credits, notes the attribution gap and explains he won't fix the page himself over Wikipedia's conflict-of-interest rule.
- [tibbar](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50001544): tibbar argues the sock-puppet angle really does matter: posting known closed forms for your own questions is far easier than solving genuine integrals, so the impressive part is engineering problems with neat answers that evade computer algebra.
- [creatonez](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50002031): creatonez reframes Cleo as deliberate rage-bait to drive engagement and better proofs — a real-world Cunningham's Law experiment, with a wink at similar effects from AI math efforts.

## 9. In Vienna and Beijing, the first (thorium) nuclear clocks begin to tick {#story-9}

This is a New York Times science story reporting that the first nuclear clocks, based on the thorium-229 isotope, have started operating in Vienna and Beijing. **Discussion**: 评论区的核心分歧是：这类「一句话生成游戏」究竟有没有价值。许多开发者认为它只是给老式「自建游戏工具箱」套上 AI 外壳，产出的都是无尽跑酷、三消这类浅层克隆，质量甚至不比 Claude 一次性生成的游戏好，还有人担心它会像 80 年代劣质游戏潮一样淹没真正优秀的独立游戏。另一方面，几位亲自试玩的人说体验出乎意料：有人拿到了「Steampunk Match」世界第二，认为浏览器里能做到这种程度很厉害；也有人玩了编辑精选后发现窗口重叠、资源数值失衡、画面夜间看不清等明显问题，给出 0/10 和 2/10，认为产品远未准备好。不少人质疑 Google 的动机——收集提示数据训练模型、做平台垄断而非真心做游戏，并以 killedbygoogle.com 调侃它活不过一两年；也有评论指出大厂在意的是内容分发而非游戏本身。关于 AI 能否做出「好玩的游戏」，较一致的意见是：AI 或许能解决「做出来」，但「好玩」仍需人类反复试玩、平衡与原创机制。

## 20. SynthID Detector {#story-20}
*#35 on HN · [synthid.com](https://synthid.com/) · 110 points · 90 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49993188)*

SynthID Detector (synthid.com) is Google DeepMind's tool for checking whether an image was generated by Google's AI models — Gemini, Imagen and Nano Banana. The linked page had almost no readable text (it appears to be a JavaScript/consent-gated app), but commenters establish that using it now requires signing in with a Google, Apple or ChatGPT account, that it only detects Google's proprietary SynthID watermark, and that Google says OpenAI, NVIDIA, Kakao and Apple have agreed to implement SynthID. A related paper (arXiv 2510.09263) covers deploying SynthID at scale but deliberately omits the implementation details.

*Article not read: page had almost no readable text (probably needs JavaScript or a login)*

**Discussion**: The dominant complaint is the sign-in wall and the lack of programmatic access: commenters note OpenAI's verifier needs no auth, that Google's older paths (Google Image Search's “About this image”, or uploading to Gemini and asking) were clumsy or have been removed, and that there is no bulk or offline detection — one reports a rate limit of about 10 checks per 24 hours. A large privacy faction calls the marks “spymarks” (citing brand.io), arguing they could carry enough entropy to identify an author or downloader and feed Google's ad data; others push back that there is no evidence personal IDs are encoded, that secretly embedding tracking would breach GDPR, and that asking for a blank white image would reveal any account-specific distortion. Technically minded commenters argue the mechanism must be deterrence rather than security — whoever has detector access can iterate until the watermark disappears — and several share attempts at reverse-engineering or training an open classifier to strip it. Other threads ask why text watermarking isn't offered, with answers that paraphrase-and-replace attacks would defeat it, and one commenter suggests the better direction is verifying camera provenance (Apple Reference Image) rather than chasing every synthetic image.

**Worth reading**
- [justhw](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49998736): A hands-on test of roughly eight AI images: six unaltered ones were caught by SynthID, but the two that had text or picture overlays applied passed undetected.
- [Retr0id](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49993881): Links the most detailed SynthID write-up found (fyx.me, covering likely internals and training your own classifier) while criticizing Google for offering no transparent, bulk or offline detection.
- [Maxatar](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50001047): Corrects the common appeal to the arXiv paper: it explicitly omits the neural architecture, training process and loss functions and focuses only on deployment lessons.
