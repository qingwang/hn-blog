## 3. FreeDNS {#story-3}
*#4 on HN · [freedns.afraid.org](https://freedns.afraid.org/) · 170 points · 42 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50222133)*

这是 FreeDNS（afraid.org）的首页。这是一项历史很久的免费托管 DNS 服务，从 2000 年运行至今：它不做域名注册，只托管 DNS 记录，让你把 A、CNAME、MX 等记录托管在它的权威服务器上，也提供可供他人使用的免费子域名以及动态 DNS 更新端点。抓取文章时超时了，所以下面的内容依据标题、站点的已知性质以及评论者的描述。

*Article not read: timed out*

**Discussion**: 长期用户的态度普遍很正面：有人称自己已经免费用了大约 15 年，有人从那一年起付费成为 5,000 多名高级会员之一，还有人把它当作自己的第二台 DNS 服务器，用来指向家里的机器。这些用户夸奖的是服务的可靠性，而不是界面，因为界面明显已经过时——这一点本身也引出了关于 Web 1.0 时代「portal」式布局的讨论（PHP-Nuke、dns.he.net），有评论推测这些网站可能都源自 XName。最尖锐的批评是技术层面的：免费子域名没有进入 Public Suffix List（公共后缀列表），因此浏览器可能允许跨站设置 cookie，存在安全风险；这些域名也非常难拿到 Let's Encrypt 证书，因为每个域名的签发都有速率限制，而 FreeDNS 没有申请提高限额；此外，这些域名还频繁被滥用黑名单拦截。另有一场争论是关于连续性的：有人问如果维护者去世，或者域名被出售，会怎么样，一方认为 26 年的历史记录比含糊的承诺更有说服力，另一方则坚持认为这个问题需要一个明确、具体的回答；另外还有人指出网站当时因为流量过大而无法访问，并且批评一项免费服务依赖捐赠来维持从而在财务上不可持续，对此的反驳是：FreeDNS 确实对部分服务收费，而「免费」向来都是指由别人来买单。

**Worth reading**
- [8organicbits](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50223665): 详细列出了几个具体的坑：免费子域名没有进入 Public Suffix List（公共后缀列表）、Let's Encrypt 的速率限制，以及黑名单封禁的问题，并推荐了 messwithdns.net 和 open-domains.com 作为替代方案。
- [jrmg](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50223410): 用直接的措辞反驳了「26 年的历史记录」这一论点，认为「如果管理员去世会发生什么」这个问题需要的是明确答案，而不是安慰性的话。
- [electrotype](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50222903): 解释了这项服务日常的实际用途：提供一个稳定的动态域名（如 example.mooo.com），让 cron 任务在家庭网络 IP 变化时更新它。

## 4. Neural chip decodes interactions between brain regions {#story-4}
*#5 on HN · [actu.epfl.ch](https://actu.epfl.ch/news/neural-chip-decodes-interactions-between-brain-r-2/) · 56 points · 10 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50222525)*

EPFL researchers led by Mahsa Shoaran have built LogSync, a 4x2 mm implantable system-on-chip that records neural activity, extracts cross-regional synchrony and oscillation biomarkers, and uses a machine-learning classifier to detect cognitive or emotional states and trigger deep brain stimulation. It runs on under 200 microwatts (an earlier 32-channel version was reported at 196 μW) thanks to logarithmic computation and co-designed hardware/algorithms, and was validated on existing human recordings — mental-task engagement and emotional arousal during video clips — plus rat experiments at the University of Minnesota where it triggered stimulation at preset oscillation phases. The work appears in the IEEE Journal of Solid-State Circuits (Sept 2026) under a CHF 6.6 million NIMH grant, and the lab is commercializing related work through EPFL spin-off Infera Neuro.

**Discussion**: The thread is small and largely skeptical or tangential. One commenter points to a 1995 Greg Egan short story as the endgame of implantable neural decoding, and another jokes that this will end up in cookie-consent banners. The only real argument is over whether psychiatric conditions are better treated by lifestyle changes like exercise: one commenter claims most mental disorders would be fixed by sport and that implants are band-aids for a bad modern environment, and is met with the counter that you wouldn't prescribe jogging for a broken leg. The most substantive exchange is about whether there is any accepted taxonomy of human cognitive states — the consensus is that no agreed list exists, certainly not one backed by neural recordings, and that Plutchik's wheel of emotions describes emotions rather than the task-focused/social state switching the asker was after.

**Worth reading**
- [torben-friis](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50224035): Turns the exercise-cures-mental-illness claim back on itself with a broken-leg analogy, the thread's sharpest rebuttal.
- [asgraham](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50223078): Argues there is no agreed-upon list of cognitive states, and certainly not one supported by neural recordings, so informal observation is the realistic route.
- [rhodey](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50223437): Offers Plutchik's Wheel of Emotions (joy, trust, fear, surprise, sadness, disgust, anger, anticipation) as a starting vocabulary, which another commenter then distinguishes from cognitive states.

## 5. Node2Shell evolves to HTTP2Shell, blast radius affects many HTTP libs and apps {#story-5}
*#7 on HN · [aikido.dev](https://www.aikido.dev/blog/python-packages-vulnerable-node2shell-brotli-cve-2026-111497) · 220 points · 101 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50219177)*

Aikido's writeup covers CVE-2026-111497, an out-of-bounds write in Google's Brotli decoder affecting versions 1.0.3 through 1.2.0 (CVSS 4.0 score 9.2), first disclosed as Node2Shell and now found to reach far beyond Node. The bug lives in DecodeBlockTypeAndLength(), where a boolean return conflates 「malformed stream」 with 「need more input」, so a crafted stream can force a block switch and overflow a working buffer, letting a remote unauthenticated attacker leak memory, crash, or possibly execute code in both directions. Fixed versions include Brotli/libbrotli 1.2.1 (older lines 1.0.10, 1.1.1), Node.js 22.23.4/24.22.1/26.12.1, Bun 1.4.4, Envoy 1.40.0 and older branches, Istio 1.29.9/1.30.6/1.31.2, plus PyPI Brotli 1.2.1 and brotlicffi 1.2.1.0 — which advisory databases did not yet map to the CVE, so pip-audit, Dependabot and Safety would not flag them.

**Discussion**: The thread splits between people annoyed at the name (HTTP2Shell implies HTTP/2 or all HTTP servers, when the real issue is Brotli decompression; several prefer Brotli2Shell, one links a spoof site broccoli2shell.com) and those who read it as deliberate marketing to maximize patch urgency — a few note the naming was likely LLM-generated and nobody reviewed it. The substantive argument is scope: multiple commenters, notably bawolff, ask how many servers actually decode Brotli-compressed request bodies versus only using Brotli elsewhere, and call the article's framing sensationalist and unclear on that point; others counter that negotiation makes it easy (Content-Encoding: br on a POST, or an attacker-controlled server serving a Brotli response to a fetching app), and Cloudflare is said to forward compressed request bodies to backends. Practitioners report concrete exposure and non-exposure: one checked all Python deployments and found no Brotli installed since httpx/requests treat it as optional, another reports Git for Windows 2.56.0.windows.3, Codex's bundled Node, Wireshark, ImHex and M365 Copilot shipping unpatched decoders, and a third notes Rust-Brotli has been a Dropbox drop-in replacement since 2016. Many doubt the claim that OpenAI found it in February and reported it — the linked OpenAI tweet reportedly does not say so, and the likelier story is that Google merged an unreleased fix without recognizing it as security-relevant. The thread then drifts into a familiar side debate on deploying daily versus cooling down upgrades, and into a C-versus-Rust tangent, with no real consensus beyond patching and blocking br at the proxy if you cannot patch.

**Worth reading**
- [bawolff](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50220629): Raises the key question the article does not answer: how many of these stacks actually accept Brotli-compressed request bodies in common configurations, versus exposing the decoder only through outbound fetches — a much less severe bug.
- [perching_aix](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50221076): Lists concrete still-vulnerable software found by hand: Git for Windows 2.56.0.windows.3 (fix in snapshot, .4 due Monday), Codex's bundled Node plus its ~/.cache/codex-runtimes, Wireshark, ImHex, and M365 Copilot's brotlidec.dll from Oct 7.
- [anttihaapala](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50222420): First-hand audit result: none of their Python deployments installed Brotli at all, because httpx, requests and frameworks treat it as an optional auto-detected dependency.

## 7. TornadoVM 7 Enables GPU-Native Workloads from Java Applications {#story-7}
*#11 on HN · [infoq.com](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/10/tornadovm-7-gpu/) · 69 points · 13 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50192975)*

这是 InfoQ 的一篇报道，介绍开源异构 GPU 编程框架 TornadoVM 发布 7.3 版本。文章的核心变化是：后端从 JNI 改为使用 Foreign Function & Memory API，移除了实验性的 JVMCI 依赖，改为通过 reflection + ASM + Unsafe 统一获取编译器元数据，从而在 JDK 21、25、26、27 上走同一条路径；同时加深了对 Nvidia 的支持，包括优化的 CUDA 后端、通过 TileContext 支持 cuTile，以及可以在 TaskGraph 中直接调用 cuBLAS 和 CUTLASS，Apple 的 MLX 也获得了同等能力，并新增多 GPU 执行支持。文章还提到同时发布的 Java 推理引擎 jitLLM v2.0（单个 JAR，对标 llama.cpp 和 Ollama），两者均由团队创立的 Beestream.ai 进行商业化。

**Discussion**: 评论区很简短，主要由几条澄清和若干跑题内容组成。被广泛认为是维护者的 mikepapadim 补充了主仓库地址，说明只要 JDK 21+、Nvidia 或 Apple 硬件即可使用；当有人问及 Google TPU 支持时，他明确答复目前没有 TPU 支持，仅限 NVIDIA、Apple 以及任何支持 OpenCL 的硬件。有人问这是否意味着可以用 Java 构建 AI 基础设施，回答指向 jitLLM，称其是一个 vllm 风格、全部用 Java 编写的推理引擎，并以 TornadoVM 作为 GPU 后端；还有人把它类比为「用 Java 代替 Python 的 JAX」。最有实质内容的分支来自 mike_hearn，他推荐了 qxotic.ai：一个零依赖的单 JAR，从分词器往上全部用 Java 编写，在 macOS 上可使用 Metal，并声称在 prefill 这类任务上明显快于 llama.cpp（decode 受内存带宽限制，语言差异不大），前提是运行在 GraalVM 上。另有若干纯玩笑回复，讽刺「抽象 AI 基础设施工厂」式命名。

**Worth reading**
- [mikepapadim](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50223100): 维护者确认目前没有任何 TPU 支持，后端只有 NVIDIA、Apple 和任意 OpenCL 硬件。
- [mike_hearn](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50223687): mike_hearn 提供了另一个纯 Java 推理项目 qxotic.ai，并给出具体经验：在 GraalVM 上 prefill 比 llama.cpp 快得多，而 decode 受带宽限制、几乎无差别。
- [mikepapadim](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50222469): 指向 jitLLM 仓库，说明已有一个 vllm 式、全 Java 编写并以 TornadoVM 为 GPU 后端的推理引擎。

## 8. Physicists trap a single antimatter particle for a month {#story-8}
*#12 on HN · [scientificamerican.com](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/physicists-trap-a-single-antimatter-particle-for-a-month/) · 234 points · 70 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50186450)*

A Scientific American report by Clara Moskowitz on work from Gerald Gabrielse's group at Northwestern, published in Physical Review Letters, that isolated one positron in a wall-less electromagnetic trap for roughly a month — potentially indefinitely. Positrons from decaying sodium-22 are slowed in a cold tungsten crystal, steered into vacuum at 0.3 °C above absolute zero, and held by electric fields above and below and a magnetic field at the sides. The point is precision: the team has already measured the electron's magnetic moment to about one part in 10 trillion, and matching that on the positron tests the Standard Model's prediction that the two are exactly opposite — any discrepancy could bear on why the universe is matter-dominated.

**Discussion**: The thread reliably splits into real physics and antimatter-propulsion argument. Commenters ask how you can be sure exactly one particle is in the trap, and others answer straight from the paper: the damping and signal width of the driven axial oscillation grow with particle number, plus independent confirmation from spin-flip events. A researcher-adjacent commenter supplies context that the electron's magnetic moment is arguably the most precisely tested Standard Model quantity, measured to 13 digits, and that the positron's has not been measured since 1987. On the energy question, one camp argues antimatter is hopelessly energy-negative — build cost worse than a gold 747, and positron annihilation yields gamma rays that cannot be funnelled into a nozzle — while another replies that CERN's annual electricity bill is only $25-50M, this experiment likely cost single-digit millions, and the ~10^9 inefficiency is an engineering limit rather than a fundamental one; antiprotons, which yield containable pions, are the better rocket candidate.
