## 1. Protecting SSH Keys with macOS Secure Enclave {#story-1}
*#1 on HN · [gatezh.com](https://gatezh.com/posts/macos-secure-enclave-ssh-keys/) · 130 points · 46 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50449748)*

一篇实操教程，讲如何用 macOS 自带的工具把 SSH 私钥放进 Secure Enclave：先用 sc_auth create-ctk-identity -l ssh-MacBook -k p-256-ne -t bio 创建不可导出的 P-256 密钥，再用 ssh-keygen -w /usr/lib/ssh-keychain.dylib -K 生成一个「密钥句柄」，并通过 SSH_SK_PROVIDER 环境变量让 SSH 走 CryptoTokenKit 而不是 Keychain。作者据此主张对每把密钥都启用 Touch ID（-t bio），因为无生物识别的密钥可能被恶意软件静默使用；他也承认这套方案体验粗糙（弹窗写着「ctccardtoken needs to authenticate」），打算转向 Secretive，并提醒密钥与 Mac 绑定、必须另备一把备用密钥。文中的问题记录包括：Touch ID 注册被重置导致的 -25293 错误、到处流传的废弃 ssh-add -K 指令、以及 SSH 不会自动发现 id_ecdsa_sk_rk 需要手写 IdentityFile。

**Discussion**: 评论区大部分人不买文章的出发点：多位用户指出，加了口令并用 ssh-agent 的私钥本就不该算「裸奔」，而且每次登录都敲口令比按指纹更烦，所以 Secure Enclave 的收益主要是防内存提取而非防文件拷贝。反方（包括引用 Matthew Garrett 的那条）反驳说，只要私钥曾在 RAM 里存在过，恶意软件就能偷走，口令挡不住键盘记录和内存漏洞。使用体验上，Secretive 被反复推荐为首选封装，但多人确认 Secure Enclave 密钥无法备份，必须另有 YubiKey 或第二台 Mac 作后备；YubiKey 派则抱怨新机器和容器里的先有鸡还是先有蛋问题、常年占用 USB 口，也有人回帖说 5 Nano 支持触摸、本就设计为常插。几处具体修正：有人质疑文中「ssh: 结尾是 FIDO2 application ID」的说法，认为它其实就是注释；有人担心这是没人文档化的隐藏功能、Apple 随时可能改掉；还有人提醒 agent socket 和 ControlMaster 路径常常抵消了这些防护。

**Worth reading**
- [database64128](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50450079): 第一手踩坑：WireGuard 更新后因 keychain 报 -25300，App 把所有配置当成孤儿删掉，至今未修，用来论证依赖系统密钥库的脆弱性。
- [LeBit](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50450330): 直接反驳文章前提：私钥本来就该加口令并用 ssh-agent + UseKeychain，他自己用 YubiKey 也不是因为文件系统存不了受保护的密钥。
- [jen729w](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50450593): 实用折中：用 1Password service account 让开发环境密钥免指纹，只对生产密钥保留 Touch ID，避免一天二十次弹窗造成肌肉记忆式盲批。

## 2. Greg Kurtzer's Ginux is now a userspace kernel {#story-2}
*#2 on HN · [github.com](https://github.com/gmkurtzer/ginux) · 47 points · 22 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50450007)*

Ginux 是 Singularity/Apptainer 作者 Greg Kurtzer 的一个项目：它最初是一个用 Rust 编写的独立内核，通过 UEFI 直接引导并运行 Rocky Linux 10 用户空间；该阶段已经结束，源码保留在某个 tag 上，现在转向“用户空间内核”——作为普通 Linux 进程运行，在被管理的进程与宿主内核之间插入一份显式、有版本的契约。它拦截受管 syscall，并选择模拟、经校验的中介操作或拒绝；实现上提供一个偏重安全的 x86-64 解释器和一个基于 ptrace 的原生后端，默认 「selective」 模式只放行目录标注为直接安全的调用，可选全量中介的 「paranoid」 模式，并带有对原生性能 4% 预算的基准门槛和一套 Rocky 10 systemd 到达 multi-user.target 的基线。作者明确它只针对具名契约承诺兼容性，并把“给能执行命令、改文件、连网的 AI agent 做可审计的收容边界”列为主要探索目标，同时声明尚不是生产级沙箱；开发环境是 macOS arm64 上的 QEMU TCG，且原型本身是由人类架构师指挥 AI 编码 agent 写成的。

## 5. A great old-timey game-programming hack (2013) {#story-5}
*#8 on HN · [blog.moertel.com](https://blog.moertel.com/posts/2013-12-14-great-old-timey-game-programming-hack.html) · 121 points · 18 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50448921)*

Tom Moertel recounts a college-era favor for a friend whose 6809-based side-scroller for a 1980s PC ran too slowly: the background redraw loop was already hand-optimized to 98 cycles per 28-pixel row, down from 157 for naive code. Digging through the MC6809 programming manual, he found that PSHS/PULS can push or pull the whole D/X/Y register set for 5 cycles plus 1 per byte, so pointing the S and U stack registers at the tile and the screen buffer lets you copy 6 bytes in 22 cycles; with a DP-register trick for the 14-byte row remainder he got a row down to roughly 66 cycles.

**Discussion**: The thread is mostly affectionate appreciation plus war stories: MS-DOS bitmask search tricks, unrolled ld/push loops in Monster Max on Game Boy, TI-BASIC parenthesis golf, and the usual Story of Mel link. The one substantive correction comes from a veteran 6809/Coco developer, who says the PSHU/PULS blit was independently found by several programmers by 1983 — DataSoft cartridge games, possibly Williams' Defender or Joust, nicknamed 「PUSH-U / PUL-Me」 — while granting that the Direct Page refinement was new to him. A side argument corrects the article's aside calling the 6502 microcoded (it isn't; the 8088 is), and another subthread pushes back on the idea that cycle-counting is obsolete on modern CPUs, noting this hack is essentially hand-rolled SIMD and that SIMD still matters. A few commenters lament the abstraction of modern toolchains, with a younger graphics programmer countering that performance-specialist work still feels much the same.

**Worth reading**
- [mrandish](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50449807): A long-time Color Computer developer dates the stack-register blit trick to 1983, attributes it to DataSoft and possibly Williams' Defender/Joust (where Eugene Jarvis's team called it 「PUSH-U / PUL-Me」), and says only the Direct Page refinement was genuinely new.
- [mrandish](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50450476): Concedes the correction that the 6502 is not microcoded — that was the 8088 — and adds that Vectrex vector routines similarly thread draw commands through the 6809's 16-bit stack pointers.
- [Rohansi](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50450078): Argues the hack is really hand-written SIMD on an ISA without SIMD, so the claim that modern superscalar CPUs make such tricks irrelevant misses that SIMD remains highly beneficial.

## 7. Apple has new Mac app coming to make running local AI models easy {#story-7}
*#10 on HN · [9to5mac.com](https://9to5mac.com/2026/11/10/apple-has-new-mac-app-coming-to-make-running-local-ai-models-easy/) · 184 points · 145 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50446520)*

A 9to5Mac write-up by Ryan Christoffel of leaker Aaron Perris’s discovery of an unreleased Apple Mac app called Harbor, described as turning a Mac into a private inference server for open-weight models on Apple silicon. Its headline feature is clustering two or more Thunderbolt 5 Macs to pool unified memory into one inference resource, for models too big for a single machine or for more concurrent requests, with in-app model download and no command line. It is said to be in private preview under Apple’s Claris subsidiary, with no ship date.

**Discussion**: The thread splits between the view that Apple quietly wins by owning the on-device layer as small models improve, and the rebuttal that local inference is a niche for enthusiasts: cloud is cheaper at scale, the Mac is a minority platform with no datacenter or enterprise presence, and Apple’s real bottleneck is memory supply, not software. A long subthread argues over whether Apple should design and manufacture DRAM in-house — one side cites the Intel and Qualcomm transitions as proof it has the engineering and cash, the other notes Apple has no fabs and cannot outbid Nvidia or OpenAI for supply. The economics get hashed out with real numbers: $200/month subscriptions break even against a $10k box in about four years, hardware holds resale value (used 3090s and 4090s cited), but several practitioners counter that their actual usage is on flat-rate plans, not API rates, and that a 128GB Mac running qwen3.8:27b-mlx thermally throttles well short of Sonnet. Commenters see the Thunderbolt 5 memory pooling as the genuine differentiator over LM Studio and llama.cpp, but the loudest practical objection is that headless macOS still throws up security and update dialogs that stall an unattended machine. The Claris branding draws heavy skepticism, read either as an SMB subscription play or as evidence this is a skunkworks project rather than a shipping product.

**Worth reading**
- [MBCook](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50448750): First-hand warning that running a Mac as a server fights the OS: security prompts, update dialogs and permission pop-ups can only be dismissed with a monitor and mouse, silently halting all work.
- [jph](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50450531): A user doing security audits on a 200k-LOC codebase reports a 128GB Mac running qwen3.8:27b-mlx hits thermal limits after hours, and compares a $30k Mac Studio against a claimed $10k/week Claude Code run rate.
- [selectodude](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50448470): Concrete hardware math: an M5 Ultra has 64 memory channels and the densest LPDDR5X chip is 16GB, so 1TB Macs are technically buildable — the constraint is that Micron and Samsung have none to sell.

## 9. Tracking Anthropic-discovered vulnerabilities {#story-9}
*#16 on HN · [vulncheck.com](https://www.vulncheck.com/blog/anthropic-glasswing-november-update) · 391 points · 181 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50447074)*

A VulnCheck blog post (November update to a series by its author) auditing Anthropic's Project Glasswing seven months in. It counts 442 CVEs attributable to Anthropic — about 0.5% of the 93,450 CVEs published this year — with only two exploited in the wild (CVE-2026-61500, CVE-2026-26980), and argues Anthropic's own disclosure ledger is damning: 37,966 findings but only 7,349 (19.4%) public and 217 marked fixed, plus 1,654 duplicates and 419 withdrawals, while the claimed true-positive rate rose from 90.8% to 94.2%. It also uses curl's handling of a 45-report batch from Anthropic's new opt-in OSS Scanner — of 34 published, 19 marked not-a-vulnerability, 6 confirmed low severity, 9 pending, i.e. ~56% false positives — as a reality check on Anthropic's severity and accuracy claims.

**Discussion**: The thread splits between people who say the numbers match their own experience of inflated, low-severity AI findings (one maintainer reports 20-30% of OSS Scanner issues are junk and many of the proposed patches are 「straight garbage」; another sees ~50% false positives) and those who say the audit proves little. Defenders of Anthropic argue the ledger only covers embargoed, unfixed open-source findings, that most Glasswing work happens on partners' proprietary code and inside large companies and will never appear in CVE datasets, and that 「only 2 exploited in the wild」 is a backwards metric — the point is to patch before exploitation. Several commenters also call the post a thinly veiled advert for VulnCheck's vulnerability-prioritization product. The strongest counter-anecdote comes from Fil-C's author, who received nine Anthropic reports and found all nine real, with exceptional write-ups and test cases, though he concedes most were bugs that would not cause real-world problems.

**Worth reading**
- [pizlonator](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50448745): Fil-C's author says all 9 Anthropic reports he received were genuine bugs with exceptional write-ups and tests, and sees an 「enormous chasm」 between that and the article's claims.
- [rurcliped](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50448662): Argues the blog conflates Anthropic's OSS disclosure ledger with Glasswing proper, which mostly scans partners' proprietary code (citing Cloudflare's 「every vulnerability … was triaged, validated, and remediated」), so fix rates and public CVE counts are the wrong measure.
- [sigmar](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50448708): Points out that 「almost none are exploited in the wild」 is circular: Glasswing's whole purpose is to find and patch bugs before anyone can exploit them.

## 16. What objective is reasoning solving? {#story-16}
*#31 on HN · [alexwang.ai](https://alexwang.ai/posts/reasoning-as-pathfinding/) · 142 points · 65 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50445013)*

A blog post by Alex Wang (a Stanford CS PhD who worked on long-context architectures at Cartesia, not the xAI/MSL Alex Wang) arguing that RL with verifiable rewards does not teach a single chain of thought to converge on an answer, but instead raises the probability that some sampled path of tokens lands in the set of verifier-accepted answers. He cites Muennighoff et al. 2025 (forcing models to keep thinking with appended 「Wait」 helps only until accuracy flattens) and Gema et al. 2025 (tasks where longer reasoning made accuracy worse).

**Discussion**: The thread splits between people who like the framing and people who think it understates the causal role of inference-time thinking. One camp, following the human analogy of inner speech, argues that each extra token is another pass through the network, and this gives real computation: commenters cite the 「Let's Think Dot by Dot」 paper (Pfau, Merrill, Bowman), where filler dots still improved results, and debate whether those dots work because they add passes or simply because they keep a summary of the task at the front of the context window. Another commenter offers a four-point restatement of the post and then claims it contradicts the post's own conclusion that gains from 「thinking longer」 come from training-time search rather than from inference; the replies largely argue that this is not a contradiction, because training teaches the model how to use the extra tokens. Practitioners push back on the framing from another direction: one commenter describes partners' RL runs where disabling reasoning for a calibrated fraction of rollouts improved the latent reasoning core and reduced the number of tokens needed, while another asks what the point of a verifier is if verifying an answer implies that we already know how to find it; the answers are SAT solvers and P=NP proofs. The sharpest disagreement is over whether libraries are worth targeting at all — some call it pointless and immoral because libraries have no money, while others argue the attacks are opportunistic and largely automated, with groups encrypting first and negotiating later, and one commenter points out that insurers, not victims, often end up paying. Several people push back on the idea that recovery is easy: the British Library is described as still missing systems more than three years after its 2023 attack, because old software no longer runs on modern hardened infrastructure, and rebuilding a five-million-item catalogue from scratch would take a decade. A well-informed side thread covers New York's three independent library systems (Queens, Brooklyn, NYPL) and the little-known fact that any New York State resident can get a card at each, plus Queens' $50/year card for anyone worldwide. Speculation about the initial vector (a recent PHP flaw versus phishing) was unresolved and unconfirmed.
