## 1. Knuth Reward Check {#story-1}
*#1 on HN · [thomas-huehn.com](https://www.thomas-huehn.com/knuth-reward-check) · 68 points · 27 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50034081)*

这是 Thomas Hühn 的一篇博客文章，讲述他在大约二十年前因在 Donald Knuth 的著作中发现错误而收到一张 Knuth 奖励支票的经历。这个错误位于《Computer Modern Typefaces》（Computers & Typesetting 丛书的 E 卷）的正文第 1 页第一段的第一个单词，因此备忘录栏写着「E1」。由于 Knuth 已多年不再开具真实支票（担心被滥用），这张支票的图像做了遮挡；作者出现在 Bank of San Serriffe 的名单上，金额为「0x$1.20」，即 2.88 美元——这是把一笔正确的发现（2.88 美元）与后来一次错误的报告合并后的结果，那次 Knuth 在解释他为何有误的同时，还因为报告里被算作有效建议的一句话，额外给了他一张 0.32 美元的支票。

**Discussion**: 评论区几乎一边倒地表示钦佩和怀旧，分享了各自收藏的支票。有几位说他们弄丢了支票，其中一位（kens）说 Knuth 又补寄了一张；CurtHagenlocher 则半开玩笑地称丢失支票是自己一生中最大的遗憾。ape4 复述了那个错误的确切性质——Knuth 曾写道有「无穷多个字母表」可以被生成，但由于参数有限且取值范围有限，这个数字实际是有限的。jll29 说他因为抓到 Knuth 声称「Alan Turing 破解了 Enigma」而拿到了一张支票，并指出真正的工作应归功于波兰密码分析学家 Marian Rejewski，Turing 和 Tommy Flowers 的贡献是将其自动化。有人（jacquesm）提出疑问，为何没有其他计算机科学作者效仿 Knuth 的做法，chompychop 对此反驳称，关键在于动机来自 Knuth 的声望，而非金钱奖励。关于谁拥有的支票最多，讨论最终引向 Knuth 自己发布的账户余额页面，不过有几位提醒说，账户余额并不等于支票张数（早于 2006 年的纸质支票时代并未列入），并且支票金额各不相同。

**Worth reading**
- [ape4](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50034463): 复述了那张改变一切的、位于《Computer Modern Typefaces》第 1 页的错误的精确技术细节——「无穷多个字母表」实际上数量有限，因为参数是有限的。
- [jll29](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50035419): 第一手报告：因抓到 Knuth 声称「Alan Turing 破解了 Enigma」而获得一张支票，并指出功劳应归于 Marian Rejewski（Turing 和 Flowers 只是将破解自动化）。
- [dhosek](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50035255): 一位 TeX 错误邮件列表成员表示，Knuth 收到报告背后的审查力度「令人难以置信」，好奇的读者可能会想打开欣赏。

## 2. Talorys – A self-hosted personal AI agent on Cloudflare's free tier {#story-2}
*#2 on HN · [github.com](https://github.com/rociiu/talorys) · 181 points · 96 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50031614)*

Talorys 是一个 MIT 许可的开源个人 AI 助手，完全部署在用户自己的 Cloudflare 账户内：Cloudflare Pages 承载 React 前端，Pages Function 通过 service binding 转发到无公开 URL 的私有 Worker，该 Worker 是一个基于 Cloudflare Agents SDK 的 Durable Object（SQLite 存储对话、记忆、任务、笔记、项目、自动化），推理用 Workers AI 的 @cf/zai-org/glm-4.7-flash 模型，定时提醒由 Durable Object alarms 驱动。安装只需一条命令 npx create-talorys@latest，安装器自动校验 Cloudflare 登录、生成 PBKDF2-SHA256 哈希的 owner 密码和 256 位会话密钥、部署资源并验证健康检查，设计上只使用免费套餐、不开启任何付费服务、无遥测。项目为单用户设计，宣称即使 Workers AI 不可用，任务、笔记、记忆和提醒仍可继续工作。

**Discussion**: 评论几乎全部围绕“self-hosted”这个词是否名副其实：多人认为把服务跑在 Cloudflare 上、依赖 Cloudflare 的四个服务，属于“self-managed”或“cloud-hosted”而非自托管，有人调侃“那我去哪下载 Cloudflare”，也有人给出更细致的定义（自己拥有物理硬件才算、VPS 算、SaaS 不算）。为项目辩护的一方（如 vlovich123、jonplackett）指出代码是开源的，把 AI 调用指向本地模型服务只是很小的改动，本地其余部分都能用 wrangler 跑起来，批评措辞而非作品显得不友好；作者本人在置顶评论里解释了动机是“拥有和控制、不付月费、不依赖他人托管”。另有若干实操反馈：有人警告 Cloudflare 免费 AI 额度和 neuron 计费混乱、用付费 Workers 时被意外扣费且客服不理，有人回复通过反复申诉才退款，还有人建议用 privacy.com 限额卡；也有人称 Durable Objects“代码和数据锁在一起、空闲冻结、毫秒级唤起”非常有用，并指出 Cloudflare Workers AI 对交互式场景“非常慢”，更适合后台任务。有评论把 neuron 换算成具体额度：1 万 neuron 约等于 0.11 美元，或约 400 万输入 token / 50 万输出 token（llama-3.2-1b）.

**Worth reading**
- [vlovich123](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50034030): 反驳“不算自托管”的批评：项目开源，把 AI 调用改成指向本地模型服务器不到 20 分钟工作量，其余全部通过 wrangler 本地运行。
- [hung](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50032212): 第一手警告：使用付费 Workers 时被 Cloudflare 混乱的 neuron 计费意外扣款，客服无视工单，Discord 上显示这是已知问题。
- [scosman](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50033838): 实践者指出 Cloudflare Workers AI 对交互式用例非常慢，只适合后台任务或评测。

## 4. OpenSCAD the Programmers Solid 3D CAD Modeller {#story-4}
*#4 on HN · [openscad.org](https://openscad.org/) · 27 points · 30 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49984349)*

OpenSCAD is a free, script-based solid 3D CAD modeller for Linux/UNIX, Windows and macOS that builds geometry from code (cubes, spheres, cylinders, boolean ops) rather than interactive sketching. The linked page is essentially its homepage: a sparse set of links — IRC channel #openscad on libera.chat, a Wikibooks manual and tutorial, a new book 「This is OpenSCAD」 by Roberto Hamm, and an article on braiding machines built from code — with no real detail beyond 「OpenSCAD is software for creating solid 3D CAD objects」. Commenters largely fill the gap, debating what the CSG approach is and isn't good for and recommending alternatives.

**Discussion**: The dominant thread is that OpenSCAD is excellent for highly parametric, code-defined parts but weak for general CAD work: erjiang warns that expressing things like arbitrary fillets is simply hard with CSG, and points to Open Cascade kernel tools (FreeCAD, CadQuery, build123d) that also give STEP import/export. Others push back that reliability is the reason they stayed — one long-time user says writing in vim reliably produced a manufacturable STL, while FreeCAD and SolidWorks cost half the time in bugs and bad exports. A common correction is that the current stable release is old (2021) and users should run the nightly/experimental build with the manifold geometry solver, which several call dramatically faster, to the point preview mode becomes unnecessary. Recurring gripes: the language itself (functional-but-odd scoping, silent variable redefinition) and the endless question of when the nightlies become a real release; frequent recommendations include BOSL2, jscad, PythonsCAD and build123d, and many note LLMs are already decent at emitting OpenSCAD.

**Worth reading**
- [erjiang](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50035553): Counter-argument from the CSG side: OpenSCAD's constructive-solid-geometry model makes common operations like arbitrary fillets hard, and Open Cascade-based tools (FreeCAD, CadQuery, build123d) add STEP interop.
- [jacquesm](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50035427): A heavy user explains the mental shift — you are describing geometry, not programming, so variables don't behave as expected — and reports the experimental build is fast enough to skip preview mode.
- [TacticalCoder](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50035552): First-hand take from a recent 3D-printer owner: OpenSCAD gives programmers modelling for free, the language is genuinely bad about variable scoping, and the BOSL2 library is worth learning.

## 5. Rampart: Browser native on-device PII radaction {#story-5}
*#6 on HN · [ndstudio.gov](https://ndstudio.gov/posts/say-hello-to-rampart) · 45 points · 16 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50024242)*

The National Design Studio (NDS) announced Rampart, an open-source, first-generation on-device PII filter that runs entirely in the browser between typing and sending, with no server in the loop. It combines a deterministic regex/validation layer (SSNs, credit cards, routing and account numbers, emails, IPs, government IDs) with a MiniLM model for names and street addresses, covering 17 entity types across seven languages (English, Spanish, French, German, Italian, Portuguese, Dutch). NDS claims a 14.7 MB model (including tokenizer), 3.9 ms p50 WebGPU latency, and 98.4% private-term recall on a 30,000-row held-out OpenPII slice; it contrasts itself with OpenAI Privacy Filter (~2.8 GB), GLiNER small v2.1 (~600 MB) and Microsoft Presidio (~13 MB), and ships on HuggingFace plus an NPM package (@nationaldesignstudio/rampart).

**Discussion**: Commenters split between skepticism and qualified praise. Several say they cannot trust the government to protect their data, with others countering that trust is beside the point since the code is on GitHub and the model can be swapped for your own or run locally. The strongest technical pushback comes from an author of a competing redaction package (zink), who argues the government's own example of a user pasting their SSN is bad advice, that the burden should be on AI companies not to store SSNs, and that quasi-identifiers are asymptotically hard to filter; a second commenter counters that giving builders a free, easy improvement over the status quo is still commendable. A practitioner who has pitched similar systems to banks says clients only accept zero data retention and deterministic redaction at the source, and that regex over arbitrary natural language is not determinism, since correlated non-PII facts can still identify someone. Others find 98.4% recall far too low to call it PII redaction, ask whether it satisfies HIPAA, and note a co-author's link to a DOGE whistleblower story about an insecure SSN database.

**Worth reading**
- [dwa3592](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50034115): Author of the competing zink redaction package argues the government's own SSN example is self-defeating, that AI vendors rather than users should bear the storage burden, and that quasi-identifiers remain unsolved.
- [bob1029](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50034124): First-hand account from pitching similar systems to banking clients: they only accept zero data retention and deterministic redaction at the source, and correlated non-PII facts can still deanonymize users.
- [iAMkenough](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50033865): Raises a co-author's (as400495) connection to a CSO Online whistleblower report that DOGE placed an SSN database covering 300 million Americans on insecure cloud infrastructure.

## 7. Mxc: Microsoft Execution Containers version 1.0.0 {#story-7}
*#9 on HN · [blogs.windows.com](https://blogs.windows.com/windowsdeveloper/2026/10/07/microsoft-execution-containers-policy-driven-containment-for-ai-agents/) · 98 points · 19 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50016956)*

A Microsoft Windows Developer blog post by Logan Iyer (CVP, Windows Platform + Developer) announcing general availability of Microsoft Execution Containers (MXC), a policy-driven containment layer for AI agents. Developers declare needed resources (files, network destinations, loopback, desktop access) in a unified JSON schema via a multi-language SDK, and MXC enforces the boundary using platform-appropriate backends: process sandboxes (AppContainer on Windows, Seatbelt on macOS, Bubblewrap on Linux), a Windows-only session container, WSL, and an experimental hardware-isolated MicroVM option. It ships with Enforcement, Learning and Permissive modes plus a Windows-only JSON activity report for authoring least-privilege policies, and is being wired into Intune management policy, Entra agent identity and Agent 365; announced integrations include NVIDIA OpenShell, GitHub Copilot, OpenClaw, OpenAI Codex, Replit, LM Studio and Unsloth, with Anthropic Claude Code, Box, Perplexity and others promised.

**Discussion**: The thread is largely skeptical. The top commenter (moomin) concedes it is good to see an answer to Linux's bubblewrap, but argues the industry has been terrible at permissioning and this does not fix it: hooking an agent up to something like JIRA introduces a separate identity system and resource model, and escalating security complexity just lets people blame users when containment fails. 3eb7988a1663 calls Microsoft security bimodal — fine-grained in SharePoint/Azure but consumer apps like Excel, VS Code and Outlook still reduce to a binary 「do you trust this?」 prompt. freeone3000 notes Windows already has multi-user RBAC and cannot tell what is new, then eddythompson80 argues the same-machine multi-user paradigm has been dead for years outside accidental-foot-gun protection, calling uid/gid an 80s antique and saying real isolation now comes from namespacing/KVM-style layers. Others ask whether MXC can serve as a generic app-permissions boundary (with the prediction it will be gated to corporate licenses), and tomrod describes already locking down agents by running them as untrusted users and wanting a local HashiCorp-Vault-like broker for agent credentials. chris_money202 sums up the likely outcome: a good idea that enterprise customers will love.

**Worth reading**
- [moomin](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50034698): Concedes MXC is a real answer to bubblewrap but argues permissioning is fundamentally broken — integrating external systems like JIRA brings another identity system and resource model, so more security complexity just shifts blame to users when it fails.
- [eddythompson80](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50035846): Argues same-machine multi-user security is obsolete: root/non-root is now only an accidental-foot-gun mechanism, uid/gid is an 80s organizational primitive, and genuine isolation comes from namespacing/KVM layers.
- [tomrod](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50035254): First-hand practice: runs agents as untrusted users because even Docker boundaries are weak, considers Firecracker a step forward, and wants a local Vault-like broker to hand agents scoped credentials and reviewable access boundaries.

## 19. Computers Cannot Make Decisions {#story-19}
*#39 on HN · [wiki.cateat.fish](https://wiki.cateat.fish/art:computers_cannot_make_decisions) · 185 points · 165 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50029982)*

A short wiki essay arguing that computer programs cannot make decisions, so blaming an LLM for an outcome is 「decision laundering」. It points to headlines about Anthropic and OpenAI — a false police tip in a Philadelphia murder case, an 「undisclosed attack on RubyGems」, an alleged hack of an Australian government site, and model risk warnings in an IPO prospectus — and claims the labs are deliberately choosing to enable this behavior, funded by users' Claude subscriptions. Headlines like those, the author says, shift fault from the people who deployed the systems to the machines.

**Discussion**: Most commenters accept the accountability conclusion but reject the premise: computers have made decisions since the first if-else statement, chess engines and 30 years of algorithmic trading being the common counterexamples, and the real question is who is legally and morally responsible, not who technically decided. A recurring objection is that the post commits its own fallacy — Anthropic is a company, and companies arguably cannot decide either, so the blame lands on individual employees, with several people noting corporations already function as 「accountability sinks」 with limited liability. Others push back on the essay's personal accusation that subscribers are funding the abuses, arguing enterprises and, above all, venture capital money are what keep the labs afloat, and one commenter challenges anyone to produce a single real case of someone escaping responsibility by blaming their AI. There is rough agreement that a human must always sit at the end of the chain, but no consensus on whether that is a philosophical fact, a legal convention, or an assertion that the frontier labs will eventually outrun.

**Worth reading**
- [dsjoerg](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50031905): Turns the essay's argument back on itself: if computers can't make decisions, then in the same sense companies can't either, so the piece should not say Anthropic chose anything.
