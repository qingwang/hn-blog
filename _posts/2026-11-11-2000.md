## 1. AWS Finally Built Its Own AWS Emulator {#story-1}
*#1 on HN · [github.com](https://github.com/aws/aws-emulator) · 291 points · 81 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50456631)*

aws/aws-emulator is an official AWS repository: a local Rust emulator of the AWS APIs that lets SDK and CLI code plus CI run without an AWS account, serving HTTPS on 127.0.0.1:9297, shipped as RPM/Deb packages on Linux and via Docker elsewhere, with an embedded mode that compiles the emulator into your test binary. The workspace has one crate per AWS service driven by Smithy models, a full debug build reportedly needs 100 GB+ of free target/ space, it is licensed CC0, takes no PRs, and ships telemetry on by default via --telemetry off or EMULATOR_TELEMETRY. It launched alongside an Amazon blog post describing how autonomous AI agents built it.

**Discussion**: Skepticism dominates: is this a maintained product or a demo for the agentic-coding blog post? An AWS engineer (ke4qqq) insists it is real and points to the aboutamazon announcement plus exec blogs and tweets, while others argue that record of AWS shipping many services and maintaining few — versus Google-style abandonment or Azure renaming and deprecating things under you — makes any commitment cheap. A large part of the thread is a LocalStack/Floci/Moto/Ministack bake-off: LocalStack is said to no longer be free for commercial use under 2026 pricing changes, Floci is praised by one commenter but called vibe-coded with releases that break randomly by another, and Moto comparisons surface a practical gap — no fast way to reset emulator state between tests other than restarting the process. Commenters also dig into the git history, citing roughly 29,000 commits in about a month and Claude-typical phrasing, and several complain that telemetry is on by default and that the docs lack a clear service-fidelity matrix. The rough consensus is guarded interest plus a wait-and-see on fidelity and maintenance.

**Worth reading**

## 3. A false smoke alarm almost drowned Jane Street's only NYC datacenter {#story-3}
*#3 on HN · [michael.bacarella.com](https://michael.bacarella.com/2026/11/10/false-smoke-alarm-jane-street-datacenter/) · 266 points · 137 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50455394)*

A first-person war story from a former Jane Street systems engineer (Michael Bacarella) about the firm's machine room on the 33rd floor of 1 New York Plaza, kept deliberately next to the trading floor so someone could always run over and yank power cords. Contractor dust set off a smoke alarm, which shut down the CRAC units and pressurized the pre-action sprinkler pipes; with no cooling the room climbed past 143 F toward the roughly 150 F at which heat-activated sprinkler heads would open. He sprinted from his apartment on Water Street, pulled every power cable by hand, and the temperature stopped at 143.6 F; markets were closed, so downtime was minor. The aftermath: sprinklers stayed, procedures were tightened, and he wrote an OCaml monitor that polls every Linux box's onboard temperature sensors each minute and alerts on a rising fleet-wide average.

**Discussion**: The thread immediately detours into Hurricane Sandy lore — Fog Creek's 5-gallon-bucket diesel brigade up 17 flights of stairs — with a long argument over why generators sit high in the building (exhaust venting, transmission losses, and the one-atmosphere limit on suction pumping fuel from a basement) versus why anyone designed it that way at all. The strongest disagreement is about heroism: one commenter who worked at Fog Creek says that story lionizes a preventable failure and that he quit largely over the absence of a backup strategy, while others defend celebrating the individuals who responded without excusing the planning, and a few push back that hindsight makes the risk look more obvious than it was. Facilities practitioners correct the technical framing: shutdown of CRACs on smoke alarm is a design choice, not a law of nature, and the IMC explicitly permits air handlers over 2000 cfm serving a single space to keep running; several note sprinklers are pre-action and usually only one head opens, so the damage is local rather than a movie-style deluge. People repeatedly ask why there was no EPO button or why nobody just switched the cooling back on — the author answers that he didn't know where the CRAC controls were but did know how to rip out cords — and others object to the headline, clarifying it means Jane Street's only NYC datacenter, not NYC's only one. Side threads cover halon and clean-agent suppression (halon is banned, CO2 and inert gas carry life-safety problems), and a tangent on oversharing with employers, where several people say mentioning you live five minutes away turns into unpaid emergency on-call duty.

**Worth reading**
- [gecko](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50456398): A former Fog Creek engineer says the celebrated Sandy diesel-hauling story was a completely avoidable failure — he quit largely over the lack of a backup strategy and argues holding it up as a positive lionizes bad planning.
- [rainbowzootsuit](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50456472): Someone who writes data-hall sequence-of-operations documents says they deliberately do not shut CRACs on smoke alarm, citing the International Mechanical Code's 2000 cfm single-space exception, and notes recirculated air doesn't feed a fire.
- [mbac32768](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50456332): The author answers why he pulled cords instead of restoring cooling: teams with overlapping responsibilities meant he might have been the only person reachable, and he knew how to yank power but not where the CRAC controls were.

## 7. Faster Retrieval for Better, More Efficient RAG {#story-7}
*#7 on HN · [machinelearning.apple.com](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/faster-retrieval) · 84 points · 19 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50455633)*

Apple's ML research blog post summarizes 「Amortizing Maximum Inner Product Search with Learned Support Functions」, an ICML 2026 paper that replaces centroid-based cluster routing in MIPS/vector search with small neural networks trained once, offline, on the actual query distribution. SupportNet learns a support function per cluster (via input convex neural networks) so routing asks how good the best key in a cluster is, not how close the centroid is; KeyNet instead predicts the best-matching key and acts as a query rewriter in front of a standard FAISS IVF index. On Natural Questions (~2.6M passages, 128 clusters), SupportNet hits 95% routing recall 1.9× faster than centroid routing (1.74 ms vs 3.36 ms) and 97% at 1.74 ms with the large model; KeyNet-mapped queries reach 99% recall in 0.23 ms vs 1.8 ms raw, and hit the true key up to 96.7% of the time while scanning 0.001% of the database versus 86% for the raw query.

**Discussion**: Almost nobody engaged with the retrieval paper itself: the thread splits into Apple strategy talk and a well-worn RAG-versus-grep argument. Several practitioners say learned retrieval is hyperscale technology — armcat, who saw Spotify present a similar 「Parallel Fusion Router」 the day before, argues that below a few million items a plain BM25 index given to the LLM as a search tool beats learned methods every time, and that the latter are mostly more publishable. mickeyp reports building one-size-fits-all RAG over codebases and concludes small embedding/BERT models lose to grep unless you hand-prune tests, docs and sidecars, and that if you use a large embedding model you may as well use a subagent instead. The grep camp's counter-argument, from goldenarm and others, is that grep cannot handle meaning, other languages or typos, nor terabytes across distributed servers under 1000 ms; ozkatz and mejutoco concede grep's literal matching but note an LLM can expand a concept into alternations like red|maroon|scarlet, shifting the similarity work from indexing time to inference time — a rebuttal undercut by the point that this still misses misspellings. Others push back that retrieval is just a tool to keep the model from ingesting the whole corpus, and one commenter posts the inevitable joke about Apple using this to make Siri worse.

**Worth reading**
- [armcat](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50457462): Reports seeing Spotify's own learned router 「Parallel Fusion Router」 and argues from experience that these methods only pay off at hundreds of millions of items — below that, BM25 plus an LLM search tool wins.
- [mickeyp](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50457673): A RAG practitioner over codebases says small embedding/BERT models can't beat grep without heavy pruning of tests, docs and sidecar files, and large embedding models make a scouting subagent the better choice.
- [ozkatz](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50457489): Counters the grep defense by showing an LLM can drive grep with synonym alternations like red|maroon|scarlet|crimson, moving similarity work from index time to inference time — though a reply notes typos still slip through.

## 8. Google SWE points 48 AI agents at NASA Lidar, finds unmapped Maya pyramid in 72h {#story-8}

## 13. Can a Linear Program Do Weight-Space Programming? {#story-13}
*#13 on HN · [nathan.rs](https://nathan.rs/posts/weight-space-lp/) · 197 points · 29 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50452655)*

这是 Nathan Barry 对 MIT CSAIL 论文《Sparks of Weight-Space Programming》（作者 Rikiya Takehi、Jayden Teoh、Alex Zhang、Omar Khattab）的一篇后续博客。那篇论文中，GPT-6 Astra 通过改权重把 Qwen3.5-0.8B 的 MATH-500 从 7.9% 提升到 48.1%，只改了 0.016% 的权重（130,188 个），但花了约 8 小时、28 次前向传播来试错搜索。Barry 指出这个搜索本质上是在解一个线性规划：在残差流的“循环方向” u 上，要求循环 token 被推离 u、正常 token 保持不变，目标是最小化 v 的 L1 范数。他用 GPU 上的 PDHG 求解器（HiGHS 需要 20 多分钟，他的实现约 1 分钟）把搜索替换成几次 LP 求解，只改 16,291 个权重就在 GSM8K、MATH-500、IFEval 上追平 Astra 的编辑；换用不用 LLM 的启发式方向仍能达到约 93% 的效果。他还发现，LP 得到的 8 个列在层 15 上对 89% 的 token 都激活、推力近乎恒定，因此一个固定强度的 steering vector（1×）几乎能复现全部修复效果，但窗口很窄：0.5× 时循环复发，2× 时推理崩坏。

- [wasabi991011](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50455365): 把争议拆成一个谱系：一端是模型直接把全部权重读进上下文再输出新权重（极难、极意外），另一端是无限制工具与算力下的普通优化（毫不意外），Astra 的结果落在哪里仍有争议。
