## 1. Ultramarine 1 Flash {#story-1}
*#1 on HN · [paperinstruments.com](https://www.paperinstruments.com/ultramarine) · 166 points · 50 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50106747)*

Paper Instruments announced Ultramarine 1 Flash, an agentic model for professional knowledge work (finance, law, consulting, operations) built by applying large-scale reinforcement learning to the open base model GLM-5.3 Flash, with the same per-million-token API pricing as its base. It claims to be 「Fable-class」 at $0.13 per task — beating Claude Fable 5 (max) at $8.19 and GPT-5.6 Sol (max) at $2.09 — while coming within three points of Claude Opus 5.5 (max) and GPT-6 Sol (max), measured on the company's internal PI Knowledge Work benchmark (which they say correlates r = 0.81 with the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index). It also says the model emits 6.5% fewer tokens than its base across six benchmarks.

**Discussion**: The dominant thread is distrust of the marketing: commenters point out the results section compares Ultramarine only to models it beats on every metric, then the cost-per-task chart suddenly reintroduces stronger-but-pricier models (Fable 5.1, GPT-6 Astra, Opus 5.5), and several call the 「Fable-class」 label wrong — one says the graphs show it as Luna-class. A recurring reaction is that this looks like a skinned/post-trained GLM-5.3 Flash, and one commenter pulls the technical report wording confirming the RL-on-GLM-5.3-Flash base; another notes it is actually slightly worse at coding (DeepSWE 61.5 vs 63.4 for base, TerminalBench 2.1 also worse). People debated what 「frontier model」 even means — the intelligence frontier (Fable, Astra, Opus 5.5) versus the cost/performance Pareto frontier — and a few asked what harness produces the Excel/PowerPoint/Word outputs, prompting a self-promo for a 「knowledge work harness」 called Radix and a link to Paper Instruments' own Feather. Cheaper-inference privacy concerns were raised and partly answered (you can buy open-weight inference from ZDR/GDPR-compliant providers). Net mood: genuinely interesting price/performance and a useful base model, but benchmarks and positioning viewed as cherry-picked.

**Worth reading**
- [CobrastanJorji](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50128230): Detailed critique of graph cherry-picking: the results chart omits better models, then the cost chart brings them back, and the model should just be presented as second-best.
- [randomblock1](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50127783): Cites the numbers showing it is slightly worse than its GLM-5.3 Flash base at coding (DeepSWE 61.5 vs 63.4, TerminalBench 2.1 also lower).
- [nl](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50128064): Argues 「frontier model」 means pushing the intelligence frontier, and that Pareto cost-per-task is a weak measure since fine-tuning a small model beats a large one on any repeated task.

## 2. Llama.cpp on a jailbroken PS5: run any GGUF model natively on the console {#story-2}
*#3 on HN · [github.com](https://github.com/cobanov/PS5LM) · 264 points · 64 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50122431)*

PS5LM 是一個以 GPL-3.0 授權的 GitHub 專案（作者 cobanov），在已越獄的 PS5 上以 llama.cpp 的 Vulkan 後端驅動主機的 RDNA 2 GPU，開放 11.4 GB 共享記憶體給模型與上下文。它在區網上提供 OpenAI 的 /v1/chat/completions、Anthropic 的 /v1/messages（可用 Claude Code 連）以及瀏覽器聊天頁，並加上電視介面、溫度／功耗監控與 Hugging Face 下載器；另可載入 Ollaya 的決策模型走 /v1/systemone。需求是韌體 7.00–13.60 的越獄主機與特定 payload，實測約 20 tokens/s、64k context，跑的是低量化（2-bit）的 Qwen 27B 級模型。

**Discussion**: 最長的一條討論串是「不該把這種專案放在 GitHub」，有人主張自架網域、有人說 Sony 根本不在乎你跑 llama.cpp、只在乎盜版遊戲，也有人說 2026 年除了 TOR 或 torrent 之外無法真正規避 DMCA，最後走向 radicle、自架 Git 等去中心化方案。硬體經濟學是另一大戰場：有人貼出 $385 的 Beelink N150 mini PC 比 $600 的 PS5 划算，隨即被反駁兩者 GPU 效能差一個數量級、N150 連小模型都很慢，也有人指出 BC-250（PS5 APU 的降規版）才是便宜又合理的替代品。多位實際用過 Qwen 3.8 27B 的人表示該模型本身接近 Opus 4.6 水準，但 2-bit 量化明顯退化，且需要極長推理時間，因此不同意「20 tps 完全沒用」的說法——有人說拿來讓它讀 codebase、找問題已經夠用。整體沒有共識，但氣氛偏向「有趣的成就」，同時提醒這台主機規格其實不划算。

## 3. Claude reveals ECDSA curve origins {#story-3}
*#5 on HN · [anthropic.com](https://www.anthropic.com/research/ecdsa-curve-origins) · 817 points · 309 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50122800)*

Anthropic 的一篇研究文章（帖子中还指向 Filippo Valsorda 关于该悬赏的博客文章）给出了长达数十年之久的 NIST/ANSI 椭圆曲线“种子”从何而来的答案。Claude 并没有找到“给 Bob 和 Jerry 加薪”这类幽默密码短语的哈希值，而是发现这些种子是 lrand48 C 库 PRNG 的原始输出，并用 Unix 时间戳作为种子——P-256 种子是第 1842 个候选项，时间戳如 1997-08-16 01:52:34，而二进制的种子映射到 1984 年 2 月的日期，加倍后为 1998 年 4 月 17 日。文章还解释了六条曲线的基点，其中来自 Lenstra 的 FreeLIP 生成器，种子为 20000、20420、20507、20509、20512、20517，并指出 B-163 以及 P-224/P-256/P-384/P-521 的基点仍无法解释；36,864 美元悬赏金捐给了 ISRG。

**Discussion**: 最大的分歧在于这一发现是否真的排除了蓄意弱化。fidotron 认为这没有证明任何东西：生成发生在数月之内，被丢弃的候选项不可见，也没有人披露时间戳作为证据，因此不能排除存在“未披露的选择标准”。Filippo Valsorda 反驳说，即使 NSA 在 1997 年尝试了每一个时间戳，挑选可行的候选项也只会带来大约 30 比特的自由度，而一类可随机撞上如此多弱曲线的弱 256 位曲线，将构成与 ECC 被攻破同等量级的突破——随后像 tptacek 这样的密码学评论者也指出，Koblitz 和 Menezes 在 2015 年就提出了同样的论证。几位评论者都赞同，对于使用同一个 PRNG、由时间播种的代码而言，“没有解释的跳过”和可疑之处都在意料之中；有人（cyphar）指出，被跳过的大多是不产生有效曲线的 n 值。一个大型并列话题是 Anthropic 的防护措施：一些评论者说，他们曾因与密码学、bio 或“C&C 服务器”相关的问题被降级或拒绝回答，而 sweis、tptacek、Retr0id 和 dogben 都表示，他们或其他人曾尝试过这个确切的种子问题，并没有被阻止——他们据此主张，开放权重模型才是答案。较轻松的话题包括多年来关于后门和 NSA 的阴谋论，以及 Jerry Solinas 的轶事遗产：sweis 说，他找了四位消息源（其中两位是前 NSA 人员）核实了那个密码短语的故事，结果大家都错了。

**Worth reading**
- [FiloSottile](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50125469): Filippo Valsorda 计算得出，即使完全尝试所有 1997 年的时间戳，加上挑选可行候选项，也只会得到约 30 比特的自由度，因此任何基于种子来蓄意安装后门的理论都必须假设 ECC 已经被攻破。
- [nullc](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50128379): nullc（libsecp256k1 的合著者）认为，发现这个方案并不能使 NIST 曲线变得“刚性”：它仍然留有 20–25 比特的自由度，外加方案选择上的自由度，因此偏好刚性参数的理由并未改变。
- [sweis](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50124233): sweis 报告了他实际提交的 FOIA 请求（NIST 以没有记录为由关闭；NSA 无视），并说他不确定如何让请求足够具体以得到答复。

## 4. You could have invented transformers from memory {#story-4}
*#6 on HN · [alexwang.ai](https://alexwang.ai/posts/associative-memory/) · 241 points · 63 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50122878)*

A blog post on alexwang.ai by Alex Wang (HN user ketothekingdom) that builds an intuition for LLMs as associative memory stores, framing each architecture by its interference-to-signal ratio and capacity. It walks from a linear outer-product memory W = Σ vᵢkᵢᵀ (capacity ≈ d_k) to the least-squares optimal linear store, which whitens keys via (KᵀK)⁻¹ and roughly doubles capacity to ≈ 2d_k, then to MLPs, which suppress low-similarity keys with a nonlinearity, and finally to self-attention, which it claims can store a potentially unbounded number of memories. With 64-dimensional keys the post cites a jump from about 64 memories to about 2,700 to over a million, and it includes interactive animations (with a kea bird as mascot) plus an aside that a single gradient-descent step on a linear layer is itself a memory write, citing Irie, Csordás & Schmidhuber.

**Discussion**: The thread splits between praise and a long fight over accessibility. Many readers call it one of the best-written explainers they have seen, and several admire the animated kea mascot; others enjoy the framing that AI memory is a communications-channel problem (interference-to-signal ratio, capacity), with one EE saying they had Gemini Argon translate the piece into DSP terms. The largest subthread is craftkiller's complaint that the article is unhelpful to a programmer audience because it uses math notation without concrete data structures or a worked example, and that pseudocode and one traced insert/query would reach more HN readers; saghm, Nevermark and soulofmischief push back that HN's stated remit is anything gratifying intellectual curiosity, that the mathematics is the content, and that the author's choice of notation is his prerogative, while mrandish partially sides with the critic, saying the approachable intro led them to expect the main idea before notation. craftkiller (who was heavily downvoted) argues the downvotes prove HN is hostile to feedback and that his position was misread. Separately, a reader asks what the values are in an LLM's attention, and gets a clarification that Q, K and V are all X times learned matrices, with K/V forming a similarity-keyed dictionary and softmax giving a weighted sum rather than a single hit — which several note is the actual link to outer-product memory, not an exact dict lookup.

**Worth reading**
- [wilkystyle](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50126542): Answers the stalled question of what keys and values mean in an LLM by spelling out Q = XW_q, K = XW_k, V = XW_v and the dictionary analogy with similarity-based lookup and a weighted sum of values.
- [craftkiller](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50127950): The sharpest version of the accessibility critique: the problem is not the math itself but undefined data structures and no worked example, likening notation-without-context to being handed a Japanese article while native speakers say 「just learn Japanese」.
- [kccqzy](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50127355): Points to the author's actual code behind the post's plots (KeAWang/associative-memory-blog, crosstalk.py) for readers who want to run the interference experiments themselves.

## 5. California is building a $400M shield for Yosemite's most dangerous highway {#story-5}
*#7 on HN · [sfchronicle.com](https://www.sfchronicle.com/california/article/yosemite-national-park-highway-140-22463847.php) · 32 points · 20 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50088937)*

An SF Chronicle feature by Rachel Swan on Caltrans' project to build a 675-foot rock shed over Highway 140 at the Ferguson Slide, the 2006 landslide that buried the road and forced a two-bridge bypass with traffic lights and 5–10 minute waits. Work began this summer; the vaulted concrete arch, made of 6,000-ton segments fitted around a road curve, is due to open around 2031 at a cost of roughly $400 million and is designed to survive a 100-year lifespan and being buried 40 feet deep by a rock avalanche. Locals interviewed welcome it but worry about slides elsewhere on 140 and about this winter's expected strong El Niño; the piece also recounts the road's 1926 opening, when the dedication motorcade suffered 300 flat tires from gravel laced with railroad spikes.

**Discussion**: The dominant reaction was that the article oversells the novelty: commenters point to comparable rock sheds and avalanche galleries across the Alps, British Columbia and the Big Sur coast, some linking photos, and argue that the Mountain West has handled this for decades — one calls the delay 「incompetence」 rather than drama. A second thread turns on cost, asking what a real tunnel would cost in Europe or China; one reply defends the $400M figure on the grounds that hard rock, thermal cycling and impact resistance make this a different problem from London's soft-ground tunnels, and suggests comparing to European rock sheds instead, while others predict overruns to $2 billion and swap stories of decades-long US infrastructure timelines, including the PATH station at the World Trade Center going from $2B to $4B+. A UK example — £100M for a 900m tunnel built mainly to protect bats — is offered as a cost benchmark. Side threads complain about the paywall (with an archive link posted) and one commenter's attempt to frame highway building in the American West as fascism, which others mock. No real consensus beyond agreement that slides on 140 are a real problem and that the schedule is likely optimistic.

**Worth reading**
- [defrost](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50128011): Argues the $400M is not directly comparable to European tunnel costs: hard rock and impact/shock resistance versus London's soft soils, so European rock-shed projects are the right benchmark.
- [collabs](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50128135): Cites the PATH station at the World Trade Center ballooning from $2B to $4B+ as evidence that US project timelines and costs are structurally broken, not the fault of environmental consultants alone.
- [fancyfredbot](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50128292): Gives a concrete UK cost comparison: £100M for a 900m tunnel whose main purpose was protecting bats, at presumably lower structural requirements.

## 6. FLUX 3 Image Klein {#story-6}
*#8 on HN · [bfl.ai](https://bfl.ai/blog/flux-3-image-klein) · 150 points · 40 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50123580)*

Black Forest Labs released FLUX 3 Image Klein, a 5.6B-parameter open-weights model for text-to-image, editing and multi-reference generation (up to 10 reference images), claiming native 4K output, bounding-box control and pixel-precise edits. BFL says internal evals put it ahead of larger open models (Qwen-Image 2.1 at 7B, Krea 2 at 12.9B) and above closed Ideogram V4.5 and FLUX.2 [pro], gaining 91 Elo in text-to-image over FLUX.2 [klein] 9B with 38% fewer parameters. It runs in INT8 on 8GB of VRAM, makes a 1MP image in ~4s on an RTX 5090 and under 0.6s in NVFP4 on a GB300; weights are under the FLUX Kommunity License (free commercial use only below $5M annual revenue) and the API starts at $0.009 per 1K image.

**Discussion**: The thread is overwhelmingly negative on the model's human anatomy: multiple people who ran it locally report half-melted faces, bad fingers and 「body horror」, with one calling it worse than its own predecessor FLUX.2 [klein] 9B and another comparing it to SD 1.5. Commenters largely blame aggressive NSFW filtering and safety fine-tuning stripping out anything anatomical, an explanation the thread treats as likely but not proven — one tester notes the failure reproduced through OpenRouter hitting the official BFL API, not just a local setup quirk. A minority is more positive: the editing capabilities are called excellent and the high-resolution stylized output pleasing, and some suggest the separate prompt-upsampling model is required for decent results, though others find that hit or miss. Discussion drifts into the licensing erosion across open image models (praise for Apache-2.0 MOSS Image and Boogu Image), the price gap versus Krea 3 at $0.06/image, and a long tangent on why small diffusion models seem to compress world knowledge better than LLMs.

**Worth reading**
- [vunderba](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50125288): A local tester says the stock ComfyUI template produces SD 1.5-level half-melted faces and calls the model 「practically lobotomized」 compared with FLUX 2 Klein 9B, with a sample image.
- [minimaxir](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50124336): minimaxir ran his usual human-generation test through OpenRouter (i.e. the real BFL API) and it failed on quality and finger proportions, linking the Reddit threads alleging NSFW-driven degradation.
- [jjcm](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50124379): A counterpoint from practice: editing is 「absolutely excellent」 while fine text detail disappoints, body horror wasn't reproduced, and MOSS Image (Apache 2.0) is recommended for text-to-image with sample images.

## 8. Monte-Carlo Simulations {#story-8}
*#17 on HN · [eli.thegreenplace.net](https://eli.thegreenplace.net/2026/monte-carlo-simulations/) · 98 points · 20 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50076089)*

Eli Bendersky 的博客文章用三个例子介绍蒙特卡洛方法：用投掷飞镖估算 π（他指出约需 100 亿个样本才能可靠地精确到小数点后 4 位，误差随 1/√N 下降）；估算 SET 纸牌游戏中随机抽 12 张牌没有「set」的概率——Knuth 的 setset-all 精确枚举出 2,284,535,476,080 种无 set 牌型，占 C(81,12)=70,724,320,184,700 总数的 3.23%，而他跑 1000 万次模拟得到 0.0323，15 张牌的情形约为 0.00037；以及退休金提取模拟：100 万美元、每年取 30,000 美元、4% 收益/2% 通胀的朴素算法显示钱能撑约 55 年，但基于历史分布抽出收益与通胀、跑 100 万次模拟后，约 25% 的情景在 22 年内耗尽资金，能撑满 30 年的概率只有约 60%。代码已发布在 GitHub。

## 17. What was a Token Ring network? {#story-17}
*#33 on HN · [dfarq.homeip.net](https://dfarq.homeip.net/what-was-a-token-ring-network/) · 109 points · 83 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50118318)*

A retrospective post on retro-tech blog dfarq.homeip.net explaining IBM's Token Ring LAN — the ring-topology, token-passing alternative to Ethernet — from its origins (credit given to Datapoint, the office-automation pioneer whose 8-bit Datapoint 2200 led Intel to the 8008/8080/x86 line) through 4 and 16 Mbit/s IBM deployments, MAUs and structured cabling, to its defeat by Ethernet. The linked text could not be retrieved (it came through as binary garbage), so the article's specifics are known only from lines HN commenters quote: that coax connectors existed for Token Ring but were more common on Ethernet, that a 16 Mbit ring brought down the whole network if a 4 Mbit node joined, and the author's claims that Ethernet won because it was cheaper and because of scale, that cheap-option Ethernet was junk, and that higher-speed Token Ring (roughly 100 and 128 Mbit) existed but was rarely deployed.

**Discussion**: The thread is mostly nostalgia and war stories, opening with people noting the sadness of the past tense in the title — Token Ring was taught as a mainstream technology in 1990s CS courses, CompTIA and Microsoft Networking Essentials exams, and even on England's GCSE curriculum, though many commenters never saw it live. Several practitioners confirm it stayed in production longer than expected: banks, hospitals (a hospital still being wired for Token Ring in 1998), Merck's late-90s migration, and IBM offices that needed Ethernet-over-Token-Ring adapters. Commenters push back on the article's framing: kjs3 argues the single reason Ethernet won was cost and that higher-speed Token Ring claims (notably 128 Mbit) look like LLM hallucination, while others add that IBM bet on ATM for scaling rather than Token Ring; baud9600 cites early Ethernet studies putting usable utilisation at ~37% under contention, making 4 Mbit Token Ring's guaranteed access a fair marketing argument despite lower raw speed. Technically minded posters note token passing gave deterministic access that underpins rate-monotonic scheduling theory and survives in token-bucket rate limiting, and that a virtual token ring is used by the 2019 10BASE-T1S automotive Ethernet standard, while one commenter objects that ARC156 Token Ring is still deployed in 2026 in Automated Logic's WebCTRL building-automation systems. Rough consensus: Token Ring lost on price and ecosystem, not on design, and the discussion is more valuable than the article for detail.

**Worth reading**
- [kjs3](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50120358): A blunt critique of the article's accuracy from someone who worked with the gear: Ethernet won on cost and chip simplicity, smart MAUs made the speed-mismatch problem moot around the same time as UTP hubs, 100 Mbit Token Ring barely shipped because IBM pushed ATM instead, and the 128 Mbit Token Ring claim smells like an LLM hallucination.
- [Animats](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50125766): First-hand account of exotic coax baseband networks: Apollo Domain's RCA-phono-plug token ring, thick and thin Ethernet, Hyperchannel and DECNET triaxial, and why non-differential baseband coax needed RF-engineer-level knowledge and never suited general offices.
- [quickthrowman](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50120669): Corrects the article's past tense: ARC156 (ARCNET) is still used in 2026, including Automated Logic's WebCTRL building-automation system, with a vendor PDF as evidence.

## 20. /dev/null: social network for developers {#story-20}
*#40 on HN · [dev-null.sh](https://dev-null.sh) · 146 points · 129 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50121004)*

/dev/null (dev-null.sh) is a Twitter-style social network for developers by Matthieu Napoli (mnapoli) and Nuno Maduro (nunomaduro), reportedly built with zero lines of code existing 48 hours before launch during a two-day livestream grind. It is a Laravel backend with a TypeScript frontend, authenticates users exclusively through GitHub OIDC, and recasts posts as commits in a shared log: your first commit is approved manually, later ones by an AI moderator, and the pitch is 「no likes, no reposts, no algorithm」. The fetched page is not an article but a scraped feed of launch-day posts (Taylor Otwell cheering Laravel, the authors livestreaming, PR links), and the source is open at github.com/nunomaduro/dev-null.sh, which commenters say drew 200+ PRs in under 48 hours.

**Discussion**: The dominant complaint is the GitHub-only login: one camp argues GitHub is simply the most widely adopted developer identity provider and avoids the anti-spam and password-leak problems of email/password, while others call it 「evil」, ask what a self-hosted or corporate developer is supposed to do, and note that requiring a public GitHub profile is a Silicon-Valley-brained definition of a 「serious developer」. Many complained the landing page explained nothing and all content sat behind the login wall — the authors (mnapoli) jumped into the thread repeatedly to say it was fixed, admitting the no-likes claim was wrong since posts have stars, and readers confirmed the site improved. Pedants spent a lot of thread on the name (dev in /dev/null is device, not developer) and suggested /tmp/social, /dev/tty0 (「it's federated」), and dev-null.social instead of .sh; someone flagged the public sitemap URLs as a security issue and was corrected that users and commits are intentionally public, which sparked a tangent about X and Facebook locking their sitemaps down. A large subthread drifted to Mastodon and the fediverse — praise for it as home of the 「real nerds」, an argument about mas.to's strict defederation policy, and complaints that feeds there are now dominated by anti-LLM posting — while a smaller group dismissed the whole thing as yet another Twitter clone (「y.a.t.c」) and preferred Bluesky or Mastodon.
