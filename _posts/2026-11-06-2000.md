## 1. US FAA says it will phase out floppy disks from air traffic control by 2028 {#story-1}

这是一篇路透社的报道（原文无法抓取，返回 HTTP 401，因此以下内容依据标题和评论转述）：FAA 宣布将在 2028 年前，把空中交通管制中仍在使用的软盘淘汰掉。评论者补充说，涉事系统是 IDS-4，运行 MS-DOS、使用 3.5 英寸软盘（有人指出它实际诞生于 1990 年代初，而不是新闻所说的 1980 年代），整个替换项目属于 EIDS 计划，预算被提到约 120 亿美元。官方给出的紧迫理由之一，是 10 月 13 日休斯敦乔治·布什洲际机场外一条光纤被挖断，导致数据服务中断并触发地面停飞。

**Discussion**: 讨论主要分成两种立场。一方以曾在 FAA 做过 NOTAM 数字化工作的承包商为代表：他认可 FAA 的安全风险管理（SRM）流程本身，但说它没有反向的制衡力量，常常变成硬性阻塞，审核委员会熟悉旧系统、不理解新系统，风险判断失真；反对者则认为航空规则是「用血写成的」，在事故率已经极低的情况下，任何变更本身都会引入风险，举证责任在提议变革的一方。另一条争论线围绕那条被挖断的光纤：多人最初嘲讽 FAA 没有备份，随即被纠正：断掉的正是备份线路，主线路当时在检修，属于「瑞士奶酪」式多重失效，有人据此论证需要三重冗余而非双冗余。关于软盘本身，多数人同意磁介质和驱动器早已停产、备件只能靠 eBay 拆机 scavenge，这种供应链风险本身就是安全隐患；但也有资深从业者强调，真正卡住升级的往往不是软盘，而是关键路径上那些无法升级的 1980/1990 年代老机器。不少人还提到，空管员至今仍用纸质飞行进程条和木钉，替换难度远超外界想象，并对「云端 21 世纪系统」的可靠性和政治化宣传表示怀疑。

## 2. Tesla to wind down Cybertruck production {#story-2}
*#2 on HN · [nbcnews.com](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/tesla-wind-cybertruck-production-years-poor-sales-markets-rcna607817) · 571 points · 1,276 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50391456)*

A Reuters story (via NBC News) reporting that Elon Musk said Tesla will wind down Cybertruck assembly at its Austin gigafactory around mid-2027, freeing capacity for the Cybercab robotaxi. US Cybertruck sales fell to 20,237 in 2025, down 48% from 38,965 in 2024, against Musk's pre-launch prediction of 250,000 a year; Musk called it the 「best vehicle Tesla has ever made」 and the shutdown the 「end of an era」. Analysts quoted (Andy Palmer, Felipe Munoz) argue electric pickups broadly flopped in the US and that the truck's stainless-steel wedge could never pass European pedestrian-impact rules, while Cybertruck lead engineer Wes Morrill says the platform 「lives on through Cybercab」.

**Discussion**: The top thread is not about the shutdown at all: a owner-adjacent commenter describes a friend running his house off a Cybertruck for 14 days after Hurricane Helene by driving to a Supercharger to refill, which set off a long debate. Dozens of replies point out this is generic V2H/V2L, not Cybertruck-specific — Ford's F-150 Lightning did it first, and Kia EV9, Ioniq 5, BYD and even a Jeep 4xe can do the same — while others argue a $500–$8k propane or diesel generator is cheaper and more reliable when roads, chargers and grid are down. On the product itself, commenters split between 「glad someone took a swing」 (comparing it to the DeLorean, praising 48V, steer-by-wire and unpainted steel) and those who call it an ugly, impractical non-truck; a recurring argument is that price, not aesthetics, killed it — a $40–50k version would have sold — countered by the observation that $65k+ F-series trucks sell in far higher volume. A second recurring thesis is that the Cybertruck was really a low-volume testbed for Tesla's next-gen electrical architecture that got scaled up before product-market fit, and many use the news to question Tesla's future with only Model 3/Y left, a Cybercab still answering NHTSA questions, and BYD dominating outside the US. The Musk-politics angle is everywhere, with some commenters objecting that the thread's 「I don't like Elon, but…」 framing is itself tedious.

**Worth reading**
- [brightball](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50394025): First-hand account of a Cybertruck powering a whole house through a 14-day post-Helene outage by shuttling to a Supercharger to recharge — the story that seeded the entire vehicle-to-home subthread.
- [crawshaw](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50396106): Argues the ignored explanation is price: the truck was designed for an era of inflated, zero-interest-rate truck prices, and at $40k it would have found a much larger market.
- [x3haloed](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50394105): A cancelled pre-order holder lists exactly why: delivered range came in far below the announcement, the price rose, the stressed-skin exoskeleton became glued-on panels, and Tesla's politics became a reason not to buy.

## 3. First steps in bare-metal programming using UEFI {#story-3}
*#5 on HN · [kevinboone.me](https://kevinboone.me/efitest_index.html) · 110 points · 7 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50394855)*

这是 Kevin Boone 撰写的一组系列文章的索引页，讲的是如何在 Linux 工作站上开发 UEFI 固件 PC 的裸机应用。目标读者是有经验的 Linux 开发者，但假定读者此前不熟悉 UEFI；系列内容包括所需工具、编译与链接、制作可启动的光盘或 U 盘，以及使用 Simple File System 协议做基本文件管理，其中已列出第 2 至第 5 部分。文章明确要求按顺序阅读，后篇依赖前篇。

**Discussion**: 评论整体比较正面：一位用 Zig 写 bootloader 的开发者说这份指南是很好的起点，但提醒 EFI 环境里的 UTF-16 字符串和各类「尖锐边角」让它和普通 Linux 开发相差很远。有人为这些怪癖提供了背景和补丁：指出 edk2-libc 可以补上缺失的 C 标准库，并说明 UEFI 的「Windows 味」源自 Intel Boot Initiative → EFI → UEFI 的演进过程。另一位资深评论者则强烈反对这种「沿用」的说法，认为 UTF-16 和 VFAT 在 1998 年和 2005 年就已经是不合理的选择。同一位评论者还提出 Multiboot2 可作为自己构建 PE32+ 镜像的替代方案，并分享了一个只用 289 行头文件、借助 LLM 生成的 UEFI 输入监视示例程序。

**Worth reading**

- [kragen](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50396963): 提出 Multiboot2 作为替代路径（仍可调用 UEFI 服务），并给出自己用 Claude 生成 289 行 UEFI 头文件和 inputmon 示例、在 QEMU+OVMF 下运行的第一手经验。

## 4. We know 15k websites are infected. how do we tell them? {#story-4}
*#6 on HN · [scotthelme.co.uk](https://scotthelme.co.uk/we-know-15-579-websites-are-infected-how-do-we-tell-them/) · 72 points · 48 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50396064)*

Scott Helme of Report URI says his threat-intelligence crawlers have identified 15,579 sites — conservatively 135 million visits a month — running malicious JavaScript: Magecart card skimmers, ClickFix fake-captcha malware lures, iOS spyware exploits (DarkSword, Coruna) and redirects to phishing pages. The infection vectors he lists are WordPress-style direct compromise, infected third-party supply-chain scripts, XSS, and 「DeadTag」 (a group buying expired domains that sites still load scripts from). He publishes no site list, says security.txt uptake is low and most outreach emails go unanswered, and asks CERTs/NCSCs and buyers of commercial feeds to mail intel@report-uri.com; he has no scalable notification plan.

**Discussion**: The dominant suggestion is to hand the list to browser vendors and Google Safe Browsing so users get an interstitial — protecting visitors first and forcing owners to notice via traffic drops or complaints; several people note this is exactly how they discovered their own site was hacked, and one asks the obvious follow-up: why aren't these already flagged? A second camp wants the list published outright (a haveibeenpwned-style lookup, or a plain text file sorted by traffic), arguing this isn't 「responsible disclosure」 at all since the harm is already happening and secrecy only protects reputations; others object that a public list of known-vulnerable sites is a target list for attackers, and one warns that charging owners for details reads as extortion. The sharpest pushback is over the definition of 「infected」: a commenter argues that if third-party analytics scripts count, the blacklist would cover most of the web, while others reply that a visitor entering card details doesn't care whether the bad JS was first- or third-party. Contact-channel advice centers on whois, abuse@, admin@, legal@ and contact pages — with a side argument about whether GDPR or pre-existing privacy-proxy services killed whois — and small-site operators doubt any of it reaches a human. A few ask Report URI what detection tooling they use, and how they'd handle sites with no findable contact at all.

**Worth reading**
- [sublinear](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50396805): Argues the 「infected」 criteria are dangerously vague — if a compromised third-party analytics script counts, a blacklist would take out most sites people use daily, since almost every site loads at least one third-party script.
- [athrowaway3z](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50397247): Strong counter-argument: 「responsible disclosure」 is being misused — the sites are already infected, so publish the URLs with proof and auto-update as they get fixed, accepting the consequences.
- [testplzignore](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50396955): Cuts through the disclosure framing with an analogy: responsible disclosure fits a pickable lock, not watching a house being looted from across the street — just put a text file on the blog.

## 5. Blender's UI and Runtime: Much Faster Than GPUI {#story-5}
*#7 on HN · [github.com](https://github.com/ppmpreetham/BUI) · 600 points · 244 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50389333)*

BUI 是一个把 Blender 界面层（控件绘制、交互规则、主题外观）抽出来做成独立 C 核心 UI 库的项目，提供 Python（tkinter 全量 API、Qt 风格、45 个 Blender 组件）、Rust、JavaScript/React/Svelte、C/C++ 和 Dear ImGui 绑定，无需安装 Blender 也不需要浏览器或 webview。README 声称按需重绘、空闲时 0% CPU，相比 gpui-fast 每帧少 2–4 倍 CPU、首帧约 5 ms，打包成单文件可执行程序为 6.3–6.9 MB，首次渲染约 70 ms、19 MB 内存。项目自述绘图代码是从 Blender 的 interface_widgets.cc 移植的 C 代码，仓库声明“完全由 LLM 编写，无人类手写代码”，并因源自 GPL 的 Blender 而只能以 GPL 发布；已知缺失无障碍支持、输入法和系统原生外观。

**Discussion**: 讨论明显分成两派：一方（最上面的评论）夸 README 第一行就说清了是什么、为什么该关心；另一方则认为整篇 README 是典型的 LLM 文风——「super duper fast」「4. The many ways in」这类措辞、大段与代码片段无关的截图，让人读几段就放弃评估，作者本人回应说他不是让 AI 生成，而是让 AI 从 Blender 里“删掉非 UI 逻辑”，并称“AI 擅长删除而非生成”。许可证是第二个焦点：多人指出 GPL 让这个库对大多数闭源或非 GPL 项目不可用，作者解释说因为派生自 Blender 源码，无法改成 LGPL，只有重写才行；也有人认为这对 Blender 这类应用合理，用来做通用库则是硬伤。技术可信度上，有人实测网页 demo 后逐条列出失败项（跨多个数值框拖动多选编辑、Ctrl+C/V、Shift 精调、UI 缩放、折叠面板、右键重置、Tab 切换、快捷键、提示气泡），作者承认网站上的示例只是 mockup 并随后更新；Rust GUI 作者则反驳「比 GPUI 快」的说法，给出自己移植 JS framework benchmark 的数据，显示 GPUI 在多数字幕场景落后于 Dioxus Native、Iced、egui 和 Slint。此外还有关于 Blender UI 本身好不好用（baby duck syndrome、3ds Max/Softimage XSI 之争）、是否需要原生控件和系统主题、以及 GIMP/Inkscape 是否该采用它的长串闲聊。

**Worth reading**
- [Lockal](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50392376): 有人按 Blender 用户的标准逐条测试网页 demo，列出跨字段多选编辑、Ctrl+C/V、Shift 精调、UI 缩放、折叠、右键重置、Tab 跳转、快捷键加速键等十项失败，认为这更像是套了 Blender 皮肤的 imgui，而非真正的 Blender UI 移植。
- [nicoburns](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50392548): Rust GUI 库作者贴出自己移植 JS framework benchmark 的实测表格：在 create 1k、update、select row、swap/remove row 等项上，GPUI 普遍比 Dioxus Native、Iced、egui、Slint 慢，直接反驳了“GPUI 最快”的前提。
- [ppmpreetham](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50391349): 作者本人回应许可证质疑：因为代码派生自 GPL 的 Blender 源码，无法改用 LGPL，除非全部重写。

## 6. Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash {#story-6}
*#8 on HN · [blog.google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/introducing-gemini-omni-1-2-flash/) · 245 points · 192 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50392118)*

A Google DeepMind product post announcing Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash, a video generation/editing model that now makes native 15-second clips (up from 10 seconds in 1.1), settable from 3 to 15 seconds in one-second steps. It claims stronger reference adherence and character/product consistency, multi-turn conversational editing of a clip, better lip-sync and multi-speaker dialogue, and a 30% price cut to $0.07 per second of 720p video, with general availability on the Gemini API and Gemini for business plus rollout in Flow, Google Vids, the Gemini app (Ultra subscribers) and Google Ads, with SynthID watermarking. Google also admits current weaknesses in fast sports action, rapid script changes and complex physics.

**Discussion**: The thread barely discusses the model: the top comments are a pile-on about how impossible it is to find, buy and use Google's AI products, with users citing mysterious payment errors, identity verification demands, Workspace accounts stuck on older models, crippled trial-plan limits and endless Cloud console steps — with OpenRouter, Vercel's AI Gateway and Antigravity as the workarounds people actually use. A smaller camp pushes back hard: the plans are just Google One subscriptions, OpenAI and Anthropic naming is no clearer, and their Gemini/AI Studio/GCP setups work fine, which prompted accusations of astroturfing from both directions. The handful of people who actually used the video model are more measured: a film-maker reports it has been live in Flow since the day before and that Flow's credit cost went up (18 credits for six seconds vs 10 for 1.1) even as the API got cheaper, and finds quality only marginally better; another long-time AI video maker says 15 seconds helps but the model still can't hold idiosyncratic character designs without first and last frames. Others flag naming confusion (Omni's 1.x numbering sits alongside Gemini 4) and note the page's own text-to-speech reads 「dropped the price 3%」 instead of 30%.

**Worth reading**
- [msabalau](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50393388): A film-maker who used it in Flow the day before says the character-consistency claim is the real draw but that it costs more, not less, in Flow and is not obviously better in limited testing — and that creatives use Flow, not AI Studio or the API.
- [fuzzythrowaway2](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50394641): Someone making AI video since early 2024 argues the core problem is unchanged: models still won't follow direction precisely, and without first/last-frame control, fat, ugly, muscular or non-human character designs regress to bland commercial looks.
- [msabalau](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50396438): Concrete pricing correction from Flow: Omni 1.2 shows 18 credits for a six-second video versus 10 credits for Omni 1.1, so the 30% API cut coincides with a cost increase in Google's own creative tool.

## 7. Size-It-Up (Game) {#story-7}
*#10 on HN · [magnitudle.com](https://magnitudle.com/size-it-up) · 31 points · 12 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50395869)*

Size It Up 是 Magnitudle 出品的每日视觉尺寸估算游戏：每轮给出一个目标物体的剪影，玩家把它缩放至在已知参照物旁边看起来尺寸正确，锁定后揭晓误差。每天五轮，每轮满分 100 分，总分 500 分；误差越小得分越高。网站还附有每个剪影的素材来源与授权记录，以及「谁在玩」的排行榜页面。

**Discussion**: 主流意见认为玩法有趣，但参照物选得不好：有人说自己从没见过发电站涡轮转子，只能靠蒙，而像鸡蛋、篮球架、送货车这类日常物品就好得多；还有人指出某一轮直接给出了某台德国核电站涡轮的具体型号，一查就知道答案，反而让游戏失去意义。有人反驳说“这就是游戏的一部分”，也有人认真回应“美国中心”的批评——一位美国用户表示自己同样没见过猫鼬、袋熊或新生熊猫幼崽，认为这正是游戏的用意。多名手机用户反馈手指拖动只会平移、无法改变大小，一位用户给出正确操作：一根手指拿起物体，再加第二根手指缩放，并有人建议加入触屏操作说明。另有建议给每轮加个人形参照物，或做一版天体尺寸的变体。

**Worth reading**

Commenters dug out that Wero's own support page states custom ROMs such as LineageOS or Pixel Experience cannot install the app, and that the Play Integrity call lives in ASN/de Volksbank code (nl.devolksbank.mobile.banking.security.data.wero.IntegrityRepository#requestIntegrityToken) with the token sent to de Volksbank's own servers.

**Discussion**: The thread splits between people who see this as normal risk management and those who see lock-in. The defence is liability: Dutch banks are on the hook for fraud losses they could theoretically have prevented, so they eliminate perceived risk cheaply; others counter that the same banks happily serve stock Android phones years behind on security patches, which suggests the motive is not really security. A technical fight dominates the back half — commenters argue EPI's claim that hardware attestation cannot prove app or OS integrity is simply wrong, since the attestation certificate carries the challenge, verifiedBootState, the verified boot key hash, and the app's package name plus signing digest, and GrapheneOS publishes its trusted key hashes in a signed JSON. There is disagreement about remedies: some want EU-mandated open banking APIs, others note PSD2/PSD3 is nominally open but gated behind licensing and feature-poor, and a few point out that the EU's own digital identity wallet also leans on Play Integrity. On the ground, commenters report Rabobank also blocks GrapheneOS (though some get it working via Aurora Store), ING only warns, Triodos still works, N26 blocks new signups but keeps existing users, and UK's Starling has fixed GrapheneOS breakage. The one recurring correction is that 「ASN」 here is the bank, not ASN.1.

**Worth reading**
- [microtonal](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50391298): microtonal rejects uattest.net as a fix, calling Unified Attestation an effort by vendors with years-old kernels that has campaigned against GrapheneOS, and argues banks should simply trust a list of signing keys such as grapheneos.org/attestation.json.
- [microtonal](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50391093): Corrects the claim that Dutch police cracked GrapheneOS: the reseller had built his own fork with MDM, lacked upstream security updates, and had likely compromised signing keys, so phones were unlocked over ADB.
- [skrebbel](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50393189): Points out the inconsistency at the heart of the security argument: GrapheneOS is rejected while stock Android missing three years of patches is accepted, so the banks cannot actually believe this is real risk.

## 20. Scriptable GeoDNS: Leaving AWS for Bunny {#story-20}
*#31 on HN · [vincent.bernat.ch](https://vincent.bernat.ch/en/blog/2026-bunny-geodns) · 41 points · 15 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50394292)*

Vincent Bernat explains moving his blog's geo-aware DNS from AWS Route 53 to Bunny DNS, which is nearly free (up to 500 domains) and runs user-supplied JavaScript on each query. He runs seven small NixOS VMs (Paris, Nuremberg, Ashburn, Singapore, San Jose, São Paulo, Sydney) and a script returns the two closest servers via Bunny's RoutingEngine.getClosestServer() helper, falling back to the second if the first is down. He documents the failure of an HTTPS/SVCB record (RFC 9460) approach — Chrome ignores records whose target is not 「.」 and Bunny doesn't return glue for the target names, forcing a second lookup — and settles on returning one server with a 2-minute TTL when the second is more than twice as far and over 1000 miles away. Rough edges remain: Bunny can't publish DS records (so the luffy.cx zone lives on deSEC and the ACME zone runs on Hickory DNS in Rust), the CDN lacks HTTP/3, and the closest-server helper is IPv4-only; the bill dropped from about US$5 to US$1 a month.

**Discussion**: The pricing comparison is the loudest thread: one commenter notes the NTP Pool serves roughly a million DNS queries per second at its hourly peak with a small old Go program called geodns, and says paying US$0.70 per million at cloud rates for ~11 billion queries a day would be ruinous for a volunteer project. Several people praise Bunny's scriptable DNS as underrated, and a sub-thread corrects the article's Terraform setup: CDKTF was deprecated in December 2025, though Bernat points to a community fork, CDK Terrain (cdktn.io), and others say LLM agents now make hand-written HCL less painful anyway. A sidebar debate asks why not anycast; a commenter with ten years at an anycast CDN explains the trade-offs — hop count isn't latency, you can't easily split traffic from one network across datacenters, and load management means withdrawing advertised IP blocks per site — while Bernat says self-run anycast is complex and BGP-close can be a continent away. One skeptic questions who this is actually for, since running a blog on seven servers worldwide isn't a realistic default; others just marvel at the setup and the post's interactive map and animations.

**Worth reading**
- [cortesoft](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50396993): A decade of operating an anycast CDN: hop counts don't reflect real latency, traffic engineering needs constant monitoring and fake-path padding, and balancing a single network across sites requires withdrawing IP blocks per datacenter.
- [askbjoernhansen](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50395556): Concrete scale counterpoint to the pricing: the volunteer-run NTP Pool handles about a million DNS queries a second at peak with a small Go program, which Route 53's per-query pricing would make unaffordable.
- [vbernat](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50396042): Notes that CDKTF was deprecated in December 2025 and points to the community fork CDK Terrain at cdktn.io.
