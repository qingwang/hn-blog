## 1. Three new discoveries in mathematics, computer science, and physics {#story-1}
*#1 on HN · [openai.com](https://openai.com/index/building-scientific-understanding-with-academic-communities/) · 815 points · 356 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50251344)*

An OpenAI post announcing three model-produced results and a new release process in which external experts act as shepherds: mathematicians get the raw AI writeup and Lean proof, the CS group (led by Russell Impagliazzo, Ramamohan Paturi and Ryan Williams) releases proof and Lean formalization immediately, and the physics group led by David Gross, Michael Douglas, Arthur Jaffe, Igor Klebanov and Nikita Nekrasov holds back the AI artifacts to write it up themselves. The claims: a proof of Sogge's local smoothing conjecture in every dimension (hence Kakeya and Stein's restriction conjecture in all dimensions, via a novel auxiliary PDE, obtained Oct 18 2026 and Lean-formalized the next day); a deterministic algorithm exactly counting satisfying assignments of a k-CNF in time 2^(n/(log n)^(1/2+o(1))) that refutes ETH (natural-language disproof Oct 16, Lean Oct 17); and a rigorous four-dimensional existence proof for interacting gauge theories including QCD, plus pure Yang–Mills — the existence half of the Millennium problem, with no mass gap.

**Discussion**: The thread splits between approval of the changed process and suspicion of motive: several commenters say reaching out to Tao and to the field's own authors is the bare minimum of academic courtesy and that the shepherding model still concentrates information, especially in physics where five named people decided to sit on the output, while others call the whole thing a publicity stunt after the previous math drop. The most substantive disagreement is over what refuting ETH means: practitioners point out the bound is subexponential but superpolynomial, so it breaches a somewhat arbitrary boundary without collapsing the exponential hierarchy, and that ETH was load-bearing for hundreds of hardness results — one commenter cites Scott Aaronson moving his P=NP credence from 3% to 5%, while another argues the update should be larger. On physics, working theorists are split between calling a rigorous QCD existence proof a lifetime-scale result and noting that having a path-integral measure while saying nothing about which functions are integrable gets you neither mass gap nor confinement, with others guessing the unified completion is the experimentally dead SU(5) Georgi–Glashow model rather than the real Standard Model. A recurring meta-point: AI is crushing formally verifiable domains (Lean gives a cheap reward signal) and will move much slower in wet-lab or scruffy sciences where verification costs days per iteration.

**Worth reading**
- [jltsiren](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50252939): A complexity theorist argues the 2^(n/polylog n) bound is subexponential, so polynomial-time reductions inflate instance sizes and the result leaves intuition about complexity classes roughly where it was — a huge result, but not a step toward P=NP.
- [abetusk](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50252781): Quotes the actual theorem from the released preprint: polynomial-space, deterministic counting of k-CNF satisfying assignments in (n+m)^{O_k(1)}·2^{n/(log n)^{1/2+o(1)}} time, i.e. superpolynomial but subexponential.
- [auntienomen](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50253757): A theorist's caveat on the Yang–Mills claim: the proof gives a path-integral measure but no characterization of integrable functions, so mass gap and confinement remain untouched — existence only makes those questions well-posed.

## 2. The Mythical Man-Month in the Age of AI {#story-2}
*#2 on HN · [j11g.com](https://j11g.com/the-mythical-man-month-in-the-age-of-ai) · 34 points · 21 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50255298)*

A short blog post by j11g.com arguing that Brooks's Law — adding manpower to a late software project makes it later — has not been repealed by AI agents, only mutated. The author's proposed 2026 rewrite: adding agents to a project with unclear requirements gets you the wrong answer faster. The core argument is that coordination overhead shifts from managing people to managing agents — someone still has to define done, review code, catch hallucinations and stitch the pieces together — and that the nine-women-one-baby constraint still applies to sequential work.

**Discussion**: The sharpest disagreement is over whether fast wrong answers are a bug or a feature: one camp argues that generating a clearly-broken proof of concept in an hour for $4 of tokens is the cheapest way to discover requirements, while others counter that this only works if verification is nearly free and if failed attempts actually reveal the right answer — which isn't always true. Several commenters push back on the article's central metaphor, noting the book's actual phrasing is about nine women not making a baby in one month, and questioning whether an “agent” maps to a “man” at all, since subagents in modern harnesses are not independent worker units. Practitioners split on the productivity claim: one says nine Claude Code instances can build a decomposed application unprecedentedly fast, while others report that open-ended multi-file changes generate review work that cancels the speedup, and that supervising agents is more cognitively draining than writing the code. The rough consensus is that the bottleneck moved to verification, design coherence and human understanding, not typing.

**Worth reading**
- [videotextly](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50255548): A solo developer argues the real cost is coordination and understanding rather than typing, so small well-bounded tasks with tests work while sprawling multi-file changes create review work that eats the speedup.
- [noduerme](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50255819): A dissenting take that delegating coding to agents is tedious and degrading — that it feels like reasoning with something intelligent but trains you to accept answers without checking them.
- [jurgenburgen](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50255791): A blunt counter to the idea of letting models review models' code: as long as a human is accountable, they have to understand the code, and 「I told the LLM to make it secure」 won't survive a breach.

## 3. The Exponential Time Hypothesis Is False [pdf] {#story-3}
*#3 on HN · [github.com](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/preprints/The-Exponential-Time-Hypothesis-is-False-October-25-2026/paper.pdf) · 576 points · 207 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50251661)*

The link is a GitHub-hosted OpenAI math preprint (the page itself returned only GitHub sign-in boilerplate) titled “The Exponential Time Hypothesis Is False.” Its claimed result is a deterministic algorithm that exactly counts satisfying assignments of a k-CNF formula on n variables in time (n+m)^{O_k(1)}·2^{O_k(n log log n/√log n)} and polynomial space, which would make 3SAT subexponential and refute ETH. Commenters say a Lean formalization and a verification effort led by Impagliazzo, Paturi and Williams accompany it.

**Discussion**: Commenters treat the claim as a major theoretical result while insisting it is not a proof that P=NP and has no near-term practical use; a self-described SAT-solving expert calls the algorithm galactic, and multiple people quote the paper’s caveat that 2^{o(n)} bounds do not survive general polynomial-time reductions, so NP as a whole need not be subexponential. Much of the thread is corrections of that misconception (and of claims that the result proves NP ≠ EXP), with patient explanations that a polynomial reduction can inflate n enough to erase the gain. On P vs NP, opinion splits: Scott Aaronson is quoted raising his probability of P=NP from 3% to 5%, while others say the result provides no real evidence either way. Cryptography worries — public-key systems, NSA backdoors — are raised, but Ryan Williams is quoted saying OpenAI checked existing cryptosystems and found them safe, and others note the algorithm is far too slow at real key sizes. Some commenters question the lookup-table construction, the paper’s length and whether the Lean proof has been properly reviewed; others note that Anthropic has a preprint claiming similar bounds and debate the centralization of mathematical research in AI labs.

**Worth reading**
- [bsubs](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50253117): A self-identified SAT-solving expert says there are no immediate applications because the paper’s results are galactic, though its ideas might later snowball into practical methods.
- [mudita](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50252719): Scott Aaronson reportedly responded to the news by raising his personal probability of P=NP from 3% to 5%.
- [JonChesterfield](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50253174): A dissenting commenter rejects the proof’s framing, objecting that one construction enumerates all SAT answers in a lookup table with more states than the observable universe and saying they do not accept the axioms.

## 5. 16-bit Windows (Windows 1.x, 2.x, 3.0, 3.1, etc.) on 64-bit Windows {#story-5}
*#9 on HN · [github.com](https://github.com/otya128/winevdm) · 80 points · 9 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50253529)*

A GitHub project, otya128/winevdm, that is an altered port of the winevdm 16-bit Windows emulator targeting 64-bit Windows; you drag a Win16 executable onto otvdm.exe (or run otvdmw.exe), or run install.inf to register it so Win16 binaries launch directly. Since 64-bit Windows refuses LDT modification (NtSetInformationProcess with ProcessLdtInformation always errors), it instead uses Wine-derived Win16-to-Win32 conversion code, convspec-autogenerated relay routines, 16-bit-to-native HANDLE conversion, and hijacks Windows' own hook for redirecting 16-bit installers. It also redirects legacy INI writes away from %WINDIR% and can run DOS executables (VDMDOSVER variable), though DOS emulation is described as incomplete and DOSBox is recommended instead.

**Discussion**: The thread is mostly nostalgia and practical reports rather than skepticism: one commenter installed MS Office 2.0 through it and used it for a week, finding it fine for email-grade word processing and confirming that Word 365, LibreOffice and Google Docs all open the files it produces — only the dictionary files failed to be recognized, and another user agreed such a setup is enough for letters, CVs and school reports. The same commenter then raised a security worry that old 16-bit file formats are probably exploitable, which nobody rebutted. Others are in it for games: one wants Civilization 2 on Windows 11 and notes recent WINE on Linux already runs 16- and 32-bit apps, while a reply points out Civ2: Test of Time has dedicated patches that avoid external tools. The one real correction is that winevdm has been around for a while, tempering the framing of this as new 16-bit support; a subthread about a Windows 3.1 word processor that 「never crashed」 drew a flat 「you must have lived in a different reality」.

**Worth reading**
- [haunter](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50255484): First-hand test: MS Office 2.0 installs and runs, is adequate for everyday email writing, and its .doc/.rtf output opens in Office 365, LibreOffice and Google Docs — but its dictionary files aren't picked up.
- [bitwize](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50254232): Corrects the framing: winevdm has existed for some time, so this 64-bit support isn't brand new.
- [gabmartini](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50255044): Adds a Linux counterpart: current WINE runs his copy of Civilization 2 without trouble, 16-bit and 32-bit included.

## 6. Cross-request KV cache exposure in the GLM-5.3 Workers AI model (155 responses) {#story-6}
*#11 on HN · [blog.cloudflare.com](https://blog.cloudflare.com/kv-cache-exposure-remediation/) · 125 points · 40 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50252159)*

Cloudflare 的事后复盘：2026 年 8 月 27 日至 9 月 9 日，其 Workers AI 上的 GLM-5.3 端点（@cf/zai-org/glm-5.3）有 155 个响应混入了其他客户的数据。原因有两处叠加：他们把模型配成 1,048,576 token 上下文，但 prefill GPU 上的 KV cache 池只能装 681,280 token，且上线前就知道这个不匹配——他们指望引擎直接报错；而 SGLang 在 disaggregated prefill/decode 模式下会把超长请求标记为拒绝、换成单 token 占位符，却仍照常做 prefill 和 KV 传输，decode 节点于是把此前请求留下的 7 页陈旧 cache 当成本次内容来生成，返回 HTTP 200 而非错误。Cloudflare 在 9 月 8 日收到 Knostic 的 Gadi Evron 报告后 40 分钟内下线模型、当日修复（池扩到约 204 万 token 并打了引擎补丁），并建议所有 SGLang 运营方升级到 v0.5.22 或更高版本。

**Discussion**: 讨论大半被标题之争占据：一位长期评论者认为这是 Cloudflare 的配置错误加 SGLang 的 bug，与模型无关，标题把无辜的 Z.AI 拖下水，应改成「Cloudflare 配置错误在用户会话间泄露数据」；Cloudflare 员工 kflansburg 回应说文中从未称这是模型 bug，标题写的是「GLM-5.3 Workers AI model」只是为了让客户知道哪个端点受影响，但多位评论者不买账，也有几人认为技术读者不会误读。其次是关于上下文窗口的技术质疑：有人追问既然池只有 681k 为何对外宣称 1M，另一位评论者补充说触发条件是单次 prefill 超过 681k token，多轮编码会话累积到 1M 通常没事，所以这种情形很罕见；也有人直言这就是明知有溢出风险仍上线的缓冲区溢出式配置，且测试只看 HTTP 状态码和吞吐、不看输出内容，才漏掉了这个 bug。多条子线程转向亲身经历，有人说在 ChatGPT 或某家 API 上收到过完全无关的回复，甚至怀疑曾拿到别人的响应，由此推论共享推理的多租户隔离普遍脆弱，敏感数据应跑在非共享主机上。整体共识是复盘写得清楚、响应快，但对「明知不匹配仍上线」和标题措辞批评居多。

**Worth reading**
- [kami23](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50252432): 认为这份 RCA 罕见地坦承自己的测试缺口（只验证状态码和性能、不验证输出连贯性），并肯定下线速度。
- [segmondy](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50255271): 从推理部署角度质疑：KV 池装不下就不可能服务那么长的上下文，猜测他们赌的是用户不会接近 600k token。
- [reassess_blind](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50254223): 提供同类多租户隔离事故的亲身案例：某浏览器测试服务的 iOS 设备之间互相泄露剪贴板，粘贴出了别人的凭据，报告后只得到一封感谢信。

## 7. Solar power is becoming bigger than nuclear {#story-7}

Others describe off-grid solar spreading without permits in the Philippines and Pakistan, and argue official generation totals undercount it. **Worth reading**

The author states plainly that it is not a drop-in replacement: fewer metrics, must be rebuilt against each NGINX version, and far less battle-tested; building requires --with-compat and --with-http_stub_status_module.

**Discussion**: The thread starts with the claim that NGINX's open-core model is why first-party Prometheus support never shipped, but that gets pushback from a commenter who says NGINX metric-export modules existed long before Prometheus and that the Prometheus pull model is inefficient anyway. The bigger argument is about whether anyone should still run NGINX at all: several say their circles have moved to reverse proxies written in Rust (with jokes about Lean 4) and that writing a small Go proxy is preferable to deploying NGINX, while others call that a bad decision for most people since hand-rolled gateways accumulate bugs, missing features and security holes, and that NGINX or Envoy should be the default. One reply concedes Go's stdlib gives a solid HTTP base but lists the real hazards — HTTP/2 and 3 to 1.1 translation, forwarding client IPs safely, header sanitization — where configurable proxies win. A dissenting view holds that NGINX has stagnated for 15 years (no metrics, no ACME) and that Traefik was the escape route.

**Worth reading**
- [lstodd](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50252149): Claims to have written two NGINX metrics-export modules himself, in 2009 and 2013, and argues Prometheus-style pull export is inefficient and unused by anyone serious.
- [Thaxll](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50252405): Strong counter-argument to the roll-your-own trend: custom gateways bring bugs, missing features, security problems and non-standard integration, so NGINX or Envoy should be the default.

## 20. The EPA Is Quietly Killing Its Web Archive {#story-20}
