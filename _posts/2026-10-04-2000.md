## 1. Run Qwen 3.8 Flash Next (125B) on consumer hardware (RTX 4090) at 100T/s {#story-1}
*#1 on HN · [github.com](https://github.com/Niko1221/Strata) · 387 points · 210 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49953495)*

Strata is an MIT-licensed open-source inference engine (github.com/Niko1221/Strata) that runs Qwen3.8-Flash-Next — a 125B-parameter MoE model with 24,576 experts, about 10 active per token — on consumer GPUs with 12GB+ of VRAM. It combines expert caching (most-used experts stay in VRAM, the rest live in system RAM, with a lookup table on SSD) with speculative decoding (claimed 1.6-1.8x) and batched prompt processing up to 8,192 tokens at over 1,000 tok/s; the README claims roughly 100-140 tok/s generation on an RTX 3090/4090. It ships several quantized variants down to Q2_0 and IQ2_XS, plus a Coder model with half the MoE experts removed that reaches 91% of the full model's SWE-bench Verified score, and exposes OpenAI- and Anthropic-compatible APIs on localhost:8080.

**Discussion**: The thread is dominated by the quantization trade-off. Skeptics argue that 2-3 bit quants and expert pruning gut quality for long-horizon work — one commenter flatly says Q2 is 「not interested」 and that 4-bit is the floor — while defenders report 3-bit IQ3_XXS/IQ3_S quants running within a point of the unquantized model on the DeepSWE harness and beating Qwen3.8-27B dense. The most concrete challenge to the article is a first-hand 50-image vision benchmark where Strata's median coordinate error was 154.8 pixels versus 46.5 pixels running the identical GGUF and vision adapter on llama.cpp, a gap the commenter calls as large as going from a 9B to a 35B model. Commenters also debate whether the headline speed is useful in practice: several report only 7-30 tok/s on 8-10GB cards, while others confirm 80-125 tok/s on 3090/4090/V100 systems. Two recurring side threads cover why llama.cpp hasn't merged expert caching (ggml's AI-contribution policy and limited review capacity) and the security of the README's 「paste this into your coding agent」 install flow and curl-pipe-bash startup scripts.

**Worth reading**
- [Jackson__](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49956534): Jackson__ ran a 50-image vision coordinate benchmark and found Strata's median error (154.8px) far worse than the same GGUF and vision adapter under llama.cpp (46.5px).
- [bitexploder](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49955352): bitexploder says a 3-bit XXS quant of Flash Next is neck-and-neck with Opus 4.7 and Sonnet 5 on the official DeepSWE harness, arguing selective quantization now works.
- [0xbadcafebee](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49953945): 0xbadcafebee argues low quants plus removing half the MoE experts are fine for quick tasks but unreliable for long-horizon agentic coding — 「there's still no free lunch」.

## 3. Car is a smartphone on wheels. Here's who's listening {#story-3}
*#3 on HN · [automatictransmission.khoury.northeastern.edu](https://automatictransmission.khoury.northeastern.edu/) · 187 points · 109 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49954882)*

An interactive report from Northeastern's Khoury College (“Automatic Transmission”) produced with Consumer Reports, which instrumented 21 US-market vehicles and 30 companion apps between October 2024 and August 2025 (using a Raspberry Pi AP, tcpdump, a Faraday tent for 11 EVs, and mitmproxy on rooted iPhones). Its headline findings: 19 of 21 vehicles contacted at least one third-party advertising/tracking domain over Wi-Fi, 7 of 30 apps sent sensitive identifiers (VINs, emails, phone numbers, precise location) to ad/tracking third parties, and pairing a companion app roughly doubled a car's exposure — sometimes adding 20+ new trackers. Honda was the notable exception, tightening its data collection to stop sending precise geolocation to a tracking-linked third party; the team estimates independently procuring the fleet would have cost about a million dollars.

**Discussion**: The top-voted critique, from randomhacker123, is methodological: the study logged destination domains over Wi-Fi but not packet contents, and counted the car's own YouTube app talking to YouTube as one “ATA” contact, so cars with fewer infotainment apps — or that tunnel traffic through the manufacturer — score artificially better; pnw agreed there was no evidence automakers transmitted anything beyond ordinary browser traffic. Much of the thread then drifted into a broader fight over mass surveillance, whether “pro-surveillance” people really exist, and whether the stance is authoritarian across the political spectrum, with some calling the stereotype of rural right-wingers unfair. Practitioners contributed the most concrete material: an Audi owner's GDPR request showed the car kept sending every navigator destination to Audi even after telemetry, location sharing and remote access were disabled; an Acura Integra owner described an opt-out that leaves a permanent yellow nag icon; and another commenter said a real kill requires cutting all power to the DCU (up to three sources, sometimes with a backup battery) because Bluetooth use or dealer service can upload a backlog. On mitigation the rough consensus is pessimistic — no viable market exists for stripping a modern car's connectivity because it's too deeply integrated and features like live traffic are wanted, so people stick with pre-2015 cars, and one Tesla owner notes monitoring is mandatory to even get Tesla insurance. The story is also a repost, discussed two days earlier.

**Worth reading**
- [randomhacker123](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49955934): Reads the paper closely and argues its domain-level methodology inflates the tracker counts — YouTube opened in the car counts as an ad/tracking contact — and links the paper PDF and the vendor-classification repo.
- [hecturchi](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49955366): First-hand GDPR request to Audi showing the car kept transmitting every navigation destination to Audi long after telemetry and location services were supposedly disabled.
- [devmor](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49956494): Explains that in-car “disable data sharing” often isn't real, since Bluetooth or a dealer visit can flush a backlog, and truly cutting it means removing all power from the DCU.

## 4. Glashütte Trash Clock – A 30-minute pendulum clock made from trash {#story-4}
*#8 on HN · [niklasroy.com](https://niklasroy.com/gtc/) · 125 points · 17 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49930439)*

Niklas Roy documents building a working pendulum clock out of discarded material found in Glashütte, Saxony — cardboard, zip ties, tape, a folding ruler and a paperclip escapement — during an artist residency at watchmaker NOMOS in 2026. The clock runs only about 30 minutes, shows seconds and minutes, strikes a glass-bottle gong at 12 o'clock, and triggers a scaffold-mounted NOW/NEVER wheel of fortune just before the weight hits the floor. Roy video-tracked the pendulum and found a GTC 「second」 averaged 1.004 s (±0.001), i.e. accurate to within 0.4%, and the piece doubles as the reference clock for a satirical new time scale called GTC.

**Discussion**: Commenters mostly receive it as charming, resourceful engineering-as-art, with several praising the write-up and the paperclip escapement; one subthread jokes that it is 「a good analogy for German engineering」 and another quips that 「Europe is a country with great potential」. The biggest substantive tangent is about time standards: one commenter argues the post buries the lede that the 2022 General Conference on Weights and Measures decided to abolish the leap second by 2035, noting Google's leap-second smearing has multiple incompatible algorithms and that even a negative leap second was never implemented. Another replies that the cleaner fix would be to keep atomic time everywhere and instead shift civil timezones, rather than inserting leap seconds at all. Glashütte itself drives several side threads — the New Yorker's watch-collecting feature, NOMOS fandom, the region's silver-mining-to-horology lineage, and its Cold War history (Wismut uranium mining and the Free Republic of Schwarzenberg). One reader also points out a hidden touch: the paperclips in the page margins follow and interact with the mouse cursor as you scroll.

## 5. Show HN: AI search for every photo and every frame of video on macOS {#story-5}
*#9 on HN · [github.com](https://github.com/allenv0/SCM) · 91 points · 51 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49952111)*

SCM 是一个免费、开源的 macOS 应用（v0.2.4，通过 Homebrew cask 分发，面向 Apple Silicon / macOS 12+），可对任意文件夹中的照片和视频帧进行本地优先的 AI 搜索。它通过 ONNX Runtime 使用 CLIP 系列视觉模型（默认权重约 435MB 的一次性下载）进行语义照片/场景匹配，使用 Tesseract 进行 OCR（英文 + 35 种可切换语言），使用 Whisper tiny.en/base.en 进行对话转写，使用 ffmpeg 镜头检测实现逐场景搜索，并提供可选的 llama.cpp sidecar，用于基于已提取文本的带引用聊天。所有推理均在本地运行——不注册账号、不上传、不收集遥测——媒体会被复制到由应用管理的库中，并附有内容哈希去重和命名的嵌入快照。

**Discussion**: 最热门的子讨论认为，在 macOS 上该应用应当使用 Apple 的 Vision 框架而不是 Tesseract 进行 OCR；一位评论者表示他们曾用相同提示测试 Claude、DeepSeek、Qwen 和 Codex，发现这些模型都推荐 Vision，只有 GPT-4.1 会推荐 Tesseract。这演变成关于「vibe coding」的辩论：批评者认为生成的代码与最佳实践来自不同的分布，因此由 LLM 构建的应用更差，而另一些人则反驳说，规划/批评以及精选的专家数据集能产生更好的结果。另一条支线讨论了著作权和「sherlocking」，有几位评论者指出著作权保护的是代码文本，而不是功能。实践者补充了具体经验：一位曾在 M1 上构建 CLIP 搜索的人表示，帧采样率是决定性成本因素（以 1 fps 处理 12k 个视频需要数天，仅关键帧才能缩短到一夜）。还有几个未回答问题：是否支持人脸/宠物搜索、索引大型媒体库需要多长时间，以及为什么不使用小型 VLM 如 Qwen-VL。也有替代方案被提出，例如 Immich 和 Apple Photos 的设备端搜索。

**Worth reading**
- [postalcoder](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49953995): 认为 Apple 的 Vision 框架在速度与准确率上都优于 Tesseract，并补充说测试过的 LLM（Claude、DeepSeek、Qwen、Codex）都推荐 Vision，只有 GPT-4.1 推荐 Tesseract。
- [hn3ufz62f7](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49953375): 有在 M1 上构建 CLIP 视频搜索的直接经验：采样率决定一切——以 1 fps 处理 12k 个视频需要数天，而仅关键帧可以在一夜之间完成。
- [radicality](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49955861): 尖锐批评认为该项目是未审查的 LLM 输出，并指出特定的 screenshot-probe.js 文件存在边界情况 bug，从而质疑 CLIP 模型选择。

**Discussion**: The thread splits sharply between admirers and detractors. Commenters also correct a common misreading: the book argues meta-consciousness, not intelligence, may be maladaptive, and at least one reader warns the thread is full of spoilers. ## 8. The Heilbronn Problem {#story-8}
*#15 on HN · [math.tejstead.com](https://math.tejstead.com/heilbronn/) · 49 points · 8 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49939637)*

The linked site is a collection of best-known configurations for the Heilbronn problem: place n points in a unit-area container (square, triangle, or optimal convex region) so that the smallest triangle determined by any three points is as large as possible — maximizing A(n). Each entry has exact coordinates, symmetry and congruence analysis, references to published proofs, and an in-browser rational arithmetic verifier; records are truncated to 8 decimals, entries marked ▲ are proven optimal, and new records ship via an Atom feed. Recent improvements are credited to contributors including Alexandar Lackovic (with help of Opus 5.5) and Marc-Emmanuel Coupvent des Graviers; the author says the list currently stops around 35–36 points only because of computational limits.

**Discussion**: The main thread is a math discussion led by arutar, who explains the asymptotics: a simple pigeonhole argument (split the square into n/3 vertical strips) shows some triangle must have area ≲ 1/n, while the best known lower bound is only about (log n)/n² — a huge gap. The Komlós–Pintz–Szemerédi 1981 result gave an upper bound near n^(-8/7), which stood for over 40 years until MIT students Alex Cohen and Dima Zakharov with Cosmin Pohoata improved it by a small factor and then to n^(-7/6). arutar also links the problem to incidence geometry, and describes two related variants: k-gons instead of triangles, and a point–line minimum-distance problem recently solved by Cosmin, noting that point–line results directly yield the best known Heilbronn asymptotics. Site author tejstead engages directly, thanking dang for a second-chance queue spot, sharing the GitHub repo (github.com/tejstead/heilbronn-site) and an animation for square n=16, and inviting amateur submissions via PR; when asked why the list of best-known values isn't infinitely long, he attributes the 35–36 point cutoff to computational limits rather than any formal criterion.

**Worth reading**
- [arutar](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49955150): Lays out the asymptotic bounds in detail — the 1/n pigeonhole upper bound, the (log n)/n² lower bound, Komlós–Pintz–Szemerédi's n^(-8/7), and the recent n^(-7/6) improvement by Cohen, Zakharov and Pohoata — with a Quanta link.
- [arutar](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49956277): Describes two related open problems (k-gons, and point–line minimum distance), explains why 'trivial incidences' are required, and shows how the point–line result implies the best known Heilbronn asymptotics.
- [tejstead](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49954856): The site author confirms the post came via a second-chance queue, links the GitHub repo, and points readers to an animation for the square n=16 entry.
