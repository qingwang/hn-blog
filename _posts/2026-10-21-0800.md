## 1. The Future of CSS {#story-1}
*#1 on HN · [joshwcomeau.com](https://www.joshwcomeau.com/css/interop-2027/) · 232 points · 87 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50175070)*

Josh Comeau's October 2026 blog post walks through the ten CSS features he upvoted for Interop 2027, the cross-vendor effort by Google, Apple, Microsoft, Mozilla, Igalia and Bocoup that Mozilla is now letting developers rank to decide next year's focus areas. He argues Interop reliably drags a feature to near-universal support within a year — subgrid shipped in Firefox in 2019, entered Interop 2023, and was everywhere by year's end — and previews the candidates: @function CSS functions with a result keyword, corner-shape (squircle, bevel, notch, scoop), border-shape, Level 2 anchor positioning container queries, an inherit() function usable inside calc(), scroll-triggered animations, reading-flow/reading-order for tab order matching visual layout, and interpolate-size plus calc-size() for animating to auto. Most of the shipped ones are, at time of writing, only in Chrome 155 and Edge 154.

**Discussion**: The thread splits hard between people delighted with modern CSS — nesting, @layer, @scope, :has(), light-dark(), clamp(), grid and flexbox have made vanilla HTML/CSS/JS pleasant again, and several say they now delete JavaScript in favour of CSS — and skeptics who think the language is bloating into JavaScript with worse syntax. Objections to @function are the sharpest: some call it a needless can of worms, others say the awkward declarative syntax is the price of a parser fast enough that pages render without a scripting VM. A separate camp complains the post is all eyecandy (bevels, squircles) while date pickers, touch-keyboard handling and printable pages remain genuinely broken, with one commenter saying he now benchmarks layout techniques against plain HTML tables. Corrections land too: all the featured features being Chrome-only is exactly why they are Interop candidates rather than a done deal, and the subgrid history reads less kindly to Chrome than the article's phrasing suggests. Recurring side arguments cover tree-shaking CSS, the AI-generated-frontend wave, and whether browser-frozen features are better than replaceable frameworks.

**Worth reading**
- [boxed](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50176795): Corrects the article's diplomatic framing of subgrid's history: it was Chrome that refused to move for years, not a vague lack of vendor momentum.
- [fodkodrasz](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50176887): A practitioner's counterargument that CSS keeps shipping decorative border shapes while fundamentals like date pickers, numeric inputs and touch-keyboard avoidance still require custom JS, making the web worse than Windows Forms for real work.
- [agstudio](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50176161): Argues CSS has become a programming language trapped in obsolete stylesheet syntax, with no standard static type checker (stylelint being the closest) to catch errors at build time.

Permit Pending 是一款浏览器中的城市规划模拟游戏，由 Peter Cai 制作，把经典城市建造游戏换成了市长视角：你是当选市长，而非全能之神，每个公寓楼、公交车道和工厂都要经过听证会、审查、反对意见、诉讼和上诉。底层经济模拟被描述为真实：居民有年龄、收入和住房，企业会招聘、成长和离开，土地价值、租金、就业、税收和公共服务相互联动；七人议会需要争取选票，投票率高的群体影响力超过其人数。游戏没有胜利条件，存档留在本地，可离线运行，使用 Three.js、React 和 Vite，图表为自研，无路由或状态库。

## 4. In Hilbert space, all things are quantumly possible {#story-4}
*#4 on HN · [quantamagazine.org](https://www.quantamagazine.org/in-hilbert-space-all-things-are-quantumly-possible-20260826/) · 67 points · 33 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50175185)*

A Quanta Magazine explainer on how John von Neumann, at 23, used a 1927 trilogy of papers to recast quantum mechanics in Hilbert space: quantum states become vectors in an abstract possibility space, and Heisenberg’s matrix mechanics and Schrödinger’s wave mechanics become two coordinate choices for the same object. It explains that a Hilbert space need only be complete and admit an inner product, that its dimension equals the number of possible outcomes, and that von Neumann later soured on it, writing in 1935 that he did not believe in Hilbert space anymore and turning to von Neumann algebras. The piece closes with the dispute over whether Hilbert space is fundamental, as Sean Carroll argues, or a pragmatic tool, as Jonathan Sorce maintains.

**Discussion**: The thread spends more energy on the headline than on the article: djsjajah objects that only “potentially possible” things are possible, illustrating with the interval [0,1] containing no 3, and others reply that one can relabel or map numbers so that 3 appears, or that this is a confusion about abstraction. A separate argument over drawing a rational from [0,1] pits LPisGood’s “probability zero does not mean impossible” against voxl’s claim that any effective sampling procedure only reaches computable reals, with fc417fc802 calling that a limit of physical computers rather than of the mathematics. The map-versus-territory strand splits commenters: one says Hilbert space is just a useful abstraction, another insists it is the territory and the classical world is the map. Several also flag the article’s von Neumann quote from 1935, surprised he had that intuition before QFT, AQFT and the Unruh effect were formulated.

**Worth reading**
- [kdavis](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50176123): kdavis calls von Neumann’s 1935 rejection of Hilbert space remarkable because it predates the Unruh effect, AQFT and most of QFT, and credits it to von Neumann being von Neumann.
- [voxl](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50175980): voxl challenges the claim that probability zero is not impossibility, arguing any effective sampler on [0,1] is confined to the countable computable reals — a claim fc417fc802 disputes as a physical, not mathematical, limit.
- [lolakutty](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50176155): lolakutty offers a direct mathematical rebuttal to the [0,1]-has-no-3 argument: map every number above 1 into the interval and 3 can be represented after all.

## 7. Good type against all odds {#story-7}
*#9 on HN · [unsung.aresluna.org](https://unsung.aresluna.org/good-type-against-all-odds/) · 88 points · 10 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50175059)*

A post by Marcin Wichary on his unsung.aresluna.org site collecting small acts of typographic craft found in unexpected places. Its central example is a display whose faux-italic slant is not a font choice at all but an artifact of line-by-line screen refreshing — the same effect as rolling shutter in photography — which raises the question of whether the designer chose left-to-right scroll and rightward slant or just inherited a default. The rest is a gallery: hand-picked small caps, a train carriage drawn in a 5×7 pixel font, a stadium icon built from four characters, a vertically ligatured 「Gorton」 carved into plastic, three-dimensional underlines, a slanted NOT, and hyphenation and an old-fashioned contraction on a typesetting machine and a keyboard.

**Discussion**: The thread is almost entirely appreciation — commenters single out Wichary's eye for craftsmanship in mundane objects, the photography and editing, and say they upvote anything he writes about typography or keyboards. The one real argument is over his claim that right-leaning italics are merely customary: one commenter insists the slant is mechanical, forced by quill and fountain-pen nibs that cannot push upward in the backslash direction plus the wrist pivot of a fast right-handed writer, and notes the slash predates the computer-age backslash for the same reason. A reply pushes back with Yiddish handwriting, where a right-to-left script still slants the same way, arguing that makes the direction look arbitrary after all — no consensus is reached. Side notes include a recommendation of the film Chronovisor for typography nerds and surprise at a 24×2 HD44780 character display, since only 16×2 and 20×4 are common.

**Worth reading**
- [weinzierl](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50176815): Argues the rightward italic slant is not custom but physics: quill and fountain-pen nibs cannot shove upward in the backslash direction, and a fast right-handed wrist pivot adds forward slant — hence the slash is centuries old while the backslash is a computer-era invention.
- [ihm](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50175859): Counter-example to that theory: Yiddish is written right-to-left yet its handwriting slants the same way, which would not be expected if the slant were purely a writing-mechanics artifact, so it may just be arbitrary.
- [LoganDark](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50175860): A display enthusiast notes they had never seen a 24×2 HD44780 module — the common sizes being 16×2 and 20×4 — pointing at the specific hardware behind one of the examples.

## 8. Upcoming critical PHP security release (CVE-2026-109618 / -109619) {#story-8}
*#10 on HN · [news-web.php.net](https://news-web.php.net/php.announce/509) · 475 points · 179 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50172041)*

**Discussion**: 该讨论帖在一个原本按流程走的公告和一场恐慌之间分裂：多位运维人员报告说他们已彻底关闭 PHP 站点（WordPress、MediaWiki、基于 WPEngine 托管的站点），因为 PoC 和视频在 Twitter 上流传，而其他人则发布临时缓解措施，包括设置 session.upload_progress.enabled = Off、在 WAF/nginx/Apache 层阻止 multipart 请求，或阻止所有 POST 请求。这些缓解措施受到强烈质疑：多位评论者引用 PHP 核心开发者（Girgias）和一位发布经理的话称，只有打补丁才有效，不过一位系统管理员表示禁用上传进度后，在他自己的服务器上利用代码失效了。关于「是否可轻易利用」也存在分歧：一些人认为任何接受 POST 的默认 PHP 服务器都可被利用，一些人认为 CVSS 9.2 也并非轻易利用，而怀疑者用全新账号称其不可利用，则遭到了质疑。有几个帖子提供了直接的技术分析：一位评论者检查了公开 PoC 并认为它在 PHP 8.4 上可能并不完整，另一位评论者则认为禁用 system() 等纵深防御措施仍然重要，其他人反驳称 RCE 链根本不会停留在 PHP 函数调用层面。全场共识是：立即打补丁，或者下线服务，等待 14:00 UTC。

**Worth reading**

## 18. Launch HN: Respan (YC W24) – Open-source Rust AI gateway built around plugins {#story-18}
