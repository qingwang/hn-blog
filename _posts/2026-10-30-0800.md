## 2. Cuis: The Modern Smalltalk-80 {#story-2}
*#3 on HN · [cuis.st](https://cuis.st/) · 86 points · 13 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50255343)*

Cuis 是 Smalltalk-80 的一个免费、可移植的实现，其网站明确表示目标是简约、强大、快速且高效。Cuis 是从 Squeak 派生的环境，删掉了多余的“杂物”，最近发布了 7.8 稳定版。Cuis 社区还深度参与了 Smalltalks 2026 大会（10 月 14 日至 16 日，阿根廷罗萨里奥 UTN 的 Facultad Regional Rosario），大会 8 个演讲来自 Cuis 社区或展示用 Cuis 完成的工作，内容包括 Milan Lajtos 用编码代理构建 Blueprint 桌面实验、新的 Stream Deck 控制插件以及 Dybo 的改进。页面上还展示了 2007 年的一句赞美“nice and clean and simple and pretty”，评论区对此lots of乐。

**Discussion**: 最受欢迎的回答解释了 Cuis 的定位：它和 Pharo 差不多在同一时期从 Squeak 分叉出来，但 Cuis 做了更严格的精简，给出的类比是 Squeak = Debian、Pharo = Arch、Cuis = Alpine，因此系统内容更少、更容易探索和理解。一位用户报告说，自己在 Cuis 中比在 Squeak、Pharo、VA 或 Dolphin 中更快完成了工作，并正在用 Cuis 搭配 Pocketbase 和 lighttpd 构建一个基本静态的网站；另一个人称赞共享的 Squeak VM 意味着性能相当。另一个人称赞 Cuis 对 Morphic 的重构：所有内容都是硬件加速的矢量图形，连字体都是这样绘制，并且在缩放和旋转时仍然清晰。讨论随后转向两个方向：一个人问编码代理是否让它适合现代 AI 辅助工作流（没有直接回答），另一个话题则从 Smalltalk 的教育起源延伸到早期版本缺乏继承，以及继承是否真的是面向对象编程的核心——几位评论者说自己刻意避免继承。几位新来者说，相比其他 Smalltalk，他们觉得 Cuis 令人望而生畏的感觉更少、更受欢迎。

**Worth reading**
- [isr](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50299839): 用一个简洁的类比和具体技术细节说明各个 Smalltalk 的关系：Squeak=Debian、Pharo=Arch、Cuis=Alpine，并解释了 Cuis 的 Morphic 如何将一切都包括字体做成硬件加速的矢量图形。
- [edfsadfer](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50299704): 第一手经验：相比 Squeak、Pharo、VA 和 Dolphin，在删减过的“Squeak”中能更快更轻松地完成工作。
- [0xpgm](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50300484): 指出早期 Smalltalk 虽然自称“纯面向对象”，却缺乏继承，并质疑继承对于面向对象编程是否真如今天所认为的那样核心。

It renders real-time positions of stars, planets, constellation stick figures and moon phases as ASCII, Unicode or braille characters, and you can set the view to any date, time, latitude or longitude, or to a named city from a bundled GeoNames list. It ships through pacman (extra repo), Fedora 40+, Homebrew, Nix, Guix and prebuilt release binaries (Linux x86_64, macOS aarch64, Windows), and its data comes from the Yale Bright Star Catalog, Stellarium constellation figures and NASA JPL orbital elements; a README example reproduces the sky over Kennedy Space Center on the morning of the Apollo 11 launch, checked against Stellarium.

**Discussion**: The thread is small and mostly appreciative: the submitter calls the creative effort impressive regardless of practical use, and one commenter suggests these projects are a good way to stress-test terminal rendering performance. The most substantive exchange is about the README itself — one person holds it up as an unusually readable example that 「vibe coders」 should copy, while another replies that it looks fairly average and asks what specifically stands out, getting no detailed answer beyond a note that the ASCII output looks decent. The real pushback comes from a commenter who tried the example image: Capella, Rigel and Sirius are labelled but Orion is not legible, so he concludes this is not yet the terminal Stellarium he wants. Two comments are tangential jokes or plugs — an astrological CPU scheduler and an unrelated social-network project — and nobody in the thread reports running astroterm extensively.

## 6. We ran AI agent swarms in Minecraft and Mindustry {#story-6}
*#7 on HN · [labs.ycombinator.com](https://labs.ycombinator.com/projects/games/index.html) · 259 points · 88 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50296486)*

A Y Combinator Labs project page documenting experiments that put LLM agents — reportedly up to 50 at a time — into Minecraft (driven through the Mineflayer bot API) and the factory game Mindustry, with agents writing their own helper scripts and talking to each other while playing. The write-up, which was not retrievable in readable form here, is described by commenters as a log of timed runs covering cooperative survival, Bed Wars matches and a GPT-vs-Claude comparison. The memorable reported behaviours are social rather than strategic: a three-minute 「no blame」 inquiry after one agent ate a chicken meant for another, four agents spending 11 minutes appointing a 「sole fertilizer operator」 for a single bone meal, and GPT agents proving more aggressive than the more defensive Claude agents, which concluded after a tool bug that breaking blocks was impossible.

**Discussion**: The dominant reaction is amused recognition: commenters say multi-agent systems collapse into bureaucracy and bikeshedding, and several report the same from their own work — one builder of an agent-run small business describes crazy ledgers, endless analysis and a purpose-built date linter written after a minor time mix-up, another notes agents drowning in process within hours. A second camp argues the wrong architecture is being tested at all: latency of a full forward pass per action makes live games a poor fit, and OpenAI Five's Dota 2 run (LSTM-based, 99.4% of 42,729 matches) is cited as proof that purpose-built RL beats LLMs here — countered by those who say the LLM should do the macro planning while deterministic scripts handle the micro. Several people who watched the live stream say the harness itself, not the models, explains the sluggish, near turn-based play, with agents spending much of their time coordinating hand-offs and waiting on delayed inventory access. There is rough agreement that shared-brain swarms add throughput rather than intelligence, and that script-writing by agents is either the winning pattern or an admission that spatial and real-time reasoning is still weak.

**Worth reading**
- [MatthiasPortzel](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50297340): Argues Minecraft is a robotics problem rather than an intelligence problem — Mineflayer gives a limited, high-latency API where a human clicks several times a second — and notes Mineflayer was originally written by Andrew Kelley before he started Zig.
- [mplappert](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50300941): First-hand report that this exact pathology shows up in production multi-agent work: an agent running a small business produced crazy ledgers and a custom date linter after one time mix-up, with links to the write-up.

## 7. A tiny world model running games on a Raspberry Pi Pico 2 W {#story-7}
*#9 on HN · [github.com](https://github.com/SheepSeb/tinyworldformer) · 193 points · 12 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50295450)*

A GitHub project (tinyworldformer) by SheepSeb that trains two small networks and runs them entirely on an RP2350 microcontroller: a world model that takes the current frame plus an action and predicts the next frame and reward, and an actor that picks actions from those dreamed frames. The world model is a single transformer block applied 1–4 times with shared weights, quantized to int8 with per-row scales and QAT, and the actor is a Stream AC(λ) agent (Elsayed, Vasan & Mahmood 2024) with no replay buffer. The games are Breakout, Pong, Flappy Bird, Pac-Man and a raycast Doom-lite, each a few hundred lines of numpy; a decoding rule that places each singleton with one global choice lifted Breakout exact-frame accuracy from 91.6% to 99.7%, and the Rust no_std firmware uses two loops per frame on core 1 while core 0 handles USB and display.

**Discussion**: The thread is almost uniformly impressed, with the sharpest technical exchange about agents exploiting the world model's hallucinations — the author confirms a Breakout agent got extra bricks from corner hits and Doom-lite agents sniped from five cells away, fixed by adding noise to input frames while keeping targets clean (as in GameNGen). Commenters initially assumed one shared model for all games; the author clarifies each is a self-contained net but says merging them would be easy, and another commenter notes a single model dreaming across games would be far more impressive. On hardware, the author says the world model trained on an RTX 4060 while the playing agent learns directly on the Pico, and one subthread has to correct the assumption that a Pico 2 W is a general computer — the author adds the core is no_std Rust, buildable to WebAssembly but not WebGPU. A final thread argues the Pico framing slightly distracts from the world-model work and floats the approach as a better form of procedural generation for larger games.

**Worth reading**
- [sheepseb](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50297422): The author answers the hardware question: world model trained on an RTX 4060, but the game-playing agent trains directly on the Pico with Stream AC.
- [sheepseb](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50300834): The author lists concrete reward hacks the actor found (extra bricks on Breakout corners, cross-map Doom-lite kills) and the fix: noise on input frames with clean targets.
- [sheepseb](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50300773): Clarifies the technical stack: no_std Rust core with embassy-rs and no OS on the Pico, buildable for WebAssembly but not WebGPU — correcting the WebGPU question upstream.

## 8. Show HN: I Turned DOOM into a Transformer {#story-8}
*#11 on HN · [github.com](https://github.com/mottopanikeiku/doomformer) · 77 points · 23 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50297697)*

A GitHub project (mottopanikeiku/doomformer) that compiles integer neural networks into playable DOOM maps: voodoo dolls cross weight lines to sum, sector light levels and floor heights store values, and gate staircases act as activations, with ACS scripts driving the logic on UDMF maps under ViZDoom 1.3.2. Every computed value is read back and compared to a NumPy integer reference at tolerance 0 — the author reports 0 mismatches across 20,000 fuzzed nets, 496 CI tests against headless DOOM, a 1,355-doll MNIST map (248 exposed values, one digit in 273 tics) and a 1,345-doll marine brain that picks 50/50 actions exactly. The README is candid about costs and limits: roughly 23,000x slower than NumPy (the post says 65,000x), integer models only, no LayerNorm, no reinforcement learning, only executed inputs are verified, and showcased level names and seeds are hand-picked.

**Discussion**: Reaction splits sharply. Many commenters call it genuinely amazing — one points to their own classifier-driven DOOM playground, another says it could be a way to visualize neural networks if you already know DOOM deeply. A vocal minority dismisses the README as AI-generated word-vomit that is too hard to read; the author concedes AI helped with the docs and promises to edit them, while pushing back that nobody expects every doc line to be hand-typed. The most substantive critique is technical: targeting ZDoom/ACS makes the hard part trivial, so a Boom-compatible version and a table of required engine features would be far more interesting; the author agrees Boom support is a goal but says scaling up, not the ZDoom backbone, was the real difficulty.

**Worth reading**
- [mattmcal](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50299687): Argues that ZDoom's ACS makes the implementation trivial and that targeting Boom-compatible ports, plus a table of required engine features, would be the genuinely interesting contribution.

## 9. A Pig, the State, and War: Porco Rosso (Kurenai No Buta) (2014) {#story-9}

It traces the film back to Miyazaki's 15-page 1990 comic Hikotei Jidai in Model Graphics No. 14-16, notes his pre-release comments tying it to the Gulf War and the Yugoslav wars, and runs a nine-scene sequence analysis of dialogue from five characters (Porco, Gina, Fio, Donald Curtis, the Mamma Aiutos) through peace-research keywords such as non-killing, soft power and conflict prevention.

**Discussion**: Almost nobody discussed the paper itself; the thread is about the film. The biggest subthread is about the opening hostage scene, where commenters read Miyazaki's relaxed, unpanicked children as a rebuke to modern anxious parenting, with one arguing for a middle ground between free-range and indifference. A second dispute is whether the film is sexist: one parent told his kids it reflects how things used to be, others insist it depicts sexism rather than endorsing it, and one says the film actually toned the sexism down from its source. A long tangent on Princess Mononoke argues over whether Lady Eboshi is a villain, with most landing on Miyazaki's habit of giving every character a relatable motive. Elsewhere: praise for the Adriatic skies, a Seiko Porco Rosso watch link, a terse Italian complaint that Miyazaki reinvented the setting from photos into something alien, a cheap Pearl Harbor jab, and one commenter dismissing the paper as 「as scientific as sociology」 — met with the reply that nobody claimed it needed to be scientific.

**Worth reading**
- [lubujackson](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50300129): A visitor to Croatia recognised the summer Adriatic sky and sea from the film, arguing Miyazaki's rendering is unusually precise for someone who never went there.

## 10. Pico CSS is no longer maintained {#story-10}
*#16 on HN · [github.com](https://github.com/picocss/pico/discussions/754) · 110 points · 41 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50298964)*

The announcement on Pico CSS's GitHub discussion page says the repository was archived on Oct 4, 2026, making v2.1.1 the final release. The maintainer argues that a minimal CSS framework for semantic HTML matters less now that AI can generate lightweight, standalone, accessible HTML, and that staying current would require a full rewrite around plain modern CSS, OKLCH colors, cascade layers, Popover, anchor positioning and a native unused-CSS compiler. npm, jsDelivr and picocss.com are promised to stay online, the code remains MIT-licensed, and forks must use a different name and logo.

**Discussion**: The thread is mostly grateful and unsurprised: commenters thank Lucas Larroche and praise the clear, responsible archival rather than silent rot, while a few say they would not pick Pico for new work today. The sharpest disagreement is over the stated need for modern CSS features: several commenters call OKLCH, anchor positioning and cascade layers unnecessary complexity for a minimal framework, while others defend them as easier for color scales and layout, and one detailed reply argues OKLCH matters mainly for perceptual interpolation, not for specifying ordinary colors. ## 14. Iterating Backwards in C {#story-14}
*#29 on HN · [davidpriver.com](https://www.davidpriver.com/Iterating-backwards-in-C.html) · 95 points · 55 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50249598)*

这是一个 GitHub 仓库（emmilco/us-tax-model），它把美国联邦个人所得税的表格和说明书编码成 YAML 数据：每一行都带有指向已归档 IRS 页面的引用，这些引用包含哈希和定位符，共 2,313 个锚点、1,252 条引文。它使用约 20 种固定原子运算来表述表达式，组合成 2,384 行计算；用 TypeScript 和 Lean 写了两套求值器，并在 1,648 个用例上得到 0 处不一致；还通过 PolicyEngine-US 2.49.4、Tax-Calculator 6.8.5 和 OpenTaxSolver 23.06 对 1,700 份生成申报表做外部引擎差异比对。作者明确说这是「100% vibe coded」的后台项目，绝对不能用于真实报税，也不会上传或提交任何申报表；项目自己的证据记分板只显示 1,515 行中有 198 行（13.1%）获得了 Claude 模型家族之外的断言支持。

**Discussion**: 最集中的质疑是维护成本：一位做过中小企业薪资系统的评论者说，即便在政策稳定的年份，团队每年也要花数周到数月来跟进并核对数字，这就是开源报税软件一直做不起来、会计师能收 $400/小时的原因，而且连 IRS 自己都有已知问题清单。反驳者认为这正是 AI 的强项——规则都写在 irs.gov 和 Federal Register 上，明年重跑同样的提示词就行；还有人反讽说 React、Vue、Kubernetes 也年年变，编码代理照样应付，并反问维持更新靠的究竟是「智能」还是别的资源。支持方有一位一手经验者：他用 AI 读 IRS 和州税法，做了自己继承 IRA（十年内必须清空、按年龄计算 RMD）的多年提款与税负模拟和优化器，结果找到了一些他不知道的抵扣，他也独立核对过。另有人指出 IRS 的 Direct File 已由内部专家做过并开源，但项目随后被砍，认为报税是最不适合 vibe coding 的领域之一；还有人提出这个形式化方法是否会发现 IRS 指引自身的矛盾，回应者则说矛盾早已被税务律师当作策略来利用，发现矛盾也没有实际救济渠道。整体共识是想法有价值、证据链设计诚实，但覆盖率和人工复核仍远不足以实用。
