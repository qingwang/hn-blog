## 2. Rust 1.100.0 {#story-2}
*#2 on HN · [github.com](https://github.com/rust-lang/rust/releases/tag/1.100.0) · 266 points · 56 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50471793)*

The GitHub release page for Rust 1.100.0 (the release notes rendered only partially; the official announcement is at blog.rust-lang.org/2026/11/12/Rust-1.100.0/). The headline items are stabilization of the never type (with Infallible now an alias to !, and never-type fallback changed to ! on all editions), custom allocators (Allocator impls for Box/Arc/Rc/&A, Vec::into_raw_parts_with_allocator), and a new Cargo lint system with a [lints.cargo] table including a built-in unused-dependency check. Cargo also gains dependency cooldowns / minimum publish age (RFC 3923), cargo install now uses the lockfile inside the .crate tarball so --locked is a no-op, and the intermediate build-artifact directory layout changed. Platforms: wasm32-wasip3 and i686-pc-windows-gnu move to Tier 2, powerpc64-sony-ps3 added as Tier 3; std bumps to Unicode 18.0.0 and WASI SDK 34.

## 3. Dwarf planet candidate appears which takes roughly 280 years to orbit our sun {#story-3}
*#3 on HN · [smithsonianmag.com](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/astronomers-found-a-mysterious-object-orbiting-far-beyond-pluto-it-could-be-a-new-dwarf-planet-hidden-in-our-solar-system-180989857/) · 42 points · 7 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50472490)*

Smithsonian magazine reports that Smithsonian astrophysicist Matthew Holman and Kevin Napier found the object, designated 2026 ED138, while sifting data from the Vera C. Rubin Observatory in Chile. It sits about 46 times farther from the Sun than Earth and takes 281.1 years to orbit, and the team traced it in archival images back to 1937. Its diameter is estimated at 370 to 930 miles, so it may be round enough to join Pluto, Eris, Makemake, Haumea and Ceres as a dwarf planet, but infrared observations or a stellar occultation are needed to settle its size.

**Discussion**: The seven-comment thread spends little time arguing about the discovery itself. The most substantive exchange revisits Pluto's 2006 demotion, with one commenter calling it correct and saying the dwarf-planet category was necessary once Ceres, Eris, Haumea and Makemake were known. Another commenter asks whether this is the long-hypothesized Planet X; replies point out that Planet X has referred to several different proposed bodies, that Planet Nine is the current name for the leading hypothesis, and that dwarf planets are far too small to produce the orbital effects such a planet would need. A side note appreciates that the object was found on photographic plates from 1937.

**Worth reading**
- [ecshafer](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50473142): Argues Pluto's demotion was the right call and that the dwarf-planet class was needed to accommodate Ceres, Eris, Haumea and Makemake.

## 6. Ask HN: Microsoft/Azure Outage? {#story-6}
*#6 on HN · text post · 247 points · 122 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50471668)*

An Ask HN text post from a user whose organization's Microsoft cloud connections all dropped within five minutes, noting that even the status page would not load. Microsoft's own updates (ticket EX1550543 / MO1550821) began by blaming “elevated load across multiple regions” from an unexpected traffic pattern, then reversed course and said the load was not from more traffic but from a large number of servers becoming unexpectedly unavailable, with deployments and config changes paused as a precaution.

**Discussion**: Most of the thread is first-hand confirmation — whole orgs halted, email and Teams dead at a non-tech megacorp, Mac CI runners on GitHub Actions never picked up — with Azure reported healthy in several regions. A long subthread pushes back on Downdetector once it showed Gmail, AWS and Cloudflare also down: commenters argue the crowdsourced signal is genuine and useful for “is it me or them?” but systematically wrong, because people whose email breaks mark the whole internet as down. Two arguments run in parallel about blame. One camp ties the run of 2026 incidents to AI-written infrastructure code and to Microsoft's voluntary-exit offers to roughly a quarter of staff, which others correct: the buyout is offered to ~25% to achieve a ~5% cut and avoids WARN notice and unemployment-insurance costs. The other camp says cascading failures in over-complicated infrastructure are not “AI slop” and not even a software-quality problem. A side argument rejects the “cloud gives you no single point of failure” framing as a sales pitch a Microsoft account team actually made, with one admin reporting more Exchange downtime in a single day than their on-prem servers had all previous year.

**Worth reading**

## 19. Our software engineering interviews are changing a little {#story-19}
*#25 on HN · [blog.janestreet.com](https://blog.janestreet.com/software-engineering-interviews-are-changing-a-little/) · 115 points · 99 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50467297)*

## 20. A live tree of systemd jobs on the boot console {#story-20}
*#26 on HN · [codeberg.org](https://codeberg.org/wvhulle/bootvine) · 316 points · 119 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50425017)*

这是 wvhulle 在 Codeberg 上发布的开源项目 bootvine（GPL-3.0-or-later）：它接管启动时的控制台，用一棵不断更新的 systemd 单元依赖树取代滚屏的 kernel 和 systemd 日志行，并在树的空白处显示最慢单元的计时线。它还覆盖关机过程、就地处理 LUKS 解锁等口令输入，并在单元失败时给出最后的 journal 行。项目包含 bootvine 自己的 systemd initrd，宣称在 NixOS、Ubuntu 等发行版上可用，并提供一个 `nix run` 示例虚拟机（故意配置了慢速和失败的单元）来演示整块屏幕。

**Discussion**: 讨论整体上是称赞：多人说这是三十年来启动界面少有的实质改进，一位用户称其为出色的 UI/UX 设计，也有人希望发行版默认采用，并建议对怕被吓到的用户留一个 splash 兜底。最常见的纠正是截图里 12 秒的启动是刻意放慢的演示机 `sample-vm-slow-services`，不代表日常启动耗时；仍有人认为在真正只需两秒启动的笔记本上渲染这棵树本身就不划算，还担心系统出问题时能否可靠显示。话题很快分叉到 UEFI、DDR5 内存训练：几位用户报告 Framework 64GB、NUC、96GB 乃至 128GB 内存的机器首次启动黑屏数分钟甚至十几分钟，有人因此误判主板 DOA 而退货。后半段是熟悉的 systemd 争论：反对者列举组件过多、难以调试、DBus/Varlink 耦合、定时器与 cron 的差异，以及一条把 XZ 后门归咎于 systemd 生态的激烈发言；支持者则说集成带来的依赖可靠性、日志与监督省去了旧式 init 脚本的重试逻辑和谎言状态。另有人指出 Codeberg 已限制 LLM 生成内容，质疑这个项目是否合规，但未得到明确答复。

**Worth reading**
- [lexicality](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50466459): 澄清截图中的 12 秒来自故意放慢的 `sample-vm-slow-services` 演示机，而非真实启动时间。
- [i80and](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50467480): 第一手经历：Framework 笔记本配 64GB DDR5，首次内存训练长达数分钟且全程黑屏，一度以为机器故障。
- [stryan](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50468718): 纠正「systemd 有 130 万行代码」的说法：该统计包含文档等，当时实际不足 50 万行，且仓库含多个非必需的独立项目。
