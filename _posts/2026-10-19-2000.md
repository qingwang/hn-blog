## 1. MiniMax M3.1 Flash {#story-1}
*#1 on HN · [minimax.io](https://www.minimax.io/blog/minimax-m31f) · 153 points · 42 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50154540)*

MiniMax's blog post announces M3.1 Flash, a successor to M3 pitched at agentic work, coding, computer use and security, with inference roughly three times faster (200+ TPS output, 170+ TPS median) and agentic usage cost 70% below its predecessor. Claimed scores include 73.7% on DeepSWE v1.1, 76.2 on BankerToolBench, 61.7 on OSWorld V2, 29.6 on Agents' Last Exam and 82% on CyberGym. Pricing is $0.15 per million input tokens, $0.60 output and $0.015 cached input; it is available via the MiniMax API, M Plan and MiniMax Code, with weights promised later. The post also reveals that the anonymous model 「Space Bunny」 was an early checkpoint of M3.1 Flash, trained on over 200 trillion tokens.

**Discussion**: The thread's most useful work is arithmetic: one commenter reconstructs the 70% cost claim against M3's $0.30/$1.20/$0.06 pricing and concludes it holds up (input halved, cache read cut from 20% to 10% of input), while noting DeepSeek V4.1 matches the token prices and still undercuts on cache reads at $0.003. A sub-thread argues the Claude subscription is still the better deal because of its heavy API subsidy and stronger model, countered by people pointing out they are optimising for price, not capability. Commenters dig into what 「Flash」 now means — one cites MiniMax's own FAQ for 428B total / 23B active parameters, close to DeepSeek 4.1 Flash, and several note that Flash no longer implies small or light when base models are 1T+. The main scepticism is benchmark trust: people report that 「Space Bunny」 made egregious errors in opencode testing and that users went back to Qwen 3.8 Flash Next or DeepSeek V4 Flash, others flag that many models cluster suspiciously around 74% on DeepSWE, and one asks why these releases never show wall-clock time per task. Thread admin also notes the minimax.io domain was shadowbanned, which is why repeated submissions appeared.

**Worth reading**
- [minimaxir](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50156178): Breaks down the 70% cost claim against M3's $0.30/$1.20/$0.06 pricing and shows it is plausible, while noting DeepSeek V4.1 still wins on cache reads at $0.003 per million.
- [penagwin](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50155897): Cites MiniMax's own FAQ for the real spec — 428B total, 23B active parameters — and argues 「Flash」 no longer means small, light or especially fast.
- [walrus01](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50156022): Says the DeepSWE and TerminalBench 4.0 scores don't match what opencode users saw from 「Space Bunny」, many of whom reportedly returned to Qwen 3.8 Flash Next or DeepSeek V4 Flash after bad errors.

## 2. Wine ported to OpenBSD {#story-2}
*#2 on HN · [marc.info](https://marc.info/?l=openbsd-ports&m=179236125692175) · 364 points · 102 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50152307)*

这是一封发到 openbsd-ports 邮件列表的帖子，宣布 OpenBSD 有了一个 Wine 移植草案，作者把它称为今年最出人意料的移植提案。主要工作由 Andrii 和 Fabien 完成；关键的障碍是 %gs/gsbase 寄存器，Andrii 通过模拟 %gs 前缀绕过了它，而不是设置 gsbase。数十个 64 位 Windows 程序都能正常运行，包括上个月刚发布的《巫师3》重制版；由于 WoW64 无法工作，32 位程序仍然完全跑不了；内置测试暂时禁用，并且上游 Wine 一直不愿意接受 OpenBSD 补丁。

**Discussion**: 讨论从惊叹转向了对 AI 使用的追问。OpenBSD 最近公开拒绝作者无法证明来源的 LLM 生成代码，这引出一种猜测，认为这个移植也是 AI 写的；但移植作者 NethIafin 解释说，他只是用 AI 做原型、学习 %gs 编码，然后自己重写了代码，帮助他测试的 asveikau 也为他说话。版权方面的怀疑者反驳说，OpenBSD 的规则针对的是版权来源，而不是 AI 辅助本身；而长期贡献者则担心，一个拒绝 LLM 工具的小众系统会进一步落后，另一些人则反驳说，Linux 已经在接受 AI 代码，BSD 保持干净是有价值的。技术层面上，brynet 和 bentley 纠正了认为 OpenBSD 没有 3D 驱动的误解：即将发布的 8.0 版本会带上从 Linux 6.18.y 移植来的 inteldrm/radeondrm/amdgpu 驱动；Wine 开发者 coldpie 读了补丁，认为这些改动写得不错，只有 gs 寄存器相关工作确实困难。另一个反复出现的主题是英伟达完全不支持，以及该移植可能不得不继续留在源码树外维护。

**Worth reading**
- [NethIafin](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50156224): 移植作者 NethIafin 详细解释了工作流程：用 AI 试探 %gs 方案是否可行，并生成解码表，然后自己写代码；他说自己现在理解了 REX/SIB/ModRM、前缀和信号桩的来龙去脉。
- [t-3](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50153536): 引用 Andrii 在列表上更早的发言：Wine 维护者不愿意接受哪怕是让 Wine 在 OpenBSD 上编译通过的补丁，而且 %gs 桩绝不能上游化，因为这会改变模拟的应用程序代码。
- [coldpie](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50153584): Wine 开发者 coldpie 读了实际补丁，说它们写得不错；gs 寄存器处理是最难的部分，因为 Windows 用它做线程本地存储；他建议上游化，同时提醒有些地方看起来有点 hacky。

## 3. Android apps running native on macOS {#story-3}
*#3 on HN · [github.com](https://github.com/kksimp/Mac.apk) · 87 points · 29 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50154624)*

Mac.apk 是 Voltare 的 Kaleb 发布的开源（AGPL v3，另有商业授权）项目，目标是在 Apple Silicon Mac 上直接运行 Android 应用：没有模拟器、没有虚拟机，也没有底层 Linux 内核；应用作为普通 macOS 进程启动，使用 Mac 的 GPU 绘制图形，具备 Dock 图标、原生窗口、深色模式；窗口标题栏左上角有一个返回按钮。技术栈包括重新实现的 Android framework、原生运行时、arm64 loader 与 Bionic shim、ARM32 重编译器，图形依赖 ANGLE 和 MoltenVK，并捆绑了基于 OpenJDK 构建的 JRE。项目自述为 alpha 阶段软件，需要 macOS 27 起（从 v1.0.1702 版本开始），安装包约 280 MB，每个应用占用 1.5 到 3 倍 APK 体积，支持 .apk/.apkm/.xapk 等格式；作者特别警告应用没有手机式沙箱，会以用户账户权限读写文件。

**Discussion**: 评论整体认可技术上有趣，但多数人强调其实用性受限于 Google Play Services 和 SafetyNet 依赖：有人指出自定义 ROM 式方案（如 Aurora Store，项目文档已列出）能补上部分 Google 服务，但银行应用、甚至火车时刻表、超市目录、打车软件都因 SafetyNet DRM 而无法运行，有人呼吁欧盟立法禁止这种状况。也有人质疑代码质量，认为命名（Mac.apk 会变成 Mac.apk.app）和数据结构带有明显 LLM 生成痕迹，并指出它似乎依赖 JVM 而不支持原生 ARM 二进制、且把应用数据存在 .app 包内。一位游戏行业从业者反驳「开发者会转向 Steam 设备」的乐观论调，称大厂通常只测 Windows 版能否在 Steam Deck 上跑，像 BG3 那样的完整 Linux 移植极为罕见。另有分支讨论 Minecraft Bedrock 在 Mac 上的需求与既有兼容层项目，以及 Windows 上已被微软砍掉的 WSA 和 BlueStacks 替代方案。

**Worth reading**
- [gambiting](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50156368): 一位在多家大型发行商工作过的游戏从业者表示，Steam Deck 测试通常只是确认 Windows 版能否运行，完整的 Linux 移植极其罕见且难以立项。
- [LoganDark](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50156053): 指出两个具体问题：似乎靠 JVM 执行而非支持原生 ARM 二进制，以及应用数据存放在 .app 内部。
- [franga2000](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50156348): 指出 SafetyNet DRM 已蔓延到火车时刻表、超市目录、打车等日常应用，而不只是银行 App，并批评 Google Play 开发者后台还在鼓励加装这类保护。

**Worth reading**

## 7. Timestral: Mistral's time-series foundation models {#story-7}
*#7 on HN · [mistral.ai](https://mistral.ai/news/timestral/) · 430 points · 82 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50150434)*

Mistral released Timestral, its first family of time-series foundation models, in four sizes from 5M to 209M parameters, claiming first place on all three main public benchmarks (GIFT-Eval, TIME and fev-bench) among pretrained zero-shot models, with the 209M version beating the previous state of the art at two-thirds the parameter count. The models produce probabilistic multivariate forecasts with 21 quantiles, accept past-only and known-future covariates including categoricals, run on CPU (10 ms for one-day-ahead with the 5M model, 140 ms with the 209M on 8 threads), and were trained mostly on synthetic data with a two-stage recipe and an 8192-token context extended 4x in post-training. Weights are on GitHub and Hugging Face, with a technical report promised later and fine-tuning offered through Mistral Forge.

**Discussion**: The dominant thread is skepticism about whether one model can span all time series: an audio/vision ML researcher argues that the whole value is in covariates like holidays, weather and fuel prices, and that a 5M model seeing an arbitrary 0/1 flag has no idea what a bank holiday is; others counter that scale lets a model learn recognition and continuation jointly, and that for 10k+ series a single pretrained model beats fitting one model per series by hand. A practitioner running 400k series every 30 minutes pushes back hard, saying a per-series XGBoost has never been beaten and is faster even including load time. Several commenters challenge the evaluation framing — one proposes testing on high signal-to-noise synthetic processes where counting-based methods set the bar, another notes a 0.03 benchmark improvement is presented graphically as a huge gap, and a third dismisses the blog's plant scenario as an imaginary customer and calls such models numerology. A separate cluster corrects the licensing: the weights are downloadable but under a “Modified” MIT that requires a commercial deal above roughly $20m monthly revenue, so calling it free or open is misleading. Off-topic but prominent, Mistral Vibe users report the new Mistral 4 being very slow, and a Mistral Vibe PM replies that rollout is A/B tested and the stack is being tuned.

**Worth reading**
- [joefourier](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50151552): Argues that external drivers — time of day, holidays, weather, gas prices — should be the basis of the model rather than an optional add-on, and that tiny models cannot know what a categorical like “bank holiday” means.
- [niltecedu](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50156050): First-hand counterpoint from someone forecasting 400k series every 30 minutes: a per-series XGBoost has never been beaten and is faster, pretrained model load included.
- [pquerna](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50152509): Corrects the “free and open weights” claim: the license is a modified MIT that requires buying from Mistral above about $20m monthly revenue.

## 8. Mercury 3: Diffusion at scale {#story-8}
*#8 on HN · [inceptionlabs.ai](https://www.inceptionlabs.ai/blog/introducing-mercury-3) · 120 points · 49 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50155245)*

Inception Labs 发布了 Mercury 3，这是其基于扩散（diffusion）架构的文本 LLM 的第三代，声称性能优于 GPT-6 Luna 与 Claude Haiku 5.5，同时在 NVIDIA Blackwell GPU 上达到 1,068 tokens/秒，快 4–8 倍。上下文窗口从 260K 提升到 1M，定价为每百万输入 $0.20、每百万输出 $0.70（发布期五折，输入 $0.10、输出 $0.35，缓存输入再打 9 折），并提供 1 亿免费 token。与 Mercury 1 相比，总参数扩大了 40 倍以上，各项基准上的智能水平约提升 3 倍；Inception 据此主张自回归模型的 scaling law 同样适用于扩散模型，并引用了 TinyFish、RevisionDojo、Octen、Lorikeet 等早期客户关于延迟的说法。

## 20. Show HN: I built a task system to handle the drudgery so I could focus on tasks {#story-20}
*#27 on HN · [3anddoneapp.com](https://3anddoneapp.com/try) · 27 points · 27 comments · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50151980)*

The page is the landing/demo for 3 & Done, a task system by a 44-year software engineering veteran (dmyle, ex-YouTube/Google and Meta) who says he burned through roughly 25 task systems before deciding the real problem was the maintenance work, not the list. The pitch: dump tasks in by voice or text (audio is stored only in the browser/device, never uploaded), and each morning an email ranks everything and names the three most important things with reasons — deadlines, urgency, items sitting too long — using Anthropic models, Next.js on Vercel and Neon Postgres. Pricing is a 14-day trial with card required, then $99/year or $9.99/month, with JSON/plain-text export and no training on user data.

**Discussion**: The dominant reaction is price shock: multiple commenters argue $10/month for a todo app is indefensible when a $20/month ChatGPT subscription can be prompted into sending a daily email, and several say they would rather vibe-code their own tool in an afternoon — one reports doing exactly that and getting a perfect fit. The author (dmayle) answers that this is not a one-shot generated app (research, UI flows, schema rewrites, prompt evals with stored expected rankings), that he rejected ads because they break the thinking moment, and that anyone can sign up, immediately cancel in billing, and still use the full two weeks. Privacy is the second thread: critics say they will not pay to hand task data to a third party, and the author replies that tasks go to Anthropic under no-training commercial terms, that support access requires explicit temporary consent, that audio never leaves the device, and that end-to-end encryption is structurally incompatible with a server that ranks your list — so there is no self-hosted version. A minority pushes back on the criticism, calling the long personal history the most valuable part of the post, and others plug cheaper or self-hostable alternatives (a $2 one-time iOS app called Grask, aventasks.dev). Nobody converged: the split is between people who think the price is absurd for a thin wrapper and people who value a maintained, zero-maintenance service enough to pay for it.

**Worth reading**
- [dmayle](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50155621): The author explains that end-to-end encryption would stop server-side ranking cold, which is why the design relies on contractual no-training terms instead, and says a self-hosted build is not something he can maintain.
